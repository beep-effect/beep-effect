# Reflection — quality-speedup closeout (2026-08-04)

## Summary

One overnight session took the packet from empty to a published PR (#548):
five Codex Sol-medium research jobs + a sequential census re-run produced the
three evidence reports; a grill session locked seven decisions; scope was
amended mid-flight (single PR; MimeType fix, CI concurrency cap, and bounded
docgen pulled forward); the tstyche removal (250 deletions / ~200 edits)
executed from the committed inventory; and five verify round-trips plus four
publish attempts drove it to a green proof and an open PR. Measured headline:
`@beep/schema` barrel importers dropped from 17.79s to 0.445s check time, and
~110s of serial lane time left every local verify.

## Tooling experience

**Worked:** the box-typecheck-cost census method reproduced the prior session's
numbers within 1% (independent twin-session probes corroborated); codex
background jobs with file deliverables kept the main context lean; the a1
surface inventory was accurate enough that the removal required zero
out-of-inventory edits; Monitor-driven background verifies allowed fixing one
round's failures while understanding the next.

**Didn't:** the publish staged-changes contract vs its own "amend and retry"
hint (finding 1); empty-changeset parsing (finding 3); the all-or-nothing
proof state (finding 2 — five round-trips, one of them for a formatting nit).

**Frustrating:** environment moved mid-ship — four OSV advisories published
between the twin's green verify and ours turned the security lane red through
no change of ours; the fix (override pin bumps + syncpack range alignment)
took three iterations because three tools (osv, syncpack, knip/fallow) each
police a different facet of the same pin.

**Wished existed:** per-lane proof resume (already designed in the
evidence-loop packet); a single command to bump a transitive pin that
satisfies overrides + syncpack + knip + fallow at once.

## Implementation improvement opportunities

Findings 4 and 5 above; plus the removal left two helper twins converged into
a clone (suppressed, consolidation queued) and five dead imports that only
surfaced lane-by-lane — a post-deletion `tsgo` sweep over BOTH src and test
projects of touched packages would have caught them in one pass.

## Goal & prompt critique

The mission prompt's inventory-first, instrument-before-treating design paid
for itself (it caught its own wrong claim about per-package dtslint
tsconfigs). Its PR topology did not survive contact with the owner's queue —
finding 6. The "ask before deleting unique assertions" gate was resolved
cleanly by the grill's delete-all decision with the committed port-later
ledger.

## Lessons

- **HIGH:** check-time bombs and instantiation bombs are different diseases;
  budget both (finding 5).
- **HIGH:** on a branch that touches turbo.json or the lockfile, every bounded
  proof escalates to full — plan one full-proof cycle into any global-config
  PR and batch such changes together.
- **MEDIUM:** when two sessions share a worktree, reads are corroboration but
  writes need an explicit single-driver decision (grill Q7 resolved it here).
- **MEDIUM:** patch-bumping a pinned transitive means touching overrides,
  dependency ranges, syncpack, and the knip/fallow ignore surfaces together —
  do them as one unit.
- **LOW:** zsh `print -r` writes literal \t; use printf for TSV emission in
  measurement scripts.
