# Reflection — repo-crispening-orchestration (2026-07-07, claude)

## Summary

The packet crispened all 97 in-scope packages across pilot + five family waves,
burning the 1,783-actionable baseline to zero-or-ledgered, flipped all four
families to blocking in `standards/schema-crispening.policy.jsonc` with a
regression fixture per flip, and shipped the tracked 4,119-entry
`standards/schema-catalog.generated.jsonc` behind a deterministic
`bun run beep lint schema-catalog` command. Verdict: the enforce-first,
family-scoped-wave design worked; nearly all post-wave cost came from
seed-dependent property-law flakes and full-graph-only CI failures that local
gates could not see.

## Tooling experience

- **Worked:** the ratchet policy design (advisory → blocking per family) let
  novel cards land repo-green and flip cheaply; `lint schema-first --write`
  then flip-status-to-exception was a reliable ledger flow; codex background
  lanes with one-writer-per-package (D2) parallelized waves without write
  conflicts; the §5.7 regression fixture caught real policy-resolution bugs at
  each flip.
- **Didn't:** local gates could not reproduce CI's full-graph lanes — TS7056
  only appears under tsc-based full compilation, and the architecture
  operation-plan ENOENT only under the repo-root vitest project — so two whole
  CI rounds were spent on failures invisible to the wave battery.
- **Frustrating:** six sequential CI rounds on the final wave PR, each
  surfacing exactly one new failure class (TS7056/docgen → lint-policy/test →
  PGLite timeout/plan ENOENT → venice flake → coverage ratchet → racy ordering
  assertion); every fix round cost ~30 minutes of watch latency. A
  pre-merge "run the union of everything CI runs" entrypoint would have
  collapsed most of these into one round.
- **Wished existed:** a seed-sweep runner for property tests (the manual
  numRuns-bump sweep across 162 suites found the venice counterexample in
  minutes and proved the rest clean — that should be a first-class command,
  not a sed loop).

## Implementation improvement opportunities

- Promote the fixed-point normalization check into `SchemaUtils` (see todo) —
  three packages now hand-roll it.
- The schema catalog's kind taxonomy is heuristic (call-shape matching);
  a follow-up could derive kinds from the checked AST nodes the schema-first
  detectors already classify, removing the parallel classifier.
- `expectSubstringBefore`-style ordering assertions over grouped-concurrent
  step logs are structurally racy; the quality-task tests should assert set
  membership or per-step exit metadata, never log order, wherever
  `runStepGroup` is involved.

## Goal & prompt critique

The SPEC was strong: locked decisions (G1–G7) and the nine fences eliminated
nearly all mid-flight relitigating, and §5.3's "byte-identical wire + one
round-trip law per absorbed invariant" was the single highest-value clause in
the packet — it caught real wire drift four times. Two revisions worth making:
(1) state in P2's exit criteria that flipping a family to blocking will
surface additional latent findings beyond the advisory baseline and that
burning them is in-scope wave work; (2) require round-trip laws to run at a
high-runs tier somewhere in CI, since 15–25-run suites systematically defer
seed-dependent failures to unrelated later PRs.
