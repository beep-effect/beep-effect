## Tooling experience

- **Worked:** The existing Effect models, CLI entrypoints, Phoenix driver,
  real root probe, pinned collector/backend images and private installed-service
  proofs supported a source-to-backend chain with observable rollback. Independent
  reviews found concrete defects and an important missing AC12 association.
- **Didn't:** A low task allowance invalidated an earlier broad CLI attempt.
  After that allowance was corrected, a scope-exit race in the protective wrapper
  stopped another run safely. These were retained as failures, not converted into
  passes because narrower tests worked. The wrapper now requires positive evidence
  that the vanished unit and resource group are both absent before continuing.
- **Frustrating:** Generic desktop OOM wording, large inherited gate surfaces,
  base-pinned scanner policy, stale receipt summaries and separate review/CI timing
  made causal attribution harder than raw pass/fail reporting suggested.
- **Wished existed:** One generated evidence index joining a declared requirement,
  exact execution source hashes, committed blobs, current hosted result and
  operational rollout state. The committed binding and acceptance audit provide
  the packet-level version; they are not a new universal platform claim.

## Implementation improvement opportunities

The useful improvement is preserving meaning across the complete path. Separate
identities, explicit unknown populations, target-bound receipts and deterministic
event IDs explain what happened and what remains uncertain. The final operator
notes now explain insufficient file limits, canonical-path refusals, fixed backup
slots and the candidate-matching risk of unsalted remote repository identities.

A backend row does not expose every OTLP resource field through the retained REST
shape. The accepted synthetic proof therefore documents its transitive host
association through unique route/event IDs and the checked local journal. It does
not invent independent backend host-field preservation.

## Goal & prompt critique

I would add this sentence to future launch instructions: “Before any live canary,
name the exact declared host/root, producer fixture classes, independent expected
IDs, privacy boundary and rollback owner, then bind those facts in a durable plan.”
That makes the AC12 proof obligation visible earlier without shrinking scope.

I would also require a proof-cost plan before broad repository verification:
identify inherited work, resource admission, task counts, runtime deadlines and
which authoritative hosted gates replace a constrained local replay. The user’s
request for first-principles work justified substantial implementation, but did not
justify treating repeated broad attempts as progress without new evidence.

## Lessons (confidence-tiered)

- **HIGH — Critical:** Preserve uncertainty at every acknowledgment boundary.
  Committed local metadata, collector acceptance and backend visibility prove
  different facts; lost replies must not become fabricated delivery or blind replay.
- **HIGH — Critical:** Verify exact source and physical ownership before reusing a
  proof or stopping a process. The v2/v3 separation and scope-exit guard demonstrate
  why historical evidence and live identities must remain distinct.
- **HIGH — Critical:** A synthetic canary is not a fleet denominator. Real Grok,
  Claude, Codex, ChatGPT Desktop, Muse and Cursor coverage remains unqualified until
  each declared producer route is separately enrolled and reconciled.
- **MEDIUM — Best practice:** Produce small, inspectable proof receipts first and
  retain failures with their actual causes. Broad CI should confirm the integrated
  change, not erase what local failures taught us.
- **LOW — Consideration:** A future integrated evidence browser could make these
  distinctions easier for operators to inspect, but a new UI was not required to
  establish this foundation's correctness.
