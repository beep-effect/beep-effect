# Reflection — ship-velocity P1 closeout (2026-08-17, claude)

P0 and P1 are complete. P1 shipped as four PRs — #737 (B1 same-argv lanes), #736 (E1 publish
regenerates the derived goals INDEX), #738 (A7 monitor hardening), #743 (C1 remote-read cache
posture) — implemented in parallel worktrees with a single serialized proof slot.

## What the parallelism actually bought, and what it cost

Four items were implemented concurrently and proved one at a time. Implementation parallelism was
real: three agents wrote code while a fourth item held the lock. Proof serialization was the
binding constraint, and every avoidable re-proof came from the same two classes — a gate that only
runs hosted (coverage), and a fingerprint that invalidates on staging rather than on content. The
second is measurable: one no-op `git add` cost 17 minutes of the shared slot.

The compounding win was intel transfer. Each lesson paid for once was handed to the agents still
holding: after E1 lost a round trip to the coverage ratchet, B1 went green first try; after A7's
`strictEffectProvide` miss, C1 was told to run the in-process gates as its *last* step. B1 and C1
each needed materially fewer cycles than E1 and A7 for equivalent work.

## The law that covers three different failures

"A gate result is only valid for the tree it ran against." It covers the stale-`dist` masking that
made a local check-lane sweep look green while CI had no dists, A7's `test-tsgo` run that preceded
the test it needed to check, and the B5 fingerprint defect. Three surfaces, one sentence.

## What review caught that proofs could not

Three of four PRs needed a fix wave after a green proof. The Greptile P1 on #736 was a real defect
— a retry re-running a recorder-mutating phase, leaving one step id with both a failed and a passed
entry — and it was fixed by snapshot/rewind and proven by removing the rewind and watching the test
fail (`expected 2 to be 1`). Against that, one reviewer finding was refuted with source citations
(`Effect.reduce`'s zero *is* `LazyArg<Z>`), and one review answer went undelivered because the bot
auto-resolved its own thread before the reply landed — the explanation had to be posted as a
PR-level comment instead. Both directions matter: a bot deserves the same evidentiary standard as a
human, and a resolved thread is not the same as an answered one.

## Evidence quality

Two habits are worth keeping from the agents' work. C1 refused to attribute 312 local cache hits it
could not separate from its own earlier runs, resting its claim on a single lane's 30/31 remote
restorations instead — and its correction to C5's metric definition probably saved the dashboard
from being built on a ratio that structurally under-reports the cache. A7 refused to present an
absent registration-backoff line as a field test, and reported the one piece of real field evidence
it did get: a `stale` reply outcome on a live PR proving "stale is not a failure" exits 0.
