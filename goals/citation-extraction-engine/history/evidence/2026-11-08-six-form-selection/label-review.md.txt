## Explicit local-link interpretation

Authority indices are local to each supplied input and index that row's expected occurrences; they are not global corpus IDs, persisted entity IDs, truth judgments, or assertions that a legal authority remains valid. A fully written case locator independently establishes its own authority using volume, canonical reporter and first page. Same volume/reporter alone does not merge different first pages. A fully written statute/regulation establishes title plus full base section; written subsections remain pin metadata. Repeated identical title/base-section occurrences share authority.

An immediately following Id. can resolve to the uniquely established preceding local authority in `local-id`. A short case resolves only when its exact volume/reporter matches one unique preceding locally established case authority. Named supra resolves only when its exact party token identifies one unique preceding locally established case authority. No first/nearest match, fuzzy party matching, external lookup, or knowledge of Roe from outside the supplied document resolves an orphan or ambiguous reference. These rules should be written into the frozen profile rather than inferred from its examples.

The two Alpha full cases remain independent despite sharing `Alpha`, `410` and `U.S.`. Their different first pages establish authorities 0 and 1. Each later short/supra has two admissible local antecedents, so candidate authorities `[0,1]` and Ambiguous are correct. A hypothetical last-citation preference would not make them unique under this rule. These are controlled synthetic ambiguity tests, not a claim that both invented Alpha cases exist at those reporter locations.
