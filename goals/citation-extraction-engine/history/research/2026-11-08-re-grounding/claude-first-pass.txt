## 1. Bottom line

1. **Stop exhaustive parity work now.** Do not finish P2-P5 as written.
2. **The goal, finished perfectly, still could not detect a fabricated citation.** eyecite's "resolution" links short forms to antecedents inside one document (V c42). Checking that an authority exists is a separate layer, and the SPEC excludes it (`SPEC.md:474-475`).
3. **Freeze the engine, don't delete it yet.** It is merged and composes end to end. Sunk cost shouldn't keep it, but replacing it unmeasured isn't justified either.
4. **Run one bake-off on an independent labelled set:** the current engine, eyecite-ts, and Python eyecite as a measured reference. Nobody has accuracy numbers for any of them on real documents.
5. **Ship the smallest product: a per-citation check report.** It covers extraction, local linking and exact quote verification against source text in hand, plus an authority-lookup port that honestly reports `NotChecked` until the operator decides on paid access.
6. **The one money question:** CourtListener's published terms treat internal firm tooling as commercial use (E c96). That is an operator decision under the `AGENTS.md` Autonomy charter.

**Decision needed from the operator:** adopt the constraint changes in [section 9](#9-explicit-changes-to-original-constraints). Most are a return to what the goal said at graduation.

## 2. The five layers

"Resolution" means two different things in this domain, and that ambiguity matters here, so I split it.

```text
draft text
   |
[L1  Extraction]        Find the citation, its span and fields.          Deterministic.
   |
[L2a Local linking]     Id./supra/short form -> antecedent in this doc.  Heuristic.
   |
[L2b Authority lookup]  Does this cite match a real authority? Which?    Needs a corpus.
   |
[L3  Source text]       Is the quote an exact slice of the source?       Deterministic, needs the source.
   |
[L4  Support]           Does the source support the proposition?         LLM triage, attorney decides.
   |
[L5  Good law]          Is it still good law?                            External feed.
```

| Layer | Answers | Failure it catches | Open tooling | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | What was cited, where? | None alone; it feeds the others | eyecite, eyecite-ts, CiteURL, the current engine | V c32, c37 |
| L2a | What does "Id." refer to? | Wrong antecedent | eyecite is heuristic | V c42 |
| L2b | Does it exist? | **Fabricated** | CourtListener lookup: case citations only | E c81, c82, c85 |
| L3 | Is the quote real? | **False quotes** | Repo's `VerifiedSpan`, already built | R `DECISIONS.md:300-304,400-403` |
| L4 | Does it support the point? | **Misrepresented** | None reliable. LLMs caught 93-100% of wrong-case but 37-61% of wrong-pinpoint errors on opinions, 52-83% on briefs | E c75-c78 |
| L5 | Still good law? | **Outdated** | CourtListener's graph is incomplete and is not a citator | E c70, c73 |

**Why extraction can't be the center.** The Charlotin database (2,266 decisions, CC BY 4.0, downloadable CSV) has filter counts of Fabricated 1,856, Misrepresented 960, False Quotes 602, Outdated Advice 38 (E c0, c2, c3). These overlap (they sum past 2,266), and the set contains only decisions where a tribunal found or implied reliance, so it is not a census (E c1). Even so, none of those categories is detected by L1 alone, and the biggest needs L2b. **Parity work is polishing an input to a guard that doesn't exist yet.**

Two more findings point the same way:

- CLERC: GPT-4o's citation false-positive rate fell from 71.49% to 6.41% when given the cited source texts (E c11). Putting sources in the model's hands may matter more than better parsing afterward. It is also the point of the planned `citation-ground-before-cite` work, which is **blocked on this engine landing** (R `DECISIONS.md:5-7`).
- The CLERC authors warn that citation-level metrics overstate analysis quality (E c12). Passing L1-L3 does not mean an authority supports the point.

**A 404 is not proof of fabrication.** It means "not in CourtListener" (E c81). Nothing in this design should ever output "Fabricated".

**eyecite-ts.** Zero dependencies, ~37 KB brotli by its README, although its GitHub About text says 7 KB (V c100). 87 versions since 2026-02-05, last release 2026-06-16 (V c105). It "started as a port and has diverged" (V c100); the maintainer's concession that Python has broader statute coverage is unverified (U c106). It adds parallels, signals, confidence scores and scope-aware resolution (L `README:179,537`), useful but none proven. Its statute patterns are hand-written (E c103), so **35 U.S.C. and 37 C.F.R. handling must be tested, not assumed**. Its LICENSE is MIT with no FLP BSD-2 text, while it bundles reporters-db-shaped data (L; E c103, c109), so an adopter should carry that notice. Vendoring a snapshot would limit the single-maintainer risk (J).

**eyecite-js: reject.** Still alpha, last commit 2025-08-21 (L). The clone was the only evidence; the workflow didn't fetch it.

**Python sidecar.** The most faithful, and upstream does the maintenance. But `goals/python-free-repo` is active and says "A future need for Python is a new decision, not an exception row" (R `SPEC.md:71`). So I'd use Python eyecite only as a measuring stick outside the repo, and take a sidecar only if both native candidates fail (J).

**CourtListener (E unless noted).**
- Per-citation statuses: 200 match, 300 ambiguous, 400 unknown reporter, 404 not in the database, 429 past the per-request limit (c81, c86).
- Coverage: case citations only. No statutes, journals, Id., supra, or citations missing volume or page (c82). So **it cannot check 35 U.S.C. or 37 C.F.R.**
- No quote or support checking (c82, c85).
- Limits: default 5/min, 50/hour, 125/day (c90). The endpoint's own 60 valid citations/min, 250/request and 64,000 characters are **U** (c80), though they match `DECISIONS.md:326`.
- Terms: membership access may not power a revenue-positive product; internal tooling counts as commercial; firms over five attorneys are pointed to a commercial agreement (c95-c97).
- `cl/citations/api_views.py` is in the AGPL CourtListener repo (R landscape `:11`). Reuse its behavior, not its code.

**Others.** CiteURL: MIT, 130+ US sources, builds links to free law, no verification (E c60-c64). Its links could be a cheap way to give attorneys "click to read" for statutes, but that isn't proof of existence (J). LexNLP: reject; AGPL by default, last release 2022 (E c65, c66). **Not researched despite being in scope:** `unitedstates/citation`, walverine and Blackstone surfaced in search but were never fetched. JOSS's 2021 dismissal of the first is old and self-interested (E c44).

**Pyodide/WASM: no evidence gathered.** Whether its native dependencies work there is unchecked. I make no recommendation.

## 6. Smallest useful product

**A per-citation check report for one document.** A conceptual shape, not Effect code; it reuses `CitationMention` and `Resolved | Ambiguous | Unresolved` (`SPEC.md:185-188`).

```text
DocumentCheck = {
  mentions : MentionCheck[]
  residue  : CitationShapedResidue[]   // looks like a citation, wasn't extracted
  coverage : { formsInScope, corpusAsOf, sourcesSupplied }
  report   : CitationEngineReport
}                                      // deliberately no overall "verified" flag

MentionCheck = { mention, local, authority, quote, support, goodLaw }

LocalLink       = Full | Resolved{antecedent} | Ambiguous{candidates} | Unresolved
AuthorityStatus = Matched{authority, corpus} | Ambiguous{candidates}
                | NotInCorpus{corpus, asOf} | UnknownReporter
                | OutOfCoverage{form} | NotChecked{reason}
QuoteStatus     = NoQuote | ExactSlice{anchor} | Altered{kind}
                | NotFound | SourceUnavailable
SupportStatus   = NotAssessed | Triage{signal, model}
                | AttorneyConfirmed | AttorneyRejected
GoodLawStatus   = NotAssessed | External{provider, asOf, signal}
```

### Failure and ambiguity rules

| Situation | Outcome | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 404 from a corpus | `NotInCorpus{corpus, asOf}` | Never "Fabricated" |
| 300, multiple matches | `Ambiguous{candidates}` | Never pick one (same principle as quotes, `DECISIONS.md:403,415`) |
| Per-citation 429 | `NotChecked{rateLimited}` | **Must never read as a pass** |
| Statute sent to a case-only corpus | `OutOfCoverage{form}` | Say so explicitly |
| No source text in hand | `SourceUnavailable` | Not a failure, and not a pass |
| LLM support signal | `Triage` only | Never `AttorneyConfirmed` |
| Resource limit hit | Document-level failure | No partial list that looks clean (`SPEC.md:359`) |
| Boundary problem | `CitationEngineFailure` | Unchanged (`GOAL.md:45-48`) |
| Nothing extracted | "No citations found" plus residue | Absence of a match isn't absence of citations |

The last row addresses the most dangerous failure: **an extractor miss is silent, and a missed citation never gets checked.** The residue list makes unknowns visible. It also gives MPEP a home: `SPEC.md:476` leaves it out, but in patent office actions it may be common (J, unmeasured). Surfacing it as residue beats ignoring it.

**A challenge to Q7.** Strict raw-slice equality (`DECISIONS.md:400-403`) is right for grounding, but legal writing legitimately alters quotes, as in `[t]he` and `...`. Treated as plain failures, these would swamp attorneys with false alarms. `Altered{kind}` keeps the rule intact, since nothing becomes `ExactSlice`, while telling a marked alteration apart from a wrong quote. This is my domain reasoning, not gathered evidence, and it needs testing.

### Sequence

1. L1 + L2a + L3 on supplied source text, with the L2b port returning `NotChecked`. Catches false quotes and shows what couldn't be checked. No money involved.
2. Operator decides on paid CourtListener access or an offline alternative. Bulk data exists (E c74), but its license and suitability are unchecked.
3. L2b live, for case citations. Statutes need a different resolver, which is not researched.
4. L4 attorney queue, with LLM triage only if it measures well. Keep L5 out, as Q2 decided.

## 10. Proposed acceptance gates

Thresholds are **my starting proposals (J), not derived from evidence**. Ratify them, or calibrate against Python eyecite's first scores.

| Gate | Requirement | Independent of the implementation because |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **G0 Gold set** | About 200 public documents, at least 1,000 in-scope citations: US opinions, briefs, AI drafts, public office actions and PTAB decisions. Labelled by someone not implementing the engine, with double annotation on a sample. Held out. No client material (`oip-confidentiality.md`) | Built from documents, not from eyecite tests |
| **G1 Extraction** | Per-form **entity-level exact-span** scores. Recall within 1 point of Python eyecite on v1 forms; precision at least 0.97. Token-level scores don't count: one study's best model fell from 93.3% to 81.65% at entity level (E c21) | Human gold |
| **G2 Real-text differential** | Run on the CourtListener 1% sample eyecite's own CI uses (L `benchmark.yml:119`). Humans adjudicate a sample of disagreements. Disagreement isn't error; either side may be wrong | Real distribution, adjudicated |
| **G3 Honest status** | Every mention has an explicit status at every layer. `NotChecked` can never count as a pass. No output says "Fabricated" | Property test on the contract |
| **G4 Corruption detection** | Take real citations, then corrupt the volume, reporter, page, pinpoint and case name. Report recall and false-positive rate per class, using the arXiv 2608.12571 method (E c78). Hold the wrong-pinpoint class to its own bar | Controlled ground truth |
| **G5 Quotes** | 100% exact-slice correctness; a perturbation set that separates exact, altered and wrong | Property-based, on real opinions |
| **G6 Hallucination realism** | Charlotin cases where the fabricated cite can be recovered, plus `reglab/legal_hallucinations` (E c8). Reported, not pass/fail, at first | External |
| **G7 Speed and safety** | Warm p95 of 2 s or less on a 250 KB document; cold start 5 s or less. Keep the `3.5x` growth gate and negative control (`SPEC.md:380-382`). Kill-safe on the adversarial corpus | Measured on a named machine |
| **G8 Repo cost** | Citation test time 15 min or less in hosted CI; fixtures 5 MB or less, excluding the adversarial corpus and G0 | Lane budget |
| **G9 Attorney use** | An attorney uses it on real drafts and says whether it caught something or wasted time | Outcome, not a proxy |

**Don't use as gates:** LegalBench's citation tasks (110 and 55 samples; they test LLM recall, and their negatives are real citations moved to the wrong sentence; E c25-c29), CLC-Citation (restricted, UK only; E c24), or reporters-db examples (useful conformance data, but not an accuracy measure; E c53).

## 11. Stop and continue criteria

**Stop now, whatever else happens:** ledger closure, donor dispositions, CPython emulation, annotation and HTML replay work, and further widening of the CI lane.

Then run one time-boxed bake-off lane (about one working week, J). It needs donor-install authorization under `GOAL.md:29-30`, and Python only outside the repo.

| Result | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Native passes G1, G2 and G7, and emulation can be removed without touching extraction | **Keep native, prune** |
| Native passes accuracy but misses G7 by more than 10x after one profiling pass, or emulation is load-bearing throughout | **Prefer eyecite-ts** if it passes |
| eyecite-ts passes G1, G2 and G7 and native doesn't | **Vendor eyecite-ts behind the port, retire the native extraction core**; keep schemas, spans and failures |
| Both pass | Prefer native if pruning is cheap, because owning the code removes the single-maintainer risk; otherwise eyecite-ts |
| Neither passes on v1 forms | Fix specific measured errors. **Do not return to parity.** A sidecar needs a new decision |
| No consumer uses the report within the timebox, or G9 says no value | **Park the goal**, on terms the operator sets |

**The trap to avoid:** `GOAL.md:70-72` says "stop without weakening scope". Taken literally, that blocks the reduction the user is now asking for. The operator's ruling has to supersede it explicitly.

## 13. Uncertainties

1. **Evidence depth.** 85 of 110 claims had a single extraction pass. Treat all CourtListener limits and terms as needing a re-check before any contract or build.
2. **Nobody knows the engine's real accuracy.** That includes mine, and it is the largest unknown here.
3. **Speed comparisons aren't like-for-like.** 5 s/doc plus 25 s install (`SPEC.md:1506`) and the 28-48 s per test including fixtures (`reflections/...:68`) are harness numbers. Bun vs Node at 90 s vs 16 s (`:51`) is unexplained. eyecite's 10 MB/s is a 2021 estimate that "depends on the density of citations". The code default is Aho-Corasick, not Hyperscan (X c41), so which path it describes is unclear, and `landscape:86` overstates that. 99.9977% refers to citations in their 55M set, not precision or recall on documents.
4. **Entanglement of Python-emulation code with extraction is unmapped.** This could flip B vs C.
5. **Not researched:** Shepard's and KeyCite (so "commercial only" for good law is background belief here, not a finding); Pyodide/WASM; linear-time regex; statute resolvers for U.S.C. and C.F.R.; eyecite-ts's ReDoS posture; `unitedstates/citation`, walverine, Blackstone.
6. **CourtListener terms.** Evidence covers membership access. Whether the free default tier may back internal tooling is unclear, and the page states no data license (E c94). Only the operator or FLP can settle this.
7. **Dataset gaps.** Whether Charlotin's CSV holds the actual fake citation strings; CLERC's license (E c13); where Stanford's preregistered queries are hosted (E c18).
8. **Single-source findings.** arXiv 2608.12571 is one workshop paper with no code release (E c79). The EMNLP result (regex 35.42% vs ModernBERT 93.3%) is UK-only with self-written regexes, and did not test eyecite (E c20), so it says little about US reporter extraction. It is still a reason not to assume regex is the end state.
9. **eyecite-ts test counts conflict.** README: 2,966 tests in 96 files (E c102). My local count: about 3,779 `it/test` calls in 323 files (L). The count method may differ.
10. **Court data is messier than its README.** Verifiers refuted the two-file description; expansion also needs `states.json`, 28 `places/*.txt` files and a hard-coded ordinals list (X c56). This is another reason to consume the vocabulary goal's output instead of re-porting.
11. **Consumers.** I did not check what currently calls `CitationEngine`.
12. **Thresholds, the timebox and the MPEP inference are my judgment,** not evidence.

**Where this report is most likely wrong, for the adversarial exchange:**

- Parity may catch real legal bugs that a smaller gold set would miss. The reflection's missing-page defect came from integrated replay of original inputs, which argues for keeping real-input replay.
- I may be underrating the cost of swapping in eyecite-ts: a span-model mismatch with `VerifiedSpan` is untested.
- `L3 first` may be low value if attorneys rarely quote without the source in hand, which would make paid L2b the true first step.
- Single-maintainer risk may be worse, or vendoring cheaper, than I've judged.
