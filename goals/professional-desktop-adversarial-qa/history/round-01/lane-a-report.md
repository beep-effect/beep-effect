## Clean areas verified

- Seven rapid `+ New thread` clicks created seven distinct threads without a crash, freeze, or duplicate-selection failure; the newest thread was selected and ordered first.
- The empty-thread state rendered `Start the conversation` and remained usable.
- Normal lifecycle rendered `Thinking…`, then structured streaming content, then a finished assistant turn.
- Stop restored the composer controls, and the stopped partial assistant content was not persisted. The immediate follow-up causality bug is reported above.
- Edit mode prefilled the selected user message, showed the amber rewrite warning, changed Send to Rewrite, and Cancel restored ordinary compose mode without changing history.
- A successful short rewrite request returned structured rich text and hid the cost line at the fixture's zero-dollar value.
- Long assistant output (approximately 1,000 requested words) rendered as structured rich text without crashing; plain code highlighting was not evaluated because it is pre-waived.
- Switching to Ontology during a stream and returning to Chat preserved the active `Thinking…` state.
- Sidebar titles updated from `New thread` to the first user-message text and the active thread stayed at the top. The pre-waived `Dec 31` date defect was observed but not reported.
- User and assistant alignment, Edit chrome, Stop chrome, and the fixed composer remained visually distinguishable in both dark and light themes.
- No turn-error toast was expected during successful requests. The reload and concurrent-send silent failures are reported above because they exposed no toast or inline recovery state.

## Additional exercise notes

- Tool-call chips were not produced by the cheap model in the exercised prompts, so their expanded/collapsed interaction could not be validated in this lane run.
- A 5,000-word composer submission and the edit-while-streaming click path were attempted after the two-tab race, but Chrome control timed out before the click reached the page; no claim is made for those two cases.
