## Findings

### 1. `AnchorId` invariance contradicts `AnchorId`'s own definition

**Severity:** blocking

**Attacked:** §4 L1: `AnchorId = ContentHash("anchor") // hash(expression, quote, disambiguating context), not hash(offsets)`; the law "`AnchorId` is invariant under re-indexing between equivalent Expressions"; §4 L2 "every annotation id is stable under re-indexing"; §4 "it survives re-normalization".

**Argument:**

- Three hash inputs move under re-indexing.
  - `expression` is keyed by `textDigest`, which differs whenever the text differs. That is the only case in which re-indexing does anything.
  - `quote` changes under whitespace or hyphenation normalization. `foo-\nbar` becomes `foobar`, and NFKC turns `ﬁ` into `fi`.
  - The context changes in the same way.
- The hash can only be invariant over an equivalence class, and no class representative is defined.
  - If you hash the normalized form, the id depends on `normalizationVersion`. It then stops being stable across normalization upgrades, which is the case that motivates it.
  - It also becomes non-injective, because raw-distinct spans collapse.
- "Disambiguating context" is non-local. How much context disambiguates depends on the whole Expression, so the function is not a function of the span alone.
- Boilerplate repeats with identical context, for example "the method of claim 1" or repeated office-action paragraphs. Two occurrences then share one id, and they can hold different claims.
- The report's praised alpha chose differently, and the report cites it both ways. `cm1:` binds `scopeRef`, `sourceRef`, locator, both digests, the UTF-16 range and the quote (read: `MentionIdentity.ts:105-135`; the report's own §2.1 says this). §4 then cites `cm1:` as support for "not hash(offsets)". §9 admits "quote-plus-range". You cannot both "generalize" `cm1:` and drop its range.
- The sketch drops `scopeRef`. Identical text in two matters therefore has one id, and a shared id crosses the matter wall the report wants enforced by named graphs. Short quotes are also dictionary-attackable from a published `InstanceIri`.
- Strongest counterargument to quote-first, drawn from the report's own source:
  - Guan's sentence, "a copied string is verifiable where a predicted offset is not", is about **verification of an LLM output**, not **identity of an occurrence**. It justifies a check, not a key.
  - The text of a quote answers "does this string exist here". Identity must answer "which occurrence". These are different questions.

**Fix:** Define two ids. `OccurrenceId` hashes scope, expression digest, unit, range and quote, which is the `cm1:` shape. `QuoteKey` hashes the normalized quote and is an explicitly non-injective grouping key. Re-indexing is then a stored map between `OccurrenceId`s, not an invariant of a hash. Delete the invariance law and replace it with the naturality law in finding 6.

### 2. `generatedBy` sits inside the annotation but outside its hash, which breaks merge and PROV

**Severity:** blocking

**Attacked:** §4 L2: `id: AnnotationId // hash(targets, kind, body)` together with `generatedBy: ActivityId`; "Union is associative, commutative and idempotent"; "merging two passes of one Expression never duplicates a target"; §6 L5 "every output id is generated by exactly one Activity".

**Argument:** Two passes that find the same mention produce the same id with different `generatedBy`. Then:

- **`AnnotationGraph = S.HashSet`.** The two records are structurally unequal, so both stay. Probed: same id with different `generatedBy` gives `size = 2`. The "no duplicates" law is false.
- **`AnnotationDocument = S.HashMap(AnnotationId, …)`.** One record overwrites the other. Probed: `union(m1,m2).gen = p2` and `union(m2,m1).gen = p1`. Merge is **not commutative**, and the second pass's provenance is silently lost.
- **PROV completeness.** One id now has two generating Activities, so the §6 law cannot hold. Worse, PROV-DM's generation-uniqueness constraint says an entity is generated by at most one activity (from memory, unverified). A content-addressed id is therefore not a `prov:Entity` at all. The entity is the occurrence of generation, and the content is an alternate-of class.

The two carriers also disagree about what the object is.

**Fix:** Split content from attribution. The value is `HashMap<AnnotationId, Attribution>` with `Attribution = HashSet<ActivityId>` under union, which is exactly a K-relation over a why-semiring. Merge is then a pointwise join and honestly a semilattice. Restate PROV completeness as "every id has a non-empty attribution, and each `(id, activity)` pair is one generation".

### 3. Semiring weights are ill-posed for the actual signals, and "propagation is a homomorphism" has no referent

**Severity:** blocking

**Attacked:** §4 L4 "Weights as semirings"; §3 "The five evidence signals are five semirings. 'Never a blended score' becomes a type error"; §6 "propagation is a homomorphism"; §9 "`Absent` is the fix, and semirings are why it composes".

**Argument:**

- **No mapping is given.** Four semirings are named for five signals, and there is no cost signal for tropical to serve.
- **The real carriers are not `[0,1]`** (read: `Claim.model.ts:94-102, 304-317`).
  - `Measurement` is a tagged sum of `Similarity` (`S.Finite`, any real), `ClassifierProbability` and `OrdinalJudgment`, documented as "different contracts [that] never share a field".
  - Viterbi × on that carrier is the blend the code forbids.
  - `validator` is `Conforms | Violations{messages}`. `reviewer` is `accepted | rejected | abstained` with reviewer, time and reason, "bound to the exact evidence basis it reviewed".
  - Neither has a natural + and ×. Propagating a reviewer's acceptance through a derivation launders human approval onto a claim that the reviewer never saw, which contradicts `BasisDigest`.
  - `sourceAssertedConfidence` is a datum about content and does not propagate at all.
- **"Homomorphism" of what?** Green's theorem is that evaluating `N[X]` under `h: N[X] → K` commutes with positive relational queries.
  - The report stores `derivedFrom: HashSet<ClaimId>`, which is at best `Lin(X)` and does not distinguish `a·b` from `a+b`.
  - No hom `Lin(X) → Viterbi` exists under a valuation: `x·x = x` in `Lin`, but `0.5·0.5 ≠ 0.5`.
  - So the claim graph as described cannot compute the weights it advertises.
  - No query algebra is defined, so the §6 test has no statement to test.
- **No negation.** `challengedBy`, `rejected` and retraction are non-monotone. Plain semirings handle only positive queries. Negation needs monus or m-semirings (Geerts and Poggi, from memory, unverified), and attack semantics is a fixpoint rather than a polynomial.
- **Independence.** × on probabilities assumes independence. The repo already models `DependenceAssessment` with "unknown is never treated as independent" (read: `Claim.model.ts:319-330`). Diamonds in the `derivedFrom` DAG double-count under Viterbi-style ×.
- **The `Absent` dilemma.** `Absent + a = a` for all `a` forces `Absent = 0` by uniqueness of the additive identity, so missing evidence means false. That is the same lie in the other direction, and the report only forbids `Absent = 1`. The alternative is additive absorption, under which one unknown signal poisons every alternative derivation. No third option is specified.
- **`Option<W>` is a regression.** Today's `makeSignal` is `Present | Absent{reason}` (read: `Claim.model.ts:69-70`). `Option` throws the `AbsenceReason` away.
- **The law fails in production representation.** Probed: `(0.1*0.2)*0.3 = 0.006000000000000001` and `0.1*(0.2*0.3) = 0.006`. A fast-check associativity property over IEEE 754 Viterbi or tropical fails immediately. Exact-rational or tolerance arithmetic makes it a different claim.
- **"Type error" overclaims.** Anyone can unwrap two numbers and multiply them. The separation already comes from `TaggedUnion`, without semirings.

**Fix:** Keep the five typed signals with no algebra by default. Use semirings only where a real derivation query exists: store `N[X]` or a why-provenance term as the derivation record, and give Boolean and lineage evaluations as the first two homs. Ratify non-propagation explicitly for reviewer and asserted confidence. Drop the "five semirings" sentence and Deciding Question 4's framing.

### 4. "Merge is exactly the pushout over the shared text" names the wrong apex

**Severity:** major

**Attacked:** §3 "merge is set union, which is exactly the pushout over the shared text"; §3 "merge is the pointwise colimit"; §6 row "L2 | Pushout | merging two passes … duplicates no target".

**Argument:**

- The pushout of `G₁ ← T → G₂` over the text `T` identifies **only `T`**. Two content-equal annotations, one in each graph, stay two elements. The result is non-idempotent: `G +_T G ≠ G`.
- Union is the pushout over `G₁ ∩ G₂`, equivalently the join in `Sub(U)` for a content-addressed universe `U`. In a poset, every pushout is a join, so the word adds no prediction.
- Idempotence comes from content addressing, not from a colimit over text. The real theorem is "content-addressing quotients the free merge into a semilattice", and its proof obligation is on the id function (finding 2).
- Acset colimits with fixed attribute types do not exist when an identification forces conflicting attribute values, because no cocone preserves both. That is exactly the `generatedBy` case. "Colimits computed pointwise" does not cover attributes without attribute variables (reasoned; paper wording unverified).
- Bird and Liberman's union is on triples over **shared node names**. Two passes with their own nodes unify only if nodes have canonical names, which means canonical offsets. So AG union grounds **offset-first** identity and works against §4's quote-first choice. The report also keeps "Node references … carry identity" while `Anchored` has no nodes at all.
- Quoted closure under union fails for unanchored nodes: `n₁→n₂` and `n₂→n₁` are each DAGs, and their union is not (reasoned; how the paper guards this is unverified).
- Re-indexing: annotations whose targets differ only by re-indexing hash differently unless finding 1 is solved. Union then keeps both, and there is no quotient by re-indexing anywhere.
- `AnnotationGraph` has no `Expression` parameter, so union across Expressions typechecks and is no pushout over anything.

**Fix:** Say "join in the lattice of finite sets of content-keyed annotations over one Expression", and make the Expression a type-level or `check`-level index. Reserve "pushout" for the map from per-pass graphs into the merged one. Test the universal property or drop the word.

### 5. `reindex` is neither total nor strictly functorial, and the law that matters is missing

**Severity:** major

**Attacked:** §4 L1 `reindex: (Expression, Expression, LocatorMap) → Anchored → Anchored (normalization is a functor between SofAs)`; "`reindex` is functorial"; §3 "Normalized or translated texts are other SofAs joined by a locator map, which is what `VerifiedSpan.normalization.ts` already computes"; "unit conversion is a bijection"; §6 test "whitespace normalization".

**Argument:**

- Normalization is many-to-one. A normalized span that covers only the `f` of `fi` from `ﬁ` has no exact raw preimage. The code maps each normalized code unit to the raw range behind it (read: `VerifiedSpan.normalization.ts:53-63`), which is a set-valued map.
- Lifting is therefore an over-approximating Galois connection. Composition is lax: `reindex(g∘f) ⊆ reindex(g)∘reindex(f)`. The signature must be partial or set-valued, and the strict equation is false off the easy path.
- Whitespace is the one case that hides this, and it is the case the test names.
- Translation has no locator map. Alignment is a relation, not a function, and that file computes nothing of the sort.
- Functoriality alone is nearly vacuous, since a constant map satisfies it. The law with content is the **naturality square**: `quote(reindex f a) = N_f(quote a)` on spans that lift exactly. It is absent.
- `SourceTextIdentity`'s `normalizationVersion` is "locator-only" and `textDigest` is the raw text (read: `SourceTextIdentity.ts:125-145`). In the code, normalization does not create a second identified text, whereas the sketch's `Expression` makes it part of identity.
- `grapheme-cluster` in `OffsetUnit` breaks the bijection, because it is coarser than code points and depends on the Unicode version. `Position<u> ⇄ Position<v>` is partial for it.
- `resolve → Option<Quote>` hides **ambiguous**, which `citation-grounding.md` requires to fail closed as a distinct outcome.

**Fix:** Make `reindex` return a tagged `Exact | Widened | Unmapped`. State a lax-functor law plus the naturality law on the `Exact` fragment. Make `resolve` return `Unique | Ambiguous(n) | NotFound`. Drop grapheme clusters from identity-bearing units. Test with NFKC and de-hyphenation, not whitespace.

### 6. Δ/Σ/Π are misapplied to projections, and unit and counit are not round trips

**Severity:** major

**Attacked:** §3 "Every projection (handoff → RDF, claim → nanopub, ontology fold → Dataset) is a schema translation … Round-trip laws are the adjunction unit and counit"; §4 L2 "`layer_K` … a Δ along the kind inclusion"; §4 L4 "`project_Δ`"; §6 L2 "functoriality of Δ"; §6 L6 "the adjunction unit on the preserved sub-schema".

**Argument:**

- An RDF dataset is not an instance on a fixed finitely presented schema: the predicate vocabulary is open. Spivak's Grothendieck construction gives a one-way encoding `∫I`, not a target category for Δ/Σ/Π.
- Blank nodes make equality an isomorphism quotient, while instances here are on-the-nose. Σ is only defined up to iso and introduces Skolem nulls, so "every projection is deterministic" is unavailable without a canonical Skolem scheme.
- Direction: for `F: S→T`, Δ goes `T-inst → S-inst`. A projection out of a source on `S` is Σ or Π along `S→T`, or Δ along `T→S`. The report never says which F, and "Δ keeps / Σ Skolemizes" presumes opposite directions in the same sentence.
- Units are not identities.
  - `I → ΔΣ I` is iso iff `F` is fully faithful (standard for Kan extensions along a f.f. functor).
  - `ΣΔ J → J` is generally neither mono nor iso.
  - The `decode∘encode=id` test is a section/retraction law, not an adjunction statement, so the adjunction predicts nothing the test checks.
- Real projections compute: minting IRIs, formatting literals and hashing. A schema functor cannot, without a typeside and queries (Schultz et al., uber-flowers; report lists it only at ○).
- Sums and recursion: `Selector` is an unbounded recursive tagged union, and Spivak's 2010 schemas have neither. Schema-as-C needs a flattened FK encoding that nobody has written.
- `layer_K` filters by an **attribute value** in one table. That is a selection, not Δ, unless each kind is a schema object. Kinds are an open-world `TermIri`, which gives an infinite schema.
- The `layer_K ∘ merge` law is plain set distributivity. Restriction can also dangle FKs across layers, so `layer_K` does not land in `AnnotationDocument`.

**Fix:** Use Δ only for genuine column-dropping between named, finite Effect-side schemas. Call RDF targets **codecs** or **queries**, with laws stated as lens (GetPut, PutGet on the kept part) or prism laws, comparing on the Dataset quotient. Say up front which projections are not schema functors.

### 7. `settle` is monotone only in the log, and the §6 law conflates log with view

**Severity:** major

**Attacked:** §4 L4 "`settle` is monotone (a matter's reality graph only grows; retraction is a new knowledge-time version, never a delete)"; §6 row "reality graph monotone".

**Argument:**

- There are two objects. The append-only log grows. The **as-of view** `R(t)`, which is what "reality" and "only the reality graph has truth conditions" mean, shrinks at a retraction. A retracted fact in the "reality graph" is exactly what must not have truth conditions. So the statement is true of the object nobody queries.
- Bitemporal collision: closing `knowledge` on the prior version mutates a content-hashed record, so its id changes. An open end cannot live in hashed content. The report also declares no `retract` object, though §6 tests "settle/retract".
- `derivedFrom` descendants of a retracted claim are untouched, with no cascade and no truth-maintenance law.
- A "reality graph = union of epistemic worlds" can be inconsistent, for example two attorneys or two filings. Only DEC's non-reality worlds are said to tolerate disagreement.

**Fix:** State three laws:

1. The log is prefix-monotone.
2. As-of stability: for all `t ≤ now`, `R(t)` never changes after `t`.
3. Per `logicalKey`, version knowledge intervals are disjoint, with `start ≥ generation time`.

Store only `knowledge.start` in the hash and derive ends from successors. Add a retraction-closure law over `derivedFrom`, and a consistency gate or an explicit "reality is paraconsistent" ruling.

### 8. The ground-before-cite invariant does not reach the envelope: `settle` takes no witness

**Severity:** blocking

**Attacked:** §4 L4 `settle: (claim: … & { stance: "conjectural" }, token: HumanAdmission) => …`; `supportedBy: S.HashSet(AnnotationId)`; §3 "This **is** the two-families boundary, formalized"; §1 invariants "Nothing is asserted without a source" and "Admission is split".

**Argument:**

- `supportedBy` may be empty, and `AnnotationId` is a receipt-level reference, not a proof. The `VerifiedAnchor` proof is consumed by `promote` and then forgotten, so a claim with no verified anchor can be settled.
- The report's law 5 (grounding is a machine witness, governance a human token) needs **both** arguments, and the signature takes only one.
- "Formalized" also conflates two different things.
  - DEC's settle is a world move.
  - The vision's "logic proves" is soundness.
  - An attorney's admission is neither a proof nor factive, so `stance: "epistemic"` is a mislabel. It is an authority's acceptance, and §3 itself sends settle to the "reality graph", not to an epistemic world.
- `HumanAdmission` needs the same unforgeable/powerless-receipt split as `VerifiedAnchor`. If it is schema-decodable, MCP write tools can forge it.

**Fix:** `settle(proposal, grounding: NonEmpty<VerifiedAnchor>, admission: HumanAdmission)`, with the `Proposal` and `Settled` cases as distinct `TaggedUnion` members. Add laws: settled ⇒ grounding non-empty and every anchor verifies against the current digest. Replace "epistemic" with a named stance such as `admitted`. Keep "proves" as a separate family with its own witness type.

### 9. The Kleisli and centrality claims need restricting, and `withActivity` is not lawful

**Severity:** major

**Attacked:** §4 L5 "morphisms in the Kleisli category over `Effect`"; "pure operations are central, effectful ones are not"; `withActivity: Operation<A,B> → Operation<A, [B, Activity]>`; "every output id appears in exactly one Activity's `generated`".

**Argument:**

- `Effect<A,E,R>` is not one monad. `E` and `R` vary, so this is a graded or indexed structure. Fix `E` and `R`, or say graded. Premonoidality is only stated for a fixed strong monad.
- Equalities hold only up to a chosen observation. Span trees, fiber ids, auto-yield and timing all differ. The test needs a declared observation, `(Exit, Ref trace)`.
- "Pure" must mean total and non-throwing. Probed: a throwing `Effect.sync` before a `Ref` update gives `[failure, 0]`, after it gives `[failure, 1]`. A JS function is not known total, so defects refute the claim as stated.
- Interruption (reasoned, not run): a delivered interrupt at the boundary gives `g` ran in one order and did not run in the other. So equality holds at best as sets of possible outcomes, not per schedule. Default: refuted until stated.
- The claim is also close to vacuous. A production `Operation` has a `name: TermIri`, an `Effect.fn` span and `withActivity`, so nothing wrapped is central, and the centrality law applies to almost nothing that ships.
- `withActivity` breaks composition.
  - `[B, Activity]` does not feed `Operation<B,C>`. It needs Writer lifting with a named monoid.
  - It is not a functor: `withActivity(g∘f)` gives one Activity, while the composite of `withActivity`s gives two. Which is the law?
  - `used` and `generated` need input and output ids, but `A` and `B` are arbitrary.
  - A composite activity also generates its outputs, contradicting "exactly one" (finding 2).

**Fix:** State laws for fixed `(E,R)` under an explicit observation. Define `Pure` as `Effect.succeed(f(a))` with total `f`, and exclude defects, tracing and interruption in the law's preamble. Model PROV as a Writer of `HashSet<Activity>` with a `HasIds` constraint, and pick the fine-grained or composite answer, with one law for it.

### 10. LTS "totality" is vacuous, and one LTS contradicts the report's own rulings

**Severity:** major

**Attacked:** §4 L4 "Replace the seven status vocabularies … with one labelled transition system"; "LTS totality (every state × event is a transition or a typed refusal)"; §6 "exhaustive over kits".

**Argument:**

- Any partial function is total into `S + Refusal`. The law can never fail unless someone omits a switch case, and TypeScript exhaustiveness already catches that. It predicts nothing.
- The missing content:
  - a refusal leaves state unchanged;
  - absorbing states are named;
  - independent events commute, which the five warrant assessments require;
  - reachability from the start state.
- "One LTS" is a category error if those vocabularies are orthogonal. The five warrant assessments are concurrent and owned by different boundaries, so a single `ClaimState` literal cannot represent `exists ✓, current ?, supports ✗`. The state is a product or a map with a lattice per coordinate. Lifecycle, gate result and verdict look like different dimensions (the report offers no census of them; Reader B not re-audited here, so this is flagged as a suspicion).
- The report quotes "collapsing them into one score destroys the information", then does the analogous collapse on status.
- `SourceAuthorityAssessment` is "legal slice", yet it is an event of a foundation LTS. That violates foundation-never-depends-on-slices. "Exhaustive over kits" is impossible with slice-parametric events, and it contradicts "names shared, vocabularies decentralized".

**Fix:** Foundation defines a **generic** product-of-coordinates structure: `Assessments<K>` as a map from slice-declared coordinate to verdict, plus a slice-supplied per-coordinate LTS. Laws: refusal is a no-op, independent coordinates commute, gates are monotone predicates over the map. Do a census before deciding which of the seven vocabularies merge.

### 11. Nanopub envelope, trusty ids, worlds and Dung-style attacks cannot all hold

**Severity:** major

**Attacked:** §3 "The claim envelope is **three graphs**; instance IRIs are **content-addressed**"; §4 L4 `challengedBy` and `state` and `stance // which named graph this record lives in`; "`Claim ⇄ nanopub`"; "support and challenge graphs are acyclic"; "Dung-style evidential argumentation"; §3 "the matter wall is a named-graph boundary".

**Argument:**

- Trusty URIs hash the entire content, so the record is immutable. `challengedBy` is written by later claims and `state` moves by design, so neither can be in the hashed graphs. An edge stored on the target of an attack is the wrong direction. In nanopub practice, a later nanopub points back (retraction). `weights` belongs to none of the three graphs, so `⇄` has no home for it.
- Worlds as named graphs, strongest counterargument: **a quad has one graph term.**
  - Nanopub uses it per record (`:assertion1`). DEC uses it per world. The matter wall wants it per tenant. Three meanings, one column.
  - "Lives in the conjectural graph" needs either copying triples (`settle` then rewrites bytes, changing the trusty id) or graph-about-graph metadata (then worlds aren't named graphs, they are a property of graphs).
  - RDF semantics also gives named graphs no fixed semantics, so "only the reality graph has truth conditions" is a convention that a SPARQL default-graph union silently ignores. A wall enforced by the fourth column fails when a query uses `GRAPH ?g`.
- Mutual attack is the **canonical** contradiction, and Dung frameworks are arbitrary digraphs. "Acyclic" outlaws a contradicts b, b contradicts a, and grounded semantics needs cycles to be non-trivial. If hash links carry attacks, cycles are structurally unrepresentable, so the law is either vacuous or wrong. Micropublications' DAG concerns the support graph (from memory, unverified that challenge is a DAG there too).
- `logicalKey` is stored as `ContentHash`, while the existing envelope takes `logicalKey: (P) => string` from the slice (read: `Claim.model.ts:276-292`). "Injective" is not testable for a hash; only `a ≡ b ⇔ key(a) = key(b)` on generated pairs is.

**Fix:**

- Hash only an immutable core: proposition, support at creation, activity, attributed agent, `valid`, `knowledge.start`.
- Store attacks, state and worlds as **separate records** pointing at the core (dispositions and edges).
- World membership is metadata over assertion-graph names, and tenancy is enforced outside RDF.
- Permit attack cycles, and require acyclicity only for `derivedFrom` and `supportedBy`.

- `LiteralKit("a","b")` is wrong: it takes an array. `OffsetUnit.Schema` and `World.Schema` have no such member in `LiteralKit.schema.ts`, and the kit is passed directly as a schema in `Claim.model.ts:314`.

Conventions:

- There is not one `S.Opaque` in any sketch, despite the claim. Plain data is all bare `S.Struct`.
- `$I` annotations are absent.
- `iri: TermIri | IRI` is a bitwise OR at value level.
- `settle`'s `Claim<P,W> & { stance: "conjectural" }` uses a value as a type, and `stance` lives at `pubinfo.stance`, not at the top level. Decoding never yields that intersection, so type-state needs a tagged case.

Structural:

- `AnyAnnotation` is undefined and is the hard part. A dependent pair `Σ(K: TermIri). Body<K>` over an **open** kind set has no static `S.Union`. ### 13. The tower is not a tower: dependency arrows contradict layer numbers and the package count

**Severity:** major

### 15. Entity and Relation as claims, and the literature over-reads

**Severity:** major

**Attacked:** §4 L2 "What is not an annotation … the vision's two families forbid that conflation"; §3 "decides" column.

**Argument, strongest counterarguments to Entity/Relation as claims:**

- The line is not where it is drawn. A `Mention` carries a machine-assigned `class` and machine-chosen boundaries, and sentence and chunk boundaries are model output too. Each is equally defeasible and has `generatedBy`. If defeasibility makes something a claim, mentions are claims. If not, neither are entities. The honest boundary is grounded vs ungrounded, or text-verifiable vs inference.
- Coreference is a **partition**. Pairwise `Claim<SameReferent>` is `O(n²)`, each with an LTS and possibly attorney state, and transitivity is not enforced (a~b and b~c admitted, a~c rejected?). `Related` needs a referent identity to attach to, and the referent is a quotient that changes as claims settle, so relation claims point at mentions and multiply per mention pair.
- Upstream-first cuts the other way. `nif:taIdentRef` and OA `oa:identifying` model linking as annotation, so OA interchange needs a claims-to-annotation lossy codec.

*Over-reads (judged from the report's own quotes; paper content unverified unless noted):*

- **LRMoo:** the report quotes "Temporal Versions — each being a distinct Work", then concludes "versions of one legal object are one chain" with a flat `Work` and `realizes: WorkRef`. The sketch cannot express abstract Work → TV Work → LV Expression.
- **`SourceTextIdentity` "is already Expression + Manifestation":** its own annotation says "extracted raw-text **manifestation**" (read). A digest of extractor output is finer than an F2 Expression: two OCRs of one document are two digests, one Expression. `Manifestation.extractor` with single `embodies` confuses carrier with derivation.
- **DEC "**is** the two-families boundary":** the paper is about world-typed statements, and attorney, matter wall and "union of epistemic worlds" are the report's additions (finding 8).
- **Green:** "five signals are five semirings" is not in the framework; see finding 3.
- **Guan:** verification, not identity; see finding 1.
- **UIMA:** SofAs are independent, and locator maps between them are not UIMA (from memory, unverified).
- **APG:** "Effect's tagged unions and structs **are** APG types" ignores `check`s, transformations and recursion through `suspend`. "One interchange replaces four" is not something the paper decides.
- **Hogan vs RDFC-1.0:** different algorithms; the report cites one and pins the other. Trusty URIs use their own normalization (unverified). RDFC-1.0 coverage of RDF-star triple terms, which DEC offers as an option, is also unverified.
- **"ISO/IEC 21838-5 in progress":** unverified.
- **Evidence grade:** papers marked ○ (abstract only) carry "decisions": Green and Tannen, Hogan, NIF, LAF, UIMA, the argumentation and calibration papers. Green is load-bearing for Deciding Question 4 at abstract level.

**Fix:** Rename the table column from "decides" to "suggests". Separate quoted content from report inference in each row, and read Green in full before Deciding Question 4 goes to the owner. For entities: use `Cluster` as a first-class versioned claim (`Claim<Partition>` over a mention set) with a stable referent id, and classify mention `class` under the same defeasibility rule.

## What holds

I tried to refute these and could not:

- **Diagnosis D1 and D2.** I checked only the sample cited in the findings above: objects without arrows, and multiple anchor encodings. The direction of the diagnosis stands.
- **HashSet union is ACI** for decoded plain structs. Effect's structural `Equal` dedupes them (probed, size 1). The defect is the id and field design, not the algebra.
- **Kleisli non-commutation.** The core of "interchange fails for effectful arrows" is right for fixed `(E,R)`, and the warning against claiming monoidality is the correct move.
- **Unit conversion** among UTF-16, code points and UTF-8 is a bijection on valid boundaries, failing closed on split surrogates.
- **`rdf.Dataset` as a quotient with a digest defined only on the canonical form.** `Literal` as `Simple | Typed | LangTagged` is also sound.
- **Separating the five signals** and refusing a blended score. Keep this without the semirings: the existing tagged shapes already do the work.
- **`Absent` not equal to `1`,** and the `1` and `0.9` defaults as defects.
- **Term IRIs decoupled from code paths** via a binding table, plus a stability snapshot.
- **API existence.** Present in 4.1.2 source: `S.TaggedUnion`, `S.Opaque`, `S.HashMap`, `S.HashSet`, `S.Natural`, `S.NonEmptyArray`, `S.link`, `S.decodeTo`, `S.check`, `S.Option`, `Graph.hasPath`, `Graph.isAcyclic`, `effect/Combiner`, `effect/Reducer`.
- **Micropublication "minimal form is statement + attribution"** and support-graph framing: fine as a shape for the envelope, subject to finding 11's mutability split.
