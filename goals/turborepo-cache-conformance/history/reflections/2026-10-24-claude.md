# Reflection — turborepo-cache-conformance (2026-10-24, claude)

## Summary

The goal now has a pinned 32-case corpus with distinct strict, stable, canary,
security, observability and capacity verdicts; four executors plus a frozen-rubric
comparison; a loopback lab for five topologies; and a disposable AWS lab that ran
two incumbent variants and was torn down with an empty inventory. The comparison
finds no eligible replacement; the evolved incumbent passes 10 of 12 gates on AWS,
so the recommendation is retain and evolve.

## Tooling experience

- **Worked:** the existing `CiTurboCache` component deployed unchanged under a lab
  name; injectable authorizer and writer factories made a faithful local emulator
  cheap; the frozen-rubric digest check kept scoring honest; `pulumi destroy
  --target` with a dry run isolated the bucket-policy teardown step.
- **Didn't:** MinIO images are gone; the canary fetch path; a bearer-distinctness
  rule that no single-token topology could satisfy.
- **Frustrating:** gitleaks flagging public digests; the incumbent's no-delete
  bucket policy blocking a naive teardown (found by the RC-029 probe, before
  destroy).
- **Wished existed:** a cost-allocation tag already active for lab budgets; a
  build manifest next to the deployed ZIP so a lab can rebuild its exact bytes.

## Implementation improvement opportunities

- Native runs share the host network because targets live outside a private
  namespace; a per-run network namespace with a forwarded target port would
  restore the stronger isolation of `cache protocol-run`.
- Gate 11 rests on a manual cutback drill; encode it as a `conformance-lab` case.
- RC-024's untagged-write check maps to no hard gate in the frozen rubric; a
  future rubric revision should decide whether it belongs under gate 6.

## Goal & prompt critique

- Replace "initially twice observed p99 size and 1.5 times peak concurrency" with
  "…of throttle-free demand; check caps and throttles first".
- Add to GOAL.md: "P2 spends money; prepare the preview and ask for a ceiling."

## TODOs worth codifying

- Throttle-aware envelope derivation as a standard step for capped backends.
- A shared digest-field gitleaks allowlist.

## Lessons (confidence-tiered)

- **HIGH — Critical:** Query throttles before trusting an observed peak — the peak
  here was the cap, and the cache had been silently missing.
- **MEDIUM — Best practice:** Probe deletion permissions before relying on a
  teardown — the RC-029 probe exposed the policy that would have stopped destroy.
- **LOW — Consideration:** Score fresh upstream releases only after the age floor —
  Ducktors 2.14.4 was hours old at pin time.
