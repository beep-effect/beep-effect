# Inspection ownership contexts

The dependency-edge diagnosis of the original 538 development claims found two
different issues. Some structural references lose explicit part-owner context;
several body-dependency differences require a later refinement-semantics audit.
Family incidence alone does not establish inconsistent references: target kind,
unit-header ownership and embedded event arguments must be distinguished. This increment addresses only source ownership. It does not remove
body dependencies to fit a family, infer event/result nominalizations, change
references, or alter the scorer. The existing 41 strict/original 37 retention
gate remains mandatory.

An inspection-only structural-reference strategy records annotated private
`SourceOwnership` evidence over existing source term anchors. Exact `of`, direct
composition (`with`, `having`, `has`, `comprises`, etc.), spatial-part `of which`,
and explicit possessive grammar can establish a source relationship. Quoted
names do not become operators. Numeric reference brackets may separate an owner
from an operator, but a colon parent's expanded child span supplies no lexical
terms to its header. Every relationship uses the terms actually owned by that
element.

Owner identities bind through an explicit introduction or an existing unique
owner reference in actual common ancestry. An ambiguous owner, missing owner,
or recursively owned part whose old owner reference could conflict does not
supply a new proof. Existing lexical compatibility proposes candidates; an
explicitly contradictory owner can reject a candidate. No global nearest
mention rule or new modifier-stripping identity rule is introduced.

A surrounding composition clause is not a blanket owner for every later noun.
The bounded scope requires an explicit relationship: an owned component at/in/on
a positively recognized spatial part (including a base) of the referring part,
or repetition of the part from an explicit
spatial-part relative. Unknown and representational middle nouns supply no
spatial-part evidence. For example, `said nut comprises a flange at a base of
the shaft` can use the nut's uniquely bound shaft relationship; `the nut has a
flange near the shaft` cannot. Another known competing owner in the source scope
declines this proof. An explicit unknown `shaft of the sleeve` cannot be replaced
with the surrounding nut context. Same-owner multiple shafts remain ambiguous.
`a signal of which the sensor is aware` is a relative predicate argument and
does not turn the sensor into a part of the signal.

Explicit referring possessives can expose their part head for structural
matching only when the source owner has one binding and the proposed donor has
the same proved owner. Emitted lexical heads and anchors remain unchanged.
Indefinite possessors, unknown owners, competing owners and unowned donors retain
the legacy fallback. A partitive does not transfer its owner's unrelated parts
to the partitive subject. Sibling claims and entities available on only one
alternative dependency route cannot donate an owner.

One narrowly supported additional dependency follows an explicit new-part
`in/on/at <spatial region> of <owner>` chain, with a unique owner introduction
in an immediate parent claim. It is a source relationship, not an inferred
operation/result or an arbitrary selection of one endpoint of `between`.
Existing claim-dependency and body-refinement rules remain intact.

The historical strict API uses its unchanged lexical and structural-reference
strategies. Inspection uses the owner strategy for current and reconstructed
ancestor terms. The private ownership evidence does not modify public tree,
tool or policy shapes and adds no driver, model, dependency or corpus lookup.

Source-supported local back-references are retained even when they disagree
with old labels. In particular, after rejecting the membrane-edge donor for a
cap's explicitly owned edge, a later unqualified edge in the same relative part
context may refer to the earlier cap-edge mention. The original cap reference
omits both links; this implementation must not suppress the legitimate local
link merely to obtain strict equality. That disagreement remains separate from
the source repair and from the provisional broader body-dependency diagnosis.

Validation covers multiple owners and parts, unknown/ambiguous owner bindings,
composition versus proximity, relative predicate arguments, possessive and
partitive scope, direct-parent part-location dependencies, numeric references,
quoted names, colon header ownership, Unicode anchors, alternative ancestry,
ordinary references and strict preservation. Focused ClaimElement tests, package
typecheck, all scoped EV rules, Fallow including tests, package laws and package
audit/docgen are required. Independent review and a fresh complete 538-claim
runtime-closure-bound replay belong to the orchestrator; no strict gain or
270/538 completion is claimed by this decision.

Independent review findings OWN001 and OWN002 are addressed in implementation003.
Ancestor owner references are resolved within each ancestor's own common natural
ancestry; unrelated claims and alternative-only ancestry cannot supply evidence.
Those ancestor edges provide binding evidence only and are never emitted as
current-claim references. The strict strategy ignores the lazy evidence provider.
The prior implementation002 and owner-review001 receipts remain preserved.
These repairs require separate independent re-review and the full retention replay.
