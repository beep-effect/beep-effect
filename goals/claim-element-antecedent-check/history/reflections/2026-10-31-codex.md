# Reflection — claim-element-antecedent-check

## Summary

The deterministic parser, advisory checker, USPTO XML adapter and review-item path are measured on the fixed public corpus. v16 recovers 37/538 exact structures and retains every protected pass. This reflection records measured engineering lessons while P4 publication/readiness remains active; it does not assert delivery completion or legal validation.

## Tooling experience

Versioned runtime freezes, exact source hashes and paired retention made narrow repairs reviewable. Separate source-grounded reviewer sessions exposed uncertain conventions and substantive alert noise without rewriting baseline labels. Package verification and focused branch/ambiguity tests provided useful code evidence. Raw-review JSON must remain byte-preserved; derived summaries can be reformatted and reproduced. A first-class receipt builder would reduce ad hoc evidence assembly.

## Implementation improvement opportunities

Closest structural matches exposed a recurring distinction between legal antecedent basis and structural result refinement. A unique result introduced by “to form” can carry an element dependency without suppressing its independent legal warning. Conservatively requiring unique source origins and immediate-parent ownership gained three exact claims; broad bare-term heuristics would create unsupported links. The remaining 443 structural mismatches and 58 normalization failures establish that the implementation remains a poor general structural parser.

## Goal & prompt critique

The protocol should have explicitly distinguished optional professional review from mechanical delivery requirements at the start. The user later authorized independent automated acceptance. That decision is recorded in a versioned authority amendment; actual attorney fields remain false, unresolved judgments remain unknown, and neither shared-model consensus nor uncalibrated confidence is represented as legal ground truth. The primary metric and original references must remain fixed even when reviewers propose corrections.

## TODOs worth codifying

Retain the paired passing set, original source denominator, version-bound review input and source-evidence validation in future benchmark work. Model the claim-level estimand before collecting judgments: this review measures whether a claim receives at least one erroneous advisory statement, not the fraction of findings that are wrong or a legal-defect prevalence. Unknown outcomes need bounds, and conditional sampling intervals need an explicit shared-model-error limitation.

## Lessons (confidence-tiered)

- **HIGH — Critical:** Preserve per-claim retention and immutable reference hashes; aggregate improvements can conceal regressions or changed labels.
- **HIGH — Critical:** Keep structural relations, advisory warnings and legal conclusions separate. A structural gain does not validate legal advice.
- **HIGH — Critical:** Report high alert noise plainly: 21/30 sampled claims have an agreed erroneous alert, with two unknown.
- **MEDIUM — Best practice:** Use separate automated source reviewers with explicit uncertainty and exact quotations when human attendance is optional.
- **LOW — Consideration:** A richer parser may improve the long tail, but this packet excludes runtime LLM decomposition and reports the bounded deterministic floor honestly.
