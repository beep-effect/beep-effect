# M3 bounded foundational audit: blinded alternative seat

Date: 2026-10-09. Authority: independent analytical proposal only.

This seat consumed only the M3 candidate lists in R3 (`explorations/legal-ontology-landscape/research/03-legal-core-ip-ontologies.md`), CQs 1/5/7/8/18 in `01-direction-grounding.md`, and the candidate descriptions in `.beep/semantic-m2m3-scratch/m3/data.json`, plus the foundational-auditor shared rules. It did not read the primary or adversarial analyses. The user-authorized deliverable is a bounded SKOS vocabulary audit, so the full OWL admission/ratification workflow and its record layout are not claimed here. No classes, entity implementations, identity keys, legal calculations, or accepted existential commitments result from this report.

The candidates can denote reusable vocabulary concepts; that is sufficient for M3. Their labels also suggest domain referents worth distinguishing in future consumers. A concept IRI identifies a vocabulary entry, not a particular person, institutional bearer, role assignment, deadline, date, occurrence, or evidence record. SKOS broader links organize concepts; they supply no OWL subclass, identity, rigidity, or disjointness axioms.

## Party category and identity tests

| Candidate | Independent category hypothesis | Identity, dependence and temporality | Strongest countermodel / discriminating evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Party | Heterogeneous grouping of person and organization concepts, rather than one demonstrated identity-supplying kind | Persons and institutions need different persistence criteria. The description deliberately separates bearers from legal roles; CQ5 requires evidence before merging records. | “Party” commonly denotes participation in a proceeding, which is relational and anti-rigid. The supplied description instead chooses the bearer reading. Preserve that scope note; do not infer that participation supplies identity. |
| NaturalPersonParty | Candidate person kind on the stated bearer reading | Personal identity persists across matters and assignments; neither a local ID nor matching name establishes sameness. Institutional/legal records may provide evidence rather than the identity criterion itself. | One person with conflicting name/identifier records and two people with the same name defeat lexical identity. Future operational identity criteria remain unaccepted here. |
| OrganizationParty | Candidate institutional continuant; rigidity and precise identity provider unresolved | Personnel changes need not terminate the institution, but legal reconstitution, merger and division can. Depends on institutional constitutive context, rather than solely on natural persons. | A trade name survives dissolution while a newly incorporated entity acquires it. Charter/registration, succession and constitutive continuity evidence discriminate persistence from name reuse. |
| LawFirm | Organizational functional/regulated category; anti-rigid role-like interpretation remains viable | Legal-services provision is a function or authorized activity of an organization, not an independent identity criterion. May depend on professional authorization, practice purpose and jurisdiction. | The same legal entity ceases legal practice and becomes another professional-services business. A rival rigid institution-type reading would require constitutive evidence showing that ceasing to be a law firm destroys this organization's identity. |
| PatentOffice | Organizational authority-role/category, with rigidity unresolved | Authority to administer patent procedures depends on mandate and jurisdiction; bearer identity can persist while mandate changes. | One agency loses patent powers but continues with trademark powers. Alternatively, a legally constituted patent office might cease to exist on removal of that constitutive function. Need the instrument and organizational granularity. |
| TrademarkOffice | Organizational authority-role/category, with rigidity unresolved | Same dependence test as PatentOffice; mandate and office subdivisions may differ from the overall agency. | One institution administers both patents and trademarks, defeating presumed distinctness or disjointness. The office subdivision and the agency are different possible referents. |

“Enduring party” is warranted as a contrast with assignments, but endurance does not prove rigidity. LawFirm, PatentOffice and TrademarkOffice can stay in the party-concept scheme as organizational categories with an explicit unresolved rigidity warning. Do not advertise all six entries as proven kinds/subkinds. Evidence in the allowed inputs cannot select conclusively between constitutive organization type and changeable institutional function.

## Roles: bearer, assignment, historical relation

All eleven role entries are contextual concepts. They supply no bearer identity. A future assignment is a relation/association with its own scope and evidence; a concept denoting the role type is neither that assignment nor its bearer. Matter, asset, invention, instrument, document or licence context is indispensable. Start/end times describe an assignment or assertion only when the evidence actually supports those bounds.

| Candidate(s) | Category/dependence and temporal counterexample |
| --- | --- |
| InventorRole | Historical contribution relation to a specified invention is a strong alternative to a uniformly time-bounded operational assignment. Inventive contribution can remain historically true after prosecution ends or the inventor dies. A recorded assertion can be corrected without the person losing an actual contribution; removal from a record is not automatically termination of inventorship. The description's “associated with” is too weak if read as contribution: a project manager can be associated without inventing. Distinguish contribution, recognized inventorship and source assertion in scope notes; do not force end times. |
| ApplicantRole | Procedural relation to a specified matter; membership can change while the person/organization persists. A successor applicant and historical applicant can both be correctly recorded for different times. |
| AssigneeRole / OwnerRole | Assignment recipient and rights holder are different relational readings. A past assignee may have transferred all rights; a recipient of limited rights need not own the entire asset. Preserve instrument, rights scope and effective/recorded time distinction; neither label settles title. |
| ExaminerRole / AttorneyOfRecordRole | Contextual official/representation relations; different assignments can overlap or change. Practitioner's authorization generally and being attorney of record in one matter are separate facts. |
| CorrespondentRole / ClientContactRole | Communication designation relations. Sender of one communication and standing designated recipient are rival readings of CorrespondentRole; avoid silently merging them. A contact can cease designation while retaining personal identity. |
| SignerRole | Participation in a particular signing occurrence or historical signed-document relation; the signing ends but “signed this document” can remain true. Not every role is an interval of current authority. The signature's validity is a separate evidential/legal question. |
| LicenseeRole / LicensorRole | Permission/contract relations scoped to a licence and rights; assignments may be bounded, renewed or terminated while historical grant/receipt facts persist. Licence expiration does not erase past participation. |

The strongest common counterexample to “all roles are time-bounded” is enduring historical participation. Anti-rigidity must not be inferred merely from a label ending in Role: particular contribution/history readings need explicit modal analysis. The safe M3 claim is contextual dependence and separation from bearer identity, with temporal bounds optional and evidence-backed.

## Docket targets, normative conditions, states and events

Deadline denotes a docket target/information object under the supplied description. It is not the date value, statutory norm, legal obligation, or an event occurring at the target. Its persistence criterion is not established: a changed target date may update one consumer-owned docket item or create a new target. Both models answer the current descriptive CQs; consumer authority/history is needed to discriminate them.

StatutoryDueDate is the authority-grounded target category; SoftDueDate is a planning target dependent on a planning decision. Authority-grounded timing does not imply a universally mandatory action. A deadline can delimit an elective procedural opportunity or a condition for preserving rights. A soft target also need not always precede one external due date; that restrictive description requires its own consumer contract.

| Candidates covered | Independent interpretation and counterexample |
| --- | --- |
| OfficeActionResponseDeadline, TrademarkOfficeActionResponseDeadline | Response targets dependent on the particular action, matter and governing authority. A docket label alone cannot establish applicable response period, extension eligibility or current status. |
| MaintenanceFeeDeadline, AnnuityDeadline, NationalStageDeadline, StatementOfUseDeadline, Section8Deadline, Section9RenewalDeadline, TrademarkRenewalDeadline | Authority/context-grounded maintenance, entry or filing targets. Failure may affect a selected right/procedural path; that is different from an unconditional duty imposed on every holder. Jurisdiction and triggering evidence are required. Section9RenewalDeadline's narrower SKOS placement does not create a class restriction. |
| IDSDeadline, RCEDueDate, AppealDeadline, ContinuationDeadline | Contextual filing/opportunity targets. One broad term may cover multiple procedural actions or eligibility windows; no universal date or duty follows. A planned RCE that is abandoned still had an internal target. |
| OppositionDeadline, TrademarkApplicationDeadline | Opposition or application-stage targets; distinguish optional action from a time limit applicable if that action is chosen. A general application intention has no demonstrated universal statutory due date. |
| Section15DeclarationDeadline | The supplied elective “declaration target” wording preserves the key distinction. A legally regulated eligibility window does not make every registration holder obliged to file. Retain the concept identifier for vocabulary continuity without treating the word Deadline as proof of mandate. |
| DeadlineState, OpenDeadlineState, ExtendedDeadlineState, FinalDeadlineState, MissedDeadlineState | Status concepts describe a target in an observation context. They provide no target identity and are not occurrence categories or demonstrated intrinsic phases. Extension/finality depend on external authority/evidence. Final and Open may coexist; Extended and Missed may coexist. Avoid assuming a disjoint exclusive state machine or deriving lateness from absence of completion records. |
| DocketingEvent | Candidate occurrence with temporal extent, distinct from the observation record reporting it and from the target it changes. Creating an entry is not necessarily the legal triggering event; a correction event changes a record without changing the original legal occurrence. Need occurrence/evidence separation in the scope note. |

State is an indexed descriptive assertion; event is an occurrence; target is an information object; authority is normative context. Their identity and temporal profiles differ even when a UI displays them in one row. CQ1's “missed” classification requires a dated observation and completeness context; vocabulary membership alone cannot supply decision-bearing negation or legal consequences.

## Bounded disposition

The three supplied groups cover 25 docketing concepts, six party concepts and eleven role concepts. All are usable as cautiously described SKOS vocabulary candidates. The strongest needed clarifications are organizational rigidity, historical inventorship/signing versus current role intervals, authority-bound opportunities versus mandatory obligations, nonexclusive status dimensions, and occurrence versus observation record.

R3 also mentions PartyIdentifier, ownership-change events and document/security categories; those are not in the supplied three-group candidate data and receive no fabricated per-term analysis here. They remain outside this bounded seat. Likewise, CQs 5 and 18 motivate distinguishing identities and roles, but this audit neither proves identity resolution works nor satisfies a legal docketing CQ through description alone. The missing TrademarkAsset entity remains an implementation limitation, not a reason to introduce an entity in M3.
