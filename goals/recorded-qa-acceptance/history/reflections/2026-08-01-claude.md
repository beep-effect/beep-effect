# Reflection — recorded-qa-acceptance (2026-08-01, claude)

## Summary

The recorded-QA system was accepted on the evidence that matters: across the
campaign the vision judge caught **five real defects that green assertions
missed**, and two independent clean rounds (worktree round 5 and round 11)
returned `requiredCount: 0` with `judge-lint` and evidence cross-check green.
The deliberate-revert falsification did **not** produce a true positive — not
because the loop failed, but because the story fixtures cannot express the
defect class even with every defense stripped, which this session proved
directly under real XTEST input. The lane question resolved decisively:
Lane B (OBS) is retired as structurally human-gated; Lane C (Xvfb + XTEST) is
proven and takes its place.

## Tooling experience

- **Worked:** The record → extract → judge-pack → judge → judge-ingest loop was
  the productive core; the `requiredCount` filter law and evidence cross-check
  made verdicts trustworthy rather than narrative. The beacon clock sync held
  at ~11 ms RMS across every Lane A round. Adversarial code review over the fix
  diff (3 lenses) found four real design gaps in my first-pass fixes — the
  concluded-drag model that shipped is materially better than what I wrote
  alone.
- **Didn't:** OBS. Portal consent, a stale RestoreToken surviving scene
  deletion, a password mismatch reported as a disabled server, and two silently
  black rounds. Every one of those cost a cycle.
- **Frustrating:** Discovering a black capture only after paying for a full
  extract, pack, and judge run. Also `pkill -f <pattern>` matching its own
  shell (exit 144) — twice.
- **Wished existed:** A capture sanity gate (luma preflight), a lane that needs
  no human, and a fixture-validity check — "does this story actually express
  the defect the falsification removes?" — runnable before a campaign commits
  to it.

## Implementation improvement opportunities

- The four driver/CLI fixes in the findings above (luma preflight, stale-token
  recreate, split OBS auth error, per-checkout port).
- `dropPreview` now discriminates tab-insertion from section creation; the
  natural next step is dedicated root-edge drop targets so the 8px band stops
  being a precision requirement.
- `TabDrag.concluded` solved the trailing-click class, but the healing path
  (next press clears a stale record) is a repair rather than a guarantee; a
  pointerId-scoped record with an explicit expiry would be stronger.

## Goal & prompt critique

- The exit criteria named lanes ("one Lane A, one Lane B") instead of naming
  the *capability* each lane supplies. Revised framing: "one clean round per
  input-capability tier required by the defect classes under test." That phrasing
  survives a lane being retired mid-campaign — which is exactly what happened.
- The falsification row should have carried a precondition: prove the fixture
  expresses the defect BEFORE the campaign depends on the revert.

## TODOs worth codifying

See frontmatter `todos`. The highest-leverage one is the input-capability
ladder: it converts two campaigns' worth of rediscovery into a routing rule.

## Lessons (confidence-tiered)

- **HIGH — Critical:** Prove the fixture can express the defect before making a
  revert-based falsification an exit criterion — a revert against a fixture that
  cannot smear yields a false negative that looks like a system failure.
- **HIGH — Critical:** A capture pipeline must sanity-check its own output; an
  all-black video that still carries 601 events is indistinguishable from
  success until a human or a paid judge looks at a frame.
- **MEDIUM — Best practice:** Pick the input tier by defect class: CDP for
  logic/layout, XTEST for anything the browser handles natively (selection,
  drag-and-drop, capture). Do not attempt native-input classes on synthetic
  pointers — three attempts proved it impossible, twice.
- **MEDIUM — Best practice:** When a lane depends on interactive OS consent, it
  cannot be an unattended exit criterion. Ask the autonomy question during
  planning.
- **LOW — Consideration:** Judge findings can be sharper than the harness
  assertion that triggered them (the grip-resize diagnosis described the wrong
  element being dragged), but they inherit the harness's fixture assumptions —
  R1-01 was ultimately a selector bug, not a product bug.
