Lesson 0001 · about 6 minutes

Where did this file come from?

What the ancestry field in an Omoide registration manifest really asks, why two of its answers are allowed and three are refused, and which answer is honest for the aubri pilot.

1. The story (like you're 5)

Imagine a lunchbox rule at school. Every lunchbox needs a sticker that says where the food came from, and the teacher will only let you eat food with a sticker she can read.

There are only two stickers she accepts. "I packed this myself" means: this is the oldest version of the food I have, I did not make it out of something else I can point to. "Made from Grandma's recipe, card attached" means: I made it from something else, and here is the exact recipe card so anyone can check.

Three stickers get the lunchbox sent home. "Found it on the floor" (nobody knows where it came from). "A robot cooked it" (a machine invented it). And "made from Grandma's recipe" with no card attached, because a claim you cannot check is the same as no claim.

The sticker is your promise. The teacher does not taste the food to check it. She writes your name next to the sticker so everyone knows who promised.

2. The same thing as a type

The lunchbox stickers are a sum type. The registry only has two constructors, and everything else is a refusal, not a third case.

// What the manifest can say about one file (paraphrasing source-registration.md §72–84)
type Ancestry =
  | { kind: "original_capture";            parent_sha256: [] }              // "I packed this myself"
  | { kind: "non_generative_derivative";   parent_sha256: NonEmpty<Sha256>;
      ancestry_complete: true }                                             // "recipe card attached"

// Every variant also carries the operator's words, stored verbatim:
//   statement: string, evidence_reference: string

// Refused at registration (error codes come from the validator, not the UI):
//   kind === "unknown" | "generative"          → refused
//   derivative with missing/partial parents    → refused ("incomplete")
//   a parent that is itself a descendant       → refused ("cyclic")

Nothing here is inferred. The doc is explicit: "The service does not invent ancestry from filenames, old flags or EXIF." An empty parent list is not "unknown parents"; it is the claim "there are no parents to name".

3. What the software checks vs what you promise

4. So what is honest for the aubri files?

A read-only census of the first twelve T7-rooted aubri files found no camera EXIF at all (no make, model, date or software tag) and no ICC profile. They are the files as downloaded or received, already re-encoded by whatever platform they came through. You hold no earlier copies and no parent hashes.

OptionWhat the registry doesIs it honest?
non_generative_derivativeRefused: no parent hashes, so the chain is incomplete.It would be truer in spirit (something upstream re-encoded them) but you cannot supply the card, so the software will not accept the sticker.
unknownRefused by design.Honest about the platform history, but it blocks admission, which is the point of the rule.
original_captureAccepted with an empty parent list.Honest if the statement says what "original" means here: the earliest form you hold, no generative ancestry known, upstream platform re-encoding possible and undocumented.

Recommended statement (stored verbatim on every file):

"As-received file from the subject's public or provided media; the earliest form held by the operator; no generative ancestry known; upstream re-encoding by the source platform is possible and undocumented."

The enum says "original_capture". Your sentence says exactly how far that word reaches. An auditor reads both.

What this does not do: it does not claim you took the photo, it does not claim a licence, and it does not prove the subject is aubri. Those are the separate rights_to_curate and subject_identity_attested fields, which are the next lesson.
