We ran into the same stranded drain on Effect Cluster (2.0.0-beta.87, two runners and a front, PostgreSQL 17, a single-node Docker swarm). We fixed it the way this PR does, so this comment adds the cluster evidence and an answer to the lease-renewal objection on #2763, not another patch.

### Evidence from the cluster

- **Before:** both runners were killed right after a command committed. The outbox stayed stranded for good: `outbox-lease` was in the future and its backstop `Wake` was already processed. A projection key stayed at `accepted 1, processed 0` with no sink row.
- **After (a drain that finds the lease held reschedules for its expiry):** every reaction of the command happened exactly once, in every run. In three runs they finished 66.8 s, 67.7 s and 67.0 s after the kill. The bound is 80 s: the 60 s lease plus two 10 s entity-message polls.
- **A projection crash** forced between its sink write and its checkpoint was redelivered, and the sink ended correct.

Most of the 67 s is waiting out the dead holder's lease.

### The objection on #2763: a live drain that outlives its lease

The objection is right. The lease is the only thing that tells a live drain from a dead one, and a live drain never renews it. A wake that reschedules for `held` makes the overlap with a drain still running after 60 s certain, where before it was only possible.

The lease can't tell the two apart because it records when it expires but not who holds it. The host knows something better than the clock: an instance runs in one activation at a time. That holds for a Durable Object by construction, and for an Effect Cluster entity on the runner that holds its shard lock. So the lease can name its holder:

1. **An activation id.** The platform mints a random `activation` when it builds an instance's state (`ObjectState.activation`). It is never stored outside the lease and never leaves the process.
2. **The lease names it.** The lease becomes `{ holder: activation, expires }`. The number form keeps reading as a lease with no holder, which only the clock expires.
3. **Acquire follows ownership.**
   - A lease from another activation was taken by a drain that is gone, or by one that no longer owns the instance, so a new activation takes it at once.
   - A lease from this activation is never taken while a drain of this activation is running. That is the in-process flag proposed on #2763, which an activation id generalises.
   - Otherwise the lease expires by the clock, as now.
4. **Hosts opt in.** A platform declares that a new activation means the old one ended (`singleActivation`). Durable Objects and Rivet do; Effect Cluster does, with the caveat below. Other hosts keep today's behaviour, which with this PR is the reschedule.

This removes both problems. A long drain is never overlapped within its activation. Recovery after a kill takes as long as it takes the new owner to activate the instance, instead of the rest of a 60 s lease plus the wake. On Effect Cluster that is the dead runner's shard-lock expiry (35 s by default) plus the next assignment refresh. A drain can also renew `expires` before each row, which bounds how stale a held lease can get on hosts that don't opt in.

**The caveat.** On Effect Cluster, a runner that stalls past its shard lock keeps its activation while another runner activates the same instance. With holder-aware leases, both may then drain the same rows for a while. That adds no new kind of behaviour: delivery is already at least once. A drain that dies after delivering and before deleting a row delivers it again, and receipts and inbox checkpoints absorb the repeat. It only changes when a duplicate can happen. Separately, #3490 is about the stale runner's commits, which a duplicate delivery doesn't cover.

If this is a direction you'd take, it could be a follow-up to this PR, with in-memory cases:

- A lease left by a dead activation is taken by the next activation at once, not after `LEASE_MS`.
- A drain that runs longer than the lease is never joined by a second drain in its own activation.
- Two activations draining together deliver each row at least once, with the same consumer results as one.

We have not built this; it is a proposal.
