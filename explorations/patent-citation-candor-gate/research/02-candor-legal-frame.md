## 1. The duty and who bears it

Each individual associated with filing and prosecuting a patent application has
a duty of candor and good faith in dealing with the Office. That duty includes
disclosing to the Office all information known to that individual to be material
to patentability under Rule 56. [cfr-1-56-lii.md, 1.56(a)]

The covered individuals are each named inventor; each attorney or agent who
prepares or prosecutes the application; and every other person substantively
involved in its preparation or prosecution who is associated with the inventor,
the applicant, an assignee, or anyone to whom there is an obligation to assign
the application. [cfr-1-56-lii.md, 1.56(c)(1)-(3)] MPEP 2001 explains that the
duty applies to individuals rather than organizations as such and excludes
typists, clerks, and similar personnel who only assist with an application.
[mpep-2001.md, MPEP 2001.01]

The duty attaches claim by claim while a claim is pending. It continues until
the claim is cancelled or withdrawn from consideration, or the application is
abandoned. Information concerning a cancelled or withdrawn claim need not be
submitted only when it is not material to any claim still under consideration;
there is no duty to submit information that is not material to any existing
claim. [cfr-1-56-lii.md, 1.56(a)] Allowance is not the end point: MPEP 2001 says
the duty continues after allowance until the patent is granted, and points to
the post-allowance IDS procedure before payment of the issue fee. [mpep-2001.md,
MPEP 2001.04]

Rule 56 says the disclosure duty is deemed satisfied when all information known
to be material to patentability of any claim that issued was either cited by the
Office or submitted in the manner prescribed by 1.97(b)-(d) and 1.98.
[cfr-1-56-lii.md, 1.56(a)] This is a universal condition over what was known and
material, not a rule that missing database events imply satisfaction.
[cfr-1-56-lii.md, 1.56(a)]

Rule 56 materiality is stated as follows, verbatim:

> Under this section, information is material to patentability when it is not
> cumulative to information already of record or being made of record in the
> application, and
>
> (1) It establishes, by itself or in combination with other information, a
> prima facie case of unpatentability of a claim; or
>
> (2) It refutes, or is inconsistent with, a position the applicant takes in:
>
> (i) Opposing an argument of unpatentability relied on by the Office, or
>
> (ii) Asserting an argument of patentability.
>
> A prima facie case of unpatentability is established when the information
> compels a conclusion that a claim is unpatentable under the preponderance of
> evidence, burden-of-proof standard, giving each term in the claim its broadest
> reasonable construction consistent with the specification, and before any
> consideration is given to evidence which may be submitted in an attempt to
> establish a contrary conclusion of patentability.

[cfr-1-56-lii.md, 1.56(b)]

MPEP 2001 adds that the Rule 56 materiality definition is intended to give the
Office the information needed for the examiner to make an independent
patentability determination, and it advises submission in close cases where
materiality or consistency is in question. [mpep-2001.md, MPEP 2001.04;
mpep-2001.md, MPEP 2001.05]

## 2. Two materiality standards, kept distinct

The captured regulatory standard is Rule 56 materiality. It governs what is
material “under this section,” and the MPEP connects it to information supplied
for Office examination and the examiner's independent patentability decision.
[cfr-1-56-lii.md, 1.56(b); mpep-2001.md, MPEP 2001.04]

For inequitable-conduct materiality, _Therasense_ adopts as its general rule a
but-for standard: withheld prior art is material if the PTO would not have
allowed a claim had it known of the art. A court making that counterfactual
patentability determination applies the preponderance-of-the-evidence standard
and gives the claims their broadest reasonable construction.
[therasense-casemine.md, opinion pt. VI]

_Therasense_ recognizes an exception when the patentee commits affirmative
egregious misconduct, such as filing an unmistakably false affidavit. Such
misconduct is material without but-for proof; mere nondisclosure of prior art or
failure to mention prior art in an affidavit does not enter the exception.
[therasense-casemine.md, opinion pt. VI]

The standards occupy different legal roles. _Therasense_ defines materiality
for the inequitable-conduct defense in patent litigation and expressly declines
to adopt the current Rule 56 definition for that purpose; Rule 56 governs the
disclosure duty in PTO practice and supports the examiner's independent
patentability determination. [therasense-casemine.md, opinion pts. III, VI;
cfr-1-56-lii.md, 1.56(a)-(b); mpep-2001.md, MPEP 2001.04]

_Therasense_ also requires the accused infringer to prove specific intent to
deceive the PTO by clear and convincing evidence. When intent is inferred from
indirect or circumstantial evidence, specific intent must be the single most
reasonable inference; materiality alone cannot establish it.
[therasense-casemine.md, opinion pt. V]

The system boundary must consequently preserve two distinct judgment slots:
Rule 56 materiality for PTO disclosure practice and _Therasense_ materiality
for inequitable-conduct litigation, including a separately representable
affirmative-egregious-misconduct exception. Both slots are now grounded; it
would still collapse distinct legal roles to derive or translate one from the
other. [therasense-casemine.md, opinion pts. III, VI; cfr-1-56-lii.md,
1.56(a)-(b); mpep-2001.md, MPEP 2001.04]

## 3. The factual state machine CandorDisposition must record

The following are enumerable facts and externally authored assertions that a
future model may need to retain. They are raw material for a schema, not a schema
design and not automated legal conclusions.

### 3.1 Application and timing-window facts

1. **FACT — application baseline.** Record the application identity and type;
   the actual national filing date; national-stage entry date, if any; Hague
   international-registration publication date, if any; each RCE or CPA filing
   date; the mailing date of each first Office action on the merits; the mailing
   dates of any final action, notice of allowance, or other prosecution-closing
   action; the filing date of any notice of appeal; the issue-fee payment date;
   and the patent-grant or abandonment date. Those events select the 1.97 timing
   lane. [cfr-1-97-lii.md, 1.97(b)-(d); mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.04(b)(I)-(IV)]

2. **FACT — 1.97(b) early window.** Record whether the IDS was filed within
   three months of a non-CPA national application's actual filing date; within
   three months of national-stage entry; before the first Office action on the
   merits; before the first Office action after an RCE; or within three months
   of Hague publication. Satisfying any applicable 1.97(b) period makes the IDS
   eligible for consideration without a 1.97(e) statement or the 1.17(p) timing
   fee, although any applicable 1.17(v) IDS size fee remains separate.
   [cfr-1-97-lii.md, 1.97(a), 1.97(b)(1)-(5); mpep-609.md, MPEP
   609.04(b)(I)]

3. **FACT — 1.97(c) middle window.** Record that the 1.97(b) period had ended
   and that the IDS filing preceded—not merely shared the date of—the mailing
   of the first final action, notice of allowance, or other prosecution-closing
   action. Also record whether the filing included either a 1.97(e) statement or
   the 1.17(p) timing fee, plus any applicable 1.17(v) size fee.
   [cfr-1-97-lii.md, 1.97(a), 1.97(c)(1)-(2); mpep-609.md, MPEP
   609.04(b)(II)]

4. **FACT — 1.97(d) late window.** Record that the IDS was filed after the
   1.97(c) period but on or before issue-fee payment. Record both required acts:
   a 1.97(e) statement and payment of the 1.17(p) timing fee, plus any applicable
   1.17(v) size fee. [cfr-1-97-lii.md, 1.97(a), 1.97(d)(1)-(2);
   mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.04(b)(III)]

5. **FACT — after issue-fee payment.** Record an IDS filed after issue-fee
   payment as received and placed in the application file, but not considered,
   unless a later Office event changes the procedural posture. If the application
   is withdrawn from issue under one of the stated provisions, MPEP 609 says it
   is treated, for IDS timing, as though no notice of allowance had been mailed
   and no issue fee had been paid. [mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.04(b)(IV)]

6. **FACT — 1.97(e)(1) statement.** Record the signed statement, the identity
   of every covered IDS item, the counterpart foreign application and foreign
   patent-office communication, the communication's mailing date, and the
   assertion that each item was first cited there no more than three months
   before the IDS filing. [cfr-1-97-lii.md, 1.97(e)(1); mpep-609.md, MPEP
   609.04(b)(V)]

7. **FACT — 1.97(e)(2) statement.** Record the signer, date, covered IDS items,
   the representation that none was cited in a foreign-office communication in
   a counterpart foreign application, the representation made after reasonable
   inquiry that no covered person knew any item more than three months before
   filing, and the fact that reasonable inquiry was attested. Do not turn those
   representations into machine findings about knowledge or inquiry adequacy.
   [cfr-1-97-lii.md, 1.97(e)(2); mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.04(b)(V)]

8. **FACT — non-extension and cure posture.** Record that no 1.136 extension
   is available for filing an IDS. Separately record an Office-granted cure
   period when a bona fide attempt to comply with 1.98 omitted required content
   inadvertently; this is additional time for full compliance, not an extension
   of an IDS filing window. [cfr-1-97-lii.md, 1.97(f); mpep-609.md, MPEP
   609.04(b)(VI)]

9. **FACT — non-representations.** Record that a compliant IDS filing is not a
   representation that a search was made and is not an admission that a cited
   item is or is considered material under Rule 56. [cfr-1-97-lii.md,
   1.97(g)-(h)]

10. **FACT — noncompliance outcome.** Record whether the Office placed a
    noncomplying IDS in the file without considering it, along with the stated
    reason and the date of any correction or resubmission. The correction or
    resubmission date is the operative IDS date for later timing compliance.
    [cfr-1-97-lii.md, 1.97(i); mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.05(a)]

### 3.2 IDS-content facts

1. **FACT — list and page controls.** Record the IDS list; every listed
   patent, publication, application, or other item; a separate section for U.S.
   patents and U.S. patent-application publications; the application number on
   each page; the examiner-initials column; and the IDS heading.
   [cfr-1-98-lii.md, 1.98(a)(1)(i)-(iii)]

2. **FACT — copies by document class.** Record whether a legible copy is
   present for each foreign patent; each non-U.S.-patent publication, or the
   portion that caused it to be listed; each cited pending unpublished U.S.
   application's specification, claims, and drawing, or the causative portion
   and related claims; and all other information, or the causative portion.
   U.S. patents and U.S. patent-application publications do not ordinarily
   require copies unless the Office requires them. [cfr-1-98-lii.md,
   1.98(a)(2)(i)-(iv)]

3. **FACT — non-English information.** Record the document language; the
   concise explanation of relevance as presently understood by the covered
   individual most knowledgeable about the content; and whether a written
   English translation is possessed, controlled, in custody, or readily
   available and, if so, whether a copy is present. [cfr-1-98-lii.md,
   1.98(a)(3)(i)-(ii)]

4. **FACT — size-fee assertion.** Record the clear written assertion that the
   applicable 1.17(v) IDS size fee accompanies the IDS or that no such fee is
   required, together with the fee-paid fact when applicable.
   [cfr-1-98-lii.md, 1.98(a)(4)]

5. **FACT — document identity.** Record, by class, the inventor, applicant,
   patent number, application or publication number, issue/publication/filing
   date, issuing country or office, appropriate foreign document number,
   publisher, author if any, title, relevant pages, and place of publication as
   required for that class. [cfr-1-98-lii.md, 1.98(b)(1)-(5)]

6. **FACT — cumulative-copy assertion.** When an attorney states that two or
   more listed patents or publications are substantively cumulative, record the
   dated assertion, its author, the grouped document identities, which one copy
   was supplied, and which copies were omitted on that basis. The legal rule
   permits one copy without the others only when the other items are stated to
   be cumulative. [cfr-1-98-lii.md, 1.98(c)]

7. **FACT — earlier-application copy exception.** Record the earlier
   application's identity, the assertion that it is relied on for an earlier
   effective filing date, the prior IDS identity and asserted compliance with
   1.98(a)-(c), and which otherwise-required copies were not resubmitted. Both
   conditions are required for the exception. [cfr-1-98-lii.md,
   1.98(d)(1)-(2)]

### 3.3 Supplemental, continuing-application, and examiner-treatment facts

1. **FACT — multiple or supplemental IDSs.** Record each IDS as a distinct
   submission with its own filing date, items, contents, fees, statements, and
   Office treatment. Multiple IDSs may be filed in one application, but each is
   considered only if it independently satisfies the applicable requirements.
   [mpep-609.md, MPEP 609]

2. **FACT — parent information already considered.** Record whether the Office
   considered each item in the parent and whether the child is a CPA,
   continuation, divisional, or continuation-in-part. MPEP 609 says considered
   parent information ordinarily need not be resubmitted for consideration in
   those children, subject to the separately stated international-parent and
   patent-printing qualifications; information considered before an RCE likewise
   need not be resubmitted. [mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.02(I), 609.02(II)(A)(1)-(3)]

3. **FACT — parent information not considered.** Record whether a previously
   unconsidered parent submission met 1.98 content requirements. In a CPA,
   content-compliant information filed in the parent will be considered without
   a specific request; in a continuation, divisional, or continuation-in-part,
   the applicant must resubmit it under 1.97 and 1.98 to ensure consideration;
   after an RCE, pre-RCE information that complied with 1.98 will be considered.
   [mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.02(II)(B)(1)-(3)]

4. **FACT — international parent.** Record whether the parent was an
   international application designating the United States and whether an IDS
   complying with 1.97 and 1.98 listed the international-search-report or
   international-preliminary-examination-report documents in the continuing
   application. MPEP 609 identifies that submission as necessary if the
   applicant wants to ensure consideration in that continuation.
   [mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.02(I)]

5. **FACT — considered item.** Record the Office's item-level indication:
   examiner initials beside a citation, or the alternative page stamp and
   electronic initials with an electronic signature on the final page. Also
   record the examiner's name and consideration date when supplied.
   [mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.05(b)]

6. **FACT — partially considered IDS.** Record each lined-through citation as
   not considered and each appropriately initialed citation as considered; an IDS
   can contain both states. [mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.05(a)-(b)]

7. **FACT — wholly not considered IDS.** Record the Office's “not considered”
   marking or diagonal line, its noncompliance reason, the notice to the
   applicant, and the fact that the IDS remains in the application file.
   [mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.05(a)]

8. **FACT — meaning of considered.** Record “considered” only as the Office
   state shown by its markings. MPEP 609 defines that state as consideration in
   the same manner as documents in Office search files during a proper prior-art
   search; it does not turn the marking into materiality, substantive reliance,
   or a search representation. [mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.05(b);
   cfr-1-97-lii.md, 1.97(g)-(h)]

## 4. The NEVER-COMPUTE boundary

For every judgment below, the system may preserve a dated attorney disposition
that identifies the author, application, claims, reference or information item,
observation/source version, stated standard, outcome, rationale or basis, and
supersession history. It must not manufacture the judgment.

1. **Never compute Rule 56 materiality.** Determining whether information is
   noncumulative and either establishes a prima facie case of unpatentability or
   refutes or conflicts with an applicant position requires the legal and
   claim-specific judgment stated in Rule 56. The system may record the
   attorney's dated material/nonmaterial/undetermined disposition and the claims
   and record against which it was made. [cfr-1-56-lii.md, 1.56(b)(1)-(2)]

2. **Never compute Therasense materiality or its exception.** The system may
   reserve a separately named attorney-authored disposition, but it may not
   derive, populate, or translate that disposition from Rule 56. The general
   test asks whether the PTO would have allowed the claim had it known the
   withheld prior art; affirmative egregious misconduct is an exception to that
   but-for requirement, while mere nondisclosure is not.
   [therasense-casemine.md, opinion pt. VI]

3. **Never compute cumulativeness.** Cumulativeness is part of Rule 56's
   materiality framing, and “substantively cumulative” is also the predicate for
   omitting copies of listed patents or publications under the IDS content rule.
   The system may record the attorney's dated cumulative/noncumulative/unknown
   assertion, the comparison set, and the one supplied copy; document similarity
   or duplication must not become the legal conclusion. [cfr-1-56-lii.md,
   1.56(b); cfr-1-98-lii.md, 1.98(a)(2), 1.98(c)]

4. **Never compute intent to deceive, bad faith, fraud, or intentional
   misconduct.** Rule 56 refers to fraud, bad faith, and intentional misconduct,
   but supplies no machine-decidable test for those mental-state or conduct
   judgments. The system may record only a dated attorney judgment or an
   explicit “not assessed” state. [cfr-1-56-lii.md, 1.56(a)] _Therasense_
   requires specific intent to deceive, proved by clear and convincing evidence;
   if inferred from indirect or circumstantial evidence, it must be the single
   most reasonable inference. [therasense-casemine.md, opinion pt. V]

5. **Never infer satisfaction or closure from missing events.** Rule 56 deems
   the duty satisfied only if *all* information known to be material to an issued
   claim was cited by the Office or submitted through the prescribed process.
   An empty event list, a reconciled set, or the absence of a new observation
   cannot prove the universal facts “all,” “known,” and “material.” The system
   may record a dated attorney closure or nonclosure judgment over an explicit
   observation-version manifest, while keeping unknown, stale, quarantined, and
   later-discovered events visible. [cfr-1-56-lii.md, 1.56(a)]

6. **Never compute that a disclosure obligation is excused.** Whether
   information about a cancelled or withdrawn claim remains material to another
   claim, whether information is clearly cumulative or nonmaterial, and whether
   an earlier-application or cumulative-copy exception applies all depend on
   predicates that should remain attorney-owned. The system may record the dated
   attorney conclusion, the asserted ground, affected claims, referenced prior
   IDS or application, and supporting factual events. [cfr-1-56-lii.md,
   1.56(a)-(b); mpep-2001.md, MPEP 2001.05; cfr-1-98-lii.md, 1.98(c)-(d)]

7. **Never compute the truth or adequacy of a 1.97(e) certification.** Date
   comparisons may be displayed, but whether an item was “first cited,” when a
   covered individual knew it in association with the application, and whether
   the signer made reasonable inquiry are human representations. The system may
   record the signed certification, signer, covered items, asserted dates, and an
   attorney disposition on its availability or adequacy. [cfr-1-97-lii.md,
   1.97(e)(1)-(2); mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.04(b)(V)]

8. **Never infer examiner reliance or approval from an IDS marking.** Examiner
   initials or the alternative electronic marking establish the captured
   “considered” state described by MPEP 609; filing is not an admission of
   materiality, and consideration is not evidence that the examiner relied on a
   reference for an Office action. The system may record the marking, date, and
   any separately observed Office-action citation or reliance event.
   [mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.05(b); cfr-1-97-lii.md, 1.97(h)]

## 5. Safe-to-represent facts

The system may represent the following without converting them into legal
judgments:

- It may store source-stamped dates for filing, national-stage entry, Hague
  publication, RCE or CPA filing, Office-action mailing, notice of allowance,
  prosecution closure, appeal, issue-fee payment, abandonment, withdrawal from
  issue, and grant. These are the raw events named by the timing rules.
  [cfr-1-97-lii.md, 1.97(b)-(d); mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.04(b)(I)-(IV)]

- It may calculate a **candidate** timing window from recorded dates, provided
  the display preserves the controlling dates and edge cases instead of calling
  the output legal compliance. The early window can be the later of the
  three-month date and first merits action; a continuing application's three
  months runs from its actual rather than effective filing date; RCEs and CPAs
  have no three-month branch; and filing on the same day as a closing-action
  mailing falls outside the 1.97(c) “before” window. [mpep-609.md, MPEP
  609.04(b)(I)-(II)]

- Even date arithmetic has visible legal edge cases. A properly executed
  certificate of mailing or transmission or qualifying Priority Mail Express
  event can change the recognized filing date; specified three-month deadlines
  falling on a Saturday, Sunday, or District of Columbia federal holiday can
  move to the next eligible business day; and a withdrawn final action, notice
  of allowance, or Quayle action changes the prosecution-closure posture.
  [mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.04(b)(I)-(II), 609.04(b)(V)] The capture expressly
  discusses the weekend/holiday rule for 1.97(b)(1)-(2) and 1.97(e)(1)-(2), not
  every conceivable three-month period; broader automatic application is
  **NOT IN SOURCES**. [mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.04(b)(I), 609.04(b)(V)]

- It may store whether the 1.17(p) timing fee and applicable 1.17(v) size fee
  were recorded as paid, whether a 1.97(e) statement is present, which statement
  type was used, whether the 1.98(a)(4) written assertion is present, and whether
  the IDS was signed. Presence does not establish sufficiency or truth.
  [cfr-1-97-lii.md, 1.97(a), 1.97(c)-(e); cfr-1-98-lii.md, 1.98(a)(4);
  mpep-609.md, MPEP 609]

- It may store document identities, list membership, page controls, copies and
  omitted-copy flags, language, concise-explanation presence, translation
  presence, and the source of every captured artifact. [cfr-1-98-lii.md,
  1.98(a)-(b)]

- It may store Office evidence exactly as observed: received, placed in file,
  cure requested, considered, not considered, partially considered, line
  through, examiner initials, electronic stamp/signature, examiner date, and the
  Office's reason for nonconsideration. [cfr-1-97-lii.md, 1.97(f), 1.97(i);
  mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.05(a)-(b)]

- It may store parent-child and RCE relationships, whether an item was submitted
  or considered in the parent or before the RCE, whether it was resubmitted, and
  whether the applicant requested patent printing. The legal consequence should
  remain the cited rule or attorney disposition, not a hidden inference.
  [mpep-609.md, MPEP 609.02(I), 609.02(II)(A)-(B)]

- It may derive technical staleness by comparing an observation's recorded
  source-version identity with the current source-version identity, without
  rewriting the old evidence. That is packet provenance behavior, not a legal
  conclusion about disclosure or materiality. [CAPTURE.md, “The deepened play”]

- It may store claim-status events such as pending, cancelled, withdrawn, and
  abandoned, but it must leave the legal consequence for a particular item to an
  attorney disposition because the item may remain material to another claim.
  [cfr-1-56-lii.md, 1.56(a)]

## 6. Open questions for align

1. The packet's second manifest question must be resolved directly: **Which IDS
   mechanics—1.97 timing windows, 1.98 content, and supplemental-IDS states—must
   CandorDisposition be able to record without the system ever computing
   materiality or closure?** [ops/manifest.json, `openQuestions[1]`]

2. Should procedural IDS facts live inside CandorDisposition, or should it point
   to separate immutable IDS-submission and Office-treatment records while
   retaining only the attorney judgment? This determines whether one record is
   being asked to represent both facts and legal dispositions.

3. What is the minimum attorney disposition vocabulary for Rule 56—such as
   material, not material, undetermined, disclose, no disclosure required, and
   superseded—without encoding any of those outcomes as a machine decision?

4. Must every disposition bind to one application, one or more claim versions,
   one information item, and the exact observation/source version, or may it
   cover a documented group? Any grouping rule must preserve the packet's
   no-false-closure invariant.

5. Should the system record a distinct attorney-authored cumulativeness judgment
   for Rule 56 and for the 1.98(c) copy exception, even when both concern the same
   document pair? The captures use cumulativeness in different rule contexts.
   [cfr-1-56-lii.md, 1.56(b); cfr-1-98-lii.md, 1.98(c)]

6. Which 1.97(e) facts belong in the candor record: the signed representation,
