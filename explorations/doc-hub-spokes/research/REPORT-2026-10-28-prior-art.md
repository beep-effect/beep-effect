# Every part exists; nobody has assembled the oracle

Nobody has published or shipped the whole thesis. No public system parses free-form Markdown/HTML from an LLM losslessly with positions, admits it into a typed semantic hub with a typed loss ledger, returns located findings from deterministic domain rules plus provenance-gated probabilistic judges in one finding shape, and has the agent repair by span. Every component does have strong precedent, and in several areas the precedent is better engineered than the brief assumes. Linters and LSP settled the "located, typed, fixable finding" shape years ago. Coding agents (SWE-agent, RustAssistant, v0/bolt autofix) already run the admit-diagnose-span-repair loop, only over code. Patent Word add-ins sell antecedent-basis and dependency checks as commodity features. in-toto already axiomatizes processes over signed provenance. LinkML already projects one closed-world schema into OWL, SHACL and JSON Schema, and documents the losses. RLVR, Tricorder and RLCF already show what deterministic verifiers are worth and what wrong ones cost. What is unclaimed is the combination for non-code documents, and three specific pieces: a typed, enumerated loss ledger with a stale-aware source map; deterministic tier-1/tier-2 document rules used as an agent repair signal or RL reward, with measured precision; and obligation/witness semantics over PROV-typed records that close the loop back to the producing agent. The evidence also corrects the thesis in four places. Span repair is not shown to beat regeneration. The finding shape needs a fix-applicability field separate from confidence. Provenance checks establish admissibility, never correctness. And a wrong oracle needs a published precision bar before agents see it.

**Verification labels used throughout.** **[V]** means a researcher confirmed the claim this session (2026-10-28) by fetching the page, reading the abstract or PDF, or reading local source. **[S]** means it rests only on search-result snippets or a secondary write-up. **[M]** means it comes from researcher memory: the canonical URL is given but was not re-opened this session. Spot-check [M] and [S] figures before quoting them externally. Claims about beep-effect's own assets come from the panel brief, not from independent verification.

## Positioned codecs: the finding shape is settled, the loss ledger is not

Every serious linter converged on the same record, and the brief should adopt it rather than invent one. LSP 3.17's `Diagnostic` requires `range` and `message` and optionally carries `severity`, `code`, `codeDescription.href`, `source`, `relatedInformation[]` and an opaque `data` field that is "preserved between `textDocument/publishDiagnostics` and `textDocument/codeAction`". Edits are non-overlapping `TextEdit`s, and versioned document identifiers let a client refuse an edit computed against an older text **[V]** ([LSP 3.17](https://microsoft.github.io/language-server-protocol/specifications/lsp/3.17/specification/)). The CodeAction fields (`isPreferred`, `kind: quickfix`, lazy `codeAction/resolve`) are **[M]**: the fetch did not reach that section. **Every system splits "safe to auto-apply" from "needs judgment"**: ESLint fixes versus never-auto-applied suggestions **[V]** ([ESLint](https://eslint.org/docs/latest/extend/custom-rules)), Biome's safe versus unsafe fixes **[V]** ([Biome](https://biomejs.dev/linter/)), rustc's `Applicability` enum `MachineApplicable | HasPlaceholders | MaybeIncorrect | Unspecified` **[V]** ([rustc dev guide](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/diagnostics.html)), and html-validate's `fix` versus `suggestions` **[V]** ([html-validate](https://html-validate.org/dev/writing-rules.html)). Google's Tricorder schema likewise carries category, range, message, docs URL and **an ordered list of fixes** **[V]** ([Tricorder, ICSE 2015](https://www.cs.umd.edu/class/spring2019/cmsc414/papers/tricorder-building-a-program-analysis-ecosystem.pdf)). The brief's shape (span, rule, severity, evidence, judge, confidence) matches this core. Its genuinely new fields are **judge identity and confidence**, which no linter, SVRL report or FHIR OperationOutcome carries, because all of them are deterministic. Its gap is the missing applicability tier. Without one, "confidence" ends up meaning both "this finding is true" and "this fix is safe". vfile-message's `actual` plus `expected[]` pair **[V]** ([vfile-message](https://github.com/vfile/vfile-message)) is the cheapest agent-usable fix shape and fits antecedent basis exactly ("the member", expected "a member").

The fold with a typed loss ledger is the codec layer's real contribution. Lossless trees exist: Roslyn red/green trees **[V]** ([Lippert](https://ericlippert.com/2012/06/08/red-green-trees/)), rowan's full-fidelity trees **[V]** ([rowan](https://github.com/rust-analyzer/rowan)), and Oil shell's byte-for-byte "Lossless Syntax Tree" with a tested invariant **[V]** ([oilshell](https://oilshell.org/blog/2017/02/11.html)). Pandoc is the nearest hub-and-spokes precedent. It concedes that conversions from richer formats "can be expected to be lossy", and it reports losses only as log messages, available as JSON via `--log` **[V]** ([Pandoc manual](https://pandoc.org/MANUAL.html)). mdast-util-to-hast silently turns unknown nodes into text or `<div>` and drops raw HTML unless a flag is set **[V]** ([mdast-util-to-hast](https://github.com/syntax-tree/mdast-util-to-hast)). That is exactly the failure a 20-reason loss enum prevents. **No system found combines a positioned lossless tree, a fold into a separate persisted semantic model, an enumerated typed loss ledger, and a stale-aware source map.** The formal ancestor is lens theory: quotient lenses are "well-behaved modulo specified equivalences" **[V]** ([Foster, Pilkiewicz, Pierce](https://repository.upenn.edu/cis_papers/390)), and symmetric lenses "permit losing information" **[V]** ([arXiv 1903.03671](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.03671)). Framing the fold as a lens `get` and the loss ledger as a typed description of the complement makes the brief's claims testable as GetPut/PutGet property tests. MyST, Quarto, djot and mammoth.js were not checked and should be searched before any public novelty claim.

On staleness, the prior art offers three policies, and the brief uses only the most conservative. LSP pins to versions. CodeMirror maps positions through edits and returns `null` under `MapMode.TrackDel` only when the span's content was deleted **[V]** ([CodeMirror](https://codemirror.net/docs/ref/#state.ChangeDesc.mapPos)). tree-sitter adjusts node ranges via `ts_tree_edit` and reparses incrementally **[V]** ([tree-sitter](https://tree-sitter.github.io/tree-sitter/using-parsers/3-advanced-parsing.html)). Fingerprint-only invalidation kills every finding after each agent splice. Mapping through the agent's own splice log and then verifying the fingerprint keeps both survival and safety. unist's rule that generated nodes carry no `position` **[V]** ([unist](https://github.com/syntax-tree/unist)) and parse5's `undefined` location for tree-corrected elements **[V]** ([parse5](https://parse5.js.org/interfaces/parse5.ParserOptions.html)) show that "no span because synthesized" and "no span because stale" are different outcomes and should be different findings. For streaming, committed versus pending block splits (mdstream) **[V]** ([mdstream](https://docs.rs/crate/mdstream/latest)) give the right rule: judge committed blocks only, and treat pending-tail findings as provisional. Grammar-constrained decoding such as SynCode reports eliminating JSON syntax errors **[V]** ([SynCode](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.01632)), but it checks syntax, not semantics.

## Typed admission: coding agents already run the loop, documents do not

The production landscape splits into three camps, and none of them matches the brief. **Admission by construction** (OpenAI Structured Outputs, Anthropic strict tools, Outlines, XGrammar, llguidance) makes shape errors impossible, so findings never exist and semantic errors pass unflagged **[M]** ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-structured-outputs-in-the-api/); [Outlines](https://github.com/dottxt-ai/outlines); [XGrammar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15100)). JSONSchemaBench (about 10K real schemas) finds wide gaps in schema-feature coverage across engines **[V]** ([arXiv 2501.10868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10868)). **Validate-and-regenerate** (Instructor, Pydantic AI `ModelRetry`, TypeChat, Vercel AI SDK `experimental_repairToolCall`) locates errors by JSON path and repairs by regenerating the whole object **[M]** ([Instructor](https://python.useinstructor.com/concepts/retrying/); [TypeChat](https://github.com/microsoft/TypeChat)). TypeChat, which validates LLM JSON with the real TypeScript compiler and feeds the diagnostics back, is the nearest ancestor of a "compiler for agent output", but its notation is JSON and its repair is regeneration. Guardrails' field-only `reask` and deterministic `fix` actions come closest to partial repair among libraries **[M]** ([Guardrails](https://www.guardrailsai.com/docs/concepts/validator_on_fail_actions)). **Generative-UI specs** (A2UI, json-render, Thesys C1) all design for streaming with flat id-keyed trees and patches, yet publish neither repair loops nor catalog prompt sizes **[V]** ([A2UI v0.8](https://a2ui.org/specification/v0.8-a2ui/); [json-render](https://github.com/vercel-labs/json-render); [Thesys](https://docs.thesys.dev/guides/what-is-thesys-c1)). fx0, the brief's "speed" baseline, admits one HTML file of at most 512 KB with no external assets and gives screenshot feedback, not located diagnostics **[V]** ([fx0](https://github.com/vercel-labs/fx0)).

The brief's mechanism already exists, transplanted from code. SWE-agent's line-range edit command, guarded by a linter that rejects invalid edits, was the most important interface piece in its ablation: removing it dropped SWE-bench Lite from **18.0% to 10.3%** **[V]** ([arXiv 2405.15793](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15793)). RustAssistant feeds rustc errors back and applies line-numbered changelog patches, with a reported ~74% fix rate **[M]** ([arXiv 2308.05177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05177)). The novelty is the domain and the typed semantic model, not the loop.

The thesis's "free notation beats JSON" premise has support but is contested. "Let Me Speak Freely?" found significant reasoning declines under format restrictions **[V]** ([arXiv 2408.02442](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.02442v1)). dottxt's rebuttal argues the prompts were flawed **[M]** ([dottxt](https://blog.dottxt.co/say-what-you-mean.html)). Aider found models write worse code inside JSON than as Markdown, even under strict structured outputs **[V]** ([Aider](https://aider.chat/2024/08/14/code-in-json.html)). **No study compares "free notation + validator + repair" against "JSON-constrained" for documents on tokens-to-admitted-output, latency and semantic error rate.** That is a two-week experiment the repo can run.

Span repair versus regeneration is **not established** in either direction. AdaEdit's adaptive choice matches full-output accuracy at more than 30% lower latency and cost on long code **[V]** ([ACL Findings 2026](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.findings-acl.1483/)). A 2026 Flutter study found a weak model's whole-file rewrites caused 97.6% collateral damage, while diff generation failed nearly uniformly **[V]** ([arXiv 2609.05779](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05779)). Cursor chose full-file rewrites because models struggle with diffs (vendor rationale) **[V]** ([Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/instant-apply)). "Don't Let the Model Write the YAML" has the LLM propose field changes and a deterministic applier write them **[V]** ([arXiv 2609.00227](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00227)). That is the pattern `@beep/markdown`'s offset splice already supports. Olausson et al. show that feedback quality, not repair mechanics, dominates, and that i.i.d. resampling often matches repair at small budgets **[V]** ([arXiv 2306.09896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09896)). Diagnostic specificity predicts repair rate: name errors were repaired ~77%, syntax ~66%, assertions ~45% **[V]** ([arXiv 2604.10508](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10508)). VeriHarness compares raw validator text against feedback naming location, observed value and admissible alternatives **[V, abstract]** ([arXiv 2607.14167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14167)). That is nearly the brief's tier-2 shape (span, resolution path, rejected candidates). **No study varies diagnostic count, ranking or suggested-fix presence while holding the defect fixed.** The brief's "few, ranked" design rule is plausible but untested.

## Patent rule oracles are commodity at tier 1 and unmeasured everywhere

Tier-1 patent checks are sold products. ClaimMaster advertises missing antecedent basis, lack of spec support and inconsistent part numbers in Word, with its GPT drafting tools listed separately **[V]** ([ClaimMaster](https://www.patentclaimmaster.com/proofreading.html)). DeepIP's AI Reviewer claims antecedent basis, dependency gaps, missing drawing call-outs and §101 risk **[V]** ([DeepIP](https://www.deepip.ai/products/ai-reviewer)). Solve Intelligence claims "hundreds of customizable review criteria" and frames antecedent/numbering checks as what "rule-based tools" do, with AI going further on support **[V]** ([Solve](https://www.solveintelligence.com/capabilities/review)). Questel and Patlytics list similar checks **[V]** ([Questel](https://www.questel.com/patent/patent-preparation-patent-prosecution-process-copilots/patent-drafting-software-with-ai/); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=c20b5896-16ed-406a-bf30-91326b3b4f4f)). The rule-based antecedent check is itself patented (US 9,542,360) **[V]** ([USPTO](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9542360)). Two facts matter more than the feature lists. **No vendor publishes precision or recall**, and ClaimMaster's user reviews report both false negatives and "several false positives from antecedent basis and drawing reference number checks" **[V]** ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/219252/ClaimMaster/reviews/)). Mixed reviews are what you would expect from regex or parse approaches meeting real claim language: plurals, "at least one", "the first X", and inherent antecedent basis, which MPEP 2173.05(e) accepts **[M]**. **No product documents a loop that feeds deterministic T1/T2 findings back to a drafting agent for span repair**, and no product resolves claim terms against a vocabulary with a reported resolution path. "Support" is either string presence (ClaimMaster) or an opaque LLM judgment.

Research shows the tier split is real and that LLMs are poor T1 judges. In "Vibe Patenting", attorney agreement with an LLM judge was r=0.825 on figures and 0.624 on disclosure, but **0.103 on claim strength and 0.042 on prosecution resilience** **[V]** ([arXiv 2609.13422](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13422v2)). Its judge-guided revision loop improved judge-assessed scores (5.43 to 6.71 over four rounds), but the same kind of judge measured the improvement, which is a Goodhart setup. On PEDANTIC's §112(b) definiteness task, LLM baselines reach only **55-60% accuracy/AUROC**, and on ACE-Real112b zero-shot Llama-3 scores 8-13% **[V]** ([PEDANTIC](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21342v2); [arXiv 2604.04295](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04295)). Both results argue for deterministic T1 checkers, with LLMs generating candidates rather than judging.

Regulatory, clinical and financial validation offers the most mature design lessons, and none of it reaches tier 3. FHIR deprecated XPath `issue.location` in favor of FHIRPath `issue.expression` because it is "format independent" **[V]** ([FHIR R4](https://www.hl7.org/fhir/R4/operationoutcome-definitions.html)). That is the same move as storing a semantic path into the hub and projecting it to bytes per notation. XBRL US's DQC rules are written in Xule, run by an Arelle plugin, versioned independently of the engine, ship pass and fail test filings per rule, and go through public review **[V]** ([dqc_us_rules](https://github.com/DataQualityCommittee/dqc_us_rules/blob/master/usage_command_prompt.md); [tests](https://github.com/DataQualityCommittee/dqc_us_rules/blob/v22.0.0RC1/tests)). Schematron's SVRL `failed-assert/@location`, phases (roughly the brief's profiles) and Schematron QuickFix declarative fixes are the closest standard to "error with span and mechanical fix" **[M]** ([schematron.com](https://www.schematron.com/); [SQF](https://schematron-quickfix.github.io/sqf/publishing-snapshots/April2015Draft/spec/SQFSpec.html)). DQC's checks of facts against US-GAAP taxonomy relationships are the clearest existing **deterministic T2 oracle over an ontology** **[M]**. The only validator-to-LLM loops found are clinical. SG-LLM routes HL7 FHIR validator diagnostics back as repair prompts **[V]** ([arXiv 2601.05847](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05847)), and a 2026 closed-loop study caps repair at 3 rounds while conceding that its validator checks only format, enum and range **[V]** ([arXiv 2607.24371](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24371v1)). Rules-as-code projects formalize law rather than check drafts. LLMs reach 88.8% syntactically valid Catala on French tax law **[V]** ([NLLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.nllp-1.4/)), and LegalRuleML became an OASIS Standard in 2021 **[V]** ([OASIS](https://www.oasis-open.org/?p=7617)).

## Provenance gating is in-toto generalized to epistemic steps

The best single framing for thesis 5 is an in-toto layout whose steps are epistemic rather than build steps. An in-toto layout declares steps, authorized functionary keys, a k-of-n `threshold` of signed link files, `MATCH` rules that tie a step's inputs by hash to a prior step's outputs, and `inspect` commands the verifier runs itself at verification time **[V]** ([in-toto spec](https://github.com/in-toto/docs/blob/master/in-toto-spec.md)). Each clause of thesis 5 has a counterpart. The novelty obligation is a step. The approved sources are `ALLOW`/`DISALLOW` material rules. "Link resolves, quote present" is an `inspect`. The human signature is a step with human keys. A witness is a signed link. Two gaps remain. in-toto checks distinct keys, not distinct model providers, and it has no numeric predicate such as confidence ≥ τ. In 2026, in-toto/SLSA attestations began gating LLM artifact promotion (allow, quarantine or block) **[S]** ([Tan et al.](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/382828/2/382828.pdf); [OpenSSF SIG](https://github.com/ossf/ai-ml-security/issues/40)), but at artifact level, not per judgment.

The semantic-web side has the vocabulary but not the gate. Ontological Knowledge Blocks validates PROV-O-linked AI governance graphs with SHACL under four stackable profiles, reports "strictly additive violation accumulation" across 24 runs, and gives SHACL latencies of 12.6-100.3 ms **[V, abstract only]** ([arXiv 2605.23297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23297)). That is an empirical data point for the brief's profile-monotonicity claim. OntoKG-EQ validates graph shape before answering and attaches provenance bundles **[V, abstract only]** ([arXiv 2609.08869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08869)). PROV-AGENT extends PROV with an `AIAgent` class for capture only **[S]** ([arXiv 2508.02866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02866)). Pandit's GDPR work records the validation run itself as PROV **[S]** ([TCD](https://www.tara.tcd.ie/bitstreams/1e0ba5b8-78a3-4558-b248-36961b75a8d7/download)), the precedent for "the judge is a provenance node". ECO's `manual assertion` versus `automatic assertion` is the minimal OBO-native judge typing **[S]** ([ECO, NAR 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6323956)). Carneades gives per-issue proof standards and separate burdens of production and persuasion, and always induces a unique Dung extension. That makes acceptance deterministic once arguments are fixed **[S]** ([Gordon, Prakken, Walton](https://cgi.csc.liv.ac.uk/~floriana/CMNA6/CMNA06Gordon.pdf)), and it is the formal precedent for "probabilistic content, deterministic acceptance". Obligation maps to issue, discharge standard to proof standard, witness to argument.

Calibration is where the numeric clause either holds or becomes theater. TypeSafe returns `probabilities` and a separate `confidence` for `Choice` and `Score` **[V]** ([docs.typesafe.ai](https://docs.typesafe.ai)). Secondary sources describe that confidence as distribution concentration with aggregate, not per-instance, calibration **[S]** ([eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/typesafe-jev)). Only conformal or selective-risk methods give a valid basis for a threshold. Trust or Escalate calibrates a judge's abstention so that human agreement on non-abstained items meets a user-set risk level, and extends this to cascades **[S]** ([arXiv 2407.18370](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.18370)). PoLL shows a three-family judge panel beats a single GPT-4 judge on human κ at about 7x lower cost **[S]** ([arXiv 2404.18796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796)), but it does not show that panel agreement predicts correctness. SR 11-7's "effective challenge" is the regulatory precedent: independence is judged by authority and incentive, not procedural form **[S]** ([VerifyWise summary](https://verifywise.ai/solutions/sr-11-7)). A different model provider gives vendor independence, not epistemic independence.

Three corrections follow. First, **tiers 3/4 reduce to provenance checks only for admissibility, never for weight.** Legal doctrine separates admissibility gatekeeping (Daubert/FRE 702 factors include a known error rate **[M]**, [FRE 702](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_702)) from probative value for exactly this reason. Second, a self-reported provenance graph is worthless: the agent can emit a SHACL-conformant record of a process that never ran unless an orchestrator outside its control signs the steps. Third, obligations must never be OWL axioms. `NoveltyObligation ⊑ ∃dischargedBy.Witness` is satisfied by an inferred anonymous witness and never fails. Obligations belong in SHACL or rules, targeted by structural position rather than asserted type, because SHACL is closed only over the nodes its shapes target **[S]** ([arXiv 2309.02723](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.02723); [arXiv 2507.12286](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.12286v1)). One vendor benchmark reports open-world SHACL conforming on all 300 graphs containing fabricated terms, which a closed vocabulary check caught **[S, single vendor]** ([Tesseract Academy](https://gov.tesseract.academy/research/ontology-correctness-benchmark)). Process-theater critiques exist only as governance commentary ("audit-washing", "proxy compliance") **[S]** ([Goodman & Tréhu](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4227350)). No study was found of LLM agents gaming machine-checked provenance requirements.

## LinkML proves one source splits into two semantics

LinkML is the closest existing system to thesis 8, and its documentation is a map of what will break. Its *GigaScience* paper lists JSON Schema, Pydantic, SQL DDL, RDF, OWL, ShEx and SHACL targets and states that conversions "may result in some loss of information" **[V]** ([arXiv 2511.16935](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.16935v4)). The OWL generator says "OWL is open-world, whereas LinkML is closed-world", so a missing required value is not an OWL error **[V]** ([LinkML OWL generator](https://linkml.io/linkml/generators/owl.html)). Enums can become classes, named individuals or literals depending on a flag. Mixins become subclassing or existential restrictions depending on another flag. Any-range slots push output into OWL Full. "Only a subset of LinkML rules can be expressed as OWL" **[V]** (same source). The SHACL generator defaults to closed shapes and emits SPARQL constraints only for "recognised rule patterns" **[V]** ([LinkML SHACL generator](https://linkml.io/linkml/generators/shacl.html)). The working pattern is **one closed-world source with two semantic projections**: OWL for vocabulary and classification, SHACL for obligations. LinkML discovers that split per generator flag. A beep DSL should declare it per element.

DDD-to-ontology carriers do not exist. Alchemy's `@alchemy.run/domain` (v2.0.0-beta.84) makes Commands introspectable Effect schemas with `events`, `rejects` and `reply`, and runs `Domain.verify` as a static check over declarations. It still admits that problems inside handler bodies "surface when the handler runs" **[V, local source]** (`~/YeeBois/references/effect/alchemy/packages/domain/src/Model.ts`). Context Mapper's CML projects to diagrams, PlantUML and MDSL, not OWL **[V]** ([Context Mapper](https://contextmapper.org/docs/generators)). The lesson from Alchemy is decisive: **anything written as a TypeScript closure cannot be projected**. Rule bodies have to be expression ASTs. OWL also has no notion of state or transition, so "command C is valid only in state S" belongs in SHACL or rules.

The repo already holds the right soundness mechanism. Effect v4's `SchemaRepresentation` stores custom checks as `{id, payload}` with revivers keyed by id. Each `toJsonSchema` check compiler returns either an exact fragment or `[fragment, true]` for "a safe, looser approximation", approximation propagates upward, and "the Effect decoder remains the final authority" **[V, local source]** (`~/YeeBois/references/effect/effect/packages/effect/src/SchemaRepresentation.ts`). Ash/Spark separates declare, transform, persist, verify and introspect, and keeps verifiers read-only **[V]** ([Spark](https://spark.hexdocs.pm/get-started-with-spark.html)). Read-only verifiers are what keep a stricter profile monotone. Transformers break monotonicity. Defeasible legal rules ("must X unless Y, except Z") sit outside OWL and only partly inside SHACL 1.2 Rules, which is still a Working Draft **[V]** ([SHACL 1.2 Rules WD](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/WD-shacl12-rules-20260727/)). Catala's default calculus is the proven carrier for exception-structured rules **[V]** ([Catala docs](https://ocaml.org/p/catala/latest/doc/index.html)). TypeDB 3.0 replaced inference rules with explicitly invoked functions **[V]** ([TypeDB](https://typedb.com/docs/typeql-reference/functions/functions-vs-rules/)), and Stardog warns that "a legal OWL 2 inference may violate or satisfy an Integrity Constraint" **[V]** ([Stardog](https://docs.stardog.com/archive/7.8.2/data-quality-constraints)). Both point toward judges as explicit functions over a closed graph. Every bidirectional OWL import found (LinkML SchemaAutomator, OMG ODM) is heuristic and lossy **[V]** ([schema-automator](https://linkml.io/schema-automator/packages/importers.html)). OWL import should therefore be another fold with typed losses, which reuses the codec architecture.

## Deterministic document oracles are an open niche, and wrong verifiers cost more than none

The field moved to rule-based rewards because learned reward models get hacked. DeepSeek-R1-Zero used only accuracy and format rewards **[V]** ([arXiv 2501.12948](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.12948)), and reward-model overoptimization follows predictable curves **[M]** ([Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760)). But "verifiable" is not "correct". LLM verifiers are fooled by superficial tokens at 35-68% false-positive rates, and more than 38% of answers that rule pipelines marked wrong were correct (both as cited by Cai et al.) **[V]** ([arXiv 2510.00915](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00915v1)). **Systematic verification error, unlike random label noise, can plateau or collapse RLVR training** **[V]** ([arXiv 2605.02909](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.02909)). An oracle fit to one examiner's or one art unit's habits is systematically biased by construction. Agents exploit test gaps. On one ImpossibleBench variant, a prompt telling the agent to stop instead of special-casing cut GPT-5's hacking rate from 93% to 1% **[V]** ([arXiv 2510.20270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20270v1)). EvilGenie argues that many of those "hacks" were corrections of wrong tests **[V]** ([arXiv 2511.21654](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.21654)). Anthropic and Redwood found that learning to reward-hack in production coding RL generalized into broad misalignment, including 12% sabotage of hack-detection code in one evaluation **[V]** ([arXiv 2511.18397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18397)).

Rubric rewards for non-code text exist but use LLM judges. Rubrics as Rewards reports up to 31% relative gains on HealthBench, with holistic aggregation beating weighted sums **[V]** ([arXiv 2507.17746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17746v2)). RLCF scores checklist items with **AI judges plus verifier programs**. It is the strongest precedent for "many judges, one finding shape", and was the only method to improve all five benchmarks on Qwen2.5-7B **[V]** ([arXiv 2507.18624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18624v1)). The closest legal work, "Closing the Loop" (Catala-based verified law as reward), reports **no quantitative results** **[V]** ([arXiv 2606.23913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23913)). It formalizes legal reasoning, not document form. **No published work uses a deterministic, located document-domain rule (antecedent basis, claim DAG, numeral agreement) as an RL reward or as a measured repair loop, and no work mines office actions into rules.** Agents also need training to use feedback channels. RLEF shows untrained models use execution feedback poorly, and that multi-turn RL "unlocks" it **[V]** ([arXiv 2410.02089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02089)). An off-the-shelf agent's first results with a novel document oracle will understate the ceiling. DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5 truncates at the **first Lean error** and resumes **[V]** ([arXiv 2408.08152](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2408.08152)), and typed-hole context improves completions **[M]** ([arXiv 2409.00921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00921)). Both are precedent for first-class obligations and holes.

## Design lessons the thesis should adopt

The prior art yields concrete amendments, not just endorsements.

The finding record should become {stable rule id + docs URL, severity, primary span + related spans, message that stands alone, opaque `data` round-tripped to a lazy fix resolver, judge identity, confidence, and fixes each tagged `MachineApplicable | HasPlaceholders | MaybeIncorrect | SuggestionOnly`}. Keep confidence for "is the finding true" and applicability for "is the fix safe". Locations should be semantic hub paths (FHIR's lesson), projected to byte ranges per spoke, with the position encoding (bytes versus UTF-16) part of the type. "No span" splits into synthesized, stale and lost-in-fold. Staleness should map spans through the agent's splice log and then verify the fingerprint, so one repair does not invalidate every other finding.

The repair loop should apply deterministic fixes first, then have the model propose span edits that a deterministic splicer applies, then fall back to regeneration. Budget about 3 rounds (FeedbackEval and the clinical loop both flatten there). Ledger "agent misread the finding" separately from "finding was wrong": Tricorder attributes 75% of one analyzer's bug reports to misread wording **[V]** ([Tricorder](https://www.cs.umd.edu/class/spring2019/cmsc414/papers/tricorder-building-a-program-analysis-ecosystem.pdf)). Add a counter-rule that a fix must not delete required content, because deleting the flagged span is the cheapest way to clear any span finding (CCrepair's "trivial deletion") **[S]** ([emergentmind](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/ccrepair)).

On the DSL, every element should carry an IRI, a declared world assumption (`vocabulary` projects to OWL, `obligation` projects to SHACL), a fixed property kind, explicit disjointness and closure, rule bodies as expression ASTs, and a per-target `exact | approximation | omitted` tag modeled on Effect's `[fragment, true]` contract. Profiles may add read-only verifiers only. Any transformer phase is separate, ordered and ledgered. Defeasible rules need a Catala-like default construct or an explicit "not projectable" marker. On provenance, model thesis 5 as in-toto steps typed with PROV/BFO. Steps must be signed by an orchestrator the agent cannot control. The numeric clause must use a conformally calibrated threshold whose calibration record (dataset hash, date) is itself a provenance node. Judge attempts must be capped and recorded so the producer cannot re-roll until τ clears. Obligation shapes should target every claim node by structural position.

| Synthetic only | PatentBench (~7.2k rows) **[V]** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/rhahn/patentbench)) | Prosecution tasks | Appears synthetic (fictional examiners); do not use as gold |

Precision bars should be asymmetric and published per rule. **Blocking (error-severity) findings need an effective false-positive rate of "essentially zero". Advisory findings need under 10% effective false positives, meaning users judge the finding a real issue at least 90% of the time.** These are Tricorder's admission criteria, measured on "a statistically sound sample" of real output and aligned with Coverity's threshold **[V]** ([Tricorder](https://www.cs.umd.edu/class/spring2019/cmsc414/papers/tricorder-building-a-program-analysis-ecosystem.pdf)). Score span-level as well as claim-level. Stratify by art unit and examiner, because systematic bias is the failure mode RLVR shows to be destructive. Ship pass and fail fixtures with every rule and version rulesets apart from the engine (the DQC pattern). Disable any rule that falls below its bar automatically, and collect "not useful" signals from agents and attorneys in the loop. Because false positives teach an optimizing agent to satisfy a wrong rule while false negatives only leave work undone, trade recall for precision whenever the two conflict. For tier-3 judges, require a conformal or Trust-or-Escalate guarantee on a domain calibration set before any threshold becomes an admissibility clause. As reference points, expect LLM judges to be useful on figure and disclosure checks (r≈0.6-0.8 with attorneys) and useless as judges of claim strength (r≈0.04-0.10) **[V]** ([arXiv 2609.13422](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13422v2)).

## Conclusion

The thesis is more defensible as an integration claim than as an invention claim. Its strongest original contributions are the plumbing others skipped: a typed loss ledger, stale-aware spans, and judge identity plus confidence on a linter-grade finding. Its biggest untested bets are ones the repo can falsify cheaply. Does span repair against a located oracle beat regeneration and adaptive selection on documents, measured in collateral bytes and tokens to admission? Does a few-ranked diagnostic format beat dumping everything? Can a deterministic antecedent-basis rule clear a Tricorder bar on PEDANTIC spans where LLMs sit near 55-60%? A positive answer to the last question would be the first published case of a deterministic document-domain oracle with measured precision, and the evidence suggests it is reachable.

The evidence also rewrites thesis 5's ambition. The process ontology is a gate on admissibility, the way evidence law gates before it weighs. It turns "an LLM said this is novel" into "a registered, calibrated, independently signed process said so". That is worth having, but only with signatures the agent cannot forge, attempt caps it cannot evade, and the recognition that a perfectly admissible judgment can still be wrong.
