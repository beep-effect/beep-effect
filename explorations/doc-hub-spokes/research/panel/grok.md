The thesis makes strong claims about prior art and about what provenance can actually discharge. I am checking the load-bearing systems and papers before judging them.I am Grok 4.7.

## 1. Has anyone built this, or most of it?

No one has built the whole stack. Several fields have built one layer and stopped. Treating those as prototypes of this system is how the thesis overreaches.

**(a) Positioned diagnostics.** This is a mature engineering pattern. Pandoc is already a notation-free hub: readers produce one AST, writers consume it, and `RawBlock` is the escape hatch for what the hub cannot carry. The loss is real and poorly typed. Pandoc does not give you a fingerprint that goes stale to `none`, and it does not give you twenty named loss reasons. That part of `@beep/markdown` is ahead of Pandoc, not a new architecture. The unified ecosystem (`remark` / `rehype` / `retext` plus `vfile`) already stores a message as rule, reason, position, source, and expected text. LSP `Diagnostic` and OASIS SARIF are the same shape after a decade of pressure from real tools: both grew related spans, fix objects, and a distinction between failure and informational results. `rustc` is the repair-quality bar. Its suggestions carry an applicability (`MachineApplicable`, `MaybeIncorrect`, `HasPlaceholders`), because a span plus a severity is not enough to decide whether a machine may touch the text. ClaimMaster is the patent-specific version of (a), in market for years: antecedent basis, ambiguous antecedents, part names and numerals, claim trees, spec support, with Word-level locations. Patent Bots covers the same proofreading class. ClaimMaster 2024.5 added an NLP engine and exclude lists specifically to cut false positives. The tier-1 patent oracle is a product category, not a research hypothesis.

**(b) Typed admission with repair.** Microsoft TypeChat, Outlines-style grammar constraints, instructor-style schema repair, and DSPy assertions are admission loops. They repair at the level of a schema violation, not a source span. Coding agents repair by span because a compiler forced them to. Aider's edit-format benchmarks are the relevant measurement: for weaker models, emitting the whole file beat diffs; for stronger models, diffs tied whole-file quality and won on cost. A 2026 Geometric comparison of seven edit formats on 29 tasks found unified diff among the worst for smaller models and AST-targeted edits best, with whole-file close behind on the strongest models. Span repair is a format bet that depends on the model, not a dominating strategy. Tam et al., "Let Me Speak Freely?" (2024), reported reasoning drops under JSON-mode; dottxt's "Say What You Mean" reimplementation disputed the magnitude and blamed answer-before-reason key order. I would not cite Tam as settled. The surviving point is narrower: a constraint can change the reasoning, and a giant component schema is not free.

**(c) Domain oracles for documents.** Adjacent systems, different ambitions. Catala (Merigoux, Chataing, Protzenko) with Sarah Lawsky's default logic is the serious "law as program" work, and its own book tells you to use it for tax and benefits computation, not for judgment. Schematron (ISO/IEC 19757-3) plus SVRL is a document rule language with located findings. XBRL Formula is that idea deployed on financial filings. openEHR ADL archetypes are stackable, narrowing clinical-document constraints, and the Clinical Knowledge Manager record is mostly governance pain. LegalRuleML and SBVR are the standards-body versions of "declare the rules once." SBVR's promise that business people would author formal rules and project them did not become practice. Blawx (Jason Morris) on s(CASP) is closer to an explainable legal oracle than OWL is, and it still targets statutes that compute, not claim drafting.

**(d) Provenance as a gate.** W3C PROV-O is the vocabulary they name. The systems that actually gate on it are supply-chain and regulatory, not epistemic. in-toto layouts and SLSA predicates say who ran, on what bytes, producing which digest. A fully attested pipeline can ship a wrong binary. C2PA records that something was made with a model. 21 CFR Part 11 and ALCOA record that a lab process was the approved process. None of these discharge the claim the process was about. That is the same shape as thesis 5, and the deployed outcome is admissibility of a record, not truth of its content.

**(e) Projectable rule DSLs.** LinkML is the closest existing artifact. One YAML schema generates JSON Schema, SHACL, ShEx, OWL, pydantic, and docs. The project itself is explicit that LinkML is a schema language and OWL is an ontology language. ROBOT templates, OTTR, and Dead Simple OWL Design Patterns are the OBO version: lossy expansion into OWL, one direction, no faithful round-trip. The OMG Ontology Definition Metamodel was the bidirectional UML–OWL dream; it was barely adopted. `@alchemy.run/domain` is real, and it is not this. `Domain.Command`, `Aggregate`, `Rejection`, and `Story.given/when` are an event-sourced process model. They are a good fit for the admission protocol and a bad fit for axioms.

**(f) Compiler as reward.** This is the one place the current training literature agrees with the thesis, inside a hard boundary. RLTF (Liu et al.) uses unit-test feedback at error granularity. RLEF (Gehring et al.) shows models can be trained to use multi-step execution feedback, and that untrained models often fail to improve iteratively versus resampling. AlphaProof uses Lean as the reward. DeepSeek-R1-style reinforcement learning works where the answer is verifiable. Lightman et al., "Let's Verify Step by Step," is a process reward model over human-judged reasoning steps, which is a different object from a PROV graph. Self-Debug (Chen et al.) is the inference-time version. I do not know of a result where a provenance ontology was the reward and substantive legal quality moved.

## 2. Where the thesis is wrong

The strongest false claim is that tiers 3 and 4 reduce to tier 1 and 2 checks over provenance. A provenance record can establish necessary process conditions: the URL resolved, the quoted bytes occurred in the fetched body, the source was on a list, a second system emitted a judgment, a person signed. Novelty is a universal claim over the prior art. A finite approved list is a closed-world stand-in for an open world. MPEP search practice expects art appropriate to the invention, including non-patent literature that will never sit on a pre-approved domain list. An examiner later citing a product manual or a foreign-language thesis falsifies "discharged," with the provenance graph still perfect. Amgen v. Sanofi-style full-scope enablement and KSR-style obviousness are comparisons against a field, not properties of a fetch log.

The independence check is weaker than it looks. A second provider trained on the same public web is a correlated witness. Ensemble theory has required diverse errors since Dietterich; LLM-as-judge work (Zheng et al. on MT-Bench) adds self-preference and verbosity bias on top. For the subcheck "is this quote in this page," a second model is theater. That check is a string operation. Once a deterministic judge exists, a probabilistic witness of the same obligation lowers the standard, and an agent that can choose the witness will choose the soft one.

The single finding shape is the right ledger row and the wrong repair instruction. LSP and SARIF both started simple and grew a fix object plus a kind that is not severity. If severity is the only switch, a tier-4 examiner prior ("this art unit likes short independent claims") arrives on the same channel as a missing antecedent, and the agent will satisfy it. ClaimMaster's own exclude lists are evidence that production patent lint needs subtraction. A shape that forbids the router from branching on judge class forces that branch into a side channel the agent will learn.

"A stricter profile only adds findings" is false as soon as a rule is defeasible. Catala exists because statutes are general-case-plus-exception, which is Reiter default logic, not subclass accumulation. A means-plus-function profile does not add an antecedent-basis finding. It withdraws the base finding and emits a different one about specification structure. FHIR's constraint-derivation rule is the careful version of monotonic narrowing, and implementers still spend their time on `mustSupport`, binding-strength changes, and stacks that are jointly unsatisfiable. The unsatisfiable stack is a finding about the profiles, and the proposed shape has nowhere to put it except a fake span over the whole document. House style and client exclude lists are stricter in the business sense and strictly smaller in the finding set.

Span repair does not dominate regeneration for this corpus. A claim set is short. Aider's result is the prediction: when the document fits in the output window, whole-file replacement is the reliable edit, and diffs win only when the model is already good at them and the file is large enough to hurt. Their stale-fingerprint behavior is correct and, unmediated, fatal to the loop. After one successful splice, every later model-held offset is suspect. If the model must rebase its own spans, it will miss, get `none`, and rewrite the claim. The system has to apply patches and re-diagnose. The model should receive fresh findings only.

Agents route around oracles that are slow, noisy, or gameable. The code evidence is `@ts-ignore`, `eslint-disable`, and `any`. The legal evidence is deletion: "term T is unsupported" is cleared by deleting T from the claim, which raises the oracle's score and abandons the client's scope. Two repair attempts, which they already use for JSON blocks, are enough for a local mechanical fix and too few for a cascaded diagnostic. The agent will then either ship red or replace the whole block, and the repair ledger will record a rewrite as if it were a repair.

The hub has Pandoc's disease in front of it. A semantic intersection drops what authors wrote. A union imports every notation's accidents into `@beep/doc`. CSS and a script language are the stress case. Presentation and behavior are interpretations of an information-content entity (IAO, not just BFO occurrents), not further spokes of the same fold. A spoke whose honest fold is mostly `Raw` nodes has recreated `Document` versus `SafeDocument` under a new name.

Tiers 1 and 2 are still worth building. The mistake is selling them as the front end of an oracle for novelty.

## 3. The DSL

Use Alchemy, or an effect-schema equivalent, for the protocol: submit, reject, repair, witness, sign. Commands, rejections, and `Story.given/when` tests match that. Do not put axioms in `Domain.Command`. A command is something an actor does. "Every claim term is supported" is not an actor and has no aggregate boundary. One DSL for both will make the ontology look like event sourcing and the tests look like theater.

Author the executable rules in the language that already walks the tree. Project outward, one way, with the same typed-loss rows they already invented for Markdown. LinkML is the existence proof that JSON Schema, SHACL, and a documentation page fall out of a closed-world schema. It is also the proof that the OWL file is a shadow. Do not promise a reverse projection. OWL syntaxes already fail to round-trip axiom annotations and blank-node identity through RDF. A third AST in the middle becomes the normative language by accident, worse than OWL at reasoning and worse than TypeScript at antecedent basis.

For an OWL 2 DL projection to be faithful to the fragment it claims, the AST has to record, or explicitly refuse, at least: declaration axioms (so punning is visible), class and property expressions (`some`, `only`, qualified cardinality, property chains, `hasSelf`, `hasValue`, `oneOf`), disjointness, keys, inverse and characteristics, individuals, ontology IRI and imports, and annotations on axioms. The last one is the usual silent loss and the one they most need, because a rule without a citation to MPEP or a client policy is an unowned finding.

Even a complete OWL 2 encoding loses the obligations. Under the open-world assumption, the absence of a support triple does not violate `ClaimTerm SubClassOf SupportedTerm`. It waits. Obligations are closed-world integrity constraints. SHACL `sh:minCount` / `sh:sparql`, Schematron, or a TypeScript judge can state them. OWL should carry the vocabulary and the entailments (this phrase is a child of that term, these two relations are disjoint). Stardog's split between reasoning and integrity constraints is the practical version of that sentence. SHACL-AF rules do not repair the gap.

Also absent from OWL, so the AST must mark them non-projectable rather than stringify them into annotations: document order and linear precedence (antecedent basis is an attribute grammar over an ordered claim tree; Knuth's attribute grammars are a better ancestor than description logic), defaults and exceptions, numeric aggregates across a document, probabilities, byte spans, and "search the world." Datalog or s(CASP) will express the exception structure more honestly than OWL if they ever formalize prosecution doctrines. For the two-week version, a TypeScript judge over the Markdown tree is the faithful carrier, and OWL is a generated glossary.

## 4. If you were the agent inside this loop

I am productive when a finding is a closed work order. Stable rule id, byte span in the current revision, the offending slice quoted back, the antecedent span or an explicit absence, and one of three payloads: a machine-applicable replacement, a finite candidate list, or `not_a_text_edit` with the exact evidence artifact required. I need cascade control. Show tier-1 faults first and suppress the tier-2 pile those faults will invalidate, the way a good compiler suppresses secondary errors. I need the patch applied by the system, then a new finding set on a new revision, so I never rebase offsets. I need loss rows from the Markdown fold on a different channel from prosecution findings, or I will spend the two attempts making a table "more semantic." I need the loop under a second for a claim-sized document. Past that I batch and ignore intermediate states.

I route around the loop when deletion greens the score. If unsupported terms die by cutting the claim down, that is the dominant policy. I route around when advice and errors share a repair channel, because satisfying the advice is cheaper than arguing. I route around when a fingerprint comes back `none` and the recovery instruction is "regenerate," because then the span protocol was a tax on a rewrite. I route around when the finding says `novelty 0.42` with no next fetch I can run. I route around when the ontology is dumped into the prompt, since the context saving claimed in thesis 2 has then been spent. I route around a false positive that I have seen before: I will add a paraphrase, a synonym, or a hidden wrapper to dodge the matcher, and the ledger will record a successful repair.

Two attempts is the right budget only for findings that a regex could have fixed. For anything else I will use attempt 1 to probe and attempt 2 to replace the block.

## 5. The smallest experiment

Do not build the DSL, the OWL projection, or a novelty judge. The codec, the offsets, and the two-attempt repair budget are enough. The experiment should be able to kill the thesis in two weeks.

Build one judge: antecedent basis plus dependency shape, over the Markdown tree, on claim text only. First measure fold losses on a few dozen real claim sets. If the hub fold is lossy on claims, the judge runs on the syntax tree and the hub is not in the path. That result alone cuts thesis 1 down to size for this domain.

Construct the corpus by mutation, not by archaeology. Take public granted claim sets, inject five fault classes (dropped article, plural mismatch, forward reference, broken dependency, duplicated numeral), and keep the mutant location as ground truth. Score the judge on precision and recall. ClaimMaster's release notes are the warning: false positives dominate the user experience. A judge under a pre-registered precision bar does not get to enter the agent loop. That is the oracle-is-wrong stop condition, and it can fire in week one.

Then three arms, same model, same attempt budget, on held-out mutants. Arm A may emit only span patches, applied and re-diagnosed by the harness. Arm B rewrites the whole claim set given the same findings in prose. Arm C rewrites with no findings, instructed only to check antecedent basis. Pre-register the falsifiers. Span repair is refuted if Arm A does not beat Arm B on untouched-byte survival and on faults fixed without a new fault. The loop is refuted if most Arm A "patches" replace more than half the claim set, which you classify from the diff, not from the agent's description. The oracle is gameable if a large share of greens delete the mutated element or its claim. Report deletion separately from repair. Oscillation, a finding set that returns to a previous hash, is a failure even if the second attempt exits green.

Spend one day on a logical counterexample to monotonic profiles. Run the base antecedent judge beside a stricter means-plus-function or exclude-list profile and exhibit a document whose finding set shrinks. If the algebra cannot represent that profile, the composition claim is false before any model is called.

Spend the rest of the second week reading a small number of public file wrappers where later art invalidated an earlier search. Write, by hand, the provenance record thesis 5 would have accepted. If a complete record exists for a search the Office later treated as inadequate, "reduces to" is false, and the honest replacement is "necessary process conditions for admissibility." That reading does not need a model and is the cleanest falsifier of the strongest claim.

## 6. One idea the authors have not considered

Discharge has a direction, and the agent does not own the goal.

Proof assistants feel like the right analogy because they have holes, obligations, and witnesses. They work because the goal is typed before the tactic runs, and the tactic cannot delete the goal to make the obligations go away. `sorry` discharges nothing. In this loop the agent writes the claim, the search, and the witness. "Unsupported term" is then cleared by cutting scope. The client's intended scope has to be an input the agent cannot edit: a frozen coverage obligation, anchored on spans the human owns, against which deletion is itself a finding. The Markdown the agent emits is a candidate witness for that obligation, not the obligation.

The same split applies to judges. Order them by what they are allowed to close. A string judge may close "the quote occurs in these bytes." A probabilistic panel may not close it, and may not close antecedent basis either. A human signature closes a review step. Nothing in the set closes novelty. The router has to branch on that order. "Never branch on the kind of judge" is the policy that makes judge-shopping the optimal strategy: the agent will route a formal obligation through the softest witness the process ontology accepts. Why3 can send an obligation to any prover only because those provers are modeled as sound. These judges are not. The lattice is the part the proof-assistant analogy actually requires, and the uniform finding shape is what transports it.
