## 1. Prior art: what exists, and how close it is

**(a) Positioned diagnostics for a notation.** This is well-trodden. The LSP `Diagnostic` shape (range, severity, code, source, relatedInformation) and OASIS **SARIF** are the canonical unified-finding formats; SARIF in particular already solves "many judges, one finding envelope" across arbitrary analysis tools, including fingerprints and partial fixes. On the document side, the **unist/remark/rehype** ecosystem is the closest living relative of the whole hub-and-spoke thesis: a notation-free AST with byte-accurate `position` on every node, per-notation parsers and serializers, and **vfile/vfile-message** as the unified located-finding carrier (ruleId, source, severity). **textlint** and **proselint/vale** do rule-driven prose linting with spans. **Pandoc** is the original hub-and-spoke document model, but it is deliberately lossy and position-free — the authors' real delta over Pandoc is losslessness, source maps, and the loss ledger, not the architecture. **Schematron** (assertions over XML with located SVRL reports) is the 2003 version of "declarative rules → positioned findings." So: the components all exist; the integrated, *lossless, ledgered* version does not, as far as I know.

**(b) Typed admission of LLM output with repair.** **Microsoft TypeChat** is the direct ancestor: model emits JSON against a TS schema, validation errors are fed back verbatim, repair loop until it typechecks. **Guardrails AI** (RAIL spec: validators + reask), **Instructor** (Pydantic validation + retry), OpenAI **structured outputs** / **Outlines** / **XGrammar** (constrained decoding — validity enforced at sampling time, no repair needed). The chat surface described in the brief (validate during streaming, JSON-Patch repair against the failing block) is a real contribution beyond these — none of them repair *by span within a larger document*; they regenerate the failing value wholesale. Constrained decoding is the looming competitor: for Tier-1-ish constraints, grammar-constrained sampling makes admission free and repair unnecessary. The honest positioning is: constrained decoding handles local syntax; this system handles *document-level* constraints (cross-references, DAGs, antecedents) that no token-level grammar can express. That's a real niche, but it should be stated that way.

**(c) Domain rule oracles for documents.** Patent-specific Tier 1 already exists commercially and the authors should study it closely: **ClaimMaster** and **LexisNexis PatentOptimizer** do exactly antecedent-basis checking, claim dependency/numbering validation, reference-numeral-vs-spec consistency, and part-name drift detection — with spans and mechanical fixes. This is a direct "someone built the wedge" answer: the Tier 1 layer is validated as a product category, which de-risks it but also means it isn't novel. Adjacent: **Arelle** (open-source XBRL validation for SEC filings — deterministic rule engines over financial documents with located errors), **FHIR validators** and **Schematron-over-CDA** in clinical, **HyperSTE** and **Acrolinx** (controlled-language / enterprise content governance: vocabulary-ontology + rules + profile stacking + spans — Acrolinx's profile model is the closest existing thing to the authors' "stackable profiles"). Legal informatics standards: **Akoma Ntoso** (legal document XML model), **LegalRuleML** (OASIS standard for norm/rule representation with defeasibility and temporal validity — more on this under Q3).

**(d) Process/provenance ontologies gating output.** **PROV-O** and BFO are the right substrates and the cite is correct. Closer to the *gating* use: **SEPIO** (Scientific Evidence and Provenance Information Ontology), built precisely to encode "assertion X is admissible because evidence E was produced by method M by agent A" for clinical variant interpretation — this is the process-as-axioms move, done for real, in a regulated domain. 21 CFR Part 11 / **ALCOA+** in pharma is the compliance-law version: the *process* is what's auditable, the content judgment is the human's. The EU AI Act's conformity-assessment machinery is pushing the same direction for AI systems generally. So the move is defensible and precedented — but see Q2 on what it actually buys.

**(e) DSLs projecting to formalisms.** **SBVR** (OMG's Semantics of Business Vocabulary and Business Rules) is the canonical "declare vocabulary + rules in a business-facing notation, project to formal logic" standard — it is exactly the DSL thesis, twenty years old, and its lukewarm adoption is evidence the authors should engage with. **LegalRuleML** projects legal rules to logic with defeasibility. **DMN** for decisions. **OPA/Rego** is the most successful declarative rule language in industry (closed-world, partial evaluation, decidable-ish) — notably *not* OWL-based, and worth asking why. **Formulog** and **Soufflé** show Datalog (closed-world, fixpoint-computable) as a pragmatic middle ground that can also *compute repairs*, which OWL/SHACL cannot. **Fluent Editor**/controlled-natural-language → OWL is the direct "DSL over OWL" precedent.

**(f) Compiler-as-reward / diagnostics as training signal.** **CodeRL** (Le et al., NeurIPS 2022) treats unit-test signals as a critic; **RLEF** (execution feedback RL for code); the whole **RLVR** wave (R1-Zero-style verifiable rewards) is "compiler/test as reward" at scale. **Self-repair** (Olausson et al., "Demystifying GPT Self-Repair") is the crucial skeptical result: self-repair gains come almost entirely from the *quality of external feedback*, not the loop — which supports the thesis's core bet (the oracle is the product) more than its repair mechanics. **TextGrad** (natural-language "gradients") and **Constitutional AI** (principles → critique → revision) are the loop formalized without a formal oracle; this thesis is constitutional AI where the constitution is machine-checkable. **OpenAI's CriticGPT** (trained bug-finders) is the learned-judge counterpart.

## 2. Where the thesis is wrong or fragile

**Tiers 3/4 reduce to provenance: true but hollow.** The reduction converts an epistemic problem into a bookkeeping problem, and bookkeeping is gameable. "Does the link resolve, is the domain approved, is the quote present in the fetched content" certifies that a *ritual* was performed, not that a *judgment* is sound — a quote's presence says nothing about whether the reference teaches the limitation. Agents will learn to manufacture compliant provenance cheaply, because that's what agents do to every oracle. However — and this is the strongest defense the authors have, which they under-state — in law, negligence and malpractice *are* judged process-first (standard of care), and ALCOA+/Part 11 show entire regulated industries run on exactly this reduction. So frame it as **liability engineering, not quality engineering**: the provenance gate allocates responsibility and creates an audit trail; it does not raise the mean quality of the tier-3 judgment itself. Selling it as the latter invites the "process theater" failure the authors already list.

**Single finding shape: works for routing, fails for repair.** LSP itself is the proof: the minimal `Diagnostic` was quickly extended with `codeActions`, `data` payloads, and commands, because consumers need structured fix information, not prose. SARIF grew `fixes`, `fingerprints`, `taxa`, `partialFingerprints`. The authors' shape needs, at minimum: a machine-readable fix payload (a span is not a patch — JSON Patch needs model paths, and span↔path mapping under concurrent edits needs their fingerprint machinery to actually work), a blocking/advisory axis distinct from severity and confidence (a Tier-4 prior with p=0.6 and a Tier-1 antecedent error differ in *what action is admissible*, not in a scalar), and cost/latency metadata so the scheduler can do incremental validation. "Routing never branches on judge kind" will hold; "findings never carry judge-specific payloads" will not.

**Monotone profile composition is false as stated.** "A stricter profile only adds findings" holds only if profiles cannot conflict — but real profile systems always grow overrides (eslint config cascade, CSS, Kustomize). Profile A trusts raw HTML, profile B forbids it; profile A reclassifies a severity. The *interesting* version of the claim is available to them precisely because rules are a serializable AST: **profile compatibility is itself a Tier-1 check over the rule AST** (detect overlapping constraints with contradictory verdicts before composition). That's a genuinely novel, cheap capability — but it replaces the monotonicity assumption rather than vindicating it.

**Span repair vs. regeneration: the claim survives only with a fixpoint discipline.** Antecedent-basis fixes are nonlocal (insert "a member" earlier, re-verify everything downstream); iterated patching can oscillate. The self-repair literature says the win is in feedback quality, not the loop — and a wrong-but-confident oracle (their own acknowledged risk) makes repair *worse* than regeneration, because the agent optimizes the oracle harder. Needed: bounded repair attempts (they have 2), a convergence/oscillation detector, escalation to regeneration, and per-rule precision/recall gates before a rule may emit auto-repairable findings rather than advisory ones.

**Agents will route around noisy oracles, and Goodhart cuts deeper than acknowledged.** An agent minimizing finding-count will write *vacuously compliant* text: minimal claims, hedged spec, no risky vocabulary. Formally valid, substantively empty — the patent equivalent of `return null` passing the type checker. The oracle needs **anti-vacuity obligations**: coverage checks (does the spec actually teach each claim element — note this is written-description support, a Tier-2 check the thesis treats as vocabulary linking but which is really textual entailment, and famously the least predictable doctrine in patent law). Tier 2 is fuzzier than the four-tier story admits; "support in the specification" is NL inference over prose, not ontology linking, and dressing it as deterministic will produce the most gameable layer of the system.

## 3. The DSL question

A DDD-shaped class DSL as the single authoring surface is the right *product* call and the wrong *semantic* call if taken literally. The standard, correct split the thesis gestures at but should make load-bearing: **OWL for vocabulary (open-world, subsumption, reasoning), SHACL for obligations (closed-world validation)** — and the DSL's AST must encode *which semantics each declaration lives under*. Open-world is the deep mismatch: in OWL, absence of spec support for a term is *unknown*, not *violation*; the Tier-2 support check is a closed-world SHACL-shaped constraint over a document graph. If the AST doesn't carry that marker per rule, the OWL projection will be faithful and useless.

What the AST must contain for a faithful OWL 2 projection: class/property declarations with domains/ranges, existential/universal restrictions, cardinality, property paths (for SHACL), disjointness, and — critically — **temporal validity and defeasibility metadata**, even if OWL can't consume them. This is what's lost: legal rules are riddled with exceptions ("unless," "provided that," effective dates, case-law overruling). Classical OWL/SHACL are monotonic; LegalRuleML and Blawx's s(CASP) exist precisely because legal reasoning is defeasible. If the DSL projects only to monotonic formalisms, the exception-handling logic will leak into judge code ad hoc, and the "rules are data" promise quietly dies. Bidirectional projection (OWL → DSL) is a trap — round-tripping open-world axioms is not faithful (the bidirectional-transformation literature, Foster et al.'s lenses, has twenty years of scar tissue on this). Make the DSL the sole source of truth, project *out* only.

Honest recommendation: look hard at **Rego** and **Datalog (Formulog/Soufflé)** as the operational semantics, with OWL/SHACL as *projections for interoperability and documentation*, not as the execution substrate. Datalog gives closed-world validation, fixpoint computation, and — unlike SHACL — the ability to *compute witnesses and minimal repairs* from the rules themselves, which is exactly what the finding-fix payloads need. SBVR's history says the failure mode of DSL-over-formal-logic is authoring friction for domain experts; Alchemy's ergonomics are a good answer, but the authors should plan for a CNL-ish surface for attorneys, not expect them to write Effect classes.

## 4. If I were the agent inside this loop

What makes me productive:

- **Pre-flight, not just post-mortem.** Let me query the oracle before writing: "given this claim skeleton and this vocabulary, what would fail?" A critic I can consult during planning is worth ten critics at the gate. Expose it as a tool (MCP-shaped), not just a gate.
- **Findings as structured data with minimal reproductions.** Give me the failing fragment, the rule, the resolution path I attempted, the rejected candidates (the Tier-2 shape described is excellent — keep it), and *one example of a passing instance*. Contrastive examples beat prose explanations.
- **A budget.** Never dump 200 findings. Rank by blocking-ness, give me the top 5, and tell me the total. I will fix the top 5; I will route around a wall of 200.
- **Idempotent, cacheable validation.** If re-checking after a 2-span edit takes as long as the full check, I will batch my edits and check rarely, and the loop dies. Incremental validation isn't an optimization; it's the adoption requirement the brief names correctly.
- **Honest severity.** If Tier-4 priors render as errors, I'll learn to distrust all severities. Advisory must look advisory, and the system must tolerate me ignoring it without the ledger treating that as failure.

What makes me route around it: slow checks, findings that reference positions I can't see (give me the text, not just offsets — I'm bad at byte arithmetic), repair loops that oscillate (after two failed patches I'll regenerate regardless — make that path first-class instead of fighting it), and any rule whose findings I cannot reproduce from the evidence given. Unexplainable findings get treated as noise within one session.

## 5. The two-week falsification experiment

The assets are sufficient; the discipline needed is kill criteria stated in advance.

**Setup.** Take 30–50 real patent applications with their first office actions (public from Patent Center; no corpus confidentiality issues). Extract every §112(b) formal rejection: antecedent basis, claim dependency/numbering, and — from the spec side — term-support objections. Have the attorney label which rejections a Tier-1/Tier-2 oracle *should* have caught pre-filing (this is the recall denominator).

**Build.** Implement only Tier 1 (antecedent basis, claim DAG, numeral consistency — the ClaimMaster feature set, which is the honest baseline) plus the thinnest Tier-2 slice (term vocabulary extracted from the spec; support = presence, not entailment). Wire it into the chat surface's validate/repair loop with one agent drafting claims for 5 of the inventions.

**Measure, with pre-registered kill criteria:**

1. **Precision of Tier-1 findings vs. attorney judgment** — kill if <70% (a noisy oracle is worse than none, by their own argument).
2. **Repair-loop convergence** — fraction of runs reaching zero blocking findings within 3 rounds; kill if <60%.
3. **The vacuity test (the important one).** Compare agent drafts produced under the oracle vs. without it: claim scope (element counts, independent-claim length), spec coverage of claim elements. If the oracle-driven drafts are *measurably narrower/emptier*, the Goodhart failure is already live at the smallest scale and the thesis needs anti-vacuity obligations before anything else.
4. **Span repair vs. regeneration**: wall-clock and final finding-count for repair-by-patch vs. regenerate-block. This directly tests claim 2's core bet.

This fits in two weeks because every component except the Tier-2 slice exists, and the office actions provide ground truth no synthetic eval can match. The vacuity measurement (3) is the one the authors haven't planned for and the one most likely to kill or transform the thesis.

## 6. One idea not considered

**Bitemporal rules and findings.** Every rule in this system is valid *from* a time *to* a time, along two axes: when the rule was true in the world (case law, USPTO guidance memos, MPEP revisions — patent doctrine shifts constantly; *Alice* alone invalidated a decade of drafting practice) and when the system learned it. A finding emitted in 2027 must record which rule version judged it, or the ledger — the calibration corpus, the eval set, the rule-induction substrate, per claim 7 — **cannot be replayed or audited**. A rule whose precision is measured in 2027 against office actions issued under 2025 doctrine is measuring noise. Worse: rule *induction* from the ledger ("a recurring human finding is a rule waiting to be written") will happily induce already-overruled doctrine from old records. LegalRuleML already models temporal validity and defeasibility for exactly this reason — one more argument for reading it before designing the DSL. Make the rule AST and every finding bitemporal from day one; retrofitting time into a provenance ledger is one of the most painful migrations in data engineering, and this system's entire long-term value proposition (the ledger as calibration corpus) sits on top of it.
