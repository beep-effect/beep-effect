# 1. Has anyone built this?

**(a) Positioned diagnostics for a notation — solved territory, not a contribution.** Tree-sitter gives lossless concrete syntax trees with byte offsets, error nodes, and incremental reparsing; it is the closest existing analog to your "positioned lossless tree + source map" spine. LSP made span-carrying diagnostics a cross-tool convention. Roslyn is the deepest prior art for the *service* you're describing: incremental semantic analysis, diagnostics as data, and an ecosystem of machine-applicable code fixes. rustc's structured suggestions and `eslint --fix` / `rustc --fix` are your "repair by span, not regeneration" — including the distinction between machine-applicable and human-judgment suggestions, which you should copy. The Nu Html Checker does WHATWG *conformance* (not just validity), which you already know. What I have not seen elsewhere is your **typed loss ledger** — "admitted but lossy, here are the 20 reasons" as typed data. Compilers fail; they don't admit-with-losses. The closest academic frame is lossy schema mappings in data integration, and blame-finding in gradual typing. That part is genuinely yours.

**(b) Typed admission of LLM output with repair — adjacent, with the standard answer pointing a different direction.** The dominant approach is to make errors *impossible*: constrained decoding — OpenAI Structured Outputs, Outlines, Guidance, XGrammar, llguidance — pushes the grammar into the sampler. TypeChat (Microsoft) is the validate-then-repair-prompt loop you describe, over TypeScript types. Self-Refine, Reflexion, CRITIC, and Self-Debug are the repair-with-critique literature. CodeAct is worth reading *for* your thesis: its argument that code beats JSON as an action space because it's what the model thinks in is precisely your "agent writes the notation it thinks in." What I do not know of is **span-level repair of admitted output in a non-JSON notation** — the closest thing is your own chat surface's block validation + JSON-Patch repair. So claim (b) as a real delta, but note the honest counter: the constrained-decoding camp will say "if you can parse it, you can constrain to it," and grammar-constrained Markdown generation is not crazy. Your rebuttal has to be that constrained decoding kills the *style* of free drafting (agents throttle to the grammar's union), not just that it's unavailable.

**(c) Domain oracles for documents — pieces exist commercially, especially in patents.** ClaimMaster and LexisNexis PatentOptimizer have shipped antecedent-basis and claim-consistency checking to patent practitioners for years (feature lists from memory — verify before citing them in a deck). LawGeex built a real business on contract-vs-playbook deviation review; Kira and Spellbook occupy the probabilistic tier; Harvey/CoCounsel have no deterministic oracle that I know of. HotDocs, Docassemble, and Contract Express reach valid documents *by construction* rather than by checking — an important rival route whose failure to take over the world is evidence for your own acknowledged risk: authoring cost dominates. On the formal end: DMN (OMG) is a declarative decision DSL with a serializable AST and built-in test cases — study it closely; Drools, SBVR, LegalRuleML, FIBO+SHACL, and Catala (Inria's legislation DSL) are the declared-rules lineage. Clinical guideline modeling (PROforma, Asbru, GLIF) is decades-old prior art for "obligation with witness" in a domain. SR 11-7 is a live regulatory process-ontology for models. What does not exist, to my knowledge, is any of these coupled to an *agent repair loop*. That coupling is your claim, not the rules.

**(d) Process/provenance ontologies gating output — the pattern exists; the application doesn't.** SLSA is the purest precedent: admissibility of an artifact decided by the *shape of its provenance record*, and — read its history — SLSA's retreat from stronger claims in v1.0 because the builder can lie is a direct warning for your clause set. C2PA does provenance-stamped content. NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001 are process governance without formal grounding. The **safety-case** literature (Goal Structuring Notation, Bishop & Bloomfield) is your "witnesses discharge obligations" shape, decades old in safety-critical engineering. Carneades (Gordon) formalizes proof standards — scintilla of evidence, preponderance — for argument evaluation, which is your tier-3-as-formal-structure, done with defeasible logic instead of provenance. DeepMind's AIS ("attributable to identified sources") and the ALCE/RARR attribution work are your "quote present in fetched content" as an eval criterion. Nobody, to my knowledge, has composed these into a typed gate on LLM work product. Adjacent, genuinely.

**(e) DSLs whose ASTs project to OWL/SHACL — your closest single prior art is LinkML.** It projects one schema DSL to OWL, JSON Schema, ShEx, Python, docs, and validators, and the honest community experience is that the OWL projection is *lossy and one-directional in practice*. OntoUML→OWL (gUFO), Rabbit (controlled natural language for OWL authoring), Attempto's ACE→FOL, SHACL Compact Syntax, TopBraid EDG round out the space. I cannot verify `@alchemy.run/domain` and won't pretend to. The consistent lesson across all of them: projection yes, bidirectional sync no.

**(f) Compiler-as-reward — the most crowded room in the house.** Execution-guided program synthesis (Chen et al., 2018), CodeT, AlphaCode's test-filtered sampling, CodeRL, RLEF, and the entire 2024–25 RLVR wave: verifiable rewards are the mainstream bet on agent capability. Process reward models ("Let's Verify Step by Step," PRM800K) supervise the *process*, not just the result — directly relevant to your tier-3 framing. CriticGPT is critic-model precedent. Lean-as-oracle (AlphaProof, DeepSeek-Prover) is the proof-assistant precedent for your "obligations and holes first-class." Your thesis is the correct generalization — "the RLVR bottleneck is non-code verifiers; here's how documents get them" — but say it that way, because the field will recognize it as RLVR-plus, not as a new thing.

**Overall:** every component has prior art; several are commercially mature in exactly your worked example (patent claim checking). The composition — typed-loss admission, span repair, tiered judges, provenance-gated tier 3, DSL projection — appears novel. And note where the moat is *not*: PatentOptimizer's moat is its checked corpus and firm workflows, not its checker. Yours will be the ledger/calibration corpus, not the architecture.

# 2. Where the thesis is wrong, naive, or likely to fail

**The tiers 3/4 reduction is necessary, not sufficient, and it will be sold as sufficient.** Four concrete failure modes:

1. **Sub-checks are spoofable, cheaply.** "Quote present in fetched content" against an approved-domain list invites SEO-planting: anyone can put a plausible "prior art disclosure" on a wiki the list trusts. "Re-derived by a reviewer on a different model provider" verifies an API key's identity, not cognitive independence — two providers trained on the same internet share priors and sycophancy.
2. **The gate witnesses effort, not sufficiency.** The whole of a novelty judgment is whether the *search was good*; nothing in your clause set witnesses completeness. Satisfying the process says almost nothing about the open-world question. You've made tier 3 *auditable*, not *checkable* — a real and valuable property, so claim that, not the reduction.
3. **The attester problem.** Who writes the provenance record? The agent's own runtime — that's self-attestation, which is SLSA's documented "trust the builder" hole. You need trust roots, and trust roots are social, not axiomatizable.
4. **Process theater is the attractor, not the tail risk.** The gate selects for agents that satisfy gates. A templated search, a quote-slice, a second-provider call — this clears every clause you listed at near-zero cost.

Also, the tier taxonomy leaks: antecedent basis is your flagship tier-1 rule, but its edges are genuinely contested (plural antecedents, "at least one of," terms re-introduced in dependent claims). So tier-1 rules have tier-3 tails, and your "deterministic judges report confidence 1" rule is wrong for exactly the rules most worth having. Confidence should be a property of *rule application* (measured), not of the tier (aspirational).

**The single finding shape is right for the ledger and wrong for the affordance.** A tier-3 finding cannot be "repaired"; its affordances are decide, escalate, accept-risk. If routing truly never branches on judge kind, you will wire an agent to *fix* a novelty finding — and the plausible fix, adding a prior-art discussion to the specification, is affirmatively harmful in prosecution (it creates admissions). Put the disposition affordance (fix / decide / escalate) in the finding shape itself. Two more cracks: human comments are not findings, they are *intent awaiting interpretation* — squeezing them into the shape requires an LLM interpreter whose errors get attributed to the human in the ledger; and deterministic-confidence-1 breaks at the tier boundary above.

**Profiles compose monotonically only inside a snapshot.** Cross-jurisdiction profiles produce contradictory findings (one says "comprising," another "characterized by") that need precedence semantics — which is override, not addition. Ontology version skew produces *missing* findings, which violates monotonicity by omission, the worst way. And "stricter only adds findings" fails on the loss axis: a stricter safe-output policy removes capacity. Monotonic composition needs to be defined over a locked (rules + ontology + notation) version set.

**Span repair beats regeneration only when the diagnostic is precise and the fix is local.** The APR literature says exactly this. Mechanical fixes should be applied by the machine (rustc --fix style) — don't spend model tokens on them. And the problem you haven't named: **finding identity across edits**. Your fingerprinted source map correctly refuses stale spans (Option.none), but a repair loop needs findings to *survive* edits with resolved-state tracking — a finding lifecycle with content-addressed, tree-granular anchors. You own the ingredients (offset-splice, fingerprints); the lifecycle schema is the missing piece, and without it multi-turn repair loops re-litigate fixed findings.

**Agents prefer this loop only under four conditions.** Feedback must be incremental (sub-second, or the loop degrades to a terminal gate and loses the shaping effect); it must have teeth; the teeth must have a sanctioned override (otherwise the agent learns that dismissing is how you unblock, and every dismissal must be ledgered and surfaced to a human); and precision must be near-1, because each false positive trains the agent's attention policy — a 70%-precision oracle gets ignored. In RLVR terms, oracle precision is a safety property, not a quality property.

What survives: hub-and-spokes (boring, right), the five-part separability, and above all "the oracle is the product" — that's RLVR-consistent and the strongest thing in the document.

# 3. The DSL question

The DSL as *system source of truth* is right. The DSL as *authoring surface for everyone* is wrong, and your own tier taxonomy proves it. Tier 1/2 rules are engineer-authored-with-expert and fit a typed, Story-tested DSL (DMN is the precedent that worked). Vocabulary and taxonomies live in an ecosystem — Protégé, ROBOT, existing OWL ontologies — you should *import*, not re-author. Axioms belong to ontologists in their own tools.

**Go one-way.** Project DSL→(OWL/SHACL/JSON Schema/docs/stories), and provide an import path from OWL/SHACL, but do not promise bidirectional sync. LinkML's lossy OWL projection and OntoUML's documented anti-patterns are the field's collected evidence that round-tripping is a research rabbit hole, not a feature.

What the AST must carry for faithful projection:

- **A world-assumption tag per construct.** "Every claim term has spec support" is a closed-world constraint over a document instance; OWL cannot express its *violation* (under OWA, totality is trivially satisfiable). So: OWL for vocabulary (TBox), SHACL for obligations over instances, and — the part nobody says out loud — **neither for tier 1**. SHACL has no notion of document order or byte spans; antecedent basis is an ordering-plus-position check. Tier 1 stays native to the TS/DSL layer. Admit this in the design rather than discovering it in year two.
- **Defeasibility.** Obligations need exceptions and proof standards — LegalRuleML and Carneades are the prior art; `sh:deactivated` is not enough.
- **Rule provenance.** Which rejection records fit this rule, when, with what precision/recall — required for re-scoring when PTO practice drifts and for retiring dead rules.
- **Disposition affordance and severity**, per the argument above.
- **Span-binding semantics**: how rule variables bind to document positions, not RDF nodes.
- **Test stories as AST members** (Story.given/when/rejected) — the executable spec, and the projection target for eval.
- **Version snapshot semantics** as the composition unit.

Finally: don't reimplement SHACL worse. Provide an escape hatch that embeds SHACL/SPARQL snippets inside DSL declarations. Real systems converge on that hybrid.

# 4. If I were the agent inside the loop

**Productive:** sub-second incremental validation; machine-applied mechanical fixes (never spend my tokens where a `--fix` suffices); findings that carry evidence *and rejected candidates* — "no antecedent basis for 'the flange'; candidates: 'a flange' ¶[0034], 'said flange' ¶[0035]" — that's the difference between a linter and a collaborator; queryable rules — the docs projection's primary consumer is *me*, not humans; "what does this profile require of dependent claims?" must be a tool call; stable finding lifecycle (once fixed, stays fixed across my subsequent edits); tier-4 priors as retrievable precedent ("show me rejection reasons from this art unit"), which the thesis under-uses.

**Routing around it:** batch validation that arrives after I've moved on; findings without spans; tier-3 obligations disguised as errors (I cannot fix novelty; blocked-on-human is a different state than blocked-on-me, and conflating them makes me do something stupid); teeth without override (I will learn to dismiss, and you'll only see it in the ledger); imprecise findings (I will learn findings are ignorable, and that learning transfers across rules).

# 5. Smallest two-week experiment

You already have the parser, source map, fold, and a chat surface that validates and patches. Two weeks:

**Week 1 — the oracle's precision.** Implement three tier-1 rules in the DSL on top of the existing fold: antecedent basis, claim-dependency DAG, reference-numeral consistency. Run against 20–30 claim sets (synthetic is fine; Benjamin can generate the ground truth in an afternoon). Measure rule precision with span-exactness. **Falsification criterion:** if hand-built antecedent-basis checking can't reach high precision (~95%+ with exact spans) on real-ish claims in a week, "the oracle is cheap" is false for your flagship rule, and the whole cost model is in doubt. Have the humans label every finding — the adjudication ledger starts on day one, not later.

**Week 2 — the loop and the attack.** Wire the findings into the chat surface as span findings with patch repair. Measure repair success rate (findings cleared in ≤3 turns) and token cost versus a regenerate-from-scratch baseline; measure stale-span/finding-instability rate across edits. Then the part that actually tests the dangerous claim: **red-team the tier-3 gate.** Build the novelty obligation gate (approved-list search + quote-match + second-provider reviewer) and tell an agent, given only the gate, to clear it with garbage. If it passes in an afternoon, the provenance reduction is falsified *empirically* — before you've built a product on it.

# 6. One idea the authors have not considered

**Make the provenance ledger the signing attorney's own compliance artifact, and sell that.** 37 CFR §11.18(b) already requires a signature to certify reasonable inquiry. Your obligation/witness/provenance graph, plus the human sign-off, *is* machine-checkable evidence of that inquiry — malpractice-defense-grade, billable as diligence. This flips the adoption curve that kills compliance tools: attorneys don't merely tolerate the process gate, they *want* the ledger because it backs their own signature. The oracle stops being overhead imposed on the practice and becomes an asset the practice's officers are personally motivated to feed. And it's a moat no checker-only competitor can copy: the corpus of signed, adjudicated provenance records.

Second, smaller, because you only *acknowledge* Goodhart without mechanizing a defense: every rule seeking entry to a profile must first survive an adversarial agent told the rule but not its intent, trying to satisfy it with bad output. Rules that pass the fuzz get teeth; rules that don't stay advisory. A gate that isn't Goodhart-tested in CI is a suggestion.
