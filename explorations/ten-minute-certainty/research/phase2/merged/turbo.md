# turbo: merged levers and skeptic verdicts

### Verdict (both seats reconciled)

Both seats agree that Turbo is not on the latency critical path in its own right. Dry-run planning takes 0.1-0.6 s, #3014 already removed most of the per-task glob CPU, and no Turbo lever on its own brings push-to-every-required-green p95 under 10 minutes. Turbo matters through the scope that the CoverageScope and LintPolicyScope classifiers hand to hosted Test Unit, Coverage and Lint Policy. Opus replayed those classifiers over 727 merged PRs. In that replay 50% of PRs fall back to full coverage and 41% escalate Lint Policy. For 135 PRs the only full-coverage trigger is an unowned generated standards file, and every reader of those files sits in repo-cli, repo-cli-kit or repo-configs. That makes owner-mapping them (TURB-01) the largest and lowest-risk compute lever in the area. CiLane.ts:2276-2310 appends coverage owners to the Test Unit selection, so the saving is real even though hosted coverage runs with --force. Astra's top lever, T6, mixed this mapping with a semantic classifier for turbo.json edits. I split it: the shared standards-owner part is TURB-01, and the turbo.json classifier is TURB-03. The replay shows turbo.json is the only trigger in just 4 of 727 Lint Policy escalations, so TURB-03 ranks low. Opus's #2 lever (Lint Policy seal narrowing, TURB-02) is still a guess until affected and full Lint Policy walls are measured. Opus's #3 lever (local TURBO_FORCE on any bun.lock change, TURB-05) is local-only. I checked that hosted workflows never set TURBO_FORCE, but the standards file calls the force a deliberate false-green control. It therefore lands only behind Astra's install-state parity ledger. Astra's #2 and #3 levers (T3 tool-dependency isolation, T2 consumer-specific root inputs) are kept as TURB-06 and TURB-07. Biome is confirmed as a root devDependency, so it belongs in TURB-06's scope. Opus's churn census shows T2's yield is small, because lock-free root tsconfig edits also change vitest.aliases.generated.json. Both seats confirm the @beep/glob setup-file gap (TURB-04). turbo.json's test and test:property tasks neither depend on glob#transit nor list glob sources as direct inputs. The gap is a real stale-green source, and closing it costs work. Hub narrowing (TURB-11) stays deferred behind TURB-04. Remote hits near zero on the workstation are explained by local-first reuse and portable hashes (195/198 match hosted), so TURB-10 only moves measurement. The residue hash split in repo-cli (TURB-09) is pure waste removal plus a tracked-inputs invariant. Together with #3005 and #3007, TURB-01 and TURB-02 stop forced full fan-out on roughly 20-40% of PR runs. That is the area's real contribution to runner-minutes, and it gives a small, secondary critical-path gain.
