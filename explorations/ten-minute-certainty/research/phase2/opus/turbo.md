# Phase 2 audit, Opus 5.5 seat: D. Turbo (global key, root deps, hub fan-out, @beep/glob setup gap, escalation to full, TURBO_FORCE on lockfile, new-workspace defaults, remote hits)

Produced 2026-11-09 by a `claude-opus-5-5` (medium) auditor from the shared brief and the phase 1 inventory; read-only. Paths are repository-relative or `~`.

## Verdict

Turbo is not on the latency critical path in its own right. Hashing and planning are cheap: dry-runs take 0.1-0.6 s, and #3014 removes most of the per-task glob CPU. Turbo matters through the scope it hands to hosted Test Unit, Coverage and Lint Policy. Selection widens to the full repo far more often than real global changes would justify. Replaying the classifiers over the 727 merged PRs since 10-25 gives these numbers. 50% of PRs would hit CoverageScope full fallback; for 135 of them (19%), the only reason is an unowned generated standards file, and every reader of those files is in repo-cli, repo-cli-kit or repo-configs. 41% of PRs escalate Lint Policy to the full sweep. Policy-implementation (seal) paths account for 142 of the sole-trigger cases; turbo.json accounts for only 4. Full fallback runs all 28 Test Unit partitions, so the biggest compute lever in this area is owner-mapping those standards paths. It also takes Test Unit off the required path's worst case once #3005 lands. The global-key levers the inventory rated high turn out smaller than framed. Of 31 global-input commits, 20 also touch bun.lock and 23 change vitest.aliases.generated.json, which build/check/test key on anyway. Only 18 of 71 lockfile commits change the root dependency resolution, so T2 alone saves little and T3 should be limited to tool-only root dependencies. T9 (local TURBO_FORCE on any bun.lock change) is stricter than the hosted authority, which already trusts Turbo's per-package lockfile hashing on PRs. It costs local wall on about 10% of PRs. T5 is a real soundness gap: 128 of 200 tests do not key on @beep/glob. Closing it adds work (about 2 glob edits a week, each replaying 128 tests), and that must be accepted. T4 (hub narrowing) is high-risk and low-yield: hub src edits are 5.5% of PRs and mostly additive. Remote hits are near 0 on the workstation for structural reasons, so there is no remote lever there. The one waste found is a residue hash split on repo-cli in the primary clone. Turbo alone cannot get this area under 10 minutes. With the coverage and lint-policy scope fixes plus #3005 and #3007, the Turbo-driven share stops forcing full fan-out on about 20-40% of PRs.
