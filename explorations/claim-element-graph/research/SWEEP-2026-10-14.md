## 1. TL;DR

- **The anchor tells us what we lack more than what to build.** IPRally parses each patent into a per-document graph of features plus relations: a part-of / example-of tree and a functional-relation DAG. In beep-effect, `PatentClaim.body` is one opaque `S.NonEmptyString`, so we have no layer for individual claim elements. We are already paying for that gap. `goals/patent-drafting-episode-ledger` (paused) specifies a `ClaimLimitationSupportSet` gate over *ordered claim limitations*, but nothing produces those limitations. `Distinction.missing_limitation` is free text. `KgNodeKind` has no element kind. `explorations/research-corpus-synthesis/research/ROUTING.md` R2-062 is gated on "one actual OA claim-element/reference/raw-span consumer".
- **Take the anchor's schema idea and its released 10k-graph sample; leave its metric and its model.** The released JSON drops edge type, and its IoU ignores type and matches exact strings. Its "gold" is the output of IPRally's rule parser (the teacher), not human annotation. The retrieval gains are +0.2 and +0.37 points of Recall@3. Its cost argument (don't run LLMs over 150M docs) does not apply to a solo practice.
- **The most decision-relevant paper is ACE** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04295). On 204 real §112(b) rejections, 55% of the misses were cross-claim or nested antecedent-basis chains. A deterministic checker over a parsed claim-dependency and element graph should catch exactly that class. PEDANTIC (https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21342) independently finds antecedent basis is about 36% of 112(b) reasons, and FLAN-Graph (https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14372) and PHAGE (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10073) give ready deterministic rules: `a/an` introduces a term, `the/said` refers back to it.
- **Claim charts have a measured operational form.** FiNE-Patents (SIGIR'26, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02392) scores three sub-tasks: per-feature and per-claim passage retrieval, novel-feature identification, and claim-level novelty. Its hierarchical workflow segments features first. Decomposition beats scale for finding novel features: a hierarchical 30B model scores 53.7 F1 against 44.7 for a single-step 397B model. A claim-only BERT scores 75.2 overall but 0.0 on the adversarial set. PANORAMA (NeurIPS'25 D&B, https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24774) is a US per-claim OA parse schema that experts validated at 92.5%. It is CC BY-NC, so internal eval use only.
- **Citation grounding has to check that the citation supports the proposition, not just that it exists.** The three commercial legal RAG tools tested (Lexis+ AI, Westlaw AI-Assisted Research, Ask Practical Law AI) hallucinated 17–33% of the time. A response counts as a hallucination if it is incorrect *or misgrounded* (https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20362). LLM verifiers catch only 37–61% of wrong-pinpoint citations in court opinions (52–83% in briefs), and they often invent support the cited page lacks (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12571). Our verified-span substrate already enforces exact slices. The next layer is span-level support plus a typed taxonomy of hallucination types (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21155, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17546).
- **Neuro-symbolic doctrine, sharpened:** an LLM will report solver verdicts it never computed, a failure the authors call "scope laundering" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16118). Any "verified" status therefore needs an execution receipt. SAT-enumerated disagreement between independent formalizations is a reference-free check that suits attorney review (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25186).
- **Temporal validity is a storage problem, not a prompting problem.** Version-indexed retrieval lifted French tax QA from about 3% to about 98% (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09393). On Chinese civil law, 69–89% of frontier models' errors are old-to-new faults: the model applied the newer law. Their accuracy on old-version cases is only 1–24%, even though they can recite the older text (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14610). For us that means AIA vs pre-AIA, MPEP editions, and eCFR point-in-time.
- **Recommendation:** open one exploration, `claim-element-graph`, to design a schema-first claim-element / invention-graph layer. Its first consumers would be deterministic §112(b) checks, the drafting-ledger support set, and OA element mapping. Detailed in §7.

## 2. The anchor paper

**"From Rules to Neural Graphs: Scalable Structured Prediction for Patent Prior Art Search"**, Zenovkin & Björkqvist (IPRally), ECML PKDD 2026 ADS, LNAI 16949. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01553 · VoR https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-37682-4_31 · repo https://github.com/iprally/invention_graph_eval (MIT)

### What it shows
- A biaffine "neural parser" (XLM-RoBERTa-Longformer, local sliding-window biaffine attention that shares weights with global scoring) is distilled from IPRally's spaCy+regex rule parser. The rule parser has been in production five years and has processed 150M patents. Distillation used 1M documents. The parser trains on 600-word documents and runs on 40k+ word documents.
- Its output is per-word "invention graphs": a feature tree (part-of / example-of) plus a functional-relation DAG.
- Agreement with the teacher is 0.653 IoU, and IoU@90 is only 0.142.
- In the downstream Graph Transformer, Recall@3 on 90,082 examiner X/Y queries moves from 0.4163 to 0.4183 on claims (p=0.04) and from 0.3336 to 0.3373 on descriptions (p<0.001). Dense baselines: PaECTER 0.280, Stella 0.273, GTE-ModernBERT 0.200.
- Lineage: SIGIR 2023 Tree-LSTM (https://doi.org/10.1145/3539618.3591842, abstract only), then the PatentSemTech'25 Graph Transformer (https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10496, about 31.7M X/Y/A citations, Recall@3 0.4046 against 0.3151 for a retrained Tree-LSTM and 0.1866 for BM25).

### How strong is the evidence?
| Claim | Strength | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Graphs + GT beat untuned dense encoders on X-citation recall | Moderate | Large n, but the dense baselines were not fine-tuned (except PaECTER) and there is no cross-encoder. Internal, proprietary eval. |
| Neural parser ≥ rule parser downstream | Weak | +0.0020 and +0.0037 absolute. No CIs or seed variance. The GT was apparently trained on rule graphs. The chemical subset is not significant (p=0.42 and 0.14). |
| 0.653 IoU means a good parse | Not established | It only measures agreement with the teacher. The metric is type-blind (a hyponym/meronym swap counts as correct), keys on exact strings (feature values lowercased, relation strings not, references to features inside relations uppercased; teacher output possibly lemmatized), and gives no partial credit. |
| Relevance to US prosecution | Assumed | Labels are EPO-style X/Y. No US-only recall is reported. The training data is 60% US plus 20% machine-translated CN. |

### What we can reuse
1. **The schema idea, adapted.** A feature hierarchy plus n-ary relation nodes whose `parts` mix feature references and connecting text. Our version would keep edge kind (`normal | meronym | hyponym | method`), keep char-exact spans, and record which producer made each node (rule, LLM, or human) so that it enters the epistemic layer as a `CandidateClaim`.
2. **The released data**: `evaluation.tar.gz` and `train_sample.tar.gz`, 5k documents each, MIT. Uses: few-shot exemplars, a regression set, and an interop target, via an adapter that converts both ways between our schema and their `Node{id,type,value,items}` JSON. Treat the data as teacher-labelled, not gold. Open questions from the deep-dive remain unresolved: whether the inputs are claims-only or full text, and whether they are lemmatized before or after.
3. **The distillation strategy, as a later option.** Rule teacher → LLM labels → small parser only becomes relevant if we ever need volume. At our volume (one matter's claims plus a few references), direct LLM extraction into our schema is affordable.

### What we should not take
- **Their IoU as the primary metric.** MarkushGlyph (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28532) is direct evidence against lenient per-feature scoring: of 566 cases where the two scorers disagreed (out of 3,821 prediction pairs), 541 were outputs the lenient scorer accepted and the strict single-bijection scorer rejected. Rankings stayed the same, but absolute scores fell. Score our graphs with strict structural equality under one consistent mapping. Report their IoU only for comparison with them.
- **Single-parent features.** A component that belongs to two assemblies can't be expressed.
- **Untyped relations and no claim-scope semantics**: transition phrases, negative limitations, numeric ranges, Markush groups. The anchor's chemical showcase loses `<` signs from ranges.
- **Training a Graph Transformer.** We don't have 31.7M citations. Retrieval stays a consumer or vendor concern.

## 3. Thematic map

### A. Claim and invention structure as a typed graph
**State of the art.** Deterministic claim parsers (punctuation hierarchy, transition words, determiner-based antecedent tracking) are old and they work. Every system that cares about structure rebuilds them. LLM decomposition is now cheap, but nobody has measured how faithful it is on US claims. Formal "certificates" over claim DAGs exist, but they certify only arithmetic.

**State of the art.** The field has moved from claim-level novelty labels, which are prone to shortcuts, to feature-to-passage mapping with adversarial controls. For US data there are now validated parse schemas for CTNF rejections (PANORAMA), §112(b) reasons (PEDANTIC), and four-tuples of pre-claim, rejection, art and post-claim (Grounded Revision). Novelty and obviousness *verdicts* remain weak: the best NOC4PC score is 45.4 against 32.3 for random.

### E. Neuro-symbolic rule reasoning, formalization and argument structure
**State of the art.** The reliable pattern is: an LLM translates facts into a fixed predicate vocabulary, and a human-authored executable rule base computes the outcome. That pattern is robust to contamination and to perturbing the rules. LLM *autoformalization* of the rules themselves is still the bottleneck: Edge-F1 is at most 0.24 on defeasible structure, and expert edge agreement is near chance even among humans. Argument mining over legal text has reliable role labels but unreliable edges.

### F. Ontologies, knowledge graphs and legal positions
**State of the art.** Patent ontologies have existed since 2007 (PATExpert) but none is released or US-prosecution-shaped. The productive recent work is about *fidelity*. Converting legal text into an ontology loses 8–27 points of task accuracy, mostly on modal and qualifier language. The best induction pipelines keep a provenance chain from each term back to source passages and use the LLM only for closed-vocabulary decisions.

### G. Drafting, evaluation and judge reliability (including interpretation)

Structured intermediates (concept graphs, evidence-bearing logic trees) beat outlines, but on metrics the authors built themselves. *Free-text* explicit plans hurt claim drafting. Domain-trained comparative judges beat n-gram, embedding and generic LLM metrics. LLM judges get gamed under optimization pressure. LLM "ordinary meaning" judgments are unstable across phrasings.

## 6. Contradictions, weak evidence, transfer risks

**Apparent contradictions, resolved:**
- *Is decomposition good or bad?* FiNE: hierarchical decomposition lifts novel-feature F1 by about 15 points but not claim-level accuracy. Verbalization-bottleneck: explicit CoT hurts claim drafting. CCX (https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3775/paper14.pdf): element weighting did not beat whole-claim embedding. **Reading:** decomposition helps when its output is anchored structured data that the next step consumes alongside the source. It hurts when it is lossy prose that replaces the source. CCX's model was never trained at element level. This is an argument for the claim-element layer, not against it.
- *Does retrieval help?* PAP2PAT and AutoPatent: retrieval per outline node helps spec drafting. Grounded Revision: random, structural and dense exemplar retrieval did not change amendment grounding. These are different tasks. Do not expect exemplar retrieval to improve OA responses.
- *Does debate or tool access help?* Gated debate helps; full debate is net-neutral (2606.16047). Debate cuts fabrications but not content errors (2606.18021). Tools help frontier models and hurt open ones (2606.05009). **Rule:** route only uncertain items to adversarial review, and measure per model.
- *Sparse vs dense.* Sparse Coverage wins R@100 while dense PaECTER wins mAP and PRES, on 48 queries. Neither result is general.
- *Novelty classifiers.* Claim-only baselines match or beat claim+art (Ikoma 2502.06316; FiNE's BERT scores 75.2% normally and 0.0 adversarially; most of PANORAMA's 12 LLMs score at or barely above random on NOC4PC). Any novelty eval without a hypothesis-only control is uninterpretable.

**Weak or self-referential evidence. Do not cite these as proof:**
- FlowPlan-G2P: self-built dataset and judge, near-ceiling scores, no IAA.
- Adaptive Multi-Stage Claim Generation: unsupervised attention-head "relations", unrelated self-citations.
- Koomullil: one synthetic claim, single author; proves arithmetic only.
- QuantumNovelty: zero overlap with examiner citations before repairs; repairs confounded.
- Falkor-IRAC: one verifiable query.
- IdeaForge and Helal: toy or manual.
- ToC: the metric is scored by the same LLM examiner.
- HalluGraph: synthetic entity swaps only.
- Code-as-Auditor's "verification" is an LLM rubric.
- The neuro-symbolic compliance paper (2601.06181) uses circular ground truth.
- The anchor's own uplift is tiny, with no CIs.
- Self-Knowledge RAG reverses on 7B.
- The PatentScore evaluator was weighted on its evaluation data.

**Transfer risks specific to US prosecution:**
1. **EPO vs USPTO labels.** X/Y/A categories and ESOP feature mappings (anchor, FiNE, PaECTER, CCX, PatentMatch) have no native US equivalent. US OAs cite column:line or paragraph ranges in prose. Only PANORAMA, PEDANTIC, Grounded Revision, ACE, ClaimCompare, the OA Research Dataset and PatentAP are US-native.
2. **Domain slices.** PEDANTIC is G06F40 only, Ikoma is G06F only, ToC is wireless 2016, Grounded Revision is drawn from PTAB-appeal cases. Category distributions will differ in mechanical and chemical work.
3. **Licenses.** PANORAMA is CC BY-NC, PatClaimEval data is CC-BY-NC, Patent-CR is CC-BY-SA. Many repos state no license (FiNE, PEDANTIC, FLAN, PatentScore, ToC, Sparse Coverage, NormBench). The anchor repo is MIT, and fiscal-fr-bench is CC-BY data plus MIT code.
4. **BRI vs Phillips.** Claim-construction and interpretation papers (Anderson & Menell, Hoopes et al.) are litigation-oriented. Prosecution uses BRI.
5. **Model staleness.** Many baselines are 2023–2024 models (Large Legal Fictions, PatentEval, Ikoma). The failure *modes* transfer; the rates do not.
6. **Contamination.** Public legal benchmarks are measurably contaminated (2605.16052). USPTO-derived public sets are plausibly contaminated too, though this has not been measured.
7. **Examiner-citation bias.** Labels move with CPC infrastructure (2609.37343) and examiner recency (Qiao et al.), so recall against them understates true recall.

## 7. Proposed exploration packet

**Slug:** `claim-element-graph`

**Problem.** beep-effect models claims at the level of claim number and dependency, but the body is opaque text. Three shipped or paused packets assume a lower layer that does not exist:
- the drafting ledger's ordered `ClaimLimitationSupportSet`
- `Distinction.missing_limitation` (free text)
- OA element mapping (R2-062's gate)

The literature converges on a typed, span-anchored element graph that is produced deterministically first, extended by an LLM second, and scored strictly.

**Appetite.** Exploration-sized: capture → research → align → shape. Graduation is bounded to one schema-first vertical slice plus a deterministic validator. No model training and no retrieval engine work.

**Research questions:**
1. **Schema.** Should `ClaimElement` be a sibling value (`ClaimElementTree` keyed by `claimNumber`) or a field inside `PatentClaim`? How do we represent multi-parent components, functional relations as n-ary `parts: (ref | text)[]`, transition scope, numeric ranges, and Markush groups?
2. **Deterministic floor.** How much of the tree and the antecedent edges do the FLAN, PHAGE and FSM rules plus `<claim-text>` XML nesting recover on real US claims? Measure against a hand-labelled set. No paper reports construction accuracy.
3. **Antecedent basis.** Can a deterministic checker over the dependency DAG and `r_term` edges catch the 55% multi-hop class ACE reports? What is its false-positive rate on allowed claims? Neither ACE nor PEDANTIC measured that.
4. **LLM extension.** Where does LLM decomposition beat the rules, measured by strict structural equality (MarkushGlyph-style) rather than IPRally IoU? Should we run "By Their Fruits"-style SAT disagreement across two independent extractions as an admission gate?
5. **Interop.** Is a lossless adapter to and from IPRally JSON feasible, given that theirs drops edge type and offsets? Is their 5k eval set claims-only or full text, and lemmatized?
6. **Consumers.** In what order: drafting-ledger support set, 112(b) self-check, OA element-mapping record (FiNE/ToC shape), `missing_limitation` → element reference, KG node kinds?
7. **Semantic loss.** Do element nodes preserve qualifiers? Run a paired raw-text vs element-projection QA delta in the style of 2608.12326.
