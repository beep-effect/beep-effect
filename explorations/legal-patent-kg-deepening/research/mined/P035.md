# Entity Alignment Method of Science and Technology Patent Based on Graph Convolution Network and Information Fusion

## Problem & positioning

Patent knowledge graphs built by different institutions are heterogeneous and redundant, with equivalent entities expressed differently across sources and languages.
Existing aligners often learn graph structure or attribute-text semantics separately and underuse the patent data's multiple views.
MIFEA targets supervised cross-graph entity alignment by fusing topology, relation, attribute, label, and description information.
The paper positions entity alignment as a prerequisite for integrating independently constructed science-and-technology patent KGs.
Its central claim is that GCN structure representations and multilingual BERT descriptions are complementary.

## Approach / architecture

MIFEA has four modules: graph-structure embedding, attribute-semantic representation, information fusion, and entity alignment.
Two two-layer GCNs process source and target KGs, sharing weight matrices so their entity embeddings occupy a comparable space.
Multi-hop topology is aggregated through normalized adjacency; current-node relations and attributes pass through fully connected/highway-gate layers.
The hybrid structural representation concatenates topology, relation, and attribute embeddings and normalizes the result.
Multilingual BERT encodes each entity's name and text description; an MLP transforms the CLS embedding.
Training examples contain a query entity, a known aligned positive, and a randomly selected negative, optimized with a margin loss.
Graph and BERT representations are combined by a learned/configured weight `tau` into the final entity embedding.
Cosine similarity ranks candidate cross-graph pairs.
A GCN-produced top-Q candidate pool avoids scoring the entire target entity set, after which MIFEA reranks candidates.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

The paper formalizes each KG as `G = (E, R, T)`: entities, relations, and triples.
It treats topology, relation types, entity attributes, labels, and descriptions as distinct evidence views for identity.
The alignment output is an equivalence mapping between source-graph and target-graph entities, not an ontology merge.
Shared GCN parameters serve as the structural bridge between graph spaces; multilingual BERT serves as the lexical/semantic bridge.
The model's useful KG pattern is evidence-fused identity: structural neighborhood agreement plus attribute/description similarity.
Relations and attributes are inputs to alignment, rather than being discarded after generic node embedding.
Pre-aligned entity pairs are explicit supervision and therefore part of the alignment provenance.
No patent-specific class/property ontology, logical axioms, temporal identity rules, or distinction among patent/publication/family/invention identity is specified.
The paper does not define how predicted equivalence edges should be represented, qualified, reviewed, or retracted in the destination KG.

## Evaluation & results

Experiments use DBP15K, DBP100K, and a Chinese/English science-and-technology patent dataset reported as 160,000 entries.
The main split uses 30% of aligned data for training and 70% for evaluation.
Hits@K is the proportion of correct matches within the top K candidates.
On the patent dataset, MIFEA reaches Hits@1 80.74 and Hits@10 92.59.
The strongest listed baseline, AttGNN, reaches 78.09 and 90.82 on that dataset.
On DBP15K, MIFEA reports Hits@1 82.94 and Hits@10 94.12, versus AttGNN's 79.60 and 92.93.
On ZH–EN, MIFEA reports 79.90/91.34, compared with AttGNN's 77.65/89.37.
On FR–EN, MIFEA reports 89.73/94.18; the prose calls AttGNN's 93.45 Hits@10 higher, contradicting the table.
The ablation structural encoder scores 56.2/85.1 on ZH–EN; removing relations gives 50.2/78.4, attributes 49.2/81.0, and highway gates 46.8/76.1.
MIFEA's runtime is reported as 6.4% more efficient than JAPE, though it remains comparatively long.
Training-proportion tests span 10%–50%; performance improves with more pre-aligned entities.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

The authors state that MIFEA has long runtime and that BERT universality and fine-tuning time need improvement.
They report weaker relative behavior on JA–EN because the pretrained model does not fully capture Japanese/Chinese semantic differences.
The conclusion acknowledges that increasing data-source scale and complexity require better efficiency as well as accuracy.
The patent dataset's origin, schema, annotation process, availability, and exact meaning of “entries” are not described sufficiently for reproduction.
Results are dominated by generic DBpedia cross-lingual benchmarks; patent-specific identity errors are not qualitatively analyzed.
Random negatives may be easier than hard candidates encountered in real patent-family/entity resolution.
No precision/recall calibration, abstention threshold, confidence reliability, or human-review workload is reported.
The paper's FR–EN prose conflicts with Table 2, and some prose percentage comparisons are ambiguous.
Equivalence is treated as binary despite identity distinctions and temporal changes common in patent data.
