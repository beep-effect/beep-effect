# Large Language Models for Patent Classification: Strengths, Trade-offs, and the Long Tail Effect

## Problem & positioning

- CPC assignment is a hierarchical, multi-label classification problem with a severely imbalanced label distribution.
- Aggregate micro scores emphasize common subclasses, so a classifier can look strong while suppressing rare, emerging, or cross-domain technologies.
- The paper compares efficient supervised encoders with open-weight instruction-tuned LLMs rather than assuming that generative models replace established classifiers.
- It asks how model family, prompting, retrieval, and adaptation change accuracy across the label-frequency distribution, not merely overall accuracy.
- It also treats inference time, energy, and carbon emissions as deployment criteria for patent offices and large-scale patentometrics.
- The core position is complementary: encoders serve high-throughput head-label classification, while selective LLM use may recover long-tail subclasses.

## Approach / architecture

- The main benchmark is USPTO-70k: 70,250 documents split chronologically into 50,250 training patents from 2006–2017, 10,000 validation patents from 2018, and 10,000 test patents from 2019.
- Every model receives normalized title-plus-abstract text; labels are normalized to four-character CPC subclasses and represented as multi-label outputs.
- Encoder baselines are BERT, SciBERT, and PatentSBERTa, fine-tuned with binary cross-entropy and independent sigmoid outputs.
- Encoder predictions use calibrated thresholds and are capped at seven labels; a comparison variant restricts decoding to 20 retrieved candidate subclasses.
- LLMs are Phi-3 Mini, Mistral-7B-Instruct, LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, and Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct.
- Prompt regimes include zero-shot, few-shot, zero-shot plus retrieval, and few-shot plus retrieval.
- Retrieval embeds official CPC subclass definitions with E5-base-v2 and supplies a per-patent allowed label set rather than external evidence passages.
- Few-shot plus retrieval uses two fixed training examples and two TF–IDF-nearest examples; generated labels must be strict JSON.
- Post-processing normalizes CPC-like strings, rejects labels outside the retrieved set, falls back when parsing is empty, and caps output at seven labels.
- Qwen-2.5-7B receives a LoRA experiment with rank 64, alpha 16, dropout 0.05, three epochs, and effective batch size 16.
- Metrics include micro/macro precision, recall, and F1; hierarchy-expanded micro scores; per-label F1; bootstrap intervals; paired Wilcoxon tests; runtime; energy; and CO2.
- External validation uses 10,000 EPO applications published from 2019–2022 without retraining the USPTO-trained encoders.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- No knowledge graph or formal ontology is constructed; the reusable semantic object is the CPC hierarchy itself.
- The modeled nodes are patents and CPC subclasses, with `patent classifiedAs subclass` as a multi-valued prediction relation.
- CPC section, class, and subclass prefixes form explicit broader/narrower relations used for hierarchy-aware evaluation.
- Official subclass definitions act as textual descriptions attached to label nodes and as retrieval keys for candidate generation.
- The allowed-set design separates candidate retrieval from final classification, a useful two-stage semantic-linking pattern.
- Training/test support is treated as a property of each subclass, enabling rare/mid/frequent strata and per-concept error analysis.
- The prediction export retains patent text, gold labels, predicted labels, full prompts, allowed sets, and retrieved-example IDs, providing provenance for each classification event.
- The work demonstrates that labeler choice changes the observed technology map; classification edges should therefore retain model/version/method provenance rather than masquerade as ground truth.
- It does not represent CPC revision history, examiner rationale, evidence spans linking text to a subclass, confidence calibration, or disagreements among authoritative assignments.
- Its four-character normalization discards subgroup granularity and may merge distinctions important to claim or prior-art analysis.

## Evaluation & results

- SciBERT is the strongest full-space encoder: Micro-F1 0.439, Macro-F1 0.059, and hierarchical F1 0.527.
- The strongest LLM Micro-F1 is Qwen zero-shot at 0.278; its Macro-F1 is 0.129 and hierarchical F1 is 0.386.
- Qwen zero-shot plus retrieval reaches Macro-F1 0.161, the best reported macro score, while Micro-F1 is 0.222 and hierarchical F1 is 0.371.
- Constraining SciBERT to retrieved candidates lowers Micro-F1 from 0.439 to 0.330 but raises Macro-F1 from 0.059 to 0.064.
- LoRA does not consistently help: Qwen plus LoRA zero-shot has Micro-F1 0.146 versus 0.278 for untuned Qwen zero-shot.
- The rare-subclass tests use the bottom 20% of subclasses by support and show significant LLM improvements over both encoder baselines for every tested LLM configuration.
- Qwen LoRA few-shot has mean rare-label F1 0.049 and the strongest reported one-sided result, `p = 3.2 × 10^-5`.
- Against SciBERT on frequent subclasses, encoder superiority is significant for all tested LLM variants (`p < 0.01`).
- The most frequent training subclass, G06F, has 6,272 examples; the rarest listed subclass, C12L, has four, a span exceeding three orders of magnitude.
- Train and test subclass frequencies correlate at Pearson `r = 0.929`, so the test retains the benchmark's imbalance.
- Encoder inference over 10,000 patents takes 1–2 minutes and about 0.010–0.011 kWh.
- LLM inference takes about 20–277 minutes and 0.135–2.113 kWh across reported configurations.
- The authors summarize this as roughly 15–250 times faster and 20–200 times more energy-efficient for encoders at inference.
- EPO validation preserves the qualitative crossover: encoders dominate frequent subclasses while LLMs are relatively stronger in the long tail.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The implemented hybrid is only a proposed future direction; no router combining encoder and LLM predictions is designed or evaluated.
- Only instruction-tuned 7–8B-class LLMs are considered, limiting conclusions about smaller, larger, quantized, proprietary, or newer models.
- Full fine-tuning was infeasible; LoRA is the sole task-adaptation method tested.
- Retrieval is limited to official CPC definitions and a fixed top-20 candidate set, not claims, specifications, citation neighborhoods, or classification notes.
- EPO records are licensed and cannot be redistributed, constraining reproduction of the external validation.
- Title and abstract input omits claims and description passages that may carry the decisive classification evidence.
- Four-character subclass prediction is materially coarser than operational CPC subgroup assignment.
- The non-empty fallback can turn parsing failure into a forced retrieved-label prediction, conflating model classification with retrieval behavior.
- Macro-F1 remains low even for the strongest LLM configuration; relative long-tail advantage should not be read as reliable rare-label assignment.
- The paper associates rare classes with emerging or interdisciplinary technologies, but rarity is not itself evidence that a technology is emerging.
- Energy and runtime values reflect one reported hardware/software setting and are not portable cost constants.
- External evaluation is described mainly through qualitative plots rather than a complete tabulated metric set in the extracted text.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **patent tooling — uspto-patent-driver-depth:** Take candidate-generation plus final-classification as separate events; retain retrieved CPC definitions, allowed labels, model output, fallback path, and gold/examiner labels for audit.
- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** Represent CPC levels as versioned broader/narrower concepts with definitions and mappings; reject four-character truncation as the only durable identity when subgroup precision matters.
- **epistemic slice:** Store predicted classification as a provenance-bearing claim with bitemporal model/CPC versions, evidence spans, confidence, and explicit contradiction against authoritative or competing assignments.
- **patent tooling — agentic-cad-patent-tooling:** Route routine head-class suggestions through an encoder and escalate rare, cross-domain, or disputed candidates to LLM-plus-human review; do not deploy an LLM universally.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Show per-label support and disagreement warnings beside automated CPC suggestions so practitioners do not mistake aggregate benchmark strength for rare-class reliability.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** Preserve prompt, retrieval set, normalized output, and correction history as replayable episodes; use past rare-label failures to improve routing rather than overwrite them with one accepted label.
