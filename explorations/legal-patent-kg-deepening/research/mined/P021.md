# An Ontological Analysis of the ISO/IEC 24744 Metamodel

## Problem & positioning

- Software-engineering organizations combine standards and reference models whose scopes, terms, process structures, quality systems, and definitions diverge.
- ISO's SC7 harmonization initiative proposed an ontology network rooted in a Definitional Elements Ontology (`DEO`).
- DEO was to draw heavily from ISO/IEC 24744's Software Engineering Metamodel for Development Methodologies (`SEMDM`) and ISO/IEC 24765's vocabulary.
- Configured Definitional Ontologies would specialize DEO for domains, and Standard Domain Ontologies would then specialize those for individual standards.
- The planned framework deferred stronger foundational distinctions to a future `AFOS` layer.
- The authors argue this ordering is unsafe: source metamodels should be ontologically analyzed before they become the semantic foundation of a standards family.
- They analyze selected SEMDM fragments using the Unified Foundational Ontology (`UFO`) and propose corrected model fragments.
- The work is a conceptual audit of process, product, and producer classes, not an implementation or empirical interoperability evaluation.

## Approach / architecture

- UFO-A provides particulars/universals, higher-order universals, substantials, intrinsic moments, relators, formal/material relations, kinds, roles, and role mixins.
- UFO-B distinguishes endurants from events, atomic from complex events, participant dependence, and time intervals.
- UFO-C adds agents, objects, intentional moments, commitments, appointments, normative/plan descriptions, actions, and object participations.
- SEMDM models both methodology and endeavour levels with powertype-like class pairs: a methodology class describes kinds instantiated at the endeavour level.
- The analysis examines SEMDM's Work Units, Stages, Producers, and Work Products and maps each to UFO categories.
- `WorkUnit` conflates an intended/scheduled job with an actually performed action; the redesign separates `Scheduled Work Unit` from `Performed Work Unit`.
- `Stage` has the same planned-versus-actual ambiguity and should split into scheduled and performed stages.
- Fuzzy large/small-grained distinctions among Process, Task, and Technique are replaced by mereological criteria.
- A composite performed work unit has at least two disjoint work-unit parts; a simple task has none; a process is not part of another composite; a composite task is.
- `Technique` is moved out of WorkUnit and modeled as a Plan Description defining a composite action universal.
- TechniqueKind and TaskTechniqueMappingKind become unnecessary; actual use of a technique is an object-usage participation.
- Work products are analyzed along two orthogonal axes: nature and mereological composition.
- The nature hierarchy includes software, hardware, model, and document and remains incomplete; the simple/composite hierarchy is complete and disjoint.
- SEMDM `Action` is recast as an object participation—creation, change, usage, or termination—inside a performed work unit, not as an effect caused by a task.
- Producer modeling is rebuilt around the agent/object distinction, anti-rigid roles, team membership relators, and work-performance participation.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- The audit demonstrates a repeatable pattern: assign each source class a foundational category, test identity/rigidity/dependence/mereology, identify overload, then remodel.
- Planned and actual work are different entity kinds; expected and actual start/end times therefore belong to different nodes or claims.
- A kind of work unit is an action universal, while a performed unit is an action particular and a scheduled unit is an appointment.
- Grain size is not a stable taxonomy discriminator; parthood and weak supplementation give inspectable definitions for simple and composite units.
- A technique is a normative plan description used during an action, not itself the action being performed.
- `WorkProduct` is a non-sortal umbrella over entities with different identity principles and should not be directly instantiated.
- Nature and composition are orthogonal facets: a document, software item, or hardware item may independently be simple or composite.
- Product parthood needs type-specific axioms; generic composition alone cannot say which component types can belong to which wholes.
- `ObjectParticipation` reifies how a product participates in work: creation, change, read-only usage, or termination.
- Only agents can bear intentions and responsibility; tools are objects used as resources rather than producers.
- `Producer` is an anti-rigid, externally dependent role mixin arising from participation in a work performance.
- Rigid `Person` and `Team` kinds cannot specialize anti-rigid Producer; contextual `Person Producer` and `Team Producer` roles bridge them.
- A team is a collective social agent with at least two agent members; roles and tools are not team members.
- Person-team and team-team allocations are relators, allowing membership context and played role kinds to carry their own identity and attributes.
- SEMDM's instance-level `Role` duplicates its `RoleKind`: “technical writer” is a social role instantiated by Mary and later John, not a collection-object that they exchange.
- A role kind is defined by a normative description recognized by the organization and linked to responsibilities.
- The paper recommends explicit OCL or first-order axioms after category repair; a clean diagram alone does not guarantee model consistency.

## Evaluation & results

- The authors inspect three selected SEMDM groups: process, product, and producer classes.
- The analysis identifies overloaded or misclassified concepts including WorkUnit, Stage, Technique, Action, Producer, Team membership, Role, and Tool.
- It proposes seven illustrated replacement fragments covering scheduled/performed work, work-unit composition, techniques, products, participations, producers, teams, and tools.
- Worked examples include a five-step CRC Cards technique, document/software/hardware composition, Mary and John successively playing technical-writer, and team allocation.
- No whole-metamodel coverage count, defect count, inter-rater agreement, competency-question suite, formal consistency run, or downstream harmonization result is reported.
- No quantitative comparison establishes that the revised fragments improve interoperability, comprehension, or implementation outcomes.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The authors explicitly analyze only fragments of SEMDM, so their conclusions do not establish that the remaining standard is ontologically sound.
- Space limits prevent development of work-product parthood axioms and truncate the performed-stage analysis.
- Proposed constraints are described but not delivered or executed as OCL/FOL in the paper.
- The audit depends on UFO's commitments; it shows coherence under that foundation, not a foundation-neutral proof of correctness.
- The paper does not validate its reinterpretations with ISO editors, standards users, method engineers, or project practitioners.
- Some replacements may alter intended standard semantics rather than merely clarify them—for example, the exact process/composite-task parthood criterion.
- Weak supplementation is imported as a formal mereological commitment and may not match every artifact or organizational aggregation convention.
- The plan-versus-performance split is useful but incomplete without revisions, cancellations, partial execution, divergence, and provenance of schedule changes.
- The model distinguishes expected from actual time but offers no bitemporal record of when either fact was asserted or corrected.
- “Only agents act” is a deliberate ontology choice; modern agentic software still needs explicit treatment as tool, delegated agent, or accountable socio-technical actor.
- No mapping to RDF/OWL, SHACL, persistence schemas, or executable validation is supplied.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- `semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology)`: adopt the category-audit sequence before promoting standards vocabulary; keep lightweight SKOS terms distinct from rigid kinds, anti-rigid roles, relators, events, and plan descriptions.
- `epistemic slice`: separate scheduled, performed, and later-reported work as different claims/events with expected time, valid time, transaction time, evidence spans, and revision links; reject one mutable `WorkUnit` record.
- `patent tooling / uspto-patent-driver-depth`: distinguish a prosecution procedure definition, scheduled response, actual filing/action, and document participation; classify artifacts as created, changed, read, or terminated with source evidence.
- `patent tooling / agentic-cad-patent-tooling`: model an automation as a tool/resource unless an explicit delegation and accountability model warrants agent status; never infer legal responsibility merely from automated execution.
- `OIP solo-practice workflows`: represent workflow templates as plan descriptions and matter activity as performances; use person/team allocations and temporary producer roles so handoffs preserve responsibility history.
- `agent memory (Cognee)`: keep plans, appointments, performances, artifacts, and role instantiations as separate memory objects, preventing an intended task from being remembered as completed work.
