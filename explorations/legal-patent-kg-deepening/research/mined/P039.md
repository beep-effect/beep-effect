# In the Defense of Ontological Foundations for Conceptual Modeling

## Problem & positioning

- This debate paper answers Boris Wyssusek's claim that ontology-based conceptual modeling is impossible in principle.
- Wyssusek's argument treats conceptual modeling as structuring human knowledge and ontology as inquiry only into concrete objects.
- Guarino and Guizzardi reject both premises as overgeneralizations from the Bunge–Wand–Weber approach.
- They agree that Wand and Weber did not systematically select among ontologies and modified Bunge's theory to fit modeling needs.
- Their broader position is that formal ontology, cognitive science, linguistics, and philosophy can justify modeling choices that logic alone leaves equivalent.
- Ontological foundations are presented as necessary for mature conceptual-modeling principles, not as one mandated metaphysical doctrine.
- The paper prefers a descriptive foundation attentive to common sense, language, and cognition over an exclusively revisionary, reductionist ontology.

## Approach / architecture

- The argument begins at Brachman's epistemological level: representation languages make knowledge-structuring choices not visible in bare logic.
- The formula “there is a red apple” can be structured around apple instances bearing redness or red instances bearing appleness.
- Although logically equivalent, these alternatives differ ontologically because `Apple` is a rigid identity-bearing natural kind and `Red` is contingent attribution.
- Identity criteria justify quantifying over apples but not treating redness as the primary sort of individual.
- The authors use this pattern to argue that modeling structure depends on theories of universals, individuation, rigidity, and identity.
- They then examine three recurring conceptual-modeling problems: role modeling, role counting, and part-whole relations.
- For roles, ontological analysis distinguishes admissible base types from contingent customer-like classifications.
- For the counting problem, it reconciles anti-rigid role universals with rigid qua-individuals as complementary entity types.
- For parthood, it combines formal ontology with cognitive and linguistic evidence to distinguish varieties of dependence and transitivity.
- The final argument broadens ontology beyond concrete material objects to universals, numbers, sets, classes, forms, and regions.
- It distinguishes descriptive metaphysics, which exposes common-sense conceptual categories, from revisionary metaphysics guided primarily by science.
- The authors argue that the two can be compatible when their different granularities are made explicit.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- `NaturalKind` is rigid: membership necessarily persists while the individual exists and supplies an identity principle.
- `Attribution` is non-rigid: a quality such as redness may change without destroying the bearer and does not identify it.
- `SortalUniversal` carries individuation and identity conditions and licenses reference, quantification, and counting.
- `RoleUniversal` is anti-rigid and contingent upon an instance of an admissible identity-bearing type.
- `QuaIndividual` is a distinct, rigid entity dependent on a bearer-in-context and can solve cases where multiple role instances must be counted.
- The two role notions are not rival definitions; they model complementary entities and should not be collapsed into one class hierarchy.
- `Customer` therefore should not serve as the identity-supplying supertype of both `Person` and `Organization`.
- Parthood is a family of relations rather than a single universally transitive predicate.
- Functional dependence and the kinds of relata determine whether a particular part relation is transitive and how whole/part lifecycles constrain each other.
- Intentional collectives such as communities require explicit identity criteria; they cannot automatically be reduced to the extensional set of current members.
- Abstract entities are legitimate ontology commitments depending on the selected theory; materialist monism is one choice, not the definition of ontology.
- Descriptive and revisionary classifications should be represented with declared viewpoint and granularity rather than silently merged.
- The general KG lesson is to attach meta-properties—rigidity, identity supply, dependence, unity, and granularity—to modeling categories and validate edge patterns against them.

## Evaluation & results

- This is a conceptual rebuttal, not an empirical system evaluation.
- It supplies three families of worked modeling problems: multiple-admissible-type roles, the role counting problem, and parthood semantics.
- The authors cite prior patterns and analyses as demonstrations that ontology can yield more parsimonious and general solutions.
- They state that cognitive relevance for identity-supplying properties is supported by multiple independent experiments, but this paper reports no new experiment or numerical synthesis.
- No dataset, ontology size, annotator study, reasoning benchmark, defect comparison, or information-system outcome is provided.
- The practical result is an argued decision criterion: prefer structures grounded in identity, rigidity, dependence, and the ontology of the relata over merely logical equivalence.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The paper is a debate response and relies on argument plus cited prior work; it does not operationally measure improved information-system quality.
- It criticizes BWW selection without presenting a reproducible procedure for selecting among alternative foundational ontologies.
- “Scientific” ontology is defended broadly, but falsification criteria and validation thresholds remain underspecified.
- The apple/red example is intuitive but does not establish how tools should encode or enforce every meta-property.
- The proposed distinctions can increase modeling cost and specialist dependence if not paired with tractable schema patterns and diagnostics.
- Role universals and qua-individuals need explicit temporal and contextual identity rules to avoid proliferating entities.
- The treatment of parthood points to extensive external work rather than giving a complete axiom set in this paper.
- Compatibility between descriptive and revisionary taxonomies is asserted at different granularities but not demonstrated with a mapping evaluation here.
- None of the examples addresses uncertain extraction, contradictory testimony, source provenance, or changing legal validity.
- Patent and legal graphs must distinguish useful conceptual discipline from metaphysical commitments that do not affect competency questions.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** Annotate concepts with rigidity, identity-supply, dependence, and viewpoint/granularity; keep SKOS labels broader than the stricter domain axioms they index.
- **epistemic slice:** Model roles as time-bounded anti-rigid classifications and introduce qua-entities only where counting or relationship-specific provenance requires them.
- **epistemic slice:** Make identity criteria explicit before entity resolution; treat competing individuation rules as claims eligible for contradiction triage.
- **patent tooling — agentic-cad-patent-tooling:** Use typed mereology for components, functional parts, materials, and assemblies; reject a blanket transitive `partOf` edge when lifecycles or functions differ.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Distinguish stable party identity from matter-specific roles such as client, inventor, assignee, examiner, or opposing counsel.
- **agent memory — Cognee:** Do not reify transient attributes as entities by default; use identity-bearing nodes plus temporal attributions unless a qua-individual solves a real counting need.
