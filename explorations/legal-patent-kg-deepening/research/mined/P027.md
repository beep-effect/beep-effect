# Can Large Language Models Generate High-quality Patent Claims?

## Problem & positioning

- Patent claims define protection scope and require technical completeness, precise terminology, clear dependencies, and legally meaningful feature linkage.
- Earlier claim-generation work commonly conditioned on abstracts, although abstracts intentionally omit details and cannot legally add subject matter beyond the description.
- The paper reframes generation as description-to-claims rather than abstract-to-claims and asks whether general, legal-specific, or patent-specific LLMs work best.
- It distinguishes claim drafting from summarization: claims are ordered independent/dependent structures, about seven times longer than abstracts in the dataset, and require stricter precision.
- The intended role remains assistive; even the best tested output requires professional revision for legal and technical robustness.

## Approach / architecture

- Construct `HUPD-DCG` from the Harvard USPTO Patent Dataset by selecting English patent documents filed in 2017.
- Keep granted documents, exclude pending/rejected applications, and restrict descriptions to fewer than 8,000 tokens for model context limits.
- Order documents by date and reserve the last 1,311 documents—about 14%—for temporal-style held-out testing.
- Compare abstract-conditioned and description-conditioned Llama-3 generation.
- Test PatentGPT-J-6B, SaulLM-7B, Mistral-7B, Llama-3-8B/70B, Mixtral-8x7B/8x22B, and GPT-4.
- Fine-tune Llama-3-8B on claim generation (`Llama-3-FT`) and jointly on claim generation plus abstract generation (`Llama-3-MFT`).
- Score BLEU, ROUGE-1, ROUGE-L, and BERTScore against reference claims.
- Add expert human review on 80 generated claim sets using completeness, clarity, terminology consistency, feature-link correctness, and overall quality.
- A licensed patent attorney and experienced patent engineer conduct the human evaluation and agree on ratings.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- The paper is not an ontology/KG proposal, but it exposes a useful claim-structure model for downstream representation.
- A patent description contains invention `Feature`s, embodiments, functions, and relations; a claim selects a legally relevant subset.
- A `ClaimSet` is ordered and contains independent claims plus dependent claims whose references form a directed dependency graph.
- `EssentialFeatureCoverage` should link generated claim limitations back to supported description spans, rather than rely only on lexical overlap.
- `TerminologyConsistency` is an entity-normalization constraint: the same element should retain an unambiguous name across the claim graph.
- `FeatureLinkage` captures technically correct relationships and grouping, especially alternatives or limitations introduced by dependent claims.
- `ClaimSupport` is evidence provenance from each limitation to the description; unsupported or nonsensical features are critical failures.
- `NullFeature`, `Repetition`, `WrongFeatureType`, `AmbiguousReference`, and `UnsupportedFeature` emerge as concrete defect classes.
- The first independent claim is often nearly extractive because descriptions may reproduce it verbatim; dependent claims require harder selection and reasoning.
- Human quality and reference similarity are distinct relations: GPT-4 is judged best by experts despite low lexical-overlap scores.

## Evaluation & results

- HUPD-DCG contains 8,244 training and 1,311 test documents, for 9,555 total.
- Average train/test description lengths are 5,491/5,451 tokens; average claim lengths are 969/962 tokens.
- Average train/test claim counts are 12.4/11.4; abstracts average 133/134 tokens.
- Description-conditioned Llama-3 improves over abstract conditioning: BLEU 21.98 to 34.32, ROUGE-1 46.30 to 56.72, ROUGE-L 31.38 to 38.44, and BERTScore 86.17 to 88.40.
- PatentGPT-J and SaulLM are not human-rated because their outputs are mostly nonsensical; automated BLEU scores are 12.86 and 12.68.
- Fine-tuned Llama-3 improves human overall quality from 4.6 to 5.6 and ROUGE-L from 38.88 to 47.35.
- Multi-task fine-tuning scores 5.4 overall versus 5.6 for single-task tuning and lowers conceptual clarity from 5.8 to 4.9.
- GPT-4 earns the best human ratings: completeness 5.4, clarity 6.3, terminology 7.4, feature linkage 5.9, and overall quality 6.0.
- GPT-4’s automated BLEU is only 15.73, showing poor alignment between reference-overlap metrics and expert judgment.
- First-claim scores exceed remaining-claim scores for every model; for Llama-3-FT, ROUGE-L falls from 59.24 to 42.58.
- Fine-tuning raises Llama-3 completeness from 4.0 to 5.3 and feature linkage from 4.1 to 5.3, but repetition and coverage gaps remain.
- GPT-4 outputs average 482 tokens and 6.56 claims, substantially shorter than references and other systems.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- Authors: descriptions are limited to fewer than 8,000 tokens, while longer applications may be harder.
- Authors: only English-language patents are studied.
- Authors: training and inference hyperparameters are not systematically tuned.
- Authors: even GPT-4 needs revision to remove null features, improve dependent limitations, and ensure legal and technical robustness.
- Authors: uncertainty estimation and human verification are proposed as future quality controls.
- Mine: only 80 claim sets receive expert evaluation, and the rating scale’s reliability is not quantified beyond evaluator agreement.
- Mine: restricting to granted patents creates survivorship and drafting-quality bias; gold claims are not necessarily optimal claim strategy.
- Mine: the experiment measures imitation of issued text, not novelty, enablement, written-description support, antecedent basis, claim construction, or prosecution outcome.
- Mine: application-level split chronology helps realism but does not report family/inventor/assignee leakage or near-duplicate descriptions.
- Mine: a description may quote the independent claim verbatim, so strong first-claim performance can be extraction rather than drafting competence.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **patent tooling (agentic-cad-patent-tooling):** generate from full description/embodiment evidence, never the abstract; require every limitation to carry a source-span edge and flag unsupported features before attorney review.
- **patent tooling (uspto-patent-driver-depth):** model claim dependencies and defect classes explicitly, then evaluate independent and dependent claims separately instead of reporting one aggregate quality score.
- **epistemic slice:** represent expert judgments, automatic metrics, and support checks as separate claims with provenance; treat their disagreement—especially GPT-4’s human/lexical split—as contradiction triage, not averaging.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** use the five expert criteria as a review rubric and route low-confidence, ambiguous-reference, null-feature, and feature-type findings to a human drafting checkpoint.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** retain invention terminology and feature-link decisions across drafting turns, but reject memory-derived limitations unless the current description provides an exact support span.
