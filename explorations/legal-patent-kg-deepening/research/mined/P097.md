# Visual Descriptor Extraction from Patent Figure Captions: A Case Study of Data Efficiency Between BiLSTM and Transformer

## Problem & positioning

Patent technical drawings are difficult to index with image models trained on colorful natural images because design-patent figures are largely black strokes, curves, and dots.
The paper moves the first annotation step from vision to language: figure captions explicitly name the depicted object and viewing aspect, so an NER model can generate labels for drawings.
Its target entities are an `OBJECT` such as “jacket with scalloped shoulder regions” and an `ASPECT` such as “front perspective view.”
This supports patent-image retrieval, figure classification, image-captioning datasets, and, secondarily, patent knowledge-graph construction.
The central comparison is data efficiency rather than model scale: BiLSTM-CRF and Transformer taggers receive the same small, manually annotated caption corpus and several pretrained embeddings.
The authors position the approach as a cheaper way to create a large benchmark than direct human annotation of every image.

## Approach / architecture

USPTO full-text XML supplies captions through dedicated tags, while the associated figures are TIFF files.
Researchers randomly selected 3,300 captions from 3,300 figures in the 2020 corpus and annotated them with brat.
Annotation uses BIO sequence tags: `B-ASPECT`, `I-ASPECT`, `B-OBJECT`, `I-OBJECT`, and `O`.
Annotators prefer descriptive superphrases, so “cooker with lid” and “men’s shirt” remain complete object spans rather than reducing to their head nouns.
The corpus contains 2,464 object mentions and 2,958 aspect mentions and is split into 2,700 training, 300 validation, and 300 test captions.
Both model families follow the sequence-tagging pipeline of pretrained token representation, contextual encoder, and tag decoder.
BiLSTM-CRF encodes each token in both directions and uses a CRF to learn dependencies among output tags and reject implausible sequences.
The Transformer uses multi-head attention with encoder-decoder processing and a softmax output layer.
Input representations tested include GloVe, ELMo, BERT, RoBERTa, ALBERT, a RoBERTa OpenAI detector variant, and DistilBERT, though the exact set varies by architecture.
Data-efficiency trials vary the training set through 500, 750, 1,000, 1,500, 2,000, and 2,700 captions.
The best DistilBERT-BiLSTM-CRF model is then applied to 66,417 figures from 11,152 design patents issued in 2019.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

The annotation schema contributes two concrete patent-figure entity types: `Object` and `Aspect`.
`Object` is deliberately phrase-level and preserves modifiers that distinguish a design, rather than normalizing every mention to a generic head noun.
`Aspect` represents a view or orientation description, such as front, right-side elevation, or perspective view.
BIO labels preserve exact caption spans and boundaries, providing evidence anchors rather than only document-level keywords.
The source structure provides an implicit pattern: a patent contains a figure, a figure has a caption and figure name, and the caption mentions an object and an aspect.
The paper does not define a formal ontology, graph schema, axioms, persistent identifiers, or relation vocabulary beyond the two NER categories.
It also does not normalize extracted strings into controlled concepts: 6,405 “distinct object names” and 2,210 “distinct aspect names” are string-level outputs, not demonstrated equivalence classes.
A KG ingestion layer should therefore retain `CaptionMention` separately from canonical `ObjectConcept` and `ViewAspectConcept` nodes.
Span provenance should record patent identifier, figure identifier, caption text offsets, model/version, extraction time, and confidence before any concept merge.
Candidate relations such as `depictsObject` and `hasViewAspect` are a useful downstream interpretation, but they are not asserted or evaluated as graph edges by the authors.
No axioms govern aspect compatibility, figure-to-embodiment links, object part structure, design-claim scope, or whether caption language is legally limiting.

## Evaluation & results

Evaluation uses strict span matching: both subphrases and superphrases of the gold entity count as errors.
The best overall result is DistilBERT-BiLSTM-CRF at 96.92% precision, 96.28% recall, and 96.60% micro-F1.
For that model, aspect extraction reaches 99.20% precision, recall, and F1; object extraction reaches 94.09% precision, 92.72% recall, and 93.40% F1.
BiLSTM-CRF outperforms the Transformer for every reported shared embedding configuration.
The largest tabled gap uses the RoBERTa OpenAI representation: 96.18% F1 for BiLSTM-CRF versus 89.52% for the Transformer.
With only 500 training captions, BiLSTM-CRF reaches 95.15% F1; the Transformer remains below it across every examined training size.
Application to the unlabeled 2019 corpus produces 66,417 object mentions and 67,105 aspect mentions.
After string deduplication, the outputs contain 6,405 distinct object names and 2,210 distinct aspect names.
Only 136 extracted object strings and 154 extracted aspect strings also occur in training, reported as 2.1% and 7% of the extracted distinct totals.
The authors interpret the corresponding 98% novel object strings and 93% novel aspect strings as evidence of generalization.
Frequent extracted objects include “display screen” 577 times, “container” 377 times, and “shoe” 311 times.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

The authors call for a more systematic future study of data efficiency.
Only 300 test captions support the reported F1 values, and the paper gives no repeated-run variance, significance test, annotator agreement, or error taxonomy.
The random split is at caption level; the text does not establish whether related patents, repeated caption templates, or near-duplicate figures cross train/test boundaries.
The comparison is not a clean architecture-only ablation because the embedding choices, parameter counts, and pretrained representations differ.
“Transformer” describes a particular encoder-decoder tagger trained on limited data, so the conclusion should not be generalized to all pretrained Transformer NER systems.
Novel surface strings are not proof of correct generalization; the 2019 bulk output has no reported manual precision audit.
String distinctness exaggerates conceptual variety when spelling, plurality, syntax, or modifier differences denote the same concept.
Conversely, normalization could erase legally material design qualifiers, so naïve entity merging is also unsafe.
Caption descriptors may omit visible features, may describe alternative embodiments, and should not be treated as exhaustive image content or claim scope.
The study is limited to US design-patent caption patterns and does not test utility-patent diagrams, multilingual captions, OCR noise, or cross-jurisdiction transfer.
No retrieval experiment demonstrates that the automatically generated labels actually improve examiner search or similarity judgments.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- `patent tooling / uspto-patent-driver-depth`: ingest figure identifiers, caption text, and exact `OBJECT`/`ASPECT` spans alongside TIFF assets; preserve mention-level provenance instead of flattening labels into patent keywords.
- `patent tooling / agentic-cad-patent-tooling`: use caption-derived object/view labels as weak supervision for CAD and drawing retrieval, but require image-caption agreement checks before treating a label as a depicted feature.
- `semantic-foundation / SKOS and @beep/ontology`: map extracted surface forms to candidate SKOS concepts with preferred/alternative labels; reject automatic string deduplication as authoritative identity because modifiers may carry design significance.
- `epistemic slice`: represent each extraction as a model-attributed claim over an evidence span, with confidence and transaction time; keep it defeasible when vision evidence or another caption contradicts it.
- `OIP solo-practice workflows`: add figure-caption search facets for object and view aspect, while clearly distinguishing search aids from claim construction or legal conclusions.
- `agent memory / Cognee`: retain the compact caption mention and its figure/patent pointers as retrieval memory rather than storing an ungrounded canonical object fact.
