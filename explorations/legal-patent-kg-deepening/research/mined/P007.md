# A Network Model of Legal Relations

## Problem & positioning

- Network analysis in law has mostly modeled citations, document cross-references, professional ties, or criminal associations rather than law's internal legal relations.
- The paper proposes a formal network treatment of Hohfeldian relations between legal actors and uses it to quantify legal modularity.
- Its jurisprudential test case is the property “bundle of rights” versus “law of things” debate: if modules emerge from atomic actor relations without representing things as nodes, thing-like structure has not merely been assumed.
- The broader position is that law manages complexity and information cost by packaging dense interactions into semi-autonomous modules.
- The contribution is intentionally a rudimentary static toy model, offered as a starting point rather than a complete legal-network formalism.

## Approach / architecture

- Nodes are legal actors; edges are bilateral, atomic Hohfeldian relations concerning actions.
- The full Hohfeldian vocabulary is right, privilege, duty, no-right, power, immunity, disability, and liability, but the worked model uses only first-order relations.
- Correlativity allows the graph to encode duty or privilege from one actor to another: a right correlates with another's duty, and a no-right with another's privilege.
- The property hypothetical starts with separately owned plots, owner privileges, third-party trespass duties, and cross-plot nuisance duties.
- Things and physical parcels are deliberately not graph nodes. Plot boundaries appear only as visual aids; community structure is derived from actors and relations.
- The model uses Newman-Girvan divisive community detection: calculate edge betweenness over shortest paths, remove highest-betweenness edges, and repeat within exposed communities.
- It then computes Newman modularity `Q`: within-community edges minus the expected number in a comparable randomly wired network, normalized by the paper's stated scale.
- Relative modularity is `Qr = Qs / Qc`, comparing the complete system with the decomposed system after cross-community edges are removed.
- The paper suggests later extensions for directed, weighted, probabilistic, dynamic, and higher-order legal-relation networks.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- Core node class: `LegalActor`; roles or offices may act as placeholders while the occupying individual changes.
- Core edge family: `HohfeldianRelation`, typed as right/duty, privilege/no-right, power/liability, or immunity/disability correlatives.
- Every edge should retain its regulated action or abstention; bare actor-to-actor links lose the legal content that makes the relation meaningful.
- Direction and correlative role are semantically important even though the modularity calculation intentionally ignores direction.
- The graph is relation-primary: `Property`, `Ownership`, and `Thing` can be treated as higher-level modules emerging from clustered atomic relations rather than compulsory primitive nodes.
- Cross-module relations such as nuisance, easement, covenant, or overlapping patent scope act as bridge edges and reduce decomposability.
- A proposed alternative graph makes legal relations themselves nodes, with complementarity, interference, or suppression between relations as edges.
- Second-order powers are network-rewriting operations: exercising a power can connect actors or change the nature of existing relations.
- Probabilistic edges could express indeterminate law or the chance that a legal relation is enforced in practice.
- The model separates nominal legal boundaries from effective relational boundaries, permitting comparison of law-on-the-books and law-in-action graphs.
- In patents, claim scope forms an abstract action-space boundary; overlapping patents and the doctrine of equivalents introduce crossings and governance-like exceptions.

## Evaluation & results

- The worked example is synthetic; the paper reports no real Hohfeldian-relation dataset or empirical legal-system comparison.
- Across all actor pairs in the example, there are 312 shortest paths.
- Each of the two cross-plot edges occurs in 121 paths, reported as 38.8%; each within-plot edge occurs 30 times, or 9.6%.
- Removing the two highest-betweenness nuisance edges exposes two disconnected communities.
- The model reports `Q = 0.34` without cross-plot nuisance relations and `Q = 0.30` with them.
- Relative modularity is `0.30 / 0.34 = 0.88`, supporting the qualitative claim that the system remains nearly decomposable.
- Collapsing each plot's internal relations into two tighter bundle relations raises the reported modularity to `0.3475`.
- These numbers demonstrate the mechanics and sensitivity of the metric; they do not validate its legal interpretation on observed data.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- Authors' own: the hypothetical uses two plots, a handful of actors, and only a few relation types rather than a rich dataset or simulation.
- Authors' own: no dataset of underlying Hohfeldian relations exists, and extracting them from legal text is not yet considered viable at scale.
- Authors' own: the calculation abstracts away direction, relation importance or frequency, and probabilistic legal indeterminacy.
- Authors' own: complex actors such as states and corporations require richer treatment than ordinary individual nodes.
- Authors' own: second- and higher-order powers, external events, and dynamic network evolution are not modeled.
- Community detection and modularity are adopted for simplicity, not shown to be uniquely correct for legal networks.
- The example's edges and abstraction level are hand-selected; different action granularity can materially change topology and scores.
- `Q` measures structural clustering, not validity, justice, enforceability, doctrinal importance, or legal correctness.
- Conflating correlatives into undirected edges is acceptable for the paper's narrow modularity question but unsuitable for an operational rights-and-duties KG.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- `goals/epistemic-bitemporal-edge-core`: take first-class, typed actor-relation edges and model powers as additive network-change events; preserve direction, evidence scope, valid time, and known time that the toy calculation omits.
- `goals/epistemic-contradiction-triage`: use relation-to-relation interference as a candidate graph for `CONTRADICTS` or `QUALIFIES`, but never let community structure or bridge-edge scores automatically change authoritative disposition.
- `goals/semantic-foundation`: represent Hohfeldian relation kinds and correlatives as a compact SKOS concept scheme; reject treating emergent modularity as an ontology axiom or replacing domain concepts with graph metrics.
- `explorations/uspto-patent-driver-depth`: test claim-scope modules as abstract action spaces and preserve overlaps, doctrine-of-equivalents bridges, actor roles, and claim evidence instead of flattening patents into citation networks.
- `goals/agentic-cad-patent-tooling`: use bridge edges and community boundaries as explainable navigation or review prioritization signals, not infringement conclusions.
- Agent memory (Cognee): the information-hiding intuition supports retrieval by coherent subgraph, but the legal authority graph must remain separate from operator-memory clustering and scores.
