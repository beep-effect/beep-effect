# Towards a High-level Controlled Language for Legal Sources on the Semantic Web

## Problem & positioning

- Web legal documents are usually annotated only at topic level, while useful queries depend on the rules, conditions, exceptions, and roles inside provisions.
- Directly translating legal prose into machine-readable rules is difficult because legal language is variable, syntactically complex, ambiguous, and interpretation-laden.
- The paper proposes a high-level Controlled Natural Language (`hCNL`) as an intermediate annotation layer between source text and formal Semantic Web rules.
- It is legal norm specification, not patent drafting or patent-LLM authoring, so it belongs in track 1.
- The target is a human-guided transformation that preserves correspondence to source text while normalizing expressions enough for parsing and comparison.
- The proposal is explicitly preliminary and partial; it frames required constructs, operations, and one worked regulatory example.

## Approach / architecture

- The source example is a two-sentence passage from 21 CFR §610.40 concerning blood-bank screening tests for communicable-disease agents.
- hCNL must cover deontic modalities, conditional rules, main verbs, semantic roles, lists, exceptions, and anaphora to phrases, lists, and exceptions.
- A human interpreter controls a cascade of systematic rewrites rather than accepting an opaque end-to-end parse.
- Each original fragment remains linked to resulting controlled statements through any number of rewrite steps.
- Lexical simplification identifies domain terms, adds them interactively to a controlled vocabulary, and substitutes stable labels into the source.
- Shallow analysis identifies noun, verb, and prepositional phrases, then uses rules to detect rule statements, exceptions, and lists.
- Sentence simplification splits subordinate and list structures while preserving the scope of negation and modal operators.
- Anaphora resolution presents candidate antecedents for user selection; unresolved references route the fragment back to lower-level analysis.
- The final normalized statements should be parsable by an ACE-like CNL tool extended with hCNL constructs.
- Each output statement receives a unique identifier so later annotations and comparisons can refer to it.
- The workflow supports multiple legitimate granularities: two analysts may produce different but source-linked annotations without pretending interpretation is automatic.
- The authors emphasize folding source text into structured form and unfolding it back, making reversibility and source isomorphism design goals.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- The paper does not publish an ontology, but it defines an annotation graph pattern with source fragments, rewrite stages, controlled terms, and normalized rule statements.
- Source-to-output links are many-step provenance edges, not a single lossy `text -> rule` relation.
- Controlled vocabulary entries record a symbol, part-of-speech role, and source phrase, such as noun and verb definitions in Figures 1 and 2.
- Semantic-role analysis makes agents and themes explicit; the worked example resolves “you” to the regulated establishment.
- Anaphora links connect expressions such as “such use” and “such tests” to explicit antecedent events or terms.
- Rule structure must preserve modality, condition, exception, list membership, quantifier scope, negation scope, and clause subordination.
- The normalized layer separates terminology creation from proposition normalization, allowing reused terms without discarding the original wording.
- Undefined terms may remain in a controlled statement, preserving an explicit interpretation gap rather than fabricating semantics.
- Multiple annotations may target the same source at different granularities, implying that interpretation identity and analyst provenance must be first-class.
- The worked output decomposes the two source sentences into three obligation statements: test use, compliance with manufacturer instructions, and sufficient testing to reduce risk.
- A Beep representation should therefore reify `RewriteStep`, `TermDefinition`, `AnaphoraResolution`, and `ControlledStatement` as evidence-bearing claims, although those class names are extraction implications rather than published hCNL vocabulary.
- Unique statement identifiers provide stable retrieval and comparison handles while exact source links preserve auditability.

## Evaluation & results

- The paper reports one worked passage from one US federal regulation; no corpus-scale evaluation is claimed.
- The example begins with two source sentences and ends with three normalized obligation statements.
- It demonstrates three annotation levels: initial term substitution, additional substitution plus anaphora resolution, and final fragment normalization.
- The final transformation is performed manually; the authors suggest only that some pattern-based analysis may apply.
- There is no precision, recall, inter-annotator agreement, parser success rate, time-on-task, participant count, or query benchmark.
- The example shows feasibility of the rewrite representation, not completeness of the proposed language or correctness of downstream formal semantics.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The authors state that full translation is out of reach because interpretation is subjective and source texts are complex.
- Exception clauses can be difficult to detect and associate with the correct rule.
- Lexical distinctions can proliferate unless the controlled vocabulary has governance and scope boundaries.
- Source modification must preserve truth conditions (`salva veritate`), especially across simplification steps.
- Quantifier, modality, negation, and anaphora scope must remain stable; the paper offers no formal proof that the rewrite operators preserve them.
- The worked example is short, contains no nested exception or cross-provision amendment chain, and does not test conflicting interpretations.
- Human control is central, but the paper does not specify analyst identity, review workflow, disagreement representation, or versioning.
- Unique statement IDs are proposed without a persistence, merge, or identity policy across revised source text.
- The final controlled statements are not compiled into LegalRuleML, RDF, executable logic, or a tested ontology in this paper.
- No evidence-span coordinate model is specified beyond linking original fragments to outputs.
- The approach predates current LLM tooling; its reversible intermediate artifacts remain valuable, but automation assumptions require fresh evaluation.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- `epistemic slice (claims and evidence spans)`: take the source-fragment -> rewrite-step -> controlled-statement chain as a claim derivation graph with exact span anchors, analyst attribution, and retained alternatives.
- `epistemic-contradiction-triage`: compare normalized statements only after modality, actor, object, condition, exception, negation, and scope fields align; unresolved anaphora should block contradiction claims.
- `semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology)`: govern interactive term definitions as provisional concepts mapped to curated schemes, while rejecting analyst-local abbreviations as canonical ontology terms.
- `OIP solo-practice workflows`: apply reversible, human-approved normalization to office actions, engagement duties, and filing requirements before rules drive reminders or agent actions.
- `patent tooling (uspto-patent-driver-depth)`: reuse the rewrite cascade for patent-office regulations and guidance, but do not route it to patent-generation tooling because the paper addresses legal rules, not claim drafting.
- `agent memory (Cognee)`: store every rewrite and anaphora decision as a regenerable interpretation artifact so later models can audit why a normalized obligation was retrieved.
