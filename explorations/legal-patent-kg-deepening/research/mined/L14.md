# LegalRuleML Core Specification 1.0

## Problem & positioning

LegalRuleML is an OASIS interchange language for formal legal rules plus the metadata required to interpret, validate, and maintain them. Its central premise is that legal rules are not ordinary monotonic implications: conclusions may be defeated, rules may conflict, norms can be violated, and applicability depends on source, authority, jurisdiction, time, and interpretation.
The language also preserves isomorphism between formal rules and the legally binding text from which they were modeled. It builds its rule expressions on RuleML while adding legal statement kinds, deontic specifications, reparations, overrides, alternatives, and legal metadata.
The specification defines an RDF/RDFS abstract metamodel and XML serializations, including normalized and compact forms.

## Approach

The model treats a norm as a conditional from antecedent formulas to an effect. Constitutive rules create or define institutional facts and concepts.
Prescriptive rules regulate behavior by producing deontic effects. Rule strength is orthogonal to statement kind: a rule can be strict, defeasible, or a defeater.
Conflicts can be resolved through an explicit superiority relation represented by `Override`. Normative violations can activate ordered compensations, penalties, and reparations.
Alternative formalizations preserve mutually exclusive interpretations of the same or overlapping legal sources. Associations attach metadata to rules or fine-grained rule fragments without repeating it.
Contexts scope combinations of source, time, strength, authority, jurisdiction, associations, and alternatives.

The principal legal statement kinds exposed by the vocabulary are:

- `FactualStatement`: an expression of fact.
- `ConstitutiveStatement`: defines concepts and does not prescribe behavior.
- `PrescriptiveStatement`: prescribes behavior through deontic effects.
- `OverrideStatement`: states precedence between legal rules.
- `PenaltyStatement`: states a sanction or correction.
- `ReparationStatement`: links a penalty to the violated prescriptive statement.

The core deontic constructs are:

- `Obligation`: a bearer is legally bound to a state, act, or course of action; nonperformance yields violation.
- `Permission`: the bearer lacks a contrary obligation or prohibition; it may be weak or explicitly strong.
- `Prohibition`: a bearer is bound not to realize a state, act, or course of action; realization yields violation.
- `Right`: gives a bearer permission and implies obligations or prohibitions on one or more auxiliary parties.

Obligation, Permission, and Prohibition are directed modalities. They qualify a formula and identify a primary `Bearer`; other affected parties can occupy `AuxiliaryParty` roles.
The specification relates prohibition to obligation of the negation and obligation to absence of permission for the negation under standard deontic logic. It remains neutral about more specialized modalities by allowing an IRI or association to identify semantics such as achievement or maintenance obligation.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

### Norms, rules, and formulas

A `PrescriptiveStatement` wraps a RuleML rule whose head is a `SuborderList` of deontic formulas. A `ConstitutiveStatement` wraps a rule whose head cannot be deontic, though either statement kind may contain deontic formulas in its antecedent.
This separation prevents institutional definitions from being collapsed into behavioral prescriptions. Rules and fragments carry keys, enabling metadata and source links at rule, atom, relation, individual, antecedent, or consequent granularity.

### Defeasibility and conflict

`StrictStrength` means an indisputable antecedent yields an indisputable conclusion. `DefeasibleStrength` means the conclusion normally holds absent contrary information.
`Defeater` cannot establish its head; it blocks the opposite conclusion. The logic is skeptical under unresolved conflict: contradictory defeasible conclusions are withheld rather than making the theory explosive.
`Override(over, under)` records that one identified legal statement takes precedence over another. Strength may be global on a rule or localized through a `Context`.
The explicit relation can represent preferences that cannot safely be reduced to numeric salience.

### Violations, penalties, and reparations

A norm and its violation may coexist without logical inconsistency. `Violation` indicates that an obligation was unmet or a prohibition was breached.
`Compliance` indicates that an obligation was fulfilled or a prohibition was not violated. `SuborderList` orders deontic consequences: each later item applies only when every preceding item has been violated.
Permissions and rights should not precede later elements because they cannot themselves be violated. `PenaltyStatement` packages a sanction as a non-empty suborder list.
`Reparation` connects a penalty to the relevant prescriptive statement. Temporal metadata permits the sanction linked to a stable rule to change over time.

### Alternatives and interpretation

`Alternatives` is a mutually exclusive collection of renderings of one or more legal norms. Alternatives can be rules, statement collections, or source-to-rule association sets.
They record divergent interpretations but do not attempt to explain how an interpreter derived them. A `Context` can select or apply alternatives under jurisdictional, temporal, authority, or other parameters.
This makes interpretive disagreement representable without overwriting one formalization with another.

### Sources and isomorphism

`LegalSource` identifies an authoritative legal text by IRI. `LegalReference` supports non-IRI identifiers plus the naming system needed to disambiguate them.
`Source` identifies relevant non-legal or external rule resources. `Association` implements many-to-many links between sources and rules or fragments.
The intended granularity extends down to provisions, paragraphs, sentences, words, atoms, and atom fragments. The source remains legally binding; the formal rule is a traceable model of it.

### Authority, jurisdiction, actors, and roles

`Authority` is a person or organization empowered to create, endorse, or enforce norms. `Jurisdiction` may be geographic or subject-matter scope.
`Agent` is any entity capable of acting, including a person, database, or bot. `Figure` is an actor instantiating a function, such as a person acting as senator.
`Role` records which actor fills a function relative to particular expressions. These distinctions let the same actor participate under different institutional capacities.

### Time and context

LegalRuleML models external validity of norms rather than the internal timing of events described by their content. It distinguishes three legal axes: entry into force, efficacy, and applicability.
`TemporalCharacteristic` pairs a time with a legal status and status development such as start. Associations can attach temporal characteristics to any rule fragment.
`Context` gathers applied source, temporal characteristics, strength, authority, jurisdiction, associations, and alternatives for an explicit scope. This is contextual qualification, not a timeless property baked into the rule.

### Schema-first TypeScript mapping

Use a closed tagged union for statement identity and payload shape:

```ts
type LegalStatement =
  | FactualStatement
  | ConstitutiveStatement
  | PrescriptiveStatement
  | OverrideStatement
  | PenaltyStatement
  | ReparationStatement
```

Each variant should be an `S.TaggedClass` or equivalent named schema with an `EntityId`-style key. Decode XML-derived input into the domain union; do not expose raw element trees as the domain model.
Represent formulas recursively as their own tagged union (`Atom`, logical connectives, negation, and rule references).
Represent deontic specifications as a second closed union:

```ts
type DeonticSpecification =
  | Obligation
  | Permission
  | Prohibition
  | Right
  | SuborderList
```

Give each directed modality `bearer`, optional `auxiliaryParties`, `content: Formula`, and an optional semantic IRI. Do not encode prohibition as a boolean flag on obligation even though a logical equivalence can derive one from negation.
Keep `Right` distinct from `Permission` because it adds correlative effects on auxiliary parties.
Constrain `SuborderList` as non-empty and validate that Permission/Right cannot precede another item.

Model rule strength as `StrictStrength | DefeasibleStrength | Defeater`, independent from statement tags.
Model override edges as entities with `overStatementId`, `underStatementId`, provenance, and context rather than sorting rules destructively.
Represent `Violation` and `Compliance` as derived or asserted status claims referencing a deontic specification and supporting facts.
Represent `Reparation` as an explicit join from a prescriptive statement to a penalty statement.

Use separate identifiers for legal sources, references, statements, rules, formulas, and fragments.
An `Association` schema should carry non-empty applied metadata references and non-empty target references.
Source spans should support structured provision identifiers plus optional character offsets without assuming one source maps to one rule.
Keep `Authority`, `Jurisdiction`, `Agent`, `Figure`, and `RoleAssignment` as distinct schemas.

Represent the three temporal axes as a literal domain rather than a generic timestamp label.
Pair the axis/status with a development (`Starts`, `Ends`, or vocabulary IRI) and a temporal entity reference.
Treat a `Context` as immutable qualification data scoped to explicit target ids.
Do not merge context into the target object during decoding; the same rule can have multiple contexts.
Represent `Alternatives` as a non-empty set of mutually exclusive interpretation targets with shared source provenance.
Selection of an alternative should be a separate contextual or epistemic assertion, not mutation of the alternative set.

The XML compact form permits skipped edges and abbreviated structures.
Normalize both compact and normalized serializations into one canonical domain representation before validation or reasoning.
Preserve original XML ids, IRIs, and source associations so the canonical model can round-trip provenance even if exact surface syntax is not retained.

## Evaluation & results

This is a specification, not an empirical system evaluation.
It supplies a normative vocabulary, RDFS metamodel, Relax NG and XSD schema design, compact/normalized serialization patterns, and worked legal examples.
The captured examples cover defeasibility, constitutive and prescriptive rules, deontic operators, penalties and reparations, alternatives, sources, actors, jurisdiction, authority, time, associations, and contexts.
Later case studies model Australian credit law, an Italian court case, and U.S. Code section 504.
The capture does not report inter-annotator agreement, reasoning-performance benchmarks, ontology-coverage metrics, or production adoption results.

## Limitations

LegalRuleML defines interchange syntax and metadata but does not mandate one deontic or defeasible reasoning semantics for every extension IRI.
It models external rule-validity time, not all temporal structure of events inside rule content.
The specification records alternative interpretations but does not determine which interpretation is legally correct.
XML flexibility—compact forms, skipped edges, distributed syntax, and external RuleML components—raises decoder and canonicalization complexity.
Correct schema validation does not establish legal correctness, source fidelity, or sound downstream inference.
Directed modalities encode parties, but a richer Hohfeldian relation model may still be needed for powers, liabilities, disabilities, and immunities.
The metamodel's placeholder links to the then-developing RuleML metamodel should be checked against current artifacts before implementation.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- Adopt the statement and deontic unions as the legal rule slice's schema backbone.
- Preserve source-to-fragment isomorphism as evidence edges into the epistemic slice.
- Implement defeasible priority as explicit, contextual override claims rather than numeric rank alone.
- Keep norm, violation fact, penalty, and reparation as separate entities connected by typed relations.
- Use bitemporal claim machinery alongside LegalRuleML's entry, efficacy, and applicability axes.
- Store competing formalizations in `Alternatives`; never overwrite disagreement during ingestion.
- Validate compact XML into a canonical schema-first representation before graph projection.
- Align directed bearer/auxiliary-party roles with UFO-L/Hohfeld patterns where richer correlatives are required.
- Treat generated inferences as provenance-bearing claims, not as source-authored legal text.
