# A Functional Model of Patent Knowledge Based on Functional Ontology

## Problem & positioning

Patent prose contains reusable design knowledge, but it hides the inventor's intent behind legal and technical language.
The paper focuses on mechanical-product patents and on extracting structure, function, and physical-effect knowledge for redesign.
Its motivating problem is semantic variation: designers describe equivalent functions with different terms and conceptualizations.
Prior extraction approaches depend on domain vocabulary recognition, NLP, and later semantic classification.
The proposed functional ontology supplies a normalized vocabulary and relations so extracted product structure can be mapped to function.
The intended outcome is not legal claim interpretation or novelty analysis, but a functional model that helps designers identify inventive principles.

## Approach / architecture

The workflow has three stages: patent-knowledge extraction, functional analysis, and construction of a functional topology.
Extraction targets the title, abstract, claims, and description, with particular attention to verbs and nearby nouns or adjectives.
The first pass identifies product components and their interactions as structure/action knowledge.
A reasoning pass infers physical or scientific effects that realize those interactions.
Effects are converted into function expressions using a verb plus an object, then normalized to a function verb.
The analysis derives a total function from system inputs and outputs, decomposes it into main, divided, and subfunctions, and links each to components.
The resulting function-structure tree exposes both functional decomposition and implementation choices.
The worked example applies the process to Chinese utility-model patent CN201420036696.9 for a fruit picker.
The authors extract components including a circular saw, motor, U-shaped support, limit devices, collection basket, rods, handle, and switch.
They infer the function/effect vocabulary cutting, rotating, supporting, limiting, collecting, installing, and starting/stopping.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

The functional-ontology system contains three central models: function structure, function carrier, and function realization method.
Function structure is a topology among functions, including total-function, divided-function, and subfunction decomposition.
The function carrier explains the purpose of a product or component's behavior.
The realization-method model associates functional knowledge and design principles with physical structures.
Functions relate material, information, and energy input/output flows: a system transforms, transmits, or changes those flows.
The concrete pattern is `component -> participates in interaction -> realizes effect -> implements function`.
A second pattern is `total function -> decomposes into -> divided/subfunction -> realized by -> component`.
Function vocabulary is normalized through the ontology rather than retained solely as patent surface text.
The fruit-picker model maps cutting to the circular saw, start/stop to the switch, rotation to the motor, and collection to the basket.
It also maps limiting to limit bosses/locking devices and supporting to support rods/brackets.
The function taxonomy enables substitution: cutting and detaching are siblings under separation, allowing redesign by replacing one function.
No RDF/OWL formalization, URI policy, logical axioms, competency questions, or machine-readable ontology artifact is presented in the text.

## Evaluation & results

Evaluation is a single qualitative worked example; there is no benchmark, user study, extraction metric, or baseline comparison.
The example yields a total purpose of picking high fruit while collecting and protecting it.
The final simplified total functions are cutting and collecting, each decomposed into implementation-level subfunctions.
The paper reports a complete correspondence from the named subfunctions to structural parts in the fruit-picker function tree.
The redesign illustration replaces cutting with the sibling function detaching and then seeks a compatible physical structure.
The evidence demonstrates feasibility and explanatory value, but not extraction accuracy, ontology coverage, or redesign effectiveness.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

The authors do not state a formal limitations section.
Much of the extraction and “deep reasoning” appears manual; no reproducible parser, annotation protocol, or implementation is specified.
One patent cannot establish portability across domains, languages, drafting styles, or non-mechanical inventions.
The model conflates extracted textual evidence with inferred effect/function knowledge unless provenance is added externally.
The hierarchy is design-oriented and does not represent legal events, claim scope, citations, applicants, or prosecution history.
No uncertainty, disagreement, alternative decomposition, or temporal versioning is modeled.
Figures carry central ontology detail, and the HTML-to-text path omitted their internal labels, limiting verification of the complete topology.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology): encode the normalized function vocabulary as SKOS concepts with broader/narrower and synonym mappings, but reject treating an informal tree as a sufficient ontology.
- epistemic slice: represent extracted component spans, inferred effects, and inferred functions as separate claims with evidence spans and confidence; retain competing decompositions rather than overwriting them.
- patent tooling (agentic-cad-patent-tooling): adopt the function-to-effect-to-structure chain as a CAD ideation index and enable sibling-function substitution for redesign prompts.
- patent tooling (uspto-patent-driver-depth): extract from section-aware patent units—title, abstract, claims, and description—while recording which section supports each structure or function assertion.
- OIP solo-practice workflows: use function-structure views as technical-intake aids, not as substitutes for claim construction, novelty, or infringement analysis.
