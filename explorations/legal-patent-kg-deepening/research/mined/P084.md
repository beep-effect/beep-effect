# Towards Automated Patent Workflows: AI-Orchestrated Multi-Agent Framework for Intellectual Property Management and Analysis

## Problem & positioning

The paper responds to the growth and technical/legal complexity of patent applications with PatExpert, an autonomous conversational multi-agent framework.
It targets patent classification, acceptance prediction, abstractive summarization, claim generation, multi-patent question answering, and scientific-hypothesis generation.
The positioning is task-specialization plus orchestration: a large meta-agent plans and routes work to smaller fine-tuned expert agents instead of relying on one general model.
For multi-patent analysis, Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation (GRAG) supplies structured context; a conversational database preserves dialogue history.
The paper emphasizes explainability, iterative critique, citations, and fact checking, but evaluates mostly output similarity and model-judged quality rather than legal correctness.
Its empirical corpus is the Harvard USPTO Patent Dataset (HUPD), which contains more than 4.5 million applications overall; experiments use only the stated 2015–2017 subset.

## Approach / architecture

Meta-Llama-3.1-405B interprets the query, decomposes it into subtasks, selects tools, extracts parameters, invokes experts, and synthesizes a final response.
Fine-tuned GPT-4o-mini expert models implement each patent task; their protocols declare usage metadata, argument requirements, and input/output schemas.
The four orchestration stages are task planning, expert selection, expert invocation, and response generation.
Selection may use cosine similarity between a subtask and tool documentation or LLM/few-shot selection.
A critique loop combines GPT-4o as Gold-LLM judge and Nemotron-4-340B-Reward as reward judge, scoring accuracy/relevance and returning iterative feedback.
The model-input sections are task-specific: claims and descriptive sections feed classification/acceptance, broader disclosure feeds summaries and claims, and multiple full patent sections feed comparative analysis.
Multi-patent experts are trained with synthetic Retrieval-Augmented Fine-Tuning data comprising question-context-answer triples and chain-of-thought-style answers.
A two-layer Mixture-of-Agents pipeline uses Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct and Nemotron-4-340B-Instruct as proposers and GPT-4 Turbo as aggregator.
The resulting synthetic corpus distills into GPT-4o-mini; at inference the expert calls the KG search engine for context.
Hypothesis generation extracts Subject–Action–Object triples from claims and detailed descriptions and synthesizes them into a conceptual invention hypothesis.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

Patent documents are split with a fixed-length, fixed-stride sliding window; each chunk stores content, title, summary, keywords, and an embedding.
Chunk nodes `VT` retain coarse-grained source context, while a Gold LLM extracts fine-grained subject–relation–object triples into entity nodes `VE` and relation edges.
Each entity is connected back to its source chunk with an explicit `MENTIONS` edge.
The described dynamic ontology consists of extracted entity node types, arbitrary extracted relation types, and mention links; it is bottom-up rather than aligned to a fixed patent ontology.
At query time, the user question is embedded, top-K entity nodes are selected by cosine similarity, and one-hop triples are traversed.
The retriever returns each selected triple together with chunks mentioning both endpoints or either endpoint, retaining a route back to text context.
Entity deduplication combines embedding cosine similarity and Levenshtein string distance, then clusters and merges variants while preserving properties.
Cross-document integration merges multiple patent graphs and uses LLM-driven ontology extraction and schema alignment to create a unified graph.
The graph is presented as supporting taxonomy creation, ontology expansion, trend/gap discovery, semantic search, and grounded multi-patent answers.
KG quality is scored with triple accuracy, modularity, conductance, and graph completeness.
The best reported GPT-4o construction has 95% triple accuracy, 0.85 modularity, 0.15 conductance, and 0.98 completeness against an LLM-generated reference.
The model lacks patent-specific entities such as applications, publications, claims, dependencies, inventors, assignees, classifications, citations, priority chains, prosecution events, and evidence spans.

## Evaluation & results

The 2015–2017 HUPD subset is split 80/10/10 for train, validation, and unseen test data; the number of records in the subset is not stated.
Reported fine-tuning settings are learning rate `1e-5`, batch size 32, and 100 epochs.
PatExpert scores 0.94 tool-usage awareness, 0.92 pass rate, 0.91 planning accuracy, and 0.95 dependency-graph consistency.
Tool selection scores are 0.95 Recall@K, 0.93 NDCG@K, and 0.91 completeness; K and query-set size are not reported.
Tool calling scores are 0.95 parameter consistency, 0.04 error rate, and 0.96 execution accuracy.
Classification reaches 0.90 exact match and 0.95 F1; acceptance reaches 0.91 exact match and 0.96 F1.
Abstractive summarization reaches 0.85 BLEU and 0.83 ROUGE-L.
Claim generation reaches 0.88 BLEU and 0.86 ROUGE-L; multi-patent analysis reaches 0.90 and 0.87.
The user-centric multi-patent evaluation reports 4.8/5 satisfaction, task completion “Yes,” 4.6 context awareness, 4.5 adaptability, and 4.4 error handling.
No participant count, prompt set, K value, statistical significance, confidence intervals, compute budget, cost, latency, or independent legal-expert validation is given.
Baseline closed models are used frozen while PatExpert's experts are fine-tuned, so the comparisons conflate architecture with task-specific training.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

The authors identify multilingual patent processing and translation as future work but provide little explicit limitations discussion.
Several “ground truths” are outputs from Gold LLMs, including multi-patent analyses, hypotheses, and the Claude 3.5 Sonnet baseline KG; this risks circular evaluation.
BLEU and ROUGE-L measure overlap, not claim validity, antecedent basis, enablement, novelty, non-obviousness, claim scope, or legal drafting quality.
The dataset slice size, sampling rules, prompt/test inventory, fine-tuning corpus sizes, and judge calibration are absent, preventing replication.
The claim-generation task uses granted claims as reference, but the paper does not establish that generated claims preserve disclosure support or avoid unlawful scope changes.
The bottom-up dynamic ontology lacks domain/range constraints, cardinalities, claim dependency semantics, authority control, temporal prosecution state, and source-span-level provenance.
One-hop traversal can miss multi-step novelty, family, citation, ownership, and claim-overlap paths; similarity-selected top-K nodes offer no completeness guarantee.
Deduplication by semantic/string similarity can incorrectly merge legally distinct assignees, inventors, claim terms, or embodiments.
The critique loop is still model-on-model evaluation and does not replace patent-attorney review, examiner-grounded outcomes, deterministic validation, or primary-record audit.
The paper repeatedly claims improved compliance and reduced manual effort without reporting compliance or labor-time measurements.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- `patent tooling / agentic-cad-patent-tooling`: take the typed expert protocol, dependency-aware plan, and critique loop; require deterministic gates for disclosure support, claim dependency, terminology consistency, and prohibited new matter before accepting generated claims.
- `patent tooling / uspto-patent-driver-depth`: replace the dynamic one-hop ontology with first-class application/publication/family/claim/classification/citation/party/prosecution-event schemas and exact source spans from USPTO records.
- `semantic-foundation / SKOS and @beep/ontology`: reuse extracted relations only as candidate concepts for curated SKOS mapping; reject LLM-emergent relation labels as the authoritative ontology without identity, domain/range, and provenance checks.
- `epistemic slice`: preserve `MENTIONS` provenance but deepen it to page/claim/paragraph spans, separate extracted assertions from verified claims, and bitemporally model filing, publication, assignment, examination, and claim-status events.
- `OIP solo-practice workflows`: route classification, prior-art comparison, summary, and draft-claim tasks through specialist services, while making attorney approval and a source-linked audit packet mandatory at every legal conclusion.
- `agent memory / Cognee`: take the conversational-history plus KG split, but record agent plans, tool calls, critiques, and accepted/rejected hypotheses as provenance-bearing memory rather than feeding all prior text back as undifferentiated context.
