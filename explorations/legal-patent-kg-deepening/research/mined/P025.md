# Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Legal Data Mining

## Problem & positioning

Legal corpora are large, jurisdiction-specific, and predominantly unstructured, so even practitioners
struggle to locate, digest, and compare relevant material. The chapter is a technical survey rather than
a new system paper. It positions legal knowledge bases and ontologies as an organizing layer for
practitioner access and downstream automation, then surveys two downstream tasks: sentence-level
rhetorical-role classification and legal-document summarization.

The historical frame runs from US e-discovery and the TREC Legal Track to Indian FIRE testbeds for
prior-case retrieval, statute retrieval, and summarization. Its main claim is that domain structure matters:
generic NLP can help, but legal labels, jurisdictional conventions, expert annotation, and changing law
must remain explicit. The chapter closes by emphasizing fairness, transparency, and explainability rather
than treating predictive performance as sufficient.

## Approach / architecture

The chapter presents a layered pipeline:

1. organize legal material with a core or domain ontology expressed through Semantic Web formalisms;
2. annotate sentences with rhetorical functions such as facts, issues, arguments, ratio, rulings,
   precedents, and statutes;
3. use those labels plus sentence position and salience to build balanced extractive summaries; and
4. expose provenance, jurisdiction, temporality, and interpretive metadata for reliable use.

For ontologies, it contrasts foundational/core models (FOLaw and LRI-Core) with practice-oriented
artifacts such as LegalRuleML and an Indian intellectual-property case graph. For rhetorical roles, it
reviews CRF, hierarchical BiLSTM, and hierarchical BiLSTM-CRF architectures. For summarization, it
organizes methods on two axes—supervised/unsupervised and domain-specific/domain-independent—and
surveys LexRank, Luhn, LSA, Reduction, DSDR, SummaRunner, BERTSUM, LetSum, CaseSummarizer,
MMR, DELSUMM, KMM, and Chinese Gist.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- An ontology is treated as a controlled domain vocabulary plus semantics for permissible statements,
  commonly represented in OWL to support inference and reasoning.
- FOLaw separates six knowledge families: normative, meta-legal, world, responsibility, reactive, and
  creative knowledge. This prevents rules, conflict-resolution policy, facts, attribution of liability,
  sanctions, and legal-entity creation from collapsing into one undifferentiated graph.
- LRI-Core partitions its top level into physical, mental, abstract, role, and occurrence worlds. It
  distinguishes a role from its bearer and stresses that agents act while roles do not.
- LegalRuleML supports multiple interpretations of a rule, creator provenance, many-to-many links
  between rules and textual provisions, temporal/status/jurisdiction metadata, and references to external
  ontologies without binding itself to one ontology or logic.
- The surveyed IPR case graph uses cases/documents as nodes; citations and similarity as relation types;
  manually curated “lawpoints” as domain features; and R-GCN label prediction. Fourteen main features
  and five relationships are visible in the reported ontology figure.
- Eight rhetorical roles provide an evidence-oriented document ontology: Fact, Argument, Issue, Ratio,
  Lower-Court Ruling, Present-Court Ruling, Precedent, and Statute. Their sequential dependence is why
  CRF-style decoding is useful.
- DELSUMM encodes a legal priority order: Final Judgment > Issue > Facts > Statute/Precedent/Ratio >
  Argument, with rules for role-specific sentence position and citation cues.

## Evaluation & results

This is mostly a survey, so it reports results from cited systems rather than a unified evaluation. For the
IPR legal graph, reported AUC is 0.620 for citation links and 0.556 for similarity links; removing
“lawpoints” lowers the score by almost 0.04. The chapter reports that deep rhetorical-role models perform
better than handcrafted-feature approaches, but gives no score in its own text.

For LetSum, the fixed summary allocation is 10% introduction, 25% context, 60% judicial analysis, and
5% conclusion. The average Indian Supreme Court case length is stated as about 4,500 words. DELSUMM
is said to outperform many alternatives on whole-document and role-wise ROUGE, but no numeric ROUGE
values are supplied here. No new dataset, ablation, or human evaluation is conducted by the chapter.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- Ontology construction can merge concepts poorly; legal logic is incomplete and must accommodate
  novelty, while legal meaning changes with time, politics, culture, and jurisdiction.
- Pre-computer legal material may contain OCR errors and omissions; input clarity directly constrains
  ontology quality.
- Expert-created features and gold rhetorical labels are costly. Deep models reduce feature engineering
  but introduce interpretability problems.
- Domain-independent summaries can overrepresent document position or generic salience and omit
  legally decisive roles; domain-specific systems may not transfer cleanly across jurisdictions.
- The survey sometimes blurs ontology, knowledge graph, and learned graph predictor, and several broad
  performance claims lack comparable datasets or exact metrics.
- The IPR graph's modest AUC and manual lawpoints warn against treating similarity or citations as
  semantic equivalence, precedential force, or legal correctness.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** keep a small core vocabulary separate from
  jurisdiction/task extensions; represent preferred labels, overloaded terms, and mappings without
  pretending one global sense is sufficient.
- **epistemic slice:** adopt LegalRuleML's separate provenance, interpretation, jurisdiction, assertion,
  efficacy, and enforcement dimensions; attach claims to exact provision/sentence spans and preserve
  competing annotations rather than overwriting them.
- **patent tooling (uspto-patent-driver-depth, agentic-cad-patent-tooling):** adapt rhetorical roles into
  patent evidence roles—claim, specification support, cited reference, examiner reasoning, applicant
  argument, and disposition—and use sequence-aware labeling only as a proposal awaiting validation.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** generate role-balanced matter summaries that always surface issues,
  governing authority, holdings, and procedural posture; reject a generic salience-only summary as the
  attorney-facing artifact.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** store document nodes, citation edges, similarity edges, and human-curated
  topic tags as distinct relation/evidence families; never infer authority or truth from embedding
  similarity alone.
