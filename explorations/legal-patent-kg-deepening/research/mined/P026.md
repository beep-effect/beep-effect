# Tutorial: Conceptual Simulation Modeling with Onto-UML

## Problem & positioning

Conceptual modeling is widely acknowledged as important in simulation engineering but lacks shared
standards for procedure, notation, and model quality. The paper argues that modeling and simulation
should reuse the mature distinction from model-driven software engineering between domain models,
platform-independent designs, and platform-specific implementations.

Its key corrective is ontological: a conceptual model describes the real-world domain independently of
computational choices, whereas a design model describes what the system will represent and how. The
tutorial introduces Onto-UML, grounded in UFO and the discrete-event-system ontology DESO, and a
preliminary Onto-BPMN variant to model information structure and process dynamics together.

## Approach / architecture

The proposed model-driven simulation chain is:

1. create a solution-independent conceptual system model;
2. derive a platform-independent simulation design model with human judgment;
3. transform it into one or more platform-specific simulation models; and
4. implement, test, and deploy on the chosen simulation platform.

The conceptual layer has two coordinated views. Onto-UML models object types, event types,
relationships, attributes, and causal laws. Onto-BPMN models dynamic succession, treating each causal
law as an event subprocess triggered in a parallel thread. The authors explicitly reject automatic
domain-to-code transformation: a truthful domain model does not contain the design decisions needed
for a computational specification.

Two examples refine initially naïve models. A service queue gains kinds, roles, event participants, and
arrival/departure causal laws before receiving a process view. A drive-through model distinguishes the
organization, restaurant, people, employee roles, customer role, orders, and meals instead of relying on
an ad hoc “logic flow.”

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- DESO distinguishes individuals from types and divides individuals into objects, trope individuals
  (dependent qualities/relationships), and events.
- A discrete event system consists of objects whose states can change and events occurring at discrete
  times. Each event type identifies participant object types; each causal law has one triggering event
  type and zero or more resulting event types.
- The DESO metamodel includes `EntityType`, `ObjectType`, `PhysicalObjectType`, `EventType`,
  `RelationshipType`, `ReferenceProperty`, `Attribute`, `Datatype`, and `CausalLaw`, with explicit
  domain/range, participant, trigger, and result relations.
- Sortal types supply identity criteria; mixins do not. Mixins cannot have direct instances and cannot be
  subtypes of sortals.
- A kind is a top rigid sortal, and every object must instantiate exactly one kind. Subkinds are rigid;
  phase types and role types are anti-rigid. A phase depends on intrinsic attributes, while a role depends
  on a relationship to another participant type.
- Onto-UML turns these axioms into stereotypes and constraints: kind, subkind, role, phase, mixin,
  role mixin, physical-object type, event type, and causal law.
- Conceptual truth and recorded knowledge are deliberately separated. A person has exactly one mother
  and father in the illustrative domain model, while the design model permits zero-or-one because the
  information may be unavailable.
- The authors advocate modeling types even for a particular organization, preserving reusable
  intensional structure rather than encoding one collection of instances as the conceptual model.

## Evaluation & results

The paper is a tutorial with worked examples, not a controlled empirical evaluation. It claims benefits
to comprehensibility, maintainability, interoperability, and evolvability from clarified real-world
semantics, but reports no measured effect sizes.

The service-queue example demonstrates a qualitative correction: adding the kind `Person` above the
roles `PrivateCustomer` and `ServiceClerk` permits one person to occupy both roles. It then identifies
three event types—customer arrival, service start, and customer departure—and represents causal links
between arrival/departure events. The drive-through example supplies a second structural illustration.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- Onto-BPMN is explicitly under development and had not been presented previously; the manuscript
  even retains a missing-reference placeholder for a BPMN introduction.
- The tutorial omits UFO/DESO theories of attribution, relationships, parthood, causality, and agency
  for lack of space, despite their relevance.
- No empirical comparison tests whether Onto-UML users produce more accurate simulation models than
  users of UML/BPMN or domain-specific notations.
- The examples are small and deterministic at the conceptual level; random variables, uncertainty,
  concurrency conflicts, observation error, and provenance are not modeled in depth.
- The “exactly one kind” axiom and possible-world rigidity commitments are powerful modeling choices
  that should not be imported into a lightweight vocabulary layer without an explicit semantic need.
- The paper addresses conceptual and simulation models, not RDF/OWL serialization or operational KG
  query performance; translation to graph storage requires additional design decisions.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** borrow the kind/subkind/role/phase distinctions as
  optional modeling profiles, but reject forcing heavyweight UFO modal axioms into ordinary SKOS
  concept schemes.
- **epistemic slice:** directly adopt the domain-truth versus information-availability split; model a
  proposition's domain cardinality separately from whether a source observes or records its filler.
- **epistemic slice:** represent events with typed participants and causal-law claims, but attach evidence
  and confidence to the asserted causal edge rather than treating every modeled transition as fact.
- **patent tooling (agentic-cad-patent-tooling):** separate an invention-domain model from CAD/tool
  implementation models so a patent claim graph does not accidentally encode one software platform's
  representation choices as limitations of the invention.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** preserve a three-stage boundary—domain conceptualization, retrieval/index
  design, storage implementation—to keep vector-store availability or missing fields from changing the
  ontology's asserted real-world semantics.
