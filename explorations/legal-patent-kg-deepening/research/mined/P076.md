# Semantic Integration of Goal and Business Process Modeling

## Problem & positioning

- Business goals explain why an enterprise acts, while business-process models explain how work is organized; the two perspectives are interdependent but poorly integrated.
- ARIS offers mature Event-driven Process Chain (EPC) modeling but only a thin account of objectives and their relationships.
- Tropos offers actors, hardgoals, softgoals, plans, resources, dependencies, decompositions, and contributions, but little detailed process modeling.
- Directly matching similarly named metaclasses risks false equivalence because each language packages different real-world commitments.
- The paper treats semantic integration as a prerequisite to an eventual syntax-level Tropos+ARIS metamodel.
- Its contribution is an ontological interpretation and correspondence analysis, not an executable integration or empirical process-improvement study.

## Approach / architecture

- The authors extract relevant Ecore metamodel fragments for the Tropos Actor and Goal diagrams and the ARIS organizational, objective, and EPC views.
- Both languages are interpreted through the Unified Foundational Ontology (UFO), creating a language-neutral semantic pivot.
- UFO separates universals from individuals, endurants from events, agents from objects, and substantials from dependent moments.
- Intentionality is modeled through an Agent bearing an Intention whose propositional content is a Goal.
- An Action is an intentional event instantiating an Action Universal, performed to satisfy an agent's goal; a business process is a Complex Action Universal.
- Institutional Agents and their Normative Descriptions provide the grounding for organizational units, social roles, policies, and other social objects.
- The analysis first interprets each language independently, then tabulates correspondences and semantic gaps between them.
- Tropos supplies the richer goal-side relations where ARIS's `supports` and `belongs to` relations conflate several meanings.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- Tropos `Agent` maps to UFO Agent; ARIS `Person` maps to Agent, while `Organization Unit` and some `Group` instances map to Institutional Agent.
- Tropos `Role` and `Position`, plus ARIS `Position` and `Person Type`, map to Social Role; `Person Type` may instead be a Social Role Mixin or Social Mixin.
- An ARIS `Group` may be a Collective Social Agent when all members play the same role, or an Institutional Agent when members play differentiated roles.
- Tropos `Plan` and ARIS `Function` map to Action Universal; an EPC as a whole maps to Complex Action Universal.
- Tropos `Resource` and ARIS `Product/Service` map to Non-Agentive Substantial participating in an action.
- A Goal is not a free-floating label: it is the propositional content of an Intention borne by a particular Agent.
- A hardgoal has satisfaction situations necessarily shared by all rational agents.
- A softgoal permits rational agents to differ over which situations satisfy it; satisfaction is therefore an Agent-Goal-Situation relation grounded in the agent's belief.
- Collective agreement about a softgoal's satisfaction criteria can be captured by a Normative Description, yielding degrees of “softness” by breadth of agreement.
- A Plan is a means to a Goal for Agent A when executions of the plan produce a post-situation that A believes satisfies the goal.
- Resource-to-Goal means are agent-relative, but Resource-to-Plan participation is structural and agent-independent.
- AND decomposition denotes propositional equivalence between the parent goal and the conjunction of child goals; OR decomposition uses disjunction.
- Contribution to a softgoal is explicitly agent-indexed and positive or negative, rather than an objective binary edge.
- Tropos `Dependency` overloads two relations: an awareness-independent formal dependency and a social Delegation grounded in a commitment from dependee to depender.
- ARIS `Objective` cannot simply equal UFO Goal because ARIS does not directly identify the intentional agent that owns it.
- A viable repair links the objective to the institutional process owner or to a social role whose definition contains the relevant behavior.
- ARIS `belongs to` fails to distinguish conjunction, disjunction, or contribution; `supports` collapses plan-goal, resource-goal, and contribution relations.

## Evaluation & results

- The result is a correspondence table covering 18 Tropos constructs or relation variants against UFO meanings and ARIS counterparts or gaps.
- The table identifies no ARIS counterpart for dependency/delegation or for negative contribution relations.
- Positive plan-to-softgoal and resource-to-softgoal contributions are only partially contained in ARIS `supports`.
- AND, OR, and positive goal contribution are all only partially represented by ARIS `belongs to`.
- The semantic analysis exposes a significant mismatch between ARIS Objective and Tropos Goal despite their superficial similarity.
- It also exposes construct overload in Tropos Dependency and multiple overloads in ARIS goal relations.
- The authors mention a hospital case in which Tropos and ARIS were used together, but provide no case design, measurements, or outcomes here.
- No implemented metamodel, reasoner, query set, user study, precision/recall measure, or interoperability benchmark is reported.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The integrated abstract syntax, modeling guidelines, and tool support are explicitly future work.
- Goal conflicts are not yet given a precise ontological account.
- The paper leaves belief-content semantics and a fuller interpretation of contribution outside scope.
- ARIS documentation is too sparse to fully interpret `Product/Service`, `belongs to`, or the process owner's relation to an Objective.
- The proposed hardgoal definition assumes agreement by all rational agents, a demanding criterion that is difficult to operationalize or observe.
- Propositional AND/OR equivalence may be too strong for practical goal refinement where subgoals are sufficient strategies rather than truth-condition decompositions.
- The analysis gives conceptual mappings but no machine-readable axioms, validation constraints, or inference rules.
- Change-impact analysis between goals and processes is promised as a benefit but not demonstrated.
- The treatment lacks time, provenance, evidentiary uncertainty, disagreement resolution, and versioned organizational commitments.
- Its enterprise-modeling examples do not establish transfer to legal or patent workflows without domain-specific roles and normative relations.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** take UFO as an alignment layer that distinguishes concepts with similar labels; represent ARIS/Tropos terms as mapped concept schemes rather than collapsing them into one vocabulary.
- **epistemic slice:** adopt the Agent-Goal-Situation satisfaction claim so subjective success criteria retain claimant, belief, context, evidence span, and valid time instead of becoming global truth.
- **epistemic slice:** split formal dependency from commitment-backed delegation, then represent the latter as a bitemporal social relation with explicit bearer, counterparty, content, and provenance.
- **patent tooling (agentic-cad-patent-tooling):** connect an inventor's claim-drafting goal to candidate plans, resources, and generated artifacts; preserve alternative OR plans and stakeholder-indexed contribution judgments.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** model a matter goal separately from the process steps intended to achieve it, enabling impact analysis when client objectives, deadlines, or available evidence change.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** retain who judged that a plan satisfied or harmed a goal; reject unqualified `supports` edges that erase the judging agent and rationale.
