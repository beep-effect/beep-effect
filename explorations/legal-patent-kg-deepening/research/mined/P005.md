# A Comprehensive Framework for Legal Dispute Analysis Integrating Prompt Engineering and Multi-Dimensional Knowledge Graphs

## Problem & positioning

The paper targets legal-dispute analysis where an assistant must identify issues, retrieve controlling norms, reason over facts and evidence, and cite authoritative material.
It attributes weak LLM performance to concept ambiguity, inconsistent multi-step reasoning, hallucinated citations, jurisdictional variation, and fast-changing law.
The proposed remedy leaves model parameters frozen and combines selective KG retrieval with structured prompt construction.
Its premise is that loading a small set of relevant legal concepts is more efficient and controllable than loading an entire knowledge base.
The contribution is a dual-core framework: a three-stage prompt hierarchy guides reasoning while a three-layer legal KG supplies concepts, norms, and examples.
The scope spans case retrieval, judgment prediction, legal entailment, and legal question answering across multiple legal systems and 13 domains.

## Approach / architecture

User queries first pass through task matching based on legal domain, question nature, implicated provisions, and other features.
An improved BM25F-style score maps the query to a professional task template; reported dimension weights are 0.35, 0.28, 0.22, and 0.15.
The prompt hierarchy contains task definition, legal knowledge background, and reasoning guidance such as IRAC-like paths.
Knowledge-background relevance fuses text relevance, KG association, case-law weight, and jurisdictional relevance with optimized weights 0.30, 0.35, 0.25, and 0.10.
All component scores use training-set 5th/95th-percentile truncation and scaling to `[0,1]` before fusion.
Prompt quality is scored across legal accuracy, logical completeness, argumentation rigor, expression professionalism, and citation standardization.
An assessment-adjustment-fusion-reassessment loop revises the prompt for at most three iterations, with thresholds of 0.85 for high-risk tasks and 0.75 for general consultation.
Retrieval first narrows the graph to 500 candidates using semantic vectors and terminology matching.
The second stage runs code matching, legal-vector similarity, ontology-path reasoning, and professional-terminology matching in parallel.
Adaptive strategy weights respond to query features, after which an entropy-driven greedy reranker selects a diverse top 12.
Path reasoning considers paths up to five hops, relation weights, attenuation `lambda = 0.841`, and expert-specified compatibility between adjacent relation types.
The framework couples the static graph to authoritative web sources through jurisdiction identification, authority scoring, temporal marking, and change tracking.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

The graph is formally presented as `G = (Lonto, Lrep, Linst, R)`.
`Lonto`, the classification ontology layer, contains legal branches, relationship types, and basic legal principles.
`Lrep`, the representation layer, contains provisions, judicial interpretations, regulations, and rules.
Representation nodes link to ontology concepts and carry effectiveness hierarchy, application scope, and temporal effectiveness.
`Linst`, the instance layer, contains judicial cases, consultations, and application scenarios linked to relevant norms and concepts.
Instance nodes record how norms were applied and the resulting adjudication outcome.
The relation family includes at least `is-a`, `part-of`, `regulates`, and `cites`, across and within layers.
Direct code matching separates exact from longest-common-subsequence partial matches, weighting exact matches at 0.8.
Semantic scoring combines legal-model cosine similarity, non-negative TF-IDF enhancement, and ontology-domain distance attenuation.
Path scoring avoids treating every graph path as equally probative: relation weights and a compatibility matrix penalize incoherent relation sequences.
Terminology scoring combines exact, stem, and semantic matches at 0.6/0.2/0.2, an inverse legal-terminology weight, jurisdictional consistency, and local PMI context.
Diversity reranking measures semantic similarity, domain-label overlap, and one-hop-neighborhood overlap to avoid returning redundant concepts.
Temporal design is explicit but not fully bitemporal: concepts carry effective time, ineffective time, and revision history, while changes update the graph.
The graph has authority and jurisdiction hooks, but the paper does not report a concrete node/edge schema, identifiers, cardinalities, graph size, evidence-span model, or public serialization.

## Evaluation & results

The test set comprises 500 Q&A pairs stratified from COLIEE 2024, LegalBench, LeCaRDv2, LexGLUE, ECHR, and JEC-QA.
Sampling allocates 35% to common-law, 45% to civil-law, and 20% to mixed/international systems; tasks are 30% retrieval, 25% prediction, 25% entailment, and 20% Q&A.
Complexity is 30% basic, 50% intermediate, and 20% advanced, assessed by two law professors.
Ten legal professionals participate in annotation or assessment: five lawyers, three professors, and two senior judges; each sample is annotated by at least three experts.
Four frozen models are tested: DeepSeek-R1-70B, Qwen3-Next-80B, Llama 4 Scout-109B, and gpt-oss-120b.
Each configuration runs three times with seeds 42, 2024, and 2025 on two A100 80GB GPUs.
Across models, F1 rises from `0.356 +/- 0.011` to `0.714 +/- 0.043`; exact match rises from `0.011 +/- 0.010` to `0.446 +/- 0.054`.
Average BLEU-4 rises from 0.134 to 0.451, and ROUGE-L F1 rises from 0.334 to 0.711.
The Qwen model's intellectual-property subset reaches 0.844 exact match and 0.829 F1, versus 0.410 and 0.582 at baseline.
Expert review of 30 cases raises citation accuracy from 56.25 to 75.25, reasoning soundness from 60.75 to 80.50, and conclusion reliability from 58.75 to 77.00 on a 100-point scale.
Average over-specification errors fall from 72% to 16%, but conceptual errors increase from 4% to 8%.
Inference averages 2.3 seconds per query at baseline and 3.8 seconds with the complete framework.
Calibration is model-dependent: Llama ECE improves from 0.3052 to 0.1756, while gpt-oss ECE worsens from 0.3107 to 0.3749 and MCE from 0.3166 to 0.9546.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

The authors identify cross-lingual analysis, multimodal evidence, and more explainable reasoning as future work.
The framework is evaluated on a newly integrated 500-item sample, but the exact item list, generated answers, graph snapshot, templates, and implementation artifact are not supplied in the extracted paper.
The construction, population, and quality validation of the three-layer KG are under-specified; no graph-scale, coverage, provenance, or retrieval-gold metrics are reported.
Web-search authority scoring and “real-time” change tracking are described architecturally without a source policy, latency/freshness evaluation, or demonstrated update experiment.
BLEU and ROUGE reward overlap and cannot establish that legal propositions, citations, holdings, or jurisdiction choices are correct.
Some ablations do not behave monotonically: for Qwen, removing path reasoning yields 0.7517 F1 versus 0.7479 for the complete system.
Calibration sometimes deteriorates sharply, making the complete system unsafe to treat as uniformly more self-aware.
The temporal fields capture legal validity intervals but do not distinguish source-valid time from ingestion/correction time or preserve superseded assertions as bitemporal history.
The relation compatibility matrix depends on expert judgments, yet its elicitation, completeness, and portability across jurisdictions are not validated.
The test set mixes datasets, task formats, languages, and jurisdictions; mapping heterogeneous references into exact-match and F1 targets may introduce construction artifacts.
Expert ratings improve, but only 30 cases receive the deep 100-point assessment and logical-rigor agreement is the weakest reported dimension (`kappa = 0.58`).
No practicing workflow study measures research time, missed authority, attorney correction effort, or downstream client outcomes.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- `epistemic slice`: take the effective/ineffective/revision fields but implement true bitemporal claim edges, source spans, jurisdiction, authority, and supersession; preserve retracted law rather than overwriting it during change tracking.
- `semantic-foundation / SKOS and @beep/ontology`: adopt the ontology/representation/instance separation and typed relation paths; reject opaque expert compatibility numbers unless encoded as reviewable, versioned policy assertions.
- `OIP solo-practice workflows`: use task-definition -> authority retrieval -> IRAC guidance as a reviewable workflow, with attorney gates and citation-to-primary-source verification before advice leaves the system.
- `patent tooling / uspto-patent-driver-depth`: reuse exact code/identifier matching before vector retrieval and domain-diverse reranking for statutes, patent records, classifications, and cases; retain deterministic identifier hits even when semantic rank is low.
- `patent tooling / agentic-cad-patent-tooling`: take the bounded three-iteration critique loop for complex patent analysis, but score disclosure support, claim-element coverage, and evidence-span fidelity rather than generic prose overlap.
- `agent memory / Cognee`: store task classification, retrieved nodes, chosen paths, temporal snapshot, and rejected alternatives as provenance-bearing memory so later agents can audit why an authority entered the answer.
