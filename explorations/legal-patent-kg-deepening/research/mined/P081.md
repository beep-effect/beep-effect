# The Ontology of Legal Facts

## Problem & positioning

- Visually identical physical events can differ completely in legal effect: a president's valid signature may enact law, while an actor's staged signature does nothing legally.
- The paper asks what kind of facts legal facts are, while deliberately bracketing the larger question of what grounds legal validity.
- It accepts Kelsen's insights that distinct legal facts exist and concern meanings objectively ascribed to actions and events.
- It rejects Kelsen's inference that facts about prescriptive legal norms must themselves be normative facts requiring normative grounding.
- The positive account grounds socially constructed meanings in individual conduct, attitudes, dispositions, and shared intentions.
- A final analogy with fiction explains why legal propositions require jurisdiction-and-time prefixes and why genuine legal gaps are common.
- This is a draft philosophical argument dated November 2025, not a computational ontology or implemented KG.

## Approach / architecture

- The paper begins with paired enactment/staging hypotheticals to isolate legal meaning from physical event structure.
- It surveys the polysemy of `fact`, recommending that each use be understood through its contrast: fact/fiction, fact/error, or fact/no fact of the matter.
- It reconstructs Kelsen's argument as five steps from legal meanings through normative grounding to the presupposed Basic Norm.
- It rejects the move from normative content to normative fact and develops a bottom-up analogy with semantic rules in natural language.
- Signification is represented schematically as X signifying Y in context C, sustained by regular use within a population and a system of related rules.
- Collective intention is analyzed through individual We-intentions, group awareness, and shared knowledge rather than an irreducible institutional entity.
- The account then distinguishes closed prefixes, such as fiction and law, from open prefixes that permit broader worldly premises.
- Legal truth is indexed to “system S at time t,” making context part of the truth conditions rather than ancillary metadata.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- Legal facts are facts about meanings assigned to action and event types and tokens, not intrinsic properties of physical occurrences.
- Legal meaning is socially objective to the extent that a population converges on legally significant attitudes and dispositions.
- Facts about norms are distinct from normative facts; prescriptive propositional content does not determine the ontological kind of its ground.
- Nonnatural rules are socially constructed and necessarily tied to a population, place, and time; natural or instrumental rules need not be.
- Semantic and legal rules are systemic: no signifying or legal rule should be modeled as context-free and isolated from a network of related rules.
- Type-token classification is a prerequisite for applying rules to particular utterances, actions, or events.
- A collective intention exists when three conditions hold: members We-intend the attitude as a group attitude; they have some sense of group identity; and they commonly know that others have similar We-intentions.
- These conditions prevent arbitrary “gerrymandering” of a population merely because individuals happen to share similar attitudes.
- Collective intentions tolerate variable instantiation: individual attitude tokens may differ while instantiating the same shared intention type.
- Individual We-intentions and related attitudes metaphysically ground the collective intention; motivating reasons may also participate without becoming normative grounds.
- The content of a nonnatural norm is determined by the grounding collective intention regardless of whether the norm is constitutive, hypothetical, categorical, or prescriptive.
- Legal norms can therefore be reductively grounded in patterns of conduct and shared attitudes without a presupposed normative Basic Norm.
- A legal proposition is incomplete unless prefixed by a jurisdiction/legal order and time.
- Law is a closed prefix: inference must remain within the world constructed by the relevant legal sources and allowable implications.
- Negative legal facts differ from legal gaps: “there is no rule requiring X” may be true, while “there is no fact of the matter whether X” marks indeterminacy.
- Some unstated propositions enter the legal context by implication, but importing unprefixed worldly premises can reproduce Lewis's invalid mixed-prefix inference.
- Legal facts can still count as worldly facts insofar as they participate in metaphysical grounding, even though they are mind-dependent social facts.

## Evaluation & results

- Evaluation consists of philosophical reconstruction and counterexample, not empirical or computational testing.
- The president/actor scenario shows that physical-event equivalence is insufficient for legal-fact equivalence.
- The Kelsen reconstruction isolates the disputed inference at step 2: from prescriptive content to the claim that legal facts are normative facts.
- The natural-language analogy supplies a reductive candidate ground for legal meaning through regular conduct and shared attitudes.
- Three explicit conditions are proposed for when an intention is genuinely shared by a group.
- The Sherlock Holmes/221B example demonstrates the invalidity of mixing a closed-prefix fictional premise with an unprefixed worldly premise.
- The same pattern is applied to law: truth-apt legal propositions require a legal system and time index.
- No formal axiomatization, dataset, annotation study, reasoner, quantitative result, or comparison against decided cases is provided.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The author repeatedly presents the collective-intention account as a sketch and leaves a full grounding theory to other work.
- The analysis deliberately does not settle what grounds legal validity or when laws give genuine reasons for action.
- Whether individual We-intentions are reducible to ordinary I-intentions is left open.
- The account assumes substantial convergence but offers no threshold for identifying the relevant population or a legally operative collective intention.
- Officials, courts, legislatures, subjects, and subcommunities may have competing attitudes; the three conditions do not resolve authority among them.
- “System S at t” is necessary but underspecified for conflicts of law, retroactivity, appeals, branching precedents, and changes known only later.
- Treating law as a closed prefix may understate legitimate incorporation of scientific, linguistic, commercial, or customary facts.
- The line between a genuine legal gap, epistemic uncertainty, contested interpretation, and future legal development remains operationally unresolved.
- The draft uses current political examples that illustrate indeterminacy but do not provide systematic case evidence.
- Translating metaphysical grounding into KG edges requires explicit provenance and confidence semantics absent from the paper.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** represent legal meanings as concepts scoped to a community and legal context; do not treat identical labels or physical event types as globally equivalent meanings.
- **epistemic slice:** make every legal claim jurisdiction-, legal-order-, valid-time-, and transaction-time-indexed, with evidence spans; model `no-rule` separately from `unknown` and `no-fact-of-the-matter`.
- **epistemic slice:** encode collective-intention support as revisable claims from identified populations rather than an anonymous consensus edge, allowing contradiction triage across officials, courts, and communities.
- **patent tooling (uspto-patent-driver-depth):** distinguish a filing event's physical occurrence from its legally recognized meaning, which depends on office rules, actor authority, procedural context, and effective time.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** warn when an answer imports unprefixed background facts into a jurisdiction-specific legal conclusion; require a source-backed bridge rule or label the inference insecure.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** store context prefixes and the population/source grounding a meaning so remembered propositions do not silently migrate across jurisdictions, times, or institutional contexts.
