# Legal Powers, Subjections, Disabilities, and Immunities: Ontological Analysis and Modeling Patterns

## Problem & positioning

- Dependable legal information systems need explicit models of dynamic legal relations rather than opaque labels for “power” or “permission.”
- Existing UFO-L patterns covered right–duty, permission–no-right, and liberty relations but not power–subjection or disability–immunity.
- Legal power differs from permission and practical ability: its exercise is an institutionally recognized act that changes legal positions.
- Disability is not merely missing permission; it is an explicit no-power whose correlative immunity protects another agent's legal positions.
- The paper extends the UFO-L legal core ontology with two reusable OntoUML patterns and demonstrates them in Brazilian municipal tax law.
- Its theoretical basis combines Hohfeld's correlatives, Alexy's triadic legal relations, and UFO's event/relator ontology.

## Approach / architecture

- The analysis distinguishes endurants, which maintain identity through change, from events, which unfold through temporal parts.
- Moments/aspects inhere in bearers; externally dependent moments also depend on another, distinct entity.
- A `Relator` bundles externally dependent moments and acts as the truthmaker of a material relation between its participants.
- UFO-L specializes relators into legal relators composed of paired legal positions.
- The authors analyze how founding legal events create relators and how institutional acts manifest powers to create, change, or extinguish later relators.
- Historical dependence records that a later event could not have occurred without an earlier one, stronger than precedence but weaker than causation.
- Three P7 variants cover creation, alteration, and extinction of derived legal relations.
- P8 models disability/immunity as a paired relational structure that defeats attempted exercises of power.
- Pattern applicability and competence conditions guide specialization in a concrete domain.
- A case study identifies relevant Brazilian constitutional, national-tax-code, and Vitória municipal-law provisions, then scopes material, temporal, territorial, quantitative, and subjective aspects.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- A simple legal relator is classified by its paired positions: `Right–Duty`, `NoRight–Permission`, `Power–Subjection`, or `Disability–Immunity`.
- `Legal Power` inheres in a `Power Holder` and externally depends on a `Subjection Holder`; the correlative `Legal Subjection` has the inverse dependencies.
- Alexy's triadic formulation makes explicit the power holder, subjection holder, and object: an institutional action that changes the latter's legal position.
- `Original Legal Power–Subjection Relations` arise from political constituent power; `Derived` relations arise through exercising another legal power.
- P7-PS-LR reifies the power/subjection relation as a relator with its own lifecycle rather than reducing it to a binary edge.
- Its derived material relations include “has the power to create legal positions against”; specific applications may likewise define change/extinction relations.
- A creation event manifests a power–subjection relator and creates a derived legal relator; that result may itself be another power–subjection relator, enabling recursion.
- Creation events and resulting relators inherit chains of historical dependence from the source powers and founding events.
- Constraints must ensure correlative moments are inseparable parts of the same relator and keep manifestation, creation, and historical-dependence cycles coherent.
- P8-DI-LR pairs `Disability` in a `Disability Holder` with `Immunity` in an `Immunity Holder`.
- An attempted institutional act under a disability is void in the legal sphere; this differs from violating a duty, which is illegal conduct potentially subject to sanction.
- The tax model separates municipality roles as power holder, creditor, right holder, and disability holder, and taxpayer roles as subjection holder, debtor, duty holder, or immunity holder.
- Constitution promulgation grounds municipal power to institute tax; local-law promulgation grounds power to levy IPTU; assessment creates credit–debt; notification creates a right–duty to pay.
- Constitutional immunity prevents taxation of religious entities; statutory exemption instead uses valid municipal power to create an exception and can be revoked.
- Keeping agents and regulated action types explicit avoids inconsistency that a monadic deontic representation can introduce.

## Evaluation & results

- Evaluation is an analytic case study, not a quantitative benchmark.
- P7-PS-LR and P8-DI-LR are instantiated against Brazilian constitutional, federal, and Vitória municipal tax provisions.
- The case reconstructs a multi-event lifecycle: constitutional grant, local tax law, property registration, annual assessment, taxpayer notification, and resulting payment duty.
- It shows that ownership on January 1 plus a valid municipal power enables assessment, while notification is required before the payment duty exists.
- The model distinguishes each property's annual credit–debt relator even when municipality and taxpayer are unchanged.
- It captures municipalities' power to tax ordinary property owners and their no-power against constitutionally immune religious entities.
- It also reveals that a former combatant's exemption is not municipal discretion: the law can give the taxpayer a right and impose a corresponding municipal duty to exempt.
- The triadic representation exposes parties and regulated acts that would disappear under a monadic `subject-to-tax` operator.
- No usability study, expert agreement, formal consistency run, query benchmark, runtime, or comparative error rate is reported.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The patterns are demonstrated in one Brazilian municipal-tax case, so cross-jurisdiction and cross-domain generality remains speculative.
- Empirical usability evaluation by legal experts is future work.
- The authors defer analysis of violations, finer power/no-power types, and systematic comparison with rights/duties and permissions/no-rights.
- Open questions remain about applying the relational pattern to non-legal power and intrinsically distinguishing original from derived powers.
- Several required integrity constraints are described in prose rather than supplied as an executable axiom or validation set.
- OntoUML conceptual precision does not automatically yield an RDF/OWL representation with the same modal and temporal semantics.
- Historical dependence is valuable provenance but does not itself encode legal validity intervals, retroactivity, repeal, appellate change, or transaction time.
- The case assumes selected legal interpretations, including the scope of religious-entity immunity, without modeling interpretive disagreement.
- Relator reification is semantically rich but can be expensive and complex for extraction, querying, and user interfaces.
- No evidence-span or citation model links each instantiated relation to the exact authoritative language that supports it.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** Adopt P7/P8 as named relation patterns with explicit holder roles and action objects; do not collapse power, permission, ability, prohibition, and disability into one deontic predicate family.
- **epistemic slice:** Translate relator lifecycles into bitemporal edges: founding event, manifestation event, created/changed/extinguished relation, validity interval, transaction history, and source-span-backed interpretive claim.
- **patent tooling — uspto-patent-driver-depth:** Model examiner authority, applicant response powers, assignment authority, and deadline effects as scoped powers over specific prosecution acts, with disabilities marking acts outside office or agent competence.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Derive workflow affordances from current role-plus-power relators; distinguish a void filing act from a prohibited or sanctionable act and show the authority/evidence chain to the practitioner.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** Use historical-dependence chains for causal provenance without mistaking them for causation; retain superseded relator states rather than destructively updating the latest legal position.
- **epistemic slice:** Represent immunity/exemption interpretations as contestable claims with jurisdiction and effective dates, rather than hard-coding one court interpretation as timeless ontology truth.
