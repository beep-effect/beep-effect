# Capturing Legal Reasoning Paths from Facts to Law in Court Judgments using Knowledge Graphs

## Problem & positioning

- Court judgments expose not just rules but the inferential path by which evidence and facts meet interpreted law.
- Existing legal ontologies tend to represent documents, citations, or static rules, omitting context-specific applications and intermediate reasoning.
- General LLMs can conflate jurisdictions, fail to reconstruct fact-to-law structure, and flatten layered or competing judicial perspectives.
- Ordinary RAG improves source grounding but retrieves unstructured passages rather than the reasoning alignment between facts, norms, and provisions.
- The paper reverses conventional provision-first legal search: a user begins with an observed fact and follows graph paths to relevant law.
- Its empirical domain is post-2008 Japanese district-court civil judgments in administrative litigation, using the decisions' reasoning sections.

## Approach / architecture

- A three-stage pipeline designs an RDF schema, extracts nodes and edges, and retrieves provisions through the resulting legal knowledge graph (LKG).
- HTML judgments from the NII legal dataset are segmented with DOM traversal, regexes, tag, punctuation, parenthetical, and length heuristics.
- Section boundaries and source provenance are retained; a case overview is supplied to extraction prompts for cross-reference resolution and legal framing.
- GPT-4o extracts sentence spans as Fact, Provision, LegalNorm, or LegalApplication; Claude Sonnet 3.5 was tested but judged unreliable.
- A fictional one-shot “Martian Law” example demonstrates hard cases without encouraging copying from real statutes.
- Provision mentions such as “the Act” and “Article 2” are resubmitted for normalization to canonical legal titles.
- Edge prompts separately construct Provision → LegalNorm, LegalNorm → LegalApplication, and Fact → LegalApplication relations.
- Provision-to-norm candidates are scoped locally within a section; application links receive all preceding facts or norms because dependencies can be distant.
- Each Fact sentence is embedded with text-embedding-3-large and indexed in Annoy; top-k similar facts contribute their linked provisions.
- Fact-Masked evaluation removes the query fact's outgoing edges to prevent trivial self-retrieval.
- Baselines are GPT-4o with the fact alone, with the case overview, and with retrieved full-section RAG context.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- Core classes are `Fact`, `LegalNorm`, `LegalApplication`, and `Provision`, with a common `LegalNode` layer.
- `LegalApplication` is a first-class reasoning event, also subclassed from `schema:Action`, rather than an untyped text-to-text edge.
- `appliesNorm` has domain `LegalApplication` and range `LegalNorm`; `toFact` has domain `LegalApplication` and range `Fact`.
- The prose describes same-category layering (Fact → Fact and LegalNorm → LegalNorm) to represent derived facts and increasingly abstract norms.
- The implemented evaluation graph uses the directed chain Provision → Norm → Application, with facts also feeding applications.
- Reusable fact and norm nodes can support several applications, capturing convergent premises rather than forcing a tree.
- Canonical provision nodes collapse surface aliases, making statutory references reusable across judgments.
- Section provenance preserves evidence spans and supports tracing extracted nodes back to their judgment context.
- Graph direction reflects the forward flow of legal inference; individual judgments remain mostly isolated components.
- The paper identifies a schema gap: the current constructed edge set does not yet support Norm → Norm links despite the intended layered model.

## Evaluation & results

- The LKG is constructed from 648 Japanese administrative court decisions.
- Node extraction was reviewed by two legal experts over 57 randomly selected judgment sections.
- Provision extraction scored 119 TP, 0 FP, 0 FN (precision/recall/F1 all 1.0000).
- Fact extraction scored 193 TP, 1 FP, 1 FN (precision/recall/F1 all 0.9948).
- Norm extraction scored precision 0.8889, recall 0.9846, and F1 0.9347; Application scored 1.0000, 0.8696, and 0.9302.
- Experts reviewed predicted edges across 92 randomly selected sections.
- Provision → Norm F1 was 0.9470; Norm → Application F1 was 0.8710; Application/Fact linkage F1 was 0.9417.
- The final graph has 44,447 nodes, 51,296 edges, density 2.59 × 10^-5, and 1,478 weak components of diameter at least two.
- Node totals are 10,552 provisions, 15,445 norms, 8,957 applications, and 11,199 facts.
- Edge totals are 18,814 Provision → Norm, 15,356 Norm → Application, and 19,242 Fact → Application.
- Provision retrieval used 1,242 gold labels. At k=3, LKG retrieval reached macro/micro recall 0.311/0.667 and macro/micro F1 0.263/0.352.
- GPT with RAG reached only macro/micro recall 0.176/0.351 and macro/micro F1 0.092/0.097.
- At k=1, LKG retrieval produced 1,253 predictions with 497 TP and micro precision/F1 of 0.397/0.398.
- Increasing k to seven raised micro recall to 0.777 while reducing micro precision to 0.129, exposing the expected retrieval tradeoff.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- Scope is one jurisdiction, one court level, administrative matters, post-2008 formatting, and only the reasoning section.
- The source PDFs/HTML contain OCR and structure errors; the authors note character substitutions and malformed nesting.
- GPT-4o remains an extraction dependency, and the paper reports Claude failure without a systematic cross-model comparison.
- LegalApplication false negatives often become LegalNorms, which the authors interpret leniently as prompt-consistent rather than fully erroneous.
- Norm → Application false positives expose overlong norm spans and omitted supporting norms in layered reasoning.
- The intended ontology allows norm layering, but the evaluated schema lacks Norm → Norm edges; this limits the claimed hierarchy.
- The prose alternates between `toFact`/Application → Fact and Fact → Application direction, leaving the canonical edge orientation ambiguous.
- Components are document-local and disconnected, so cross-case identity, precedent, temporal validity, and jurisdictional applicability are not modeled.
- Provision retrieval gold labels derive from the constructed LKG, which risks coupling evaluation truth to upstream extraction choices.
- No calibration, contradiction handling, temporal versioning, or uncertainty representation is attached to generated nodes and edges.
- The legal-search comparison measures provision recovery, not whether a practitioner would accept the complete legal analysis.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **epistemic slice:** Reify `LegalApplication` as a claim-bearing inference event with explicit fact and norm premises; add bitemporal validity and extraction-time provenance absent here.
- **epistemic slice:** Adopt scoped-history edge proposals plus evidence spans, but require contradiction triage and confidence per edge before graph acceptance.
- **semantic-foundation:** Express Fact, Norm, Application, and Provision as named ontology concepts with domain/range axioms; close the paper's Norm → Norm mismatch in schema and validators.
- **patent tooling — uspto-patent-driver-depth:** Adapt fact-masked nearest-neighbor retrieval to invention disclosures: retrieve analogous claim limitations and linked statutory/office-action grounds without self-matches.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Surface fact-first issue spotting as auditable paths, preserving the case overview and source section so a lawyer can verify each hop.
- **agent memory — Cognee:** Store reasoning events rather than only document chunks, while rejecting the paper's document-isolated graph design in favor of canonical cross-document entities and temporal edges.
