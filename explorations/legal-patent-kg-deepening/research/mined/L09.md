# Ontology-Driven AI Framework for Commercial-Law Automation

## Problem & positioning

The paper targets manual, error-prone review of commercial-law documents and the automation of legal reasoning, contract lifecycle management, and dispute resolution.
It positions hybrid ontology, rule-engine, and transformer methods as a route to explainable and regulation-aligned automation.

## Approach

The framework combines OWL and LegalRuleML with transformer NLP, SPIN constraints, SWRL rules, SHACL validation, clause-to-rule mapping, and XAI feature analysis.
The captured introduction says the system reasons over contract execution, breach criteria, compliance, enforcement paths, and event-driven disputes.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

Formal ontologies represent obligations, rights, liabilities, clauses, and regulatory semantics.
Clause-to-rule mappings provide traceability from document text to executable formalization.
SHACL supplies structural validation while SWRL/SPIN supply inference and constraints; the abstract does not expose the vocabulary or rule set.

## Evaluation & results

The reported corpus contains more than 220,000 procurement contracts, leases, insurance policies, and service-level agreements.
The abstract reports clause accuracy above 94.6%, F1 up to 0.94, reasoning/compliance scores above 0.95, 5.3%–5.6% gains over baselines, up to 61% less manual review, and latency below 1.3 seconds.

## Limitations

The capture does not define the corpus splits, labels, baseline systems, score formulas, jurisdictions, or error analysis.
The assertion of multi-jurisdiction flexibility and production readiness is not independently supported by the visible methodology.
Only the opening of the introduction is available beyond the abstract.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- Preserve clause-to-rule isomorphism and SHACL-like validation as auditable pipeline stages.
- Keep extraction quality, reasoning fidelity, and compliance conclusions as separate metric families.
- Demand jurisdiction-specific evaluation and calibrated error analysis before operational use.
