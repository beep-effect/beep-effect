# LegalGraphRAG: Multi-Agent Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Reliable Legal Reasoning

## Problem & positioning

Flat RAG retrieves independent text segments by semantic similarity and does not respect the different abstraction levels of cases, statutes, and interpretations.
Flat GraphRAG likewise mixes factual details, applied rules, and abstract principles, producing a “granularity bias” toward surface-similar facts.
Direct retrieve-then-generate pipelines also pass noisy context to the model without checking legal applicability.
The preliminary noise study shows why that matters: adding plausible but irrelevant documents sharply degrades charge and article prediction.
LegalGraphRAG positions reliable legal judgment as retrieval, validation, and synthesis over a hierarchical legal graph rather than one-shot generation.
The evaluated task takes a criminal fact description and defendant, then predicts charges; extended experiments also cover applicable articles and penalty terms.

## Approach / architecture

The system has two phases: hierarchical knowledge construction and evidence-based legal reasoning.
`HierarGraph` separates three linked subgraphs: Fact Graph, Ontology Graph, and Rule Graph.
The Fact Graph connects case (`C`), article (`A`), and offense (`O`) nodes through case-cites-article and case-convicted-of-offense edges.
The Ontology Graph aligns narratives to four legal-feature dimensions, embeds the structured features, creates k-nearest-neighbor similarity edges, and uses Leiden communities.
Each community receives a summary node, enabling broad-to-specific retrieval from a legal theme to member cases.
The Rule Graph links articles to judicial interpretations and equips each article with a diagnostic checklist of constituent conditions.
A Researcher maps the query to ontology features, predicts candidate charges, and unions semantic-match, community-expansion, and charge-anchored retrieval.
An Auditor evaluates each candidate article’s checklist against raw facts and judicial interpretations, then prunes inapplicable articles and connected case/offense nodes.
An Adjudicator synthesizes only the verified articles, cases, and offenses, returning charge, statutory basis, penalty, reasoning, and citations.
GPT-4o-mini constructs the graph, BGE-m3 supplies embeddings, and Qwen3-8B is the default reasoning backbone in the main experiment.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

The principal modeling contribution is stratification by epistemic/legal role rather than a single undifferentiated graph.
Fact Graph entities are `Case`, `Article`, and `Offense`; relations encode which article a case cites and which offense produced conviction.
Ontology Graph entities include `Case Feature` and `Community`; its four feature families are Defendant Attributes, Criminal Behaviors, Victim Characteristics, and Subjective Mental States.
The ontology layer abstracts dates, locations, and other instance noise into what the authors call legal essence.
Semantic similarity edges and community membership are retrieval aids, not asserted legal equivalence; this distinction should remain explicit in reuse.
Rule Graph entities include `Article`, `Judicial Interpretation`, and checklist conditions; article–interpretation links supply interpretive context.
Diagnostic checklists operationalize article applicability as multiple atomic factual tests instead of a single similarity score.
The three graphs are operationally linked: ontology features find cases, cases lead to articles/offenses, and rules test whether articles apply.
The paper does not present OWL/RDF axioms, cardinalities, disjointness, identity rules, or a reusable public ontology vocabulary.
It also does not model document/version validity intervals, jurisdiction, authority hierarchy, provenance spans, confidence, or explicit contradictions as first-class graph objects.
“Verified” means accepted by the Auditor LLM against retrieved text; it is not external legal-expert verification.

## Evaluation & results

The preliminary flat-versus-naive-hierarchical retrieval study reports a 25.3% retrieval improvement from separating articles and cases.
With correct context, standard RAG scores 42.8% charge accuracy, 74.7% article accuracy, and 24.3 months penalty MAE.
Adding six irrelevant documents reduces those accuracies to 29.8% and 46.8%, while MAE rises to 31.7 months.
Main evaluation uses CAIL2018 and CMDL across Public Safety, Economic, Social Order, and Person Rights subdomains.
With Qwen3-8B, LegalGraphRAG’s average charge accuracy is 49.5%; the strongest listed RAG baselines average 43.1%.
The paper reports improvements of 6.3%–19.1% over the strongest comparison baselines, depending on baseline.
The best listed LegalGraphRAG subdomain result is 70.1 accuracy / 72.7 micro-F1 for CMDL Person Rights.
With stronger backbones, the reported peak on CMDL is 78.7%.
Traceability analysis reports 38.1 points as Traceable Correct and 2.4 as Untraceable Correct, totaling 40.5 accuracy.
Comparable baselines have only 1.3–13.4 Traceable Correct despite totals of 15.6–32.6.
On CAIL ablation, the full system scores 40.9 accuracy; removing HierarGraph drops 7.2 points, Researcher 4.0, and Auditor 3.4.
Removing semantic match, community expansion, or charge-anchored retrieval drops 1.8, 2.4, and 1.6 points respectively.
The retrieval-neighbor sensitivity analysis peaks at `k = 5` with 40.9 accuracy and 52.8 F1.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

The authors limit the system to textual input; photographs, video, handwriting, and audio must be transcribed, losing potentially material nuance.
They acknowledge that historical judgment data can reproduce regional or gender bias.
They frame the system as assistance for professionals and researchers, not a replacement for judges or lawyers or an automated adjudicator.
The evaluation is Chinese criminal judgment prediction, so it does not establish reliability for civil practice, other jurisdictions, open-ended research, or client advice.
Auditor validation is model-mediated and may confidently accept a mistaken interpretation or reject a controlling but semantically distant authority.
Traceable Correct requires the cited statute to be present, but presence is weaker than correct quotation, current validity, jurisdictional authority, or sound application.
Ontology-feature extraction and checklist creation can inject errors before retrieval; no expert agreement or per-feature accuracy is reported in the main text.
The graph lacks bitemporal legal validity, amendment lineage, precedential status, source-span provenance, and contradiction representation.
Community summaries risk lossy aggregation, while charge prediction used to anchor retrieval can reinforce an early model error.
Claims of transparent reasoning should be read as visible evidence flow, not proof that the model’s internal reasoning is faithful.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology): adopt separate concept, fact, and rule views, but represent similarity/community links as retrieval annotations rather than ontological equivalence.
- epistemic slice: adapt diagnostic checklists into typed claim gates whose outcomes carry supporting/contradicting spans, model identity, confidence, and bitemporal validity; reject the paper’s unqualified `verified` label.
- patent tooling (uspto-patent-driver-depth): layer patent facts, classification/concept features, and governing prosecution rules while preserving authoritative source identity and amendment history.
- patent tooling (agentic-cad-patent-tooling): reuse Researcher–Auditor–Adjudicator role separation for prior-art collection, claim-element verification, and cited synthesis, with deterministic checks before LLM judgment.
- OIP solo-practice workflows: expose retrieved-but-rejected authorities and checklist failures so a lawyer can review exclusion decisions, not merely the final citations.
- agent memory (Cognee): use hierarchical communities for recall, but retain atomic evidence nodes and prohibit community summaries from becoming unsupported claims.
