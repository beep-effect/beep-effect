# Conceptual Modeling of Legal Relations

## Problem & positioning

Legal concepts frequently appear in information-system models, but common deontic approaches are
monadic: they state an obligation or permission without fully representing the counterparties, their
roles, or their correlated legal positions. They also overload “right,” losing distinctions among a claim
right, permission, liberty, and institutional power.

The paper addresses this with UFO-L, a legal core ontology grounded in the Unified Foundational
Ontology and informed by Hohfeld's fundamental legal concepts plus Alexy's triadic theory of
constitutional rights. It targets structural conceptual modeling, especially contracts, rather than only
formal rule execution or compliance checking.

## Approach / architecture

The architecture has three layers. UFO supplies foundational categories, particularly moments, relators,
roles, mediation, and existential dependence. UFO-L specializes these into legal moments and legal
relators. A catalog of eight ontology design patterns packages each paired relation with a rationale,
competency questions, restrictions, usage/checking guidance, and a model.

The paper applies five patterns to clauses from AWS service terms: duties to act and omit, liberty,
power/subjection, and disability/immunity. A legal relation is objectified as a relator that mediates the
parties in their contextual legal roles and contains the legal moments borne by those parties. This lets
the relation have identity, lifecycle, properties, and parts rather than reducing it to a binary edge.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- Hohfeld's eight positions are paired as correlatives: Right/Duty, Privilege-or-Permission/No-Right,
  Power/Liability-or-Subjection, and Disability/Immunity.
- Alexy's triadic form records holder, counterparty, and action/object, and distinguishes positive action
  from omission. Thus rights, duties, permissions, and no-rights each have positive and negative forms.
- An unprotected liberty is complex: permission to act plus permission to omit, correlated with the
  counterparty's no-right to demand either omission or action.
- Power enables its holder, through an institutional act, to create, modify, or extinguish another legal
  position; subjection is the correlative. Disability is absence of that power; immunity is its correlative.
- UFO distinguishes substantials from dependent moments. Externally dependent modes inhere in one
  party while depending on another; a relator bundles those modes and serves as truthmaker for the
  material relation.
- `LegalRelator` specializes `SocialRelator`, which specializes UFO's `Relator`. A simple legal relator
  has one correlated pair of legal moments; a complex legal relator contains other legal relators.
- Legal moments are correlated and are essential, inseparable parts of their relator. Mediation connects
  the relator to parties playing contingent roles such as Right Holder and Duty Holder.
- The pattern makes hidden converse positions explicit. A provider-oriented clause stating a customer
  burden also entails a provider position, and vice versa.

## Evaluation & results

Thirty-seven computer-science/law students and professionals participated; 92% reported some
conceptual-modeling experience and 65% no legal-aspects experience. With contract text alone, subjects
gave 215 correct answers out of 296 (72.6%), 36 incorrect, and 45 blank. Twenty participants then used
UFO-L diagrams and gave 233 correct answers out of 280 (83.2%), 39 incorrect, and 8 blank.

For 200 paired responses across five related questions, McNemar's statistic was χ² = 6.26 with 1 degree
of freedom and p < 0.0125, supporting improved correctness. Text clarity received 45/111 points (40.5%);
diagram clarity received 97/140 (69.3%). The clarity test gave χ² = 12.19, 1 degree of freedom, p <
0.0005. Three participants explicitly noticed immunity, power, and liberty only after seeing diagrams.

A separate ten-person, diagram-only experiment produced 79.3% correct answers versus 83.2% in the
ordered text-then-diagram treatment, suggesting only minor learning-order influence.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The authors identify weak support for inference about violations and plan work on relation lifecycle,
  exercise of power, compliance/non-compliance, and specialized legal visual syntax.
- The study is small, treatment totals differ, only twenty of the initial subjects received diagrams, and
  most had modeling experience; generalization to practicing lawyers or other legal domains is limited.
- The evaluation uses selected AWS clauses. Statutes, adjudication, multi-party relations, exceptions,
  defeasibility, temporal amendment, and conflicting authority remain untested.
- Objectifying every relation is semantically expressive but graph-heavy. Implementations need criteria
  for when a first-class relator is warranted versus a qualified edge or simpler SKOS relation.
- The model distinguishes positions but does not itself prove that a clause is valid, enforceable, in
  force, correctly interpreted, or supported by an authoritative text span.
- The paper compares text with diagrams, not UFO-L against equally explicit alternative diagrams, so
  some gain may come from visualization or explicit converse positions rather than UFO-L's full ontology.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** use UFO-L as a richer domain-model layer above
  SKOS; keep terminology labels in SKOS but represent legally operative relations as typed relators with
  roles and correlated positions.
- **epistemic slice:** model a claim about a legal relation separately from the relator itself; attach exact
  clause spans, interpretation provenance, valid/transaction time, and contradiction links to the claim.
- **epistemic slice:** use paired positions for contradiction triage—a claimed permission conflicts with
  a claimed duty to omit only after aligning holder, counterparty, action, conditions, jurisdiction, and time.
- **patent tooling (uspto-patent-driver-depth):** represent applicant/examiner powers, duties, responses,
  and procedural immunities as lifecycle-bearing relators; do not flatten every prosecution event into an
  unqualified `hasRight` edge.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** generate contract review views that reveal the usually implicit
  correlative position and distinguish claim-right, permission, liberty, power, and immunity before
  drafting advice or obligations matrices.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** first-class relators are suitable memory nodes when a relationship has
  evidence, changing status, conditions, or internal parts; reject universal relation reification because it
  would inflate routine retrieval graphs.
