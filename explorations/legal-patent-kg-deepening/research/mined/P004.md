# A Pattern for the Representation of Legal Relations in a Legal Core Ontology

## Problem & positioning

- Legal core ontologies should expose generic legal concepts reusable by narrower legal ontologies, but models built only from normative text often leave actors, roles, and correlated positions implicit.
- The paper presents the first catalog pattern from UFO-L, a Legal Core Ontology grounded in the Unified Foundational Ontology (UFO) and informed by Robert Alexy's theory of constitutional rights.
- Its deliberate stance is relational rather than norm-centric: legal meaning is modeled through positions held between role-bearing subjects, not only through the legal norm that prescribes them.
- The target is the right-duty relation, with the authors arguing that combining a legal theory and a foundational ontology can expose otherwise missing participants and dependencies.

## Approach / architecture

- UFO supplies the foundational layers: UFO-A for endurants such as Universal, Individual, and Relator; UFO-B for perdurants such as Event; and UFO-C for social reality, including Social Agent and Social Role.
- UFO-L specializes UFO-A and UFO-C with legal aspects. A legal role specializes social role and is prescribed by a legal norm before an agent or group plays it.
- Alexy's legal positions are represented as triadic structures and reified as legal relators rather than left as bare binary predicates.
- The right-duty pattern is expressed in OntoUML and separates a right to something into right-duty relators concerning an act and an omission.
- The pattern makes modeling question-driven: identify right-holder, duty-holder, required or forbidden action, and the intended result of action or omission.
- The worked example expands “Everyone has the right to life” into a negative right against State killing and a positive right to State protection against unlawful third-party intervention.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- Core entities are `LegalNorm`, `LegalRole`, `SocialAgent`, `LegalRelator`, `RightHolder`, `DutyHolder`, `Right`, `Duty`, `Act`, `Omission`, and the result expected from conduct.
- `RightHolder` and `DutyHolder` are contingent legal roles played by agents within a legal relation; they should not be collapsed into permanent actor types.
- A legal relator existentially depends on the individuals it mediates, giving the relation first-class identity and a place for relation-level attributes.
- Each material relation has a corresponding legal relator that mediates its subjects.
- `RightDutyToOmission` is composed of a `RightToOmission` mode inhering in the right-holder and a `DutyToOmit` mode inhering in the duty-holder.
- A right to something is a bundle that can contain both act-directed and omission-directed relators; this prevents a broad right label from hiding distinct obligations.
- The norm prescribes legal roles and positions, while a concrete situation instantiates them for particular agents.
- The pattern distinguishes action polarity and outcome: act versus refrain, and outcomes such as fact, legal norm, non-obstruction, non-disruption, or non-removal of a legal position.
- The modeling invariant is correlativity: a concrete right is represented together with its addressed duty and the parties who bear both positions.
- This is a conceptual pattern, not an RDF/OWL serialization, rule language, temporal model, or extraction procedure.

## Evaluation & results

- Evaluation is limited to one illustrative application of the pattern to a constitutional right-to-life norm.
- The example exposes two duties of the State that the short norm leaves unstated: refrain from killing and protect life against illegal third-party interventions.
- No empirical study, competency-question suite, inter-annotator test, reasoning benchmark, or quantitative result is reported.
- The authors explicitly leave empirical experiments for future work.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- Authors' own: whether the pattern's elicitation questions would be asked, and adequately modeled, without UFO-L remains open pending empirical experiments.
- Authors' own: balancing and weighing, the second part of Alexy's theory, is future work.
- Authors' own: validation of UFO-L's full pattern catalog and a UFO-L-based domain-specific language are future work.
- The paper presents only the right-duty fragment; liberties, powers, and their correlatives are mentioned but not developed here.
- The relation is structurally rich but temporally flat: enactment, applicability, change, adjudication, and retrospective correction have no model.
- The pattern does not attach claims to source passages, interpretations, jurisdictions, or confidence, so it cannot by itself support evidence-sensitive legal KG ingestion.
- The example demonstrates intelligibility, not completeness or correctness across contested legal interpretations.
- The foundational commitment to UFO and Alexy's theory may be heavier than a SKOS-first semantic layer needs; importing the full hierarchy would require a demonstrated consumer.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- `goals/semantic-foundation`: take the right-holder/duty-holder and act/omission distinctions as named legal concept-scheme terms, but reject importing the whole UFO upper ontology into the SKOS-first core without a query-driven need.
- `goals/epistemic-bitemporal-edge-core`: take the reified legal-relator pattern so a relation can carry source, jurisdiction, valid/known times, and lineage; add those dimensions rather than treating UFO-L's static relator as authoritative fact.
- `goals/epistemic-contradiction-triage`: compare claims at the position tuple level—holder, counterparty, action polarity, object, and result—so apparent conflicts are not inferred from broad right labels alone.
- `explorations/uspto-patent-driver-depth` and `goals/agentic-cad-patent-tooling`: use the elicitation checklist to make every asserted entitlement identify its holder, duty bearer, regulated act or omission, and consequence; do not infer these silently from claim text.
- OIP solo-practice workflows: render a lawyer-reviewable relation card that pairs each asserted right with its addressed duty and exact evidence span, keeping automated extraction provisional.
