# Semantic Web Ontology Design Patterns for the Hohfeld Semantic-Conceptual Model

## Problem & positioning

The thesis asks how, and to what extent, Semantic Web ontology design patterns can express Van Engers and Nijssen's Hohfeld semantic-conceptual model.
That model extends Hohfeld's eight legal positions with actions, events, legal-fact qualification, temporal effectiveness, and transitions between legal relations.
The motivating problem is that organizations struggle to turn changing legislation into consistent process and information-system requirements.
The work positions reusable ontology design patterns (ODPs) as a bridge between legal experts and system engineers: smaller documented building blocks rather than a single monolithic legal ontology.
Its target is machine-interpretable compliance reasoning, including event ordering, the qualification of observable facts as legally consequential facts, and identification of active rights and duties.
The implementation uses RDFS, OWL, SWRL, Protégé, the SWRLAPI, Drools, and the SWRL Temporal Built-Ins Library.

## Approach / architecture

The author follows a design-oriented method: conceptualize each recurring modeling problem, implement an OWL pattern and SWRL rules, then exercise it against a car-sale scenario.
Each ODP is documented with intent, competency questions, a schema diagram, example individuals, and reasoning outcomes.
The Temporal Information ODP extends the SWRL Temporal Ontology to attach both valid time and transaction time to propositions.
It distinguishes valid instants, valid periods with known boundaries, and valid intervals that express duration before their start and finish are known.
Allen-style temporal built-ins establish `before`, `after`, and duration constraints and sequence offers, acceptances, payments, and deliveries.
The Legal Fact ODP realizes a qualification pipeline from real-world action or event to institutional record to legal fact.
SWRL combines references to the relevant subject, object, legal-subject/object, and reference source to infer that an action or event counts as a legal fact.
The Temporal Legal Relations ODP represents a relation between a right-side party, duty-side party, Hohfeldian roles, and a domain-specific matter.
Rules infer the correlative duty from a known right and, in the reverse direction added by the thesis, the right from a known duty.
Start and end timestamps sequence relations, while a date-sensitive rule derives `ActiveTemporalLegalRelation` rather than retracting expired assertions.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

The Temporal Information ODP makes `Proposition` the temporal bearer and separates `hasValidTime` from `hasTransactionTime`.
`ValidTime` specializes into `ValidInstant`, `ValidPeriod`, and the added `ValidInterval`; a period has start and finish, while an interval has a count and granularity.
`TransactionTime` specializes to `TransactionInstant`, whose creation time records when a proposition entered the database.
This is a concrete bitemporal pattern, although it records transaction creation rather than full transaction-time intervals or correction history.
The Legal Fact ODP types both `Action` and `Event` as candidate factual occurrences and derives `LegalFact` only through an explicit qualification pattern.
Qualification edges preserve the legal subject, object, source, and the fact's referent, making the legal meaning depend on a conjunction of graph relations rather than a lexical label.
The Temporal Legal Relations ODP models `Party-Right-Side`, `Party-Duty-Side`, `Matter`, `Kind-Of-Right`, and `Kind-Of-Duty` around a reified legal-relation node.
Right kinds are Claim, Privilege, Power, and Immunity; duty-side correlatives are Duty, NoRight, Liability, and Disability.
Permitted correlations are Claim–Duty, Privilege–NoRight, Power–Liability, and Immunity–Disability.
The combination of right-side party, kind of right, duty-side party, and matter determines the kind of duty; the thesis also specifies the inverse determination.
Each temporal relation carries establishment, effectiveness-start, and effectiveness-end concepts, allowing successive relations to share transition boundaries.
The patterns retain ended relations as historical assertions and query an active projection, an important distinction between existence and current validity.
The three ODPs remain separate artifacts; importing inferred legal facts as relation matters and importing their dates into temporal relations are proposed future integrations.

## Evaluation & results

Evaluation is a worked case study, not a quantitative benchmark or expert-user study.
The Temporal Information ODP is tested on a car offer, acceptance, and seven-day exchange interval; SWRL distinguishes an acceptance after publication from one before it and computes whether performance fits the interval.
The Legal Fact ODP is tested with two car-sale qualification scenarios and infers legal facts from the required reference combinations.
The Temporal Legal Relations ODP is tested with two scenarios: Power–Liability changes when an offer is made, and Privilege–NoRight changes when ownership/use passes to the buyer.
The examples show that an ending relation can supply the start date of its successor and that one known Hohfeldian side is sufficient to infer its correlative.
The active-relation rule avoids treating an expired Privilege–NoRight relation as currently operative even though the underlying OWL assertions remain.
The thesis supplies an OWL file for each pattern and SWRL code segments, but reports no precision, recall, competency-question coverage rate, reasoner runtime, scalability result, or comparison with another ontology.
The conclusions are therefore proof-of-concept results for the chosen scenario, not evidence of legal-domain generality.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

The author notes that all dates and SWRL rules are inserted manually and that using the patterns requires SWRL expertise.
Uncertain or implicit time expressions such as “soon” and “a little earlier” are not represented.
OWL/SWRL monotonicity prevents retraction: incompatible historical legal positions coexist, and “active now” is a time-dependent derived classification whose answer changes between runs.
Other scenarios may require additional rules, yet the work does not test the performance or consistency consequences of rule growth.
The choice between one large conjunctive qualification rule and several smaller rules is left unresolved.
The patterns are not integrated end to end, so provenance and timestamps do not automatically flow from qualified facts into legal-relation transitions.
The evaluation reuses a single stylized car-sale scenario and does not test statutory text, contested interpretation, incomplete evidence, concurrency, jurisdiction, or conflicting sources.
Transaction time is modeled as creation time only; corrections, superseded database beliefs, and full bitemporal transaction intervals are absent.
SWRL rules encode selected integrity implications but do not validate closed-world completeness or prevent forbidden Hohfeldian combinations under OWL's open-world semantics.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- `semantic-foundation / SKOS and @beep/ontology`: take the three small, documented ODPs and their competency questions as reusable concept modules; keep Hohfeld roles as a controlled `LiteralKit`-style domain with explicit correlative mappings.
- `epistemic slice`: adopt the valid-time/transaction-time distinction and the separation of historical relation from active projection, but upgrade transaction creation instants to bitemporal intervals and preserve supersession rather than recomputing against ambient “now.”
- `epistemic slice`: model qualification as a claim supported by subject, object, source, and evidence-span edges; reject an unqualified SWRL inference as final truth when the source or interpretation is contestable.
- `OIP solo-practice workflows`: use reified party–position–matter relations to generate a timeline of who held which right, duty, power, or liability at each contract event, with explicit warnings for simultaneous opposites.
- `agent memory / Cognee`: retain ended legal relations as historical memory and expose an effective-date query view; do not delete old edges or conflate stored-at time with legally-valid time.
