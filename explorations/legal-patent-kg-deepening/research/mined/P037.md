# From Instructions to ODRL Usage Policies: An Ontology Guided Approach

## Problem & positioning

- Dataspaces require machine-readable usage policies, but authoring W3C ODRL demands knowledge of RDF serialization, ODRL vocabulary, and policy semantics.
- The paper targets cultural-domain experts who can state permissions and prohibitions naturally but cannot reliably construct compliant RDF graphs.
- Passing the raw ontology alone to an LLM is insufficient: large, formal inputs confuse property roles and encourage undefined terms.
- The hypothesis is that curated ontology documentation, examples, and explicit correction rules improve end-to-end KG generation.
- The work frames policy generation as constrained KG construction, not free-form contract drafting.

## Approach / architecture

- A natural-language Task Description is combined with an LLM Guidance Template (LGT) to generate an ODRL KG.
- The base LGT includes the ODRL 2.2 Turtle ontology, its OWL domain/range information, and human-readable SKOS annotations.
- An enriched OSES variant verbalizes and curates Ontology, Syntax, Semantics, and Examples from the W3C material.
- Distillation removes lengthy and duplicated specification prose that the authors found to cause confusion and hallucination.
- Separate templates cover Agreement, Offer, and Set/Rule policy forms so each receives type-specific instructions.
- OSES explains distinctions such as an obligation attached to a Policy versus a duty conditioning a Permission.
- A Self-Correction Model receives the task, initial KG, and human-readable correction rules, then rewrites the KG for compliance.
- The authors prefer LLM correction rules over returning raw SHACL messages, arguing that violation messages alone provide insufficient corrective context.
- SHACL remains the deterministic evaluator: criteria become shapes and property constraints used to score graph conformance.
- Experiments compare ontology-only guidance, OSES guidance, and refinement with GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4, and GPT-4o.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- ODRL `Policy` contains rules and is specialized into `Set`, `Offer`, and `Agreement` forms.
- `Agreement` binds an `Assigner`, `Assignee`, and `Asset`; `Offer` specifies terms from an assigning party; `Set` expresses general usage terms.
- `Permission` authorizes an `Action` over an `Asset`; `Prohibition` restricts an action; a `Duty` can encode an obligation or permission condition.
- `Constraint` uses `leftOperand`, `operator`, and `rightOperand`; confusing `Action` with `LeftOperand` is an observed LLM error.
- The LGT combines formal triples/axioms with `skos:definition`, `skos:note`, and `rdfs:label`, demonstrating dual machine/human ontology guidance.
- Nine evaluation criteria require UIDs, explicit XSD datatypes, policy metadata, party functions, targets, actions, rules, valid constraints, and profiled extensions.
- Five SHACL shapes—Policy, Permission, Party, Asset, and Constraint—check generated instance graphs.
- The shapes collectively evaluate 22 property ranges for Agreement, 20 for Offer, and 16 for what the paper calls “Asset policy types.”
- The correction layer contains 17 rules for Agreement and 16 for Offer and ODRL Rule; rules are derived manually from the recommendation and ontology.
- New LLM-introduced elements must be defined by ODRL or conform to its Profile Mechanism, constraining vocabulary drift.

## Evaluation & results

- The benchmark contains 12 cultural-dataspace use cases of increasing complexity: four Agreements, five Offers, and three Sets.
- Each use case starts as plain text and represents realistic rights-management requirements inspired by Datenraum Kultur.
- The study reports 108 experiments: 12 cases × three methods × three LLMs, or 36 experiments per model.
- Each focus node/property shape receives 1 for conformance and 0 otherwise; scores are aggregated as obtained points over possible points.
- The worked refined result for Use Case 1 is 217/236, or approximately 91.95% accuracy.
- The reported maximum is 91.95% KG accuracy.
- GPT-4 and GPT-4o show similar performance patterns; refinement is reported as the strongest method across models and use cases.
- Ontology-only prompting struggles with complex constraints and the ontology's size; OSES adds missing relations and properties.
- Self-correction improves completeness by repairing crucial omissions, although its effectiveness depends on the initial graph.
- The PDF's figure, rather than a numeric table, carries most cross-model results, so exact per-model/per-method values are not recoverable from extracted text.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- Evaluation covers only ODRL and 12 authored cultural-domain cases; claimed generality to other ontologies remains untested.
- LGT guidance and correction rules require manual interpretation of the W3C recommendation and ontology.
- The benchmark appears designed by the authors rather than sampled from independent production policies.
- A conformance score measures SHACL satisfaction, not whether a policy faithfully captures user intent or has the desired legal effect.
- Binary shape points weight all constraints equally and may obscure a catastrophic semantic error behind many easy metadata successes.
- The correction model is probabilistic and rewrites graph content; the paper does not report semantic regressions introduced during correction.
- Consolidating rules reduces cost but may reduce accuracy; iterating rules improves focus but increases calls, with no quantified tradeoff.
- The claim that SHACL messages cannot guide correction is not backed by a direct hybrid baseline using structured violations plus ontology context.
- No held-out prompt development protocol, repeated-run variance, confidence intervals, or human legal review is described.
- Policy evolution, revocation, conflict resolution, rule precedence, temporal validity, and enforcement outcomes are outside the model/evaluation.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation:** Adopt the dual representation of formal axioms plus curated SKOS definitions/examples; keep generated prose derived and versioned from ontology truth.
- **semantic-foundation:** Use SHACL-like deterministic constraints as acceptance gates, but avoid treating structural conformance as semantic/legal correctness.
- **epistemic slice:** Record initial KG, detected violations, correction rule, revised KG, and timestamps as claim revisions instead of destructively replacing policy state.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Translate client instructions into reviewable permission/prohibition/duty graphs, with a lawyer confirming intent separately from graph conformance.
- **patent tooling — agentic-cad-patent-tooling:** Reuse ontology-guided generation plus validator-driven repair for structured invention/claim graphs; reject one aggregate “accuracy” score for mixed constraints.
- **agent memory — Cognee:** Curate compact ontology guidance for context loading and use deterministic validators to select memories, rather than injecting an entire ontology into every prompt.
