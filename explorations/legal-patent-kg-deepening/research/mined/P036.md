# Epistemology and Ontology in Core Ontologies: FOLaw and LRI-Core, Two Core Ontologies for Law

## Problem & positioning

- The paper asks what a reusable core ontology for law should contain after more than a decade of legal-domain ontology work.
- It contrasts FOLaw, a useful functional account of legal reasoning, with LRI-Core, a developing common-sense ontology of legal concepts.
- The central diagnosis is category confusion: reasoning structures, plans, task structures, and database schemas are often presented as ontologies.
- FOLaw models dependencies among kinds of knowledge used to justify legal decisions; the authors therefore reclassify it as an epistemological framework.
- A terminological ontology and a reasoning framework can both be reusable, but they have different meanings, alignment surfaces, and reuse contexts.
- Mixing them limits interoperability because a workflow role such as `resource` need not denote the same thing as an ontological resource concept.
- Law especially invites the confusion because legal practice centers on justification and adversarial argument more than specialized object taxonomies.
- LRI-Core is positioned as a shallow, common-sense foundation plus a legal-core layer linked through an `is-a` backbone.

## Approach / architecture

- The authors reconstruct FOLaw's six knowledge roles: normative, meta-legal, world, responsibility, reactive, and creative knowledge.
- Normative knowledge represents desirable or undesirable conduct using permission, obligation, and forbidden modalities.
- Meta-legal knowledge resolves conflicts and determines validity, including priority patterns such as the more-specific rule defeating the general rule.
- World knowledge mediates between legal terms and events, including causal interpretation of conduct.
- Responsibility knowledge links or delinks an agent from a norm violation; reactive knowledge selects sanctions or rewards.
- Creative knowledge represents institutions and legal persons created by authoritative decree.
- They compare these dependencies to CommonKADS inference structures: task-specific input/output roles are separated from reusable domain knowledge.
- They then motivate LRI-Core from common-sense categories rather than adopting an existing foundational ontology unchanged.
- LRI-Core has a shallow foundational layer and a legal layer whose concepts inherit foundational properties.
- It is implemented in OWL with Protégé, allowing consistency checking of specifications.
- The paper uses conceptual argument, prior applications, comparison with other foundations, and prospective reuse—not a controlled empirical study.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- LRI-Core's five top-level portions are physical concepts, mental concepts, abstract concepts, roles, and vocabulary for occurrences.
- Physical reality is organized primarily around `PhysicalObject` and `Process`; objects are matter-bearing continuants and processes change matter or energy.
- `Action` is a process initiated by an agent acting as actor; its intentional origin is distinguished from its physical execution.
- Processes are classified both by change form—transformation, transduction, or transfer—and by affected object properties.
- Mental objects contain representations and concepts; mental processes operate on them under an intentional rather than merely causal stance.
- Belief, desire, and norm are propositional attitudes toward mental content; a plan structures intended primary actions.
- Communication actions aim to change another agent's mental state and require a physical transmission medium.
- Roles provide functional views of objects, agent behavior, and mental processes; evidence, hypothesis, and conclusion are epistemic roles.
- The paper sharply separates `Role`, `RoleTaker`, and `RolePerformance`: an agent acts, while a role supplies idealized behavioral requirements.
- Social roles are relational and complementary, such as teacher/student; legal positions likewise pair rights and duties.
- Norms prescribe qualification and performance requirements for roles; violations compare prescription with actual performance and attach responsibility to actors.
- Abstract classes cover lightweight proto-mathematical concepts such as collections, sequences, counts, and geometric forms.
- Occurrences such as event, state, situation, and history are vocabulary for talking about instances; individual happenings are not themselves ontology classes.
- Framework edges such as `supports` or `produces` express reasoning dependencies, whereas ontology relations should contribute to concept meaning.
- The paper therefore implies two linked but separate graphs: a domain/concept graph and a claim/reasoning graph.

## Evaluation & results

- FOLaw informed multiple systems and projects, including ON-LINE, CLIME, PROSA, and KDE.
- CLIME covered about 15,000 maritime safety, environmental-care, and ship-classification articles.
- Its ontology contained more than 3,500 concepts used to expand keyword terms with implied terms.
- CLIME supported both ontology-expanded retrieval and a more expensive question-answering/compliance mode tied to applicable or violated articles.
- The authors report that almost 99 percent of terms in their legal-domain ontologies fell into FOLaw's broad `world knowledge` category.
- That concentration is evidence that FOLaw offered too little semantic guidance for acquiring new legal-domain ontologies.
- A prototype LRI-Core supported an ontology of Dutch criminal law in the e-Court project.
- OWL enabled consistency checking, but the authors do not report class counts, axiom counts, reasoner timings, error rates, or competency-question coverage.
- Validation remained face-validity comparison, especially against DOLCE, plus prospective operational validation through reuse.
- A separate project was beginning to use LRI-Core for inference of causal relations among events described in legal cases.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- LRI-Core was explicitly still under development and had not resolved whether agenthood/life deserved another major category.
- Its validity was difficult to assess; philosophical comparison tended to increase insight but not convergence.
- The authors concede that reuse supplies weak empirical validation, even if it is the most practical validation available to them.
- FOLaw's six buckets are reasoning roles rather than an ontology, so treating them as entity types would repeat the diagnosed mistake.
- The five-world presentation is conceptually rich but does not expose the OWL axioms, cardinalities, disjointness, or identity criteria in this paper.
- The common-sense grounding is argued rather than tested against annotator agreement, legal-user comprehension, or cross-jurisdiction transfer.
- Examples are largely European and include Dutch criminal law and maritime regulation; jurisdictional portability is not demonstrated.
- The role account needs temporal qualification: the same person can acquire, perform, violate, and relinquish roles across time.
- No explicit provenance model links a legal conclusion to evidence, interpreter, source passage, or valid-time/transaction-time interval.
- The ontology/framework boundary is useful but not absolute: applications still need typed bridge relations between concepts and reasoning occurrences.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** Keep concept taxonomy and semantic relations separate from task/reasoning workflows; align LRI-Core-like anchors without calling every OWL graph an ontology.
- **epistemic slice:** Model evidence, hypothesis, conclusion, support, and conflict as roles in time-bounded reasoning occurrences, not timeless subclasses of document content.
- **epistemic slice:** Encode `Role`, `RoleTaker`, and `RolePerformance` separately, with bitemporal qualification for acquisition, execution, violation, and responsibility.
- **patent tooling — uspto-patent-driver-depth:** Separate invention/world concepts from prosecution reasoning, validity rules, responsibility, and office reactions while retaining typed bridge edges.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Reuse FOLaw's normative → conflict/validity → facts/causality → responsibility → remedy pipeline as workflow structure, not as the matter ontology.
- **agent memory — Cognee:** Maintain distinct semantic-memory and justification/provenance graphs so recurrent agent workflows do not contaminate stable concept identity.
