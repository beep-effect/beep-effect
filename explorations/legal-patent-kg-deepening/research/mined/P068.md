# PatentGPT: A Large Language Model for Patent Drafting Using a Knowledge-Based Fine-Tuning Method

## Problem & positioning

- Patent drafting requires both technical understanding and legally constrained form, while general LLMs lack reliable patent-domain knowledge and context.
- The paper targets idea-to-patent generation, especially titles, abstracts, and claims for U.S. patent applications.
- It positions knowledge fine-tuning (KFT) as an alternative to relying only on patent-text pretraining or supervised instruction data.
- The central hypothesis is that structured domain knowledge injected during continued pretraining makes a small model more responsive to later task tuning.
- PatentGPT is presented as a 1.5B-parameter patent model intended to assist researchers and junior engineers, not merely answer patent questions.
- The paper claims novelty for a four-stage loop joining graph extraction, graph-to-text injection, patent-task SFT, and human-feedback optimization.

## Approach / architecture

- Base model: Qwen2-1.5B, selected as a compute/performance compromise.
- Stage 1, knowledge extracting: an LLM prompted with USPTO patent texts emits head-relation-tail triples and unions them into graph `K`.
- Stage 2, knowledge injecting: another prompt verbalizes each triple as a natural-language sentence, producing knowledge corpus `S`.
- Continued pretraining mixes `S` with a general corpus `G`; coefficients alpha and beta weight their log-likelihood objectives.
- Stage 3, knowledge learning: SFT over patent-application dialogue pairs teaches scenario-specific use of the injected knowledge.
- The SFT dialogues focus on producing patent titles, abstracts, and claims.
- Stage 4, knowledge feedbacking: preferred and rejected answers from attorney-applicant interactions train a reward model.
- PPO then optimizes the policy against that reward signal, with clipping used to stabilize training.
- Training used LoRA, batch size 1, learning rate `5e-5`, and 3 epochs on NVIDIA 4090 GPUs.
- Inference used temperature 0.7 and a 1,024-token maximum via LLaMA-Factory and lm-evaluation-harness.
- Training resources comprise USPTO patent text, a constructed patent KG, patent dialogue data, and mixed human-feedback data.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- The graph representation is a generic triple set `T = (h, r, t)` extracted from each patent document and aggregated by set union.
- Mentioned entity families include technical fields and patent classifications; the paper does not enumerate a complete entity or relation schema.
- Patent text remains attached indirectly: both extracted triples and original/general text are used for continued pretraining.
- The graph is converted back into sentences before model ingestion, so graph topology is internalized parametrically rather than queried at generation time.
- The knowledge corpus pattern is useful as graph-to-language augmentation, but it discards explicit identifiers, provenance, confidence, and edge-level evidence spans.
- No ontology axioms, class hierarchy, cardinality rules, temporal semantics, contradiction model, or entity-resolution procedure are specified.
- No graph-construction precision/recall, triple examples, graph size, or extraction-review protocol is reported.
- The practical contribution is therefore a KG-conditioned training pipeline, not a reusable patent ontology or inspectable retrieval architecture.

## Evaluation & results

- The patent-writing test has 1,000 instances and compares generated titles, abstracts, and claims with real patent text.
- PatentGPT scores BERTScore 90.0036, BLEU-4 45.3602, ROUGE-1 41.9612, ROUGE-2 25.9777, and ROUGE-L 36.0227.
- On the same table, the strongest baseline BERTScore is phi-2 at 81.496; its BLEU-4 is 15.7996.
- KPT alone improves over ordinary PT from 79.6514 to 82.9026 BERTScore and from 11.6635 to 22.0156 BLEU-4.
- KPT+SFT reaches 88.3056 BERTScore and 44.661 BLEU-4, versus PT+SFT at 86.6251 and 30.8483.
- KPT+SFT+RLHF reaches 90.0036 BERTScore and 45.3602 BLEU-4.
- The corresponding PT+SFT+RLHF path scores only 83.4596 BERTScore and 12.0153 BLEU-4.
- Ablation from the full system to no RLHF yields a modest decrease: BERTScore 90.0036 to 88.3056 and BLEU-4 45.3602 to 44.661.
- Patent-bench scores are 0.524 on IP Quiz, 0.33 on IP Exam, 0.262 on IP Match, and 0.535 on MMLU.
- PatentGPT leads the listed models on IP Quiz and IP Match, ties Qwen2-1.5B on IP Exam, and trails Qwen2-1.5B's 0.55 on MMLU.
- A qualitative hybrid-vehicle abstract case favors PatentGPT for specificity and patent-like structure, but no blinded expert-rating protocol is described.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The authors note that general models can produce generic, incomplete, irrelevant, informal, or insufficiently specific patent text in the case study.
- They do not provide a dedicated limitations section or discuss deployment risks, inventorship, confidentiality, hallucination, or unauthorized-practice concerns.
- Training-set sizes, licenses, splits, deduplication, graph size, prompts, annotation agreement, and human-feedback collection details are absent.
- Reference-overlap metrics reward imitation of existing patent language and do not establish novelty, enablement, claim scope, validity, or legal correctness.
- The 1,000-instance test's sampling method and leakage controls are not shown; overlap between USPTO pretraining and evaluation patents is therefore unclear.
- The ordinary PT+SFT+RLHF collapse is not explained, making the comparison sensitive to optimization quality rather than only knowledge injection.
- The graph is neither inspectable at inference nor tied to citations, so generated propositions cannot be traced back to authoritative patent passages.
- Automatically extracted triples may preserve model errors; the paper supplies no validation gate before those triples become training material.
- The claim of state-of-the-art or first-of-kind status is asserted by the paper but cannot be established from its experiments alone.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** take the explicit entity-relation extraction stage, but require named concept schemes, stable IDs, typed predicates, and validation rather than an untyped triple union.
- **epistemic slice:** reject provenance-free graph-to-text injection; retain patent/document/version, extraction time, confidence, and exact evidence spans on every asserted edge before model training.
- **patent tooling (uspto-patent-driver-depth):** adapt the title/abstract/claim task suite as fixtures, while adding family-aware deduplication and held-out temporal splits to detect memorization.
- **patent tooling (agentic-cad-patent-tooling):** use the extract-inject-learn-feedback stages as separable agents with review gates; do not collapse technical facts and legal drafting preferences into one reward.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** treat generation as attorney-supervised drafting assistance and expose sources, uncertainty, and validation checklists instead of presenting fluent text as legally robust.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** store accepted/rejected drafting feedback as scoped, versioned claims linked to matters and evidence, not as undifferentiated RLHF preferences.
