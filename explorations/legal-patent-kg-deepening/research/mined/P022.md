# An Ontologically-Founded Reification Approach for Representing Temporally Changing Information in OWL

## Problem & positioning

- OWL's description-logic semantics favor decidable reasoning over static scenarios with immutable truth values.
- Open-world semantics, non-unique names, and monotonicity allow knowledge to be completed but do not directly retract a formerly true class or property assertion.
- This makes ordinary mappings from mutable UML attributes, relations, and role instantiations semantically misleading.
- The paper seeks an OWL-compatible representation rather than extending description logic with temporal modal operators.
- Its contribution is a higher-level reification framework grounded in Formal Ontology's objects and moments, plus modeling guidelines.
- It separates two orthogonal dimensions for attributes and relations: necessary versus contingent, and mutable versus immutable.
- For class membership, it separates necessary kinds from contingent roles.
- The authors explicitly exclude a full ontology of time and temporal-cardinality reasoning from the paper's scope.

## Approach / architecture

- The running model has a person with immutable mandatory name, immutable optional social-security number, mutable mandatory age, mutable optional nicknames, and a mutable marriage relation.
- A `Moment` is a reified property particular existentially dependent on one or more bearer individuals; an `Object` is not necessarily dependent on another individual.
- A `Quality` is an intrinsic moment inhering in one bearer and carrying a datatype value, such as John's particular age.
- A `Relator` depends on multiple individuals and captures the relational properties that jointly connect them, such as a particular marriage.
- A `QuaIndividual` represents one participant insofar as it plays a relational role, such as John-qua-husband-of-Mary.
- Qua-individuals inhere in their bearer, are parts of the relator, and depend on the other participating qua-individuals.
- Every object or moment receives a temporal extent; existential dependence entails corresponding temporal inclusion constraints.
- Necessary domain classes specialize Object; contingent role classes specialize QuaIndividual.
- Each domain relation becomes a Relator subclass; each attribute becomes a Quality subclass.
- A qua-individual must inhere in exactly one object, belong to exactly one corresponding relator, and depend on the other role aspects in that relator.
- A relator mediates only the allowed object classes and contains only the corresponding qua-individual classes.
- `minC`, `maxC`, and `exactC` restrictions constrain participant and qua-individual cardinalities.
- A quality must inhere in exactly one permitted object and have exactly one value of the corresponding datatype.
- Necessary attributes require at least one quality instance per bearer; contingent attributes impose no minimum.
- Immutable attributes cap the number of quality instances per bearer; mutable attributes permit a new quality particular for each value interval.
- Immutable relations cap relator instances involving a participant; mutable relations permit multiple temporally distinct relators.
- The authors compare this pattern with a perdurantist/4D strategy based on whole-life individual concepts and time slices.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- Temporal change is localized to property particulars instead of represented by retracting binary facts or cloning the whole domain object.
- A stable object identity can coexist with successive age qualities, nicknames, marriages, or role aspects.
- Necessary/contingent controls whether a bearer must have the property; mutable/immutable controls whether multiple property particulars may occur over its life.
- A necessary immutable property should share the bearer's temporal extent.
- Successive instances of a necessary mutable quality should collectively project over the bearer's full temporal extent.
- Existential dependence constrains a moment's lifetime to the lifetime of its bearer or bearers.
- Relator reification gives an n-ary relationship an identity to which interval, provenance, and other qualifiers can attach.
- Qua-individuals distinguish each participant's relationally acquired properties rather than assuming both parties relate in exactly the same way.
- The pattern supplies explicit `inheresIn`, `mediates`, `partOf`, `existentiallyDependentOf`, `hasValue`, and `hasTimeExtent` relations.
- Contingent class membership becomes a time-bounded qua-individual, while necessary class membership remains classification of the stable object.
- The approach avoids 4D time-slice proliferation when one property in a connected graph changes.
- It also avoids repeating immutable contingent information on every slice and gives necessary and contingent concepts more homogeneous interpretations.
- Under non-unique-name semantics, overlapping fillers of a functional mutable relation may be inferred identical; non-functional relations remain ambiguous without additional identity evidence.
- This shows that interval overlap, cardinality, and identity constraints must be modeled together rather than assumed from a versioned property table.

## Evaluation & results

- Evaluation is an analytical comparison over one running person/marriage example, not an empirical benchmark.
- The paper walks through 16 guideline groups, labeled `a` through `p`, for mapping kinds, roles, relations, attributes, dependencies, and cardinalities.
- It compares the reification pattern against four stated 4D drawbacks: time-slice proliferation, obscure contingent-class interpretation, repetition of immutable information, and inability to guarantee immutable contingent values.
- The authors judge reification more scalable because each changed property adds its own reified individual without duplicating every connected time slice.
- No ontology-size experiment, reasoner runtime, consistency suite, memory measurement, query benchmark, or user study quantifies that scalability claim.
- No legal, patent, or production KG case study is evaluated.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The paper does not model the time domain itself or provide the interval algebra needed to enforce the proposed temporal constraints.
- It does not solve temporal cardinality under open-world and non-unique-name assumptions.
- The authors identify incomplete lifetimes and overlapping fillers as non-trivial issues deferred to future work.
- The comparison is against a particular 4D formulation and remains qualitative; hybrid, named-graph, event-sourcing, and RDF-star patterns are not assessed.
- Reification increases node and join counts even if it avoids whole-graph slice duplication.
- Immutable-property cardinality prevents multiple quality individuals but does not by itself prove that the recorded value is correct or never revised epistemically.
- Valid-time change is conflated with knowledge correction unless transaction time and assertion provenance are added.
- Exact cardinalities under open-world semantics do not imply operational completeness of imported data.
- Social-security number, sex, and marriage examples embed simplifying assumptions that do not generalize safely to all jurisdictions or identity models.
- `hasTimeExtent` on every entity is a strong requirement; timeless concepts, uncertain intervals, open bounds, and granularity differences need separate handling.
- The paper supplies a conceptual UML-like schema but not complete executable OWL axioms or validation artifacts.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- `epistemic slice / bitemporal edges`: take property-particular reification for valid-time change, then add assertion/evidence nodes with transaction time so a changed world-state is distinct from a corrected belief.
- `epistemic slice / claims and contradiction triage`: represent overlapping incompatible qualities or relators as competing sourced claims until identity, interval, and cardinality evidence resolves them; reject destructive OWL-assertion replacement.
- `semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology)`: keep stable kinds separate from contingent roles and promote `Quality`, `Relator`, and `QuaIndividual` as optional high-rigor patterns; SKOS labels alone should not encode dependence or temporality.
- `patent tooling / uspto-patent-driver-depth`: reify assignments, ownership, examiner roles, claim status, and application relationships with validity intervals and source spans, rather than attaching mutable timeless predicates to a patent.
- `OIP solo-practice workflows`: model engagement roles, representation, deadlines, and matter assignments as interval-bearing relators/qua-individuals; preserve successive states and uncertain end dates for audit.
- `agent memory (Cognee)`: store stable subjects once and attach time-bounded, provenance-bearing property memories; reject whole-object snapshot multiplication, but add observed time absent from the paper.
