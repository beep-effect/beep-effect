# A Pattern for the Representation of Legal Relations in a Legal Core Ontology

## Problem & positioning

- Legal rules often state a right without making the corresponding duty bearer, required conduct, or result explicit.
- Modeling directly from a constitution, statute, or agreement can therefore preserve a semantic gap around actors and roles.
- The authors also argue that legal ontologies not grounded in a foundational ontology risk ontological inconsistency and incompleteness.
- They position a legal core ontology as a reusable conceptualization of generic legal concepts for more specific legal ontologies.
- The paper extends UFO-L, which combines Robert Alexy's theory of constitutional rights with the Unified Foundational Ontology (UFO).
- Its distinctive stance is relational rather than norm-centered: legal positions are analyzed through the parties and relators that connect them.
- The paper presents the first entry in a planned UFO-L pattern catalogue: the right-duty relation.

## Approach / architecture

- UFO supplies the foundation: UFO-A models endurants such as Universal, Individual, and Relator; UFO-B adds perdurants and events; UFO-C adds social agents and social roles.
- UFO-L specializes categories from UFO-A and UFO-C for the legal domain.
- A legal role specializes social role, is prescribed by a legal norm before assignment, and is played by an agent within a legal relation.
- A legal relation is represented as an Alexy-style triadic structure and reified as a legal relator.
- The relator is existentially dependent on the participating individuals in their legal roles.
- The pattern is expressed in OntoUML and illustrated by instantiating the constitutional statement “Everyone has the right to life.”
- The method prompts the modeler to identify right holder, duty holder, required act or omission, and the intended result.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- `LegalRole` specializes `SocialRole`; concrete examples include citizen and personal taxpayer.
- `LegalRelator` reifies a legal relation and mediates subjects playing legal roles, avoiding an unqualified binary `hasRight` edge.
- UFO-L's relator taxonomy follows Alexy's classes of rights to something, liberties, and powers.
- A `RightToSomething` is modeled as a bundle containing `RightDutyToAnOmission` and `RightDutyToAnAct` relators.
- `RightDutyToAnOmission` is composed of two dependent modes: `RightToAnOmission`, inhering in the right holder, and `DutyToOmit`, inhering in the duty holder.
- The same holder-to-holder material relation is thus supported by paired, perspective-specific legal positions rather than one free-standing norm node.
- The action facet distinguishes positive conduct from omission and asks whether the result is a fact or a legal norm.
- Omission results are refined into non-obstruction of acts, non-disruption of situations or characteristics, and non-removal of legal positions.
- In the worked example, a person qua right holder is related to the State qua duty holder.
- The negative right requires the State to refrain from killing the person; the positive right requires protection against illegal third-party intervention.
- The pattern concretely separates bearer role, counterparty role, conduct polarity, and resulting state—useful dimensions for provenance-bearing KG edges.

## Evaluation & results

- Evaluation is illustrative, not empirical: one constitutional sentence is instantiated against the pattern.
- The example exposes two duties behind the general right to life: State non-killing and State protection from third parties.
- It also demonstrates that one surface right can denote both a negative/defensive right and a positive right.
- No dataset, competency-question suite, reasoner test, user study, precision/recall measure, or comparative baseline is reported.
- The authors leave open whether unaided modelers would ask or adequately represent the same questions.
- Empirical experiments are explicitly future work rather than a completed validation result.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- Authors' own: only one part of UFO-L and one right-duty pattern are presented.
- Authors' own: balancing and weighing, the second part of Alexy's theory, remains to be modeled.
- Authors' own: empirical validation of UFO-L and its pattern catalogue remains future work.
- Authors' own: a domain-specific language based on UFO-L has not yet been built.
- The worked example is a highly simplified constitutional relation and does not test multi-party, delegated, conditional, conflicting, or jurisdiction-specific duties.
- The paper gives a conceptual OntoUML pattern but no machine-readable ontology, formal axioms, queries, or executable conformance tests.
- Temporal validity, enactment history, defeasibility, evidence provenance, disputed role assignment, and contradiction handling are outside the presented pattern.
- Existential dependence clarifies relation structure but does not by itself determine whether a claimed legal relation is valid, current, or evidentially supported.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** adopt named legal-role and legal-position concepts in governed schemes, but keep the UFO metaphysics as a mapped donor model rather than silently equating SKOS concepts with universals or relators.
- **epistemic slice (bitemporal edges, claims, evidence spans):** reify each right-duty assertion with right holder, duty holder, act/omission, result, source span, valid time, and transaction time; do not store a bare `hasRight` edge.
- **epistemic contradiction triage:** treat competing identifications of the duty holder or required result as rival claims about one source passage, not destructive edits to a single relation.
- **patent tooling / agentic-cad-patent-tooling:** use the paired-position pattern for claim obligations and prosecution responses—actor, counterparty, required/forbidden act, and expected legal result—while rejecting automatic transfer of constitutional-right semantics to patent law.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** turn the pattern's four elicitation questions into an attorney review checklist for agreements, office actions, and legal opinions, preserving the exact text that supports each assigned role.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** index the relator pattern as reusable modeling guidance, but keep client-specific legal relations and their truth status in the deterministic epistemic store rather than memory-generated graph facts.
