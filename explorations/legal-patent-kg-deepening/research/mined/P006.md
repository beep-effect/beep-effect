# A Linked Open Data Representation of Patents Registered in the US from 2005–2017

## Problem & positioning

USPTO patent data is large and valuable but cumbersome to search, integrate, and analyze in PDF or heterogeneous XML form.
Google made OCR-derived XML available, but XML releases vary by year and remain isolated from other web datasets.
The paper publishes 2005–2017 US patent grants as machine-readable RDF under Linked Data principles.
Its contribution is primarily a reusable data asset and conversion/interlinking pipeline, not a new retrieval model.
The intended uses include structured patent search, trend analysis, citation networks, and societal-progress measures.

## Approach / architecture

Weekly USPTO/Google XML archives are split into one file per patent before transformation.
Code-valued fields—patent kinds, classifications, assignee roles, and geographic codes—are enriched with textual labels.
RML mappings convert XML nodes into RDF subjects, predicates, and objects using the RMLProcessor.
Mappings are adjusted for changing document type definitions and fields across publication years.
The output is loaded into a Virtuoso triple store and exposed through a SPARQL endpoint, RDF dump, and VoID description.
LIMES discovers links to DBpedia, LinkedGeoData, World Bank data, and EU Patents.
The link specification selects source/target properties, similarity metric, relation, and threshold; the reported threshold is 0.9.
RML Validator/RDFUnit generates schema-based tests from RDFS/OWL axioms to validate the mappings.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

The core `Patent` class carries document number, invention title, abstract, kind, year/date, country, and classifications.
Contributor roles include Applicant, Assignee, Inventor, Examiner, Examiner Assistant, and Agent.
Contributor nodes carry names, organization names, nationality, residence, city, state, country, and role where available.
Patent-to-patent citation is represented with `citedBy`; cited documents also have names/categories in the source model.
National and international classifications are modeled as links rather than flattened descriptive text.
The ontology reuses FOAF for people/agents/names, Dublin Core Terms for abstract/date, RDFS for labels, and GNDO for inventor.
Dataset-specific namespaces separate ontology classes (`patents:`) from properties (`patentp:`).
External equivalence links use `owl:sameAs` for countries, cities, states/territories, and invention titles.
The publication pattern combines stable HTTP URIs, retrievable RDF, SPARQL access, vocabulary reuse, and outbound links.
The paper demonstrates federated queries that join patent country/year facts to World Bank indicators.
Its ontology is bibliographic/administrative; it does not model claims, claim dependencies, evidence spans, legal status, or uncertainty.

## Evaluation & results

The conversion covers 12 years, 2005–2017.
The source cadence is approximately 52–55 weekly files per year with about 5,000 patents per file.
Average conversion time is reported as approximately 18 hours per year.
The methods section reports 213,973,552 produced triples, while the Data Records section and Table 3 report 212,234,735; the discrepancy is unresolved.
Interlinking evaluation reports precision 1.0 for every row in Table 2.
Country-link recall is 0.87 for DBpedia, 0.89 for World Bank, and 1.0 for EU Patents and LinkedGeoData.
Reported link counts include 351 USPTO–DBpedia country links, 281 World Bank country links, and 986,524 invention-title links to EU Patents.
City linking reports 2,331 DBpedia links, zero EU Patents links, and 3,112 LinkedGeoData links.
RML validation passed all generated tests except one range-definition failure, which the authors say did not affect conversion.
The paper provides SPARQL examples for patents per year, patents and R&D expenditure by country, and citations by patent class.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

The paper notes imperfect OCR/indexing in the upstream Google corpus and schema variation across years.
One mapping-validation range failure remains rather than being resolved.
The two total-triple counts conflict, weakening reproducibility of the precise dataset size.
Perfect precision/recall values are difficult to assess because the gold-set construction and sample sizes are not explained in the surrounding text.
`owl:sameAs` for invention titles is semantically risky: identical or similar titles do not necessarily denote the same patent/invention.
The graph reflects source fields and mappings, not extraction-level provenance or confidence.
Coverage stops at grants in the selected years and does not establish current endpoint availability or post-2017 updates.
The CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 dataset license may constrain commercial reuse and should be reviewed independently.
