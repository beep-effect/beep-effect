# Knowledge Graphs Construction from Criminal Court Appeals: Insights from the French Cassation Court

## Problem & positioning

- Sentencing research traditionally depends on fixed government databases, laborious case-file coding, or subjective courtroom observation sheets.
- French Judilibre decisions offer an open corpus, but their narrative form resists scalable statistical analysis.
- Property graphs and RDF can preserve actors, events, crimes, punishments, decisions, causality, and chronology more expressively than rigid tables.
- The paper proposes an end-to-end, ontology-guided LLM pipeline for appeals to the criminal chamber of France's Cassation Court.
- Its emphasis is practical distillation and downstream analysis rather than formal legal reasoning or rule execution.

## Approach / architecture

- Inputs are anonymized PDF decisions, typically six to eight pages, describing crimes, convict status, punishments, and prior decisions.
- The authors compare a property-graph route with direct RDF Turtle generation.
- The property-graph route uses LlamaIndex `SchemaLLMPathExtractor`, supplied entity types, relation types, and admissible type/relation schema.
- It also uses embeddings for entity matching and can ingest the resulting graph directly into Neo4j.
- The RDF route prompts an LLM with a custom criminal ontology and requests Turtle output.
- GPT-4o mini and Claude 3.5 Sonnet assist iterative ontology design; experts check standardization, domain fit, and edge cases.
- LangChain sends the ontology plus guidance rules to GPT-4o mini; PyMuPDF extracts PDFs.
- RDFLib validates Turtle syntax before graphs enter an Apache Fuseki triple store.
- Explicit prompt guidance requests ISO 8601 duration/date values, non-negative numeric literals, and currency codes.
- The prompt also solicits model comments and ontology-improvement suggestions, creating a feedback channel for schema iteration.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- The custom namespace is `fca:<https://growgraph.dev/fcaont#>` and models the criminal judicial process.
- Principal entities include `Case`, `Appeal`, `Person`, `Event`, `Crime`, `Conviction`, `Punishment`, and appeal-decision types.
- `Person` can bear roles such as `Convict` and `Victim` rather than collapsing role and identity.
- Punishment is decomposed into custodial duration and monetary amount, with typed literal constraints and preferred serializations.
- Location is modeled but requires carefully restricted relations: an unconstrained `Crime has Location` property can cause court location to be misread as crime location.
- The ontology represents that a case may receive an appeal and connects offense, conviction, punishment, parties, and appellate outcome.
- FOAF, RDFS, and schema.org are reused where appropriate for interoperability rather than recreating every term locally.
- RDF's standardized vocabulary and ontology constraints are preferred over property-graph schema-on-read flexibility for cross-system integration.
- The ontology itself is placed in every extraction prompt, acting simultaneously as vocabulary, relation constraint, and generation guide.
- Expert/model iteration exposes a core pattern: ontology granularity directly affects extraction ambiguity, prompt size, and processing cost.

## Evaluation & results

- A ground-truth CCP2023 dataset is built from selected excerpts in 20 appeals and expert-validated derived graphs.
- The property-graph approach is estimated at 50–60% accuracy; preliminary RDF triple output is reported above 90%, motivating focus on RDF.
- On the CCP dataset, out-of-the-box GPT-4o mini precision is 93% and recall is 89%.
- Llama 3.1 7B is excluded after preliminary experiments because output quality was low; no metric is supplied.
- The production run processes 2,820 criminal-chamber appeals from 2023.
- It generates about 30 triples per appeal on average.
- Estimated processing cost is $2.5 per 1,000 documents as of December 2024.
- The derived graphs support distributions of offense types, custodial durations, fine amounts, and upheld versus rejected appeals.
- The preliminary analysis reports negligible effects of punishment duration and fine amount on Cassation Court decisions.
- The paper does not give a statistical effect size, uncertainty measure, or model specification for that substantive conclusion.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- Performance depends on LLM architecture/training and on ontology self-consistency, domain alignment, and granularity.
- Generative output remains stochastic and sometimes violates Turtle syntax or datatype formats despite explicit instructions.
- The 20-appeal ground truth uses selected excerpts, not full decisions, which simplifies extraction and weakens full-pipeline validity.
- “Accuracy in the ballpark” for the property graph and “a priori >90%” for RDF are informal, under-specified comparisons.
- Precision and recall lack unit definitions: the paper does not state whether matching is per triple, entity, relation, or field.
- Experts helped build the ontology and validate ground truth, but their number, agreement, adjudication, and independence are not reported.
- Ontology development with the same model family used for extraction risks benchmark and prompt-design leakage.
- Larger ontologies improve guidance but increase token cost and can push important information out of the model's effective context.
- Appellate decisions are anonymized and limited to one chamber/year; cross-year, lower-court, and identity-linking behavior is untested.
- Syntax validation with RDFLib cannot detect hallucinated but well-formed facts or legally incorrect relations.
- The claimed negligible sentencing-variable effect is observational and preliminary, so it should not be treated as causal.
- No temporal model represents the appealed decision, appeal event, judgment dates, or changing legal provisions bitemporally.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation:** Reuse FOAF/RDFS/schema.org only where semantics align, while giving criminal-domain roles and relations explicit domain/range constraints to prevent location projection errors.
- **epistemic slice:** Turn each extracted triple into a source-span-backed claim with extraction time, court-event time, confidence, and expert validation status; RDF syntax validity alone is insufficient.
- **patent tooling — uspto-patent-driver-depth:** Copy the ontology-in-prompt plus typed-literal validation pattern for prosecution events, parties, rejections, claims, and cited references, but evaluate on full files.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Use role-separated parties and decomposed outcome/punishment fields for matter intake and appeal timelines; retain the underlying decision passage for every assertion.
- **agent memory — Cognee:** Accept model suggestions only as ontology-change proposals requiring deterministic checks and review; do not let extraction-time models silently mutate durable vocabulary.
- **semantic-foundation:** Prefer interoperable RDF for canonical exchange, while allowing a derived property-graph projection for developer-friendly exploration rather than choosing one store as ontology truth.
