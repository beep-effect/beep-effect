# UFO-L: Uma Ontologia Núcleo de Aspectos Jurídicos Construída sob a Perspectiva das Relações Jurídicas

## Problem & positioning

This Portuguese doctoral thesis develops the completed UFO-L artifact previewed by P086 and proposed by P087.
It argues that norm-centered legal core ontologies hide the agents, roles, paired legal positions, dependencies, and events that constitute subjective legal relations.
The problem is framed as a conceptual-modeling gap between Computing and Law, not primarily as document retrieval or automated legal decision-making.
The thesis combines Robert Alexy's post-positivist Theory of Fundamental Rights with Guizzardi's ontological foundations for structural conceptual models.
Its scope is the analytical, static structure of subjective fundamental rights in substantive law; procedural law, legal argumentation, and Alexy's balancing theory are largely excluded.
The result consists of UFO-L, an eight-pattern modeling catalog, applications to legal-domain ontologies and language design, and an empirical contract-interpretation study.
The thesis uses Design Science Research, with rigor, relevance, design, evaluation, contribution, and communication cycles.

## Approach / architecture

The research begins with a systematic mapping of legal-core-ontology work, then aligns selected legal theories with UFO-A, UFO-B, and UFO-C.
UFO-L specializes foundational categories rather than mixing top-level and legal-domain concepts in one flat hierarchy.
OntoUML expresses the reference model and carries UFO constraints such as rigidity, identity, mediation, inherence, existential dependence, essential parthood, and disjointness.
Legal relations are reified as `Legal Relator` instances grounded in events and mediating legal-role bearers.
The legal positions composing a relator are modeled as externally dependent moments, each inhering in one bearer and depending on its counterparty.
Competency questions ask for participating agents, role categories, constituent positions, bearers, external dependencies, grounding event, and defining norm.
Eight catalog patterns package rationale, related UFO patterns, competency questions, application guidance, verification questions, axioms, and examples.
Validation combines an AWS service-contract interpretation experiment, constitutional-right analysis, judicial-case scenarios, reconstruction of a flight-incident ontology, a service-contract ontology, an ArchiMate extension, and the LawV visual language.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

`Legal Thing` specializes `Social Thing` and divides into legal individuals and legal universals; the thesis concentrates on legal endurants and selected leaf universals.
`Legal Agent` includes singular human agents with legal personality and agentive legal institutions composed of multiple agents and roles.
`Legal Role` is anti-rigid, sortal, and relationally dependent; `Legal RoleMixin` groups roles with different identity principles by shared legal position.
Five role-category pairs are explicit: Right/Duty Holder, Permission/NoRight Holder, Liberty/NoRight Holder, Power/Subjection Holder, and Immunity/Disability Holder.
`Legal Normative Description` is the normative text or utterance; `Legal Norm` is its meaning, preventing document nodes from being conflated with interpreted norms.
A normative description may express multiple norms, and a norm may be expressed through different descriptions; UFO-L restricts modeled descriptions to those expressing at least one norm.
Legal norms divide into rules and principles, and into conduct norms versus power or constitutive norms; the detailed theory of norms remains incomplete.
Fourteen positions are modeled: rights and duties to act or omit, no-rights and permissions to act or omit, legal power, subjection, disability, immunity, protected liberty, and unprotected liberty.
Correlated positions are one-to-one pairs and essential, inseparable parts of the legal relator that reifies their relationship.
Simple relators cover Right-Duty, NoRight-Permission, Power-Subjection, and Disability-Immunity; complex relators cover protected and unprotected Liberty.
Unprotected Liberty composes permissions to act and omit plus their correlated no-rights; Protected Liberty adds at least a supporting Right-Duty or Power-Subjection pair.
Legal Power creates, changes, or extinguishes another agent's positions through a valid institutional act; Subjection is its correlate.
Disability negates power and correlates with Immunity; it is distinct from a duty to omit because it concerns capacity to alter positions, not conduct qualification.
Every legal relation has a converse and a triadic shape: relation type, addresser, addressee, and an action or omission object.
Relations may be transitive or anti-transitive; this property belongs to the relevant relation type rather than to legal relations uniformly.
`Legally Defined Event` captures events specified a priori by normative descriptions, while other legally relevant events may acquire legal meaning a posteriori in an open legal system.
Events ground relators but are ontologically distinct: events unfold in time; relators endure through phases and changes.
The eight reusable patterns are action-right/duty, omission-right/duty, permission-to-act/no-right-to-omission, permission-to-omit/no-right-to-action, unprotected liberty, protected liberty, power/subjection, and disability/immunity.
UFO-C is extended with social permission, freedom, social power, social subjection, social no-power, and social no-subjection so UFO-L's legal specializations have social foundations.
The thesis identifies future association types—`Results in`, `Depends On`, `Manifestation of`, `Satisfies`, and `Violates`—but does not formalize them in the delivered ontology.

## Evaluation & results

The systematic mapping refined more than 5,000 initial hits to 269 candidates and 128 selected studies from 1995–2014.
Among the 128, 35 (about 27%) used legal theory directly, 44 (about 34%) indirectly, and 49 (about 38%) not at all; among 47 ontology proposals, 32% used no foundational or core ontology.
The controlled interpretation experiment had 37 participants: 20 in text-plus-UFO-L and 17 in text-plus-Nòmos; 25 participated offline and 12 online.
Participants had 60 minutes; 65% reported no legal knowledge, 76% knew or had experience with UFO/OntoUML, and 78% did not know UFO-L.
Text received 45/111 subjective clarity points, UFO-L 97/140, and Nòmos 66/119; 16/20 UFO-L participants said relations became clearer and 17/20 did not need to return to the text.
A chi-square clarity comparison reported `X² = 12.19`, one degree of freedom, and `α = 0.001`, rejecting the text-versus-UFO-L null under the thesis's test construction.
Correct-answer totals were 215/296 for text, 233/280 for UFO-L, and 119/187 for Nòmos; unanswered counts were 45, 8, and 24 respectively.
A McNemar comparison over 200 paired responses reported `X² = 6.26` against critical `5.2`, favoring UFO-L over text on selected matched questions.
One selected UFO-L-versus-Nòmos comparison reported 85% versus 6% correct.
A 17-person order-control check excluded seven participants without conceptual-modeling knowledge; the retained comparison was 83% correct for G1 versus 79.29% for the control group.
Crucially, the thesis's final chapter characterizes the qualitative results as satisfactory but the quantitative tests as inconclusive and calls for at least 30 participants per group.
Applications showed that UFO-L could expose correlated positions omitted by text, represent multiple perspectives in a judicial case, reconstruct flight-passenger rights, and ground LawV and an ArchiMate contract extension.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

Alexy's civil-law foundation may not interoperate cleanly with common-law systems in which precedent has a different structural role.
The artifact covers a narrow slice of substantive subjective rights; legal process, jurisdiction, responsibility, institutions, intentionality, and many legal universals remain incomplete.
Norm propositions, detailed norm classification, rule conflict, principle collision, balancing, defeasibility, violation, and temporal/spatial norm interaction are future work.
The experiment is small, predominantly computing-oriented, and biased toward participants already familiar with UFO/OntoUML; only three participants reported legal professional experience.
Every group saw text first, so learning and carryover remain concerns despite the small order-control check and excluded outliers.
The clarity measures pool non-equivalent question counts and subjective responses; the author acknowledges insufficient statistical power and inconclusive quantitative results.
Explicit correlated positions improve completeness but add lines and visual density, creating an unresolved explicitation-versus-comprehensibility trade-off.
The contract study uses a small set of AWS clauses and does not test drafting accuracy, enforceability, jurisdictional interpretation, or real lawyer work product.
The reference ontology has no native bitemporal assertion model, exact source-span provenance, uncertainty status, competing interpretation objects, or operational KG serialization.
Treating every modeled normative description as expressing at least one norm excludes preambles and other legally relevant text that may matter as evidence or interpretive context.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- `semantic-foundation / SKOS and @beep/ontology`: take the explicit distinction among normative text, interpreted norm, legal role, position, and relator; reject importing the full UFO-L metaphysics into SKOS labels until an application needs its constraints.
- `epistemic slice`: port Legal Relator as a bitemporal, evidence-bearing n-ary edge whose paired positions, grounding event, valid interval, observation interval, jurisdiction, and exact text spans are independently addressable claims.
- `epistemic contradiction triage`: implement separate pathways for rule conflict, principle collision, disputed event occurrence, and competing norm interpretation; UFO-L names these gaps but its delivered static model does not solve them.
- `patent tooling / uspto-patent-driver-depth and agentic-cad-patent-tooling`: reuse power/subjection and disability/immunity for examiner authority, applicant procedural powers, assignments, licenses, and agent authorization; make every inferred correlate traceable to the governing record.
- `OIP solo-practice workflows`: use the eight relation patterns as contract-review completeness checks and matter-diagram primitives, but validate with lawyers and manage the visual-density trade-off before treating diagrams as clearer work product.
- `agent memory / Cognee`: preserve normative descriptions, interpretation claims, relator instances, events, and agent viewpoints as separate memory nodes so later agents can revisit a conclusion without overwriting its source or temporal context.
