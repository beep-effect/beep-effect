# LLM-Assisted Ontology Engineering and Construction of a French Legal Knowledge Graph

## Problem & positioning

- French industrial-maintenance regulations are complex, evolving, and difficult to integrate into Computerized Maintenance Management Systems.
- Existing legal extraction work often stops at dataset-level entities and relations instead of delivering an ontology-grounded, end-to-end knowledge graph.
- ELI and SEMLEG provide legal foundations, while maintenance ontologies provide activity and asset concepts, but neither directly supplies the required maintenance-regulation relation vocabulary.
- The paper combines open LLM extraction for ontology induction with closed, ontology-guided extraction for full-corpus graph construction.
- The work is explicitly preliminary: it emphasizes vocabulary compactness and structural alignment rather than adjudicated factual correctness.

## Approach / architecture

- Competency questions cover actors, affected artifacts, contextual conditions, and legal sources for maintenance obligations.
- A SEMLEG-derived core retains `Actor`, `Action`, `Artifact`, `Condition`, `Source`, `Location`, `Reason`, `Situation`, and `Time`, plus compatible object properties.
- The corpus contains 6,370 official Légifrance regulatory articles across 20 topics, selected through references in the Apave regulatory guide.
- Ontology engineering uses a stratified sample of 1,389 articles, approximately 10% of each domain–document-title pair.
- Prompt one extracts entities and types them with compact Turtle Light class declarations.
- Prompt two performs open relation extraction without a predefined predicate vocabulary, constrained only by subject/object classes.
- Prompt three assigns triples to `maintenanceActivity`, `anotherLegalActivity`, or `legalCrossReference`; only maintenance triples drive induction.
- Entity labels are fused within each class at cosine similarity greater than 0.7; legal references and numeric labels are excluded.
- Predicate labels are fused within subject-class/object-class signature groups at the same threshold; the most frequent variant becomes canonical.
- Property discovery samples 5% of triples per property, preserves at least one triple per signature, and divides the sample into 15 batches.
- The discovery prompt outputs label, domain, range, textual evidence, definition, competency-question alignment, and confidence; results become OWL axioms.
- Full-corpus extraction is closed over ontology-provided admissible properties and signatures, followed by fusion, tabular staging, and RML lifting to RDF.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- Two `SemLegM` ontology variants preserve the same SEMLEG core while isolating OpenAI- and Mistral-induced maintenance properties.
- Shared induced relations include `appliesTo`, `composedOf`, `performedAtLocation`, and `responsibleFor`.
- OpenAI induces finer purpose, sequence, and interaction predicates such as `aimsToAction`, `precededBy`, and `transmittedTo`.
- Mistral favors normative/compliance predicates such as `hasModality`, `verifiedBy`, and `performedUnderCondition`.
- A relation signature is the predicate plus its subject-class/domain and object-class/range combination; property reuse and exact signature reuse are evaluated separately.
- ELI and DCTERMS model legal documents and metadata; CNT models content; SKOS and PROV describe extracted entities and provenance.
- Web Annotation connects mentions to textual evidence.
- Each relation is reified as both `rdf:Statement` and `semleg:ExtractedRelation`, allowing evidence and contextual metadata to attach to an assertion.
- This reification pattern preserves the relation-statement count through label fusion while entity, predicate, signature, and annotation duplicates collapse.
- The pipeline makes ontology induction evidence-bearing, but its closed extraction constraints remain prompt guidance rather than formally enforced validation.

## Evaluation & results

- GPT-4.1/`text-embedding-3-large` and `mistral-large-2512`/`mistral-embed-2312` run under the same prompting protocol at temperature 0.
- Entity extraction uses a fixed 4k-token context; relation discovery uses 10k tokens.
- The OpenAI ontology has 75 maintenance-specific properties and 105 signatures; Mistral has 44 properties and 59 signatures.
- They share 21 maintenance-related properties and 18 signatures.
- Fusion preserves 75,870 Mistral and 51,657 OpenAI reified relation statements.
- Mistral fusion reduces entities from 74,035 to 20,827, object properties from 2,643 to 500, and RDF triples from 2,119,485 to 1,131,066.
- OpenAI fusion reduces entities from 53,804 to 38,339, object properties from 2,649 to 2,031, and RDF triples from 1,507,755 to 1,311,916.
- JSON validity is 100% in all four fused/non-fused settings.
- Class coverage is 99.97% for Mistral and 99.99% for OpenAI.
- Property coverage rises with fusion from 82.51% to 96.42% for Mistral and from 81.52% to 85.11% for OpenAI.
- Exact signature compliance rises from 49.86% to 72.61% for Mistral and from 56.94% to 61.03% for OpenAI.
- Qualitative review finds 39 inconsistent signatures in fused Mistral output and 52 in fused OpenAI output.
- SPARQL competency-question tests retrieve actor roles and legal justifications, but no query-level accuracy is reported.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- Prompt constraints are not backed by a formal validation or repair loop; SHACL is deferred to future work.
- Exact signature compliance remains only 72.61% at best, revealing substantial unseen or incorrect domain–range combinations.
- Entity typing mistakes propagate into relation errors, including legal sources classified as artifacts.
- Models encode polarity or modality inside predicates such as `cannotApplyFor` and `mustNotExceed`, causing vocabulary explosion.
- Some predicates remain French despite instructions requiring English labels.
- Embedding fusion can erase legally important distinctions among obligations, prohibitions, responsibilities, and conditions.
- Metrics measure structured output and ontology alignment, not truth, completeness, legal interpretation, or evidence-span accuracy.
- The qualitative audit does not state sample size, reviewer count, agreement, or adjudication procedure.
- Each ontology is paired with the LLM that induced it, so model and ontology effects are not independently crossed.
- The corpus is limited to French maintenance regulation; temporal updates and cross-domain portability are future work.
- Reified statements provide slots for provenance, but the evaluation does not establish whether every evidence link is correct or sufficiently precise.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** Take the SEMLEG-core-plus-induced-vocabulary layering and signature-aware term proposals; reject automatic embedding fusion as ontology identity without lexical review and SKOS mapping status.
- **epistemic slice:** Reuse reified extracted relations with PROV and Web Annotation evidence spans, adding assertion confidence, reviewer state, valid time, transaction time, and contradiction edges.
- **patent tooling — uspto-patent-driver-depth:** Apply open discovery on a stratified prosecution sample, then closed signature-guided extraction over office actions; validate applicant/examiner/document/event ranges with SHACL before ingestion.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Answer competency questions over responsible actors, applicable conditions, sources, and justifications, while exposing the exact regulation passage behind every operational recommendation.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** Treat unseen predicates and signatures as quarantined schema-change candidates, not durable vocabulary; preserve model, prompt, evidence, and fusion lineage for replay.
