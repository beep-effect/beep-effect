# An Empirical Analysis of Patent Citation Relevance and Applicant Strategy

## Problem & positioning

- Patent-citation studies commonly treat every citation as a binary, equally informative link, even though citations may be technologically remote or strategically submitted.
- That assumption contaminates patent-value, innovation, spillover, and prosecution analyses when a citation exists for reasons other than technological similarity.
- The paper adds a continuous textual-relevance measure—the Jaccard Index of unique title/abstract keywords—to each backward citation.
- It uses relevance to test applicant search strategy, patent value, grant outcomes, and “burying” of a few relevant references among many less relevant ones.
- The legal tension is explicit: applicants must disclose known material art but need not search; relevant citations may threaten allowance while strengthening an issued patent against later validity challenges.
- Two large observational datasets separate value among granted public-firm patents from grant outcomes among both granted and abandoned applications.
- The work is empirical legal strategy analysis, not a novelty classifier: textual overlap is a relevance proxy and office-action use is a more direct signal of rejection relevance.

## Approach / architecture

- For two patent documents, the Jaccard Index is the size of the intersection of unique substantive title/abstract words divided by their union, bounded from 0 to 1.
- Cleaning removes stop words, numeric-only and one-character keywords; references with fewer than 10 keywords and citation pairs scoring exactly 0 or 1 are excluded in the Patent Dataset construction.
- The Patent Dataset covers patents issued from 2003–2012 to publicly traded firms, requires at least one applicant citation, excludes same-firm cited patents and most domestic-priority continuations, and includes 442,609 observations initially.
- Patent value is proxied by whether owners pay maintenance fees at four, eight, and twelve years.
- OLS linear-probability models use firm fixed effects, application-year-by-USPC-class fixed effects, controls, and standard errors clustered by firm and application year; a logit robustness check gives unchanged inferences where reported.
- Applicant citation count and average relevance are decile-ranked to compare coefficient magnitudes; maximum relevance is an alternate specification.
- The Application Dataset covers noncontinuation filings from 2009–2012, excludes cases still pending in 2015, includes zero-citation cases as zero, and totals 474,864 granted or abandoned applications.
- Grant models separately examine applicant citations, examiner citations, and citations actually used in office actions, controlling for entity size, forward citations, inventors, foreign priority, examiner experience, U.S. address, and pendency.
- Burying is tested through applicant citation count; citation-volume classes (`routine <20`, `difficult 20–100`, `extreme 101–250`, `impossible 251+`); and a signed average Jaccard ratio.
- The ratio normalizes each applicant citation by the application's maximum Jaccard score, averages the ratios, then multiplies by -1; values nearer zero indicate a few high-relevance items amid many weak ones.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- The natural graph has `Patent`, `Application`, `PriorArtDocument`, `Applicant`, `Examiner`, `Firm`, `OfficeAction`, `Claim`, `TechnologyClass`, and `MaintenanceEvent` entities.
- A citation edge is not homogeneous: it needs `citedBy` provenance (applicant, examiner, both), textual similarity, submission time, office-action use, rejection role, and source record.
- `CITES` should therefore be a qualified edge or citation-event node rather than a bare adjacency; the study shows that edge attributes materially change inference.
- `USED_IN_REJECTION` is a distinct relation from appearing on a face citation list: office-action references behave as expected, while aggregate applicant/examiner citations do not.
- The Jaccard score is a derived evidence feature over document versions, tokenizer rules, and fields used; it is not a semantic equivalence axiom.
- Burying is a distributional graph pattern: one or more high-relevance citation edges co-occur with a large low-relevance neighborhood on the same application.
- Maintenance payments are time-indexed value-proxy events, not timeless patent properties; eligibility and prior payment condition later observations.
- Grant/abandonment, filing, citation submission, office action, maintenance, and lapse are temporally ordered prosecution events suitable for bitemporal representation.
- Applicant and examiner citation origins can overlap: 6.6% of office-action references in the study group were identified by both, making provenance multi-valued rather than exclusive.
- The paper supplies no formal ontology or KG, but demonstrates why patent graphs require provenance, edge weights, event context, and temporal qualifiers before analytics.

## Evaluation & results

- Patent Dataset means are 16.62 applicant citations and 0.09 average Jaccard; the citation count is strongly right-skewed, with maximum 2,633.
- Four-, eight-, and twelve-year maintenance observations number 442,609, 396,924, and 170,887; payment rates in the conditioned samples are 90%, 81%, and 70%.
- For four-year payment, applicant citation count and average Jaccard coefficients are 0.018 and 0.010, both statistically significant.
- At eight years those coefficients are 0.021 and 0.026; at twelve years citation count falls to an insignificant 0.009 while average Jaccard remains significant at 0.026.
- Maximum-Jaccard robustness tests preserve the conclusion that relevance predicts later maintenance better than raw count.
- Application Dataset grant rate is 67%; examiners average 7.13 citations at Jaccard 0.09, applicants 10.64 at Jaccard 0.06.
- Eighty percent of applications have fewer than 20 total citations and 16% have 20–100; higher-volume categories are rare.
- Contrary to H3a/H3b, aggregate examiner relevance and applicant relevance positively associate with grant: average-Jaccard coefficients are 0.021 and 0.028.
- Maximum-Jaccard grant coefficients are likewise positive: 0.053 for examiner citations and 0.142 for applicant citations.
- Office-action evidence reverses the direction: citation count is -0.036, average Jaccard -0.010, and maximum Jaccard -0.020, all statistically significant.
- In the study group, 93.4% of office-action-cited references were examiner-identified; 6.6% had both applicant and examiner provenance.
- All three burying proxies positively associate with grant; applicant citation count is 0.161 and signed average Jaccard ratio is a stronger predictor at 0.187.
- Relative to routine volume, difficult, extreme, and impossible categories have coefficients 0.082, 0.085, and 0.033.
- These are observational associations with adjusted R-squared values around 0.11–0.18, not causal estimates that burying itself produces allowance.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The authors acknowledge that maintenance payment is an imperfect value proxy affected by finances, valuation effort, expectations, and strategic motives.
- Publicly traded, well-resourced assignees and historical 2003–2012 grants limit generalization to individuals, small entities, later CPC practice, and current examination.
- Patent ownership and classification data availability drives the time windows; post-2012 applications are not studied.
- PatentsView's “other” citation category is treated as applicant-origin because third-party submissions are rare, which can still misattribute individual edges.
- Title/abstract bag-of-words Jaccard ignores claims, semantic synonymy, functional equivalence, citation passages, legal availability dates, and claim-specific anticipation or obviousness.
- Removing Jaccard 0/1 pairs, short references, same-firm art, and several continuation types improves tractability but changes the population and may remove legally important cases.
- Firm fixed effects mitigate time-invariant assignee confounding but do not establish causation; attorney quality, claim drafting, examination unit, and unobserved invention traits may drive both citations and outcomes.
- The paper itself offers multiple unresolved explanations for positive relevance/grant associations: drafting around art, examiner underuse, or an unknown confounder.
- Burying proxies infer a strategy from citation distributions; they do not observe intent, examiner attention, document order, or whether the highest-overlap reference is legally material.
- Statistical significance is unsurprising with hundreds of thousands of cases; practical effect sizes and out-of-sample predictive performance receive less attention.
- Office-action use is stronger evidence than face citation, but even it does not establish that a reference was dispositive or that every claim was rejected on it.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- `patent tooling / uspto-patent-driver-depth`: ingest citation provenance, office-action use, filing/version dates, applicant/examiner overlap, and similarity-method metadata; reject a single unqualified `cites` edge.
- `epistemic slice / bitemporal edges and evidence spans`: model each citation submission and rejection use as separate events linked to exact office-action passages and claims, with known/valid times and USPTO source identifiers.
- `epistemic contradiction triage`: keep face-list relevance, office-action reliance, and model similarity as separate claims; the paper's sign reversal shows they must not be collapsed into one “prior art relevance” truth.
- `agentic-cad-patent-tooling`: add citation-burying diagnostics that rank the high-relevance tail and explain distribution shape, but present them as review prioritization rather than accusations of intent.
- `OIP solo-practice workflows`: generate prosecution-review queues from office-action-linked art first, then high-similarity applicant art; preserve attorney judgment on materiality, claim mapping, and drafting-around strategy.
- `semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology)`: type citation roles and prosecution events as governed concepts while keeping Jaccard and future semantic scores as versioned observations, not ontology axioms.
