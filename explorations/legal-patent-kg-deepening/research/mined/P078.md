# Beyond Probabilistic Similarity: Structural, Temporal, and Causal Limitations of Retrieval-Augmented Generation in the Legal Domain

## Problem & positioning

The paper argues that persistent legal-RAG failures are not merely residual hallucinations but symptoms of an architectural mismatch.
Similarity retrieval optimizes topical relevance, whereas legality-critical retrieval must recover hierarchical context, the operative state at a target time, and the institutional acts grounding that state.
The scope is `quaestio juris`: which norms apply, in what version, and with what provenance.
It primarily treats legislative and constitutional retrieval; disputed facts, evidence assessment, judicial discretion, and open-textured moral judgment are excluded.
The method is a critical, theory-driven survey organized by functional relevance, not a systematic mapping review or benchmark comparison.
The paper derives computational requirements from legal theory and uses them to diagnose vector RAG, Graph RAG, temporal KGs, provenance systems, and tool-using agents.
It calls the target posture “deterministic-by-design”: probabilistic models may plan and synthesize, but legality-critical retrieval operations use explicit, reproducible contracts.

## Approach / architecture

The analysis begins with three ontological commitments of legal knowledge: hierarchical/mereological structure, diachronic change under institutional operational closure, and causal traceability of provenance.
It maps those commitments to three retrieval pathologies with operational definitions, mechanisms, examples, and detection criteria.
`Mereological blindness` returns authentic fragments without governing ancestors or cannot exhaustively answer hierarchy-scoped queries.
Its tests are descendant completeness, ancestor closure/subordination signaling, and boundary/expansion integrity.
`Diachronic blindness` cannot reconstruct the normative state for a date or distinguish legally material coexisting versions.
Its tests cover point-in-time recovery, valid versus transaction time, structural-unit versioning, event-bounded validity, and interpretive–statutory coherence.
`Causal opacity` cannot trace a returned provision, interpretation, or synthesized claim through the institutional acts that produced it.
Its tests cover grounding-event attribution, full lineage, dual statutory/interpretive chains, claim-level provenance, and deterministic audit-chain composition.
The positive architecture has four jointly necessary commitments: ontological primacy, event reification, bitemporal correctness, and deterministic interaction protocols.
The language model translates intent into calls and synthesizes outputs; fixed primitives perform point-in-time retrieval, lineage traversal, impact analysis, version comparison, and provenance reconstruction.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

The paper's formal retrieval substrate is a graph whose first-class nodes include normative acts, stable legal references, structural units, versioned textual states, interpretive states, authorities, and validity-affecting events.
Typed `partOf` relations encode asymmetric legal mereology so a dependent item can recover its governing caput, article, section, and chapter.
Stable references are separated from versioned states, avoiding the collapse of enduring legal identity into one mutable text blob.
Events are reified with date, authority, type, affected provisions, and typed effects such as create, amend, suspend, repeal, supersede, interpret, or terminate.
Each textual or interpretive state has independent valid-time and transaction-time intervals.
Valid time denotes when the state has legal effect; transaction time denotes when the official information system records or knows it.
This accommodates vacatio legis, retroactivity, delayed or conditional effectiveness, corrigenda, and later-recorded knowledge.
Statutory-version and interpretive-authority chains remain distinct but are surfaced together when operative meaning diverges from unchanged text.
Parallel operative interpretations require contextual indexing by court, chamber, procedure, territorial scope, precedential force, and affected parties rather than a single global state.
Claim-level support is possible because provisions, versions, interpretations, events, dates, and authorities are individually addressable and connected.
The paper explicitly rejects bottom-up LLM-extracted entity graphs as sufficient substitutes for a legally decreed hierarchy.
It also rejects timestamps on edges as a complete temporal model because they answer when a relation held but not which act caused the transition.

## Evaluation & results

No empirical system evaluation is reported; the contribution is formal, diagnostic, and prescriptive.
Canonical examples illustrate each pathology using Brazilian constitutional law.
The mereological example shows that Article 150 item VI(b) is legally underdetermined without the caput and item identifying the addressees and tax prohibition.
The temporal example tracks additions to Article 6: housing in 2000, food in 2010, transportation in 2015, and basic income in 2021; a current-text retriever would misanswer a 1999 query.
The provenance example traces shifts in the interpretation of presumption of innocence through named judicial decisions while the constitutional text remains stable.
The paper reviews partial solutions and concludes that no widely documented system establishes full co-satisfaction of the four commitments.
It proposes six future benchmark families: structural-context closure, descendant completeness, point-in-time/bitemporal correctness, interpretive–statutory coherence, provenance-chain reconstruction, and claim-level auditability.
Precision, recall, ranking metrics, RAGAS-style faithfulness, and LLM judges are judged insufficient alone because they test answer–context alignment rather than context–law alignment.
There are no dataset sizes, measured error rates, confidence intervals, or performance comparisons attributable to this paper.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

The authors delimit the framework to norm identification, not factual reconstruction, witness credibility, evidentiary sufficiency, discretion, or substantive application of law to facts.
Statutes and constitutions are first-class; case law and interpretive time are sketched as an extension requiring substantially more modeling.
The survey is selectively theory-driven rather than systematic, so coverage and selection bias cannot be quantified.
The commitments are abstract and admit multiple implementations; the paper does not prove their sufficiency or provide an executable reference architecture.
Determinism guarantees reproducibility only conditional on a correct, complete, maintained substrate; ontology, ingestion, classification, and legal interpretation errors move upstream rather than disappear.
Ontology-primary curation is expensive and jurisdiction-specific, and hybrid legal orders complicate authority and event taxonomies.
Tacit overruling and unresolved doctrinal conflict cannot safely be reduced to deterministic transitions without authoritative recognition or explicit `unknown` states.
Typed primitives constrain agent errors but may become an incomplete API whose omissions prevent valid research paths.
The framework needs empirical study of latency, maintenance cost, recall under incomplete coverage, user trust, and the legal consequences of clean audit trails over wrong substrate data.
