# The FLINT Ontology: An Actor-Based Model of Legal Relations

## Problem & positioning

- The paper targets normative coordination: helping a community make explicit which norms it accepts and how those norms regulate behavior.
- Its practical problem is the difficulty of recording and documenting both human and AI-driven normative decisions.
- FLINT is positioned as a high-level language in which legal experts can agree on interpretations while retaining a route to technical execution.
- Rather than beginning with violations or static obligation labels, FLINT models the action space available to agents across normative states.
- The authors argue that an explicit state model shows both how acts change a normative system and how the current state determines whether an act is legal.
- This makes the work closer to a transition-system account of legal powers and duties than to a complete Hohfeldian catalog.
- UFO-L is the closest comparison: both are Searlean and represent power-liability relations, but FLINT decouples institutional acts from legal relations and makes state consequences explicit.

## Approach / architecture

- Informal FLINT concepts from prior work are converted into competency questions, a subset of which appears in Table 1.
- Questions cover act performers and recipients, valid situations, post-act duties, atomic versus complex facts, agent capabilities, and duty creation or termination.
- The ontology is implemented in RDF/OWL and organized around frame individuals rather than treating each normative pattern as an OWL class.
- A FLINT interpretation is a graph of mutually referring Act frames and Fact frames.
- Fact frames characterize a normative state; Act frames specify state transitions through preconditions and postconditions.
- Logical consistency is checked with HermiT 1.4 against sample data.
- Expressiveness is exercised through competency questions, example models, and use of the data model in software tooling.
- The worked library case turns three prose regulations into lending, returning, and fine-giving acts connected to facts and duties.
- The approach assumes normative-state change occurs through an act, so the implicit imposition of a fine becomes an explicit act with its own conditions.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- The paper reports a compact ontology of 13 classes; this count describes the paper version, not the later repository snapshot mined as R25.
- `Frame` is the central container, partitioned into `Act` and `Fact`.
- Facts cover propositions, agents, objects, and actions that can characterize a state.
- Atomic facts are distinguished from complex facts; complex facts include conjunctive, disjunctive, and negated structures.
- An Act links to `hasActor`, `hasRecipient`, `hasObject`, `hasAction`, and `hasPrecondition` fillers.
- The same Act links to facts made true or false through `creates` and `terminates` postcondition relations.
- Actor and recipient encode a Hohfeldian power-liability relation: the actor may exercise the state-changing power against the recipient.
- A Duty is modeled as a special Fact because whether the duty holds is part of the normative state.
- Each Duty has a holder and claimant, representing the two ends of a Hohfeldian duty-right relation.
- Duties are not timeless: an act creates them and another act must be capable of terminating them.
- The library model uses one shared Book node across lend and return frames so the terminating act applies to the same object that was borrowed.
- A fine-giving act has a complex precondition, terminates the return duty, and creates a pay-fine duty.
- Frames are prototypes for scenario-level acts and facts, not the concrete occurrences themselves.
- Frames are individuals so they can carry intricate links without OWL punning and can express identity across slots, such as the same borrowed and returned book.
- The authors also reject the claim that a concrete act is conceptually an instance of an act-frame prototype.
- `hasSource` connects frames to source elements, establishing a basic trace from interpretation nodes back to legal material.
- The state-machine reading is operational: facts define possible states, preconditions gate valid acts, and postconditions define reachable successor states.

## Evaluation & results

- The ontology contains 13 classes in the paper version and is published as an RDF/OWL implementation.
- HermiT 1.4 found the sample data logically consistent; the paper gives no run count, ontology metrics, or failure cases.
- Table 1 presents 11 example competency questions across Act, Fact, and Duty concepts.
- The illustrative library regulation has three source rules and is interpreted as three acts: lend a book, return a book, and give a fine.
- Figures 2 and 3 demonstrate an object-preserving duty lifecycle and a conjunctive precondition for fine imposition.
- Validation also included modeling example cases and using the ontology data model in software tooling, but no user-study or coverage numbers are reported.
- There is no precision, recall, runtime, competency-question pass rate, legal-domain coverage measure, or comparison benchmark.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The paper validates intelligibility on a toy library example, not on a contested or procedurally complex body of law.
- Automated normative reasoning is future work: the described norm engine, SHACL constraints, and inference rules were still under development.
- A dynamic-logic formalization and its decidability and complexity analysis were also future work.
- Automated conversion from legal text relies on ongoing semantic-role-labeling work for actions, actors, recipients, and objects.
- The proposed trace from annotations to a full formal model was an extension in progress, not evaluated here.
- Only power-liability and duty-right relations are explicit; privilege-no-claim and immunity-disability are not modeled as a closed eight-position schema.
- The frame model does not itself provide bitemporal validity, transaction history, uncertainty, competing interpretations, evidence spans, or contradiction adjudication.
- Preconditions say when an act is legal, but the paper does not model defeasibility, exception priority, jurisdiction, authority levels, or burdens of proof.
- Duties are expected to have creating and terminating acts, yet the paper does not report OWL or SHACL cardinality constraints enforcing that requirement.
- Treating all normative change as act-mediated is a useful discipline but may force exogenous events, time passage, or automatic legal effects into artificial act nodes.
- Prototype frames and concrete occurrences need an explicit relation and identity policy before the model can drive event-sourced workflows.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- `semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology)`: take FLINT's named Act/Fact/Duty vocabulary as a donor scheme, but reject it as a replacement for the synthesis's closed eight-position Hohfeld domain.
- `epistemic slice`: model each interpreted frame as a revisable claim linked to exact evidence spans, then add valid/transaction time and competing-interpretation state absent from FLINT.
- `epistemic-bitemporal-edge-core`: take preconditioned `Act -> creates|terminates -> Fact` transitions as power-exercise events that append edge revisions instead of overwriting legal positions.
- `patent tooling (uspto-patent-driver-depth, agentic-cad-patent-tooling)`: use actor/recipient/object/precondition slots as an authorization gate for assignments, licenses, waivers, and prosecution acts before changing the matter graph.
- `OIP solo-practice workflows`: express filings, notices, deadlines, and fee consequences as explicit acts and state facts, while keeping time passage and office-generated events distinguishable from practitioner action.
- `agent memory (Cognee)`: retain frame prototypes, concrete act occurrences, and their source-backed state transitions as separate nodes so memory can distinguish a rule from an attempted or effective act.
