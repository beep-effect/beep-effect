# Enhancing Semantic Text Similarity with Functional Semantic Knowledge (FOP) in Patents

## Problem & positioning

Patent similarity supports portfolio analysis, prior-art retrieval, litigation analysis, forecasting, and novelty work.
The authors argue that keywords, co-word methods, and SAO triples miss fine-grained properties, non-functional relations, and flexible functional combinations; standardized patent STS benchmarks were also absent.

## Approach

The paper introduces Function–Object–Property knowledge, prepares matching and ranking STS datasets, trains sequence-labeling models on annotated patents, and compares similarity methods.
The preview names IPC codes, weighting, patent-pretrained vectors, and interaction-based deep models as ingredients.
Three representative patents are used in retrieval case studies.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

FOP separates functions, their target objects, and property/parameter features.
It accommodates functional and non-functional combinations that verb-centered SAO triples can miss.
A functional semantic ontology supports extraction and normalization, but the capture does not expose its classes, relation inventory, axioms, or serialization.

## Evaluation & results

The abstract reports FOP methods outperforming SAO approaches on the released STS tasks.
IPC codes, weights, and pretrained patent embeddings improve similarity, while averaged FOP embeddings in interaction models are recommended.
The capture shows qualitative claims but no complete metric tables or dataset sizes.

## Limitations

The page is an article preview; detailed dataset construction, annotation quality, metrics, and ablations are paywalled.
Three retrieval cases do not establish broad legal-retrieval reliability.
Textual similarity must not be treated as a conclusion about claim scope, novelty, or infringement.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- Make FOP extraction evidence-bearing at the text-span level and preserve non-functional relations.
- Keep matching and ranking benchmarks separate and version their label provenance.
- Reuse FOP as the immediate methodological predecessor to FOPNet, not as an interchangeable final graph.
