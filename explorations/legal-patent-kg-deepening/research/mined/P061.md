# Ontological Foundations for Conceptual Modelling

## Problem & positioning

- This guest editorial argues that conceptual modeling inevitably carries ontological and epistemological commitments, whether or not designers make them explicit.
- Conceptual modeling identifies essential domain concepts and constraints for understanding, communication, and problem solving; a domain ontology is a consensus-oriented special kind of conceptual model.
- Languages such as UML, ORM, ER, REA, TROPOS, and OWL can therefore be evaluated against formal-ontology theories rather than only syntax or computational behavior.
- The special issue positions philosophical ontology, logic, linguistics, and cognitive science as foundations for sound modeling languages, methods, and tools.
- A recurring problem is collapsing conceptual modeling, logical design, and implementation into one artifact and letting tractability constraints impoverish the domain model.
- The editorial synthesizes five selected contributions and extracts a common engineering agenda rather than proposing a single new domain ontology.

## Approach / architecture

- The authors review five papers spanning ontology-engineering method, automated OntoClean support, measurement semantics, part-whole reasoning, and epistemological analysis.
- They organize ontology engineering into conceptual, logical-design, and implementation levels, echoing DOGMA's meaning-independence principle.
- Conceptual models prioritize domain truthfulness and human comprehensibility; logical designs respond to architecture and nonfunctional constraints; implementations target concrete languages and platforms.
- Strong conceptual axiomatization may therefore coexist with a less expressive but decidable deployment representation.
- Engineering support should expose usable patterns, profiles, transformations, and checks while hiding formal complexity from ordinary modelers.
- The discussion distinguishes descriptive metaphysics, which tracks categories in language/cognition, from revisionary metaphysics, which may depart from common sense to track scientific reality.
- Because conceptual models serve human communication, the authors favor a cognitively and linguistically serious foundational theory while requiring its commitments to be explicit.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- DOGMA separates a language-independent conceptual level, a chosen logical paradigm, and an implementation in a concrete language/environment.
- Its methodology combines construction phases with vision, feasibility, project management, natural-language analysis, meaning negotiation, and collaborative interaction patterns.
- OntoClean classifies universals through rigidity, unity, identity, and notional dependence, then constrains admissible subsumption relations.
- `Person` is rigid and `Student` anti-rigid; `Student` cannot subsume `Person` without ontological inconsistency.
- AEON detects lexico-syntactic evidence on the Web, proposes OntoClean meta-property tags, reifies concepts and subsumption links into an OWL DL ontology, and checks constraints with a DL reasoner.
- A `Quality` inheres in a bearer; an observation yields a quale located in a structured `QualitySpace`.
- A `SemanticReferenceSpace` partitions a quality space and associates communicable signs with regions; measurement identifies the named region containing an observation result.
- Multiple reference spaces can partition the same quality space at different granularities and support explicit transformations among measurement systems.
- The reviewed parthood work aligns a taxonomy of parts/wholes with DOLCE categories: processes, physical objects, social objects, amounts of matter, and spatial regions.
- Its `RBox compatibility` service checks that a hierarchy of specialized parthood roles remains compatible with the corresponding hierarchies of allowed role fillers.
- The epistemology framework asks whether modeling represents external reality or purpose-bound consensus, whether evaluation uses reality or expert agreement, and whether quality is correspondence or social acceptance.
- The editorial's own key pattern is artifact separation: conceptualization, conceptual model, logical design, and executable encoding are related transformations, not interchangeable representations.

## Evaluation & results

- The special issue received 17 submissions from 10 listed countries; 5 papers were selected after review.
- This editorial reports no new controlled experiment, ontology population, competency-question suite, or reasoner benchmark of its own.
- It summarizes AEON experiments comparing automatic tagging/checking with expert-produced “Golden Standards,” but gives no numeric outcomes in the editorial.
- The measurement contribution is evaluated through analytic examples of color, granularity, and transformations between reference systems rather than reported quantitative testing.
- The parthood contribution identifies a reasoning gap in then-current description-logic support and proposes RBox compatibility as an additional ontological-correctness test.
- The epistemology paper applies its framework to the highly cited BWW representation model; no scoring or comparative result is reported here.
- The editorial's result is a convergent architecture: expressive conceptual modeling, explicit commitments, constrained mappings, and tools that compile theory into modeler-facing checks.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- As an editorial synthesis, it depends on abbreviated descriptions of five papers and cannot expose all their axioms, datasets, experiments, or boundary conditions.
- The reported submission count and selection process indicate curation, not evidence that the five approaches outperform alternatives.
- “Truthfulness to reality” is not operationalized into a single validation protocol and sits in tension with negotiated meaning and expert-produced golden standards.
- Descriptive metaphysics can preserve human conceptual bias; revisionary science can conflict with practitioner language; the editorial advocates explicit commitments but does not resolve the tradeoff.
- Web-frequency evidence for OntoClean tags may reproduce linguistic ambiguity, popularity bias, and context collapse.
- A three-tier architecture needs traceable, testable mappings or conceptual distinctions can disappear during logical design and implementation.
- The injunction not to sacrifice expressivity for decidability applies to the conceptual layer, but implementers still need a principled tractability budget and failure semantics.
- Neither bitemporal change, source provenance, jurisdiction, defeasibility, contradiction, nor evidentiary uncertainty receives a developed model.
- The domain-ontology-as-consensus formulation risks obscuring minority or adversarial conceptualizations unless competing schemes and provenance remain first-class.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** Preserve the conceptual/logical/implementation split: use richer modeling notes and constraints to design schemes, then publish the tractable SKOS projection with explicit loss mappings.
- **epistemic slice:** Treat expert consensus, Web evidence, and reality-correspondence as different claim warrants; store assessor, method, confidence, source span, and valid time instead of one “correct” tag.
- **patent tooling (uspto-patent-driver-depth):** Use OntoClean-style rigidity and dependence checks on patent taxonomies, but reject automatic Web evidence as dispositive for technical or legal classification.
- **patent tooling (agentic-cad-patent-tooling):** Model a measured feature as bearer → quality → observation/quale → reference-space region → signed value, retaining units, granularity, and transform provenance.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Compile expressive legal conceptualizations into workflow rules only through reviewable mappings; do not let executable-rule limitations redefine the lawyer-facing domain model.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** Keep competing conceptual schemes and their epistemological commitments side by side so shared labels do not create false semantic agreement.
