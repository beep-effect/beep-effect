# Using the Interest Theory of Rights and Hohfeldian Taxonomy to Address a Gap in Machine Learning Methods for Legal Document Analysis

## Problem & positioning

- Legal NLP commonly emphasizes named entities, sentiment, and outcome prediction while omitting the rights, duties, and power relations that make legal texts legally meaningful.
- The paper argues that a model can reach a plausible result for the wrong implicit reasons unless its representation exposes principal legal dimensions.
- It selects the interest theory of rights and Hohfeld's first-order taxonomy as relatively decomposed, cross-theory dimensions for legal-text analysis.
- Interest theory treats a party as holding a right when another party's duty protects that party from a typically detrimental development.
- The paper operationalizes this through a Golden-Rule-style harm-aversion heuristic: ask whether the affected party would wish the act upon itself.
- Its empirical aim is feasibility, not a complete legal reasoner: detect wanted versus unwanted interactions, then distinguish right-duty from privilege-no-right sentences.
- The tested legal domain is United Kingdom religious-discrimination policy text, supplemented by constructed simple subject-verb-object sentences.

## Approach / architecture

- Study 1 reformulates interactions as masked sentences such as whether an affected actor would `[MASK]` like an event, then uses ALBERT's top prediction.
- The Study 1 corpus has 100 randomly generated typically wanted and 100 typically unwanted sentences; expected labels are compared with the predicted masked word.
- Study 2 embeds an input sentence and compares it with four paired semantic poles: require/despise, happy/unhappy, demand continuation/stoppage, and wish continuation/stoppage.
- The four difference vectors are summed into an `axiom` vector; cosine-sign classifies an interaction as wanted or unwanted.
- An alternative retains the four cosine scores, applies PCA, and trains scikit-learn logistic regression with a stated 1-to-7 test split.
- Study 2 uses 100 subject-verb-object sentences written voluntarily by three male lab members over age 22 and manually labels the intended class.
- Study 3 embeds 100 expert-labelled right-duty sentences and 100 privilege-no-right sentences drawn from UK anti-religious-discrimination policy research.
- Six sentence-transformer families are visualized through UMAP and tested by logistic regression; SetFit directly fine-tunes classifiers with an 80:20 split.
- LegalBERT is separately tested; reported training settings for the language models are batch size 16, 20 iterations, and 4 epochs.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- The first-order relation family is `Right ↔ Duty` and `Privilege ↔ NoRight`; each pair is correlative and expresses the same relation from opposing party positions.
- The oppositions are `Right ⟂ NoRight` and `Duty ⟂ Privilege`: duty means an act is required, while privilege means it is optional rather than required.
- A relation is inherently directed and dyadic: a right-holder has a right *against* a duty-bearer regarding an act; reversing actors changes the represented proposition.
- Interest theory supplies a proposed allocation rule: detrimental breach for an affected party is evidence for a duty toward that party and therefore its correlative right.
- The paper deliberately excludes Hohfeld's second-order `Power ↔ Liability` and `Immunity ↔ Disability` relations, although it notes their independent status.
- A useful graph pattern is therefore a reified normative-relation node or qualified edge with bearer, counterparty, regulated act, polarity, source sentence, and classification confidence.
- Correlatives should be derived views of one underlying normative relation, not two independent facts that can drift apart.
- Opposites are logical incompatibility constraints only under the same actors, act, scope, time, and authority; the paper's sentence classifier does not model these qualifiers.
- The work contributes a taxonomy-guided extraction target, not an ontology implementation: it defines no RDF/OWL vocabulary, identities, domains/ranges, axioms in a reasoner, or KG evaluation.
- The harm-aversion vector is best modeled as evidence for a candidate classification, not as an axiom that establishes legal validity.

## Evaluation & results

- ALBERT correctly classifies 99 of 100 typically wanted sentences and 74 of 100 unwanted sentences, for 86.5% overall accuracy.
- The summed four-vector axiom reaches 79.5% accuracy on 200 labelled wanted/unwanted observations; 22% of wanted and 19% of unwanted sentences are misclassified.
- Retaining the four vector comparisons and applying PCA plus logistic regression produces 72.0% accuracy.
- On Hohfeldian relation classification, logistic-regression accuracy ranges from 79.3% to 89.7% across the six reported sentence embeddings.
- Direct language-model classification ranges from 85.0% to 92.5%; `paraphrase-mpnet-base-v2` is highest at 92.5%.
- `all-mpnet-base-v2` and `all-MiniLM-L12-v2` each score 90.0%; the former has the strongest logistic-regression result at 89.7%.
- LegalBERT, pretrained on legal texts, scores 91.7%, below the best general semantic-similarity model.
- The paper contrasts these results with earlier Hohfeldian annotation work reporting F1 values of 40% and 73% on two documents, while noting different setups.
- No confidence intervals, repeated-split variance, external test jurisdiction, calibration analysis, or end-to-end span and actor extraction score is reported.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The authors acknowledge that three demographically narrow, self-selected writers are not population-representative and that personality may influence sentence style.
- Straightforward sentences intentionally avoid vague or convoluted language, named-entity recognition, and coreference resolution; real provisions regularly require all three.
- Random noun-verb combinations create ambiguous labels, as the ALBERT errors involving professional context illustrate.
- The Golden Rule is asserted as a cross-cultural harm heuristic, but preferences, consent, role obligations, collective interests, and lawful sanctions cannot be reduced reliably to lexical wantedness.
- Interest-based detriment may help identify a protected interest without proving that a particular legal source creates an enforceable duty.
- The binary task collapses bearer, counterparty, act, negation, exception, modality, jurisdiction, authority, and temporal scope into a sentence label.
- Only first-order Hohfeldian positions are learned, preventing analysis of amendment, waiver, competence, authorization, liability, and immunity.
- Random train/test splits over 200 related sentences may overstate transfer to new instruments, drafting styles, jurisdictions, or contested facts.
- Accuracy alone hides asymmetric legal costs, class confidence, and whether the correct relation was attributed to the correct parties.
- The paper's explainability claim remains prospective: it does not evaluate explanations, causal feature use, or whether users can audit a classification back to exact text.
- The proposed classifier must remain decision support with human legal review, a safeguard the discussion itself favors for consequential decisions.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- `semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology)`: encode the eight Hohfeldian positions as a curated concept scheme, but put correlativity/opposition constraints in a richer ontology or SHACL layer rather than treating SKOS links as legal axioms.
- `epistemic slice / bitemporal edges and evidence spans`: represent one qualified normative relation with actor roles, act, scope, authority, valid time, known time, source span, extractor version, and confidence; derive the correlative view deterministically.
- `epistemic contradiction triage`: compare right/no-right and duty/privilege only after scope alignment, and queue unresolved classifier disagreements instead of overwriting one relation with another.
- `OIP solo-practice workflows`: surface candidate rights, duties, privileges, and no-rights in policy or agreement review with side-by-side text spans; reject automatic legal conclusions from sentence-level wantedness.
- `agent memory (Cognee)`: use Hohfeldian relation types to structure remembered commitments and permissions, while keeping conversational preferences distinct from legally sourced normative positions.
- `patent tooling / agentic-cad-patent-tooling`: apply the actor-act-counterparty pattern to licenses, assignments, inventor obligations, and prosecution duties; do not use the harm heuristic to infer patent-law obligations.
