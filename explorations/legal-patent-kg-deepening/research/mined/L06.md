# A Patent Recommendation Method Based on KG Representation Learning

## Problem & positioning

Growing patent volume makes product-relevant knowledge increasingly difficult to retrieve.
The paper targets gaps in patent-KG construction accuracy/recall, graph representation of semantic association, and recommenders that ignore direct product–patent relations.

## Approach

The method combines rules and deep learning to extract keywords and construct patent triplet facts.
Graph neural networks encode the graph, while conventional KG representation learning models semantic associations and constraints.
Recommendation operates over a heterogeneous product–patent graph and is evaluated in a practical case.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

The core graph is a heterogeneous network containing products, patents, and extracted triplet facts.
Direct product–patent relationships coexist with learned semantic proximity, avoiding a similarity-only recommender.
The capture does not name the entity/relation schema, extraction rules, embedding model, or negative-sampling policy.

## Evaluation & results

The abstract reports that the method outperforms baselines and can guide innovative design.
The conclusion claims the hybrid construction balances accuracy and recall.
No dataset size, baseline names, metric values, ablations, or statistical tests are visible in the capture.

## Limitations

Evidence is limited to an abstract and truncated section snippets.
The practical case's domain and generalizability are not exposed.
Recommendation relevance is not distinguished from legal relevance, novelty, or infringement risk.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- Preserve explicit product–patent edges alongside embedding-derived candidates.
- Attach extraction method and confidence to every generated triplet.
- Require abstention and human review before using design recommendations for legal conclusions.
