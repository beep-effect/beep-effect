# A Hohfeldian Knowledge Base for LLM-Assisted Legal Information Retrieval in Marine Biodiversity Law

## Problem & positioning

Overlapping biodiversity treaties make actor-specific obligations and entitlements difficult to retrieve for concrete marine genetic-resource scenarios.
The study jointly covers the Convention on Biological Diversity, Nagoya Protocol, and Biodiversity Beyond National Jurisdiction Agreement.
It uses Hohfeld's relational legal positions to avoid flattening norms into broad topics and to make LLM retrieval outputs inspectable.
The evaluated task is rule retrieval: select the structured rows needed for a fact pattern, not generate or score a complete legal opinion.
Contributions comprise HSNKB, an expert-gold fact-pattern dataset, full-scope and actor-constrained pipelines, and model comparison.

## Approach / architecture

Two international-law experts manually map operative treaty clauses into the Hohfeld-Structured Normative Knowledge Base.
Each provision is divided until a row expresses one dominant normative relationship; definitions, preambles, and aspirational provisions are left out.
Fifteen IRAC-style questions progress from single-instrument issues to scenarios requiring cross-treaty reasoning.
One marine-biodiversity-law expert assigns the gold HSNKB row set for every question.
A separate actor-definition dictionary resolves implicit actor descriptions and standardizes names across instruments.
The full-scope pipeline presents every row's identifier, provision number, and natural-language relation sentence to the model.
The actor-constrained pipeline predicts actors first and restricts candidates to rows involving those actors.
Both pipelines use zero-shot Chain-of-Instructions prompting and require structured JSON row identifiers.
Instructions parse actors, jurisdiction, and resource type; apply jurisdictional gating; filter for allocated legal positions; and rank specific rows.
Ranking also accounts for lifecycle stage and traditional knowledge.
Four models run through OpenRouter: Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4, and Kimi K2.
Runs use a fixed seed, temperature zero where supported, and high reasoning effort for the three reasoning models.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

The HSNKB has 377 rows, divided into 193 BBNJ-A, 96 CBD, and 88 NP entries.
Its row schema is: unique ID, treaty, provision number, actor-holder, legal position or action, actor-affected, and expert-written relation sentence.
The atomic semantic pattern is `holder --position/action--> affected actor`, anchored to an article, paragraph, or sub-paragraph.
Hohfeldian correlatives are logically deduced, so a row can preserve paired and directed normative positions rather than an undifferentiated obligation label.
`Contracting Parties` is the modal actor and `duty` is the modal Hohfeldian relation.
Atomic row splitting provides stable retrieval and evaluation identifiers for provisions containing multiple prescriptions.
The actor dictionary is a cross-instrument alignment device for express and implicit actor references.
Retrieved relationships can be visualized as an actor-and-treaty graph in the prototype interface.
The representation intentionally focuses on normative allocations; it is not an event, evidence, or factual world-state ontology.
The paper does not publish formal ontology axioms, RDF/OWL shapes, temporal qualifiers, conflict rules, or a provenance model beyond provision location.

## Evaluation & results

Predicted row sets are compared with expert gold sets using macro precision, recall, and F1 over 15 equally weighted questions.
Full-scope GPT-5 is best overall: precision 0.77, recall 0.53, F1 0.59.
Other full-scope results are Gemini 0.69/0.45/0.53, Claude 0.61/0.50/0.52, and Kimi 0.28/0.58/0.33.
Actor-constrained results are Gemini 0.68/0.49/0.56, Claude 0.64/0.44/0.51, GPT-5 0.74/0.50/0.57, and Kimi 0.30/0.58/0.35.
For actor retrieval, Gemini scores P 0.171/R 0.944/F1 0.286; Claude 0.234/0.992/0.372; GPT-5 0.239/1.000/0.379; Kimi 0.238/0.880/0.368.
The systems predict 23.9-35.6 actors per question, far above the gold mean of 6.2.
The actor filter removes only around 15% of HSNKB because the ubiquitous `Contracting Party` actor is relevant to every fact pattern.
Conditional and multi-actor benefit-sharing duties dispersed across treaties dominate false negatives.
Programmatic cooperation language misread as operative duties creates occasional false positives.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

The authors acknowledge the deliberately small benchmark and propose expansion plus inter-annotator validation.
They identify stable precision with improved recall as the central next step and propose adding IR baselines.
A single expert's gold row sets cannot expose disagreement, multiple valid retrieval sets, or uncertain relevance.
Three closely related treaties do not establish transfer to larger corpora or different legal domains.
Removing definitions and non-operative context can make conditions and specialized treaty terms harder to interpret correctly.
One dominant Hohfeldian relation is useful for atomization but can under-model exceptions, scope, modalities, and nested conditions.
High actor recall is produced by severe over-selection, so it is not evidence of useful actor understanding.
The generic `Contracting Party` hub defeats the actor-filter hypothesis and illustrates the limits of high-degree-node gating.
The evaluation stops at row retrieval and does not test downstream analysis, explanations, legal conclusions, or user outcomes.
No bitemporal validity, reservations, amendment history, textual evidence spans within clauses, or competing doctrinal claims are represented.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- `semantic-foundation / SKOS and @beep/ontology`: encode Hohfeldian positions and treaty actors as controlled concepts with narrower/broader and exact/close mappings; preserve instrument-local terminology.
- `epistemic slice`: turn each row and derived correlative into distinct claims with source spans, derivation provenance, effective/recorded time, confidence, and contradiction or interpretation links.
- `OIP solo-practice workflows`: adopt ranked atomic-rule retrieval as the transparent first half of IRAC, with the selected provision rows reviewable before analysis and conclusion generation.
- `patent tooling / uspto-patent-driver-depth`: reuse one-operative-relation-per-row and stable row IDs for claim limitations and prosecution obligations; do not reuse generic actor filtering where hub actors dominate.
- `agent memory / Cognee`: treat retrieval traces, missed conditional duties, and cooperation-clause false positives as reusable evaluation memories tied to exact benchmark questions.
