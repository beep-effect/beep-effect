# Towards an Ontological Foundation of Discrete Event Simulation

## Problem & positioning

- Discrete Event Simulation (DES) lacks agreed definitions for core terms such as entity, object, event, state, and flow.
- Simulation engineering has many formalisms and technologies but no modeling standard comparable to UML or BPMN.
- Existing simulation ontologies mainly use OWL to drive or exchange simulations; they do not ground modeling primitives in a foundational ontology.
- Ambiguous language constructs reduce comprehensibility, interoperability, maintenance, reuse, and faithful reasoning about simulated reality.
- The paper transfers foundational-ontology methods from conceptual software modeling to simulation engineering.
- Its main artifact is DESO, a foundational ontology derived from UFO for evaluating general-purpose DES languages.

## Approach / architecture

- The authors tailor UFO layers A and B into an Essential UFO (eUFO) covering objects, tropes, events, universals, datatypes, and sets.
- They then select the eUFO leaf concepts needed for discrete-event systems and organize DESO into run-time and design-time layers.
- DESO-I describes run-time individuals: physical objects, events, object participation events, situations, object snapshots, references, attributions, slots, and selected material relationships.
- DESO-U describes design-time universals: entity types, object types, event types, attributes, and reference properties.
- A discrete-system abstraction is mental; a model is its concrete representation in a language.
- Executable simulation advances through successive events that change variable values or object states and may schedule follow-up events.
- A language metamodel is compared with DESO through representation mappings from DESO concepts and interpretation mappings back to DESO.
- Deviations from an isomorphism reveal missing, overloaded, redundant, or semantically ungrounded constructs.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- eUFO distinguishes Individuals from Universals and separates substance individuals, trope individuals, events, and abstract entities.
- Tropes are existentially dependent particulars: intrinsic qualities depend on one bearer, while relational tropes or relators depend on and mediate multiple bearers.
- A material relationship is grounded by a relator; a binary material relationship is represented by a reference property.
- Relators are commonly founded on events, such as a marriage relation founded on a wedding or contract-signing event.
- Qualities are non-transferable particulars; equivalent color values on two objects do not make their color qualities numerically identical.
- Attributes map qualities to datatype values, and multiple attributes may expose one quality through different datatypes.
- Attributions and material relationships are treated as truth makers for corresponding statements.
- Events may be atomic or complex and existentially depend on their participants.
- Object participation events specialize into object creation, change, and destruction.
- Events transform a pre-state situation into a post-state situation; situations comprise object snapshots plus material relationships.
- Atomicity and instantaneity are orthogonal: an atomic event may extend through time, and an instant may contain multiple events.
- Temporal intervals may be open or bounded, continuous or discontinuous, durative or instantaneous, supporting linear, branching, parallel, or circular structures.
- A process is a complex event, not an enduring object or a vague flow of “entities.”
- DES is specifically an event flow: a sequence of events at times from a discrete set of time points.
- DESO-I pragmatically calls modeled physical objects, events, and objectified material relationships `entities`, while excluding qualities, relators, and amounts of matter from direct simulation representation.
- A slot is either an attribution or a reference, giving state snapshots a uniform property representation.
- Object and event types are both entity types with attributes and reference properties.
- Sortal types supply identity criteria; mixins do not. Rigid base types are essential, while anti-rigid roles and phases can be entered or left.
- The paper states constraints including exactly one ultimate base type per object, no rigid subtype of an anti-rigid type, and no direct mixin instances.
- A role is defined by an extrinsic relation, while a phase is defined by intrinsic conditions.

## Evaluation & results

- The paper defines four evaluation properties for a DES language relative to DESO.
- Soundness is the fraction of language elements having a DESO interpretation.
- Completeness is the fraction of DESO concepts represented by language elements.
- Lucidity is the fraction of language elements with at most one DESO interpretation.
- Laconicity is the fraction of DESO concepts represented by at most one language element.
- Low scores predict incorrect communication and invalid domain-semantic inferences.
- The “entity flow” idiom is used as an illustrative lucidity failure because it conflates event, message, and physical-object flows.
- DESO itself is presented through the eUFO, DESO-I, and DESO-U category diagrams and their textual definitions.
- No DES language is actually scored; Petri nets and DEVS are named only as planned future evaluations.
- No simulation execution, competency-question suite, ontology size, reasoner performance, or user evaluation is reported.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- DESO intentionally omits several UFO concepts and simplifies run-time relations to binary material relationships.
- Qualities and relators are excluded from direct simulation entities for pragmatic reasons, potentially losing causally important relationship identity and provenance.
- The wildcard sense of `object` and broad `entity` category are practical but may recreate ambiguity at implementation boundaries.
- The framework evaluates expressiveness and mapping structure, not whether a model's empirical assumptions or event distributions are correct.
- Relative scores treat concepts or language elements equally even though some omissions and overloads are more consequential than others.
- The laconicity formula's denominator is not explicitly restated in the extracted prose, unlike the first three metrics.
- No thresholds establish when a score is adequate, and no inter-rater method is given for contested mappings.
- UFO layers about intentional agents are deferred, limiting application to agent-based simulations and institutional action.
- Open-world ontology semantics, executable event scheduling, stochastic behavior, concurrency conflicts, and state validation are not integrated.
- The paper does not demonstrate that DESO-based refactoring improves a real simulator or model.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** adopt soundness, completeness, lucidity, and laconicity as mapping-quality checks when aligning package vocabularies to shared SKOS concepts; reject overloaded `entity` or `flow` concepts.
- **epistemic slice:** take the event → participation → pre/post situation pattern for state transitions, while retaining valid time, transaction time, evidence, and the identity of relationship-changing events.
- **patent tooling (uspto-patent-driver-depth):** model prosecution as a complex event sequence whose filings, office actions, responses, and status changes create snapshots rather than mutating one timeless patent record.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** distinguish enduring matter objects from events and role relations so deadlines and procedural transitions can be replayed and audited.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** use representation/interpretation mappings to audit whether memory-edge labels are missing, overloaded, or redundant relative to a small reference ontology.
