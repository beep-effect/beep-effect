# PatentWriter: A Benchmarking Study for Patent Drafting with LLMs

## Problem & positioning

- Patent-generation studies use different datasets, tasks, prompts, and metrics, making model comparisons unreliable.
- PatentWriter supplies a controlled benchmark for generating a patent abstract from the patent's first claim.
- The first claim is treated as a compact statement of legally important invention features; the abstract is treated as a searchable technical summary.
- The benchmark compares six LLM variants under one dataset and task formulation rather than proposing a new drafting model.
- Its main contribution is multidimensional evaluation: textual similarity, perturbation robustness, downstream patent utility, and stylistic behavior.
- The paper positions LLMs as drafting aids and explicitly retains human supervision and legal compliance obligations.

## Approach / architecture

- Dataset source: PatentsView records for U.S. patents granted in 2022.
- The final dataset contains about 21,000 records, approximately 1,000 from each of 21 CPC subclasses.
- Covered domains are A61 medical/hygiene, G06 computing, and H04 telecommunications.
- The generation task pairs each first claim with its abstract; described metadata fields are patent ID, title, abstract, and CPC subclass.
- Models: Llama 2, Llama 3, DeepSeek, GPT-3.5, GPT-4o, and GPT-4.1.
- Prompting variants are zero-shot, three-example few-shot, and chain-of-thought.
- Robustness transformations use `nlpaug`: simulated keyboard typos, BERT-context substitutions, and adjacent-word swaps.
- Surface/semantic evaluation uses BERTScore, ROUGE-L, BLEU, and embedding cosine similarity.
- Domain evaluation trains an all-MiniLM-L12-v2 subclass classifier and compares results on original versus generated abstracts.
- Retrieval evaluation embeds abstracts with all-MiniLM-L6-v2, ranks other patents by cosine similarity, and compares neighbor sets and ranks.
- Retrieval reports overlap at 5, 10, and 25 plus Spearman correlation; shuffled generated abstracts form a chance baseline.
- Stylistic evaluation measures token length, readability, and passive-voice percentage.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- PatentWriter does not construct an ontology or knowledge graph.
- CPC provides the only explicit semantic organization: a hierarchical classification reduced to 21 subclasses under three top-level classes.
- For classification, the inherently multilabel CPC problem is deliberately simplified into separate single-label tasks within A61, G06, and H04.
- Patent ID links claims, titles, abstracts, and CPC labels as views of the same document-level entity.
- Retrieval neighborhoods create an implicit similarity graph, but edges are embedding-derived, untyped, and not persisted as a KG contribution.
- The benchmark's strongest reusable modeling pattern is multiple representations of one patent artifact with shared identity and task-specific evidence.
- It does not model claim elements, dependencies, inventors, citations, prior art, legal events, provenance, or temporal changes.

## Evaluation & results

- Across all model/domain combinations, BERTScore is at least 0.85 and reaches 0.89, while BLEU ranges from 0.04 to 0.19.
- Llama 2 records the highest tabled similarity in G06/H04: BERTScore 0.89, cosine 0.65/0.66, ROUGE 0.44/0.45, BLEU 0.18/0.19.
- On A61 with GPT-4o, zero-shot, few-shot, and CoT all score 0.87 BERTScore; CoT raises ROUGE from 0.30 to 0.34 and BLEU from 0.06 to 0.10.
- GPT-4o perturbations lower A61 BERTScore only from 0.87 to 0.86; BERT substitutions lower ROUGE from 0.30 to 0.26 and BLEU from 0.06 to 0.05.
- GPT-4o-generated abstracts produce classification accuracies of 0.59 A61, 0.55 G06, and 0.59 H04.
- Original-abstract classification accuracies are 0.56, 0.53, and 0.57 respectively; generated text slightly exceeds them in this setup.
- GPT-4o retrieval Spearman correlations are 0.67 A61, 0.62 G06, and 0.65 H04.
- GPT-4o retrieval overlap ranges from 0.24 to 0.28, far above randomized overlap of 0.001 to 0.005.
- Human abstracts average 92.7 ± 44.8 tokens; GPT-4o averages 133.8 ± 27.3 and Llama 3 averages 115.9 ± 20.1.
- Readability is 20.8 ± 10.9 for humans, 16.1 ± 2.1 for GPT-4o, and 15.0 ± 3.1 for Llama 3.
- Passive voice averages 43.6% ± 36.6% for humans versus 32.4% ± 18.6% GPT-4o and 32.6% ± 21.5% Llama 3.
- Human abstract length weakly correlates with first-claim length at `r = 0.27`; generated abstracts track claim verbosity more strongly, though the extracted text does not expose that coefficient.
- Llama 3 took about 39 hours for the full dataset on an NVIDIA H200; GPT-4o mini and GPT-4.1 each took 21 hours.
- DeepSeek took about 12 hours for only 856 samples per subclass and was therefore evaluated on a smaller portion.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The authors state that the benchmark does not examine implementation details of LLMs deployed in practice and needs further feasibility/scalability study.
- They warn that patent generation should not be fully automated and must remain under human supervision and patent-law constraints.
- The benchmark covers only abstract-from-first-claim generation, not claims, specifications, drawings, prosecution amendments, or end-to-end filing quality.
- The corpus is limited to 2022 U.S. grants and three broad technical domains; performance may not generalize across time, jurisdictions, or sparse CPC areas.
- Single-label subclass classification removes real CPC multilabel structure and hierarchy.
- High reference similarity can reward copying or templating and does not test novelty, enablement, written description, definiteness, or claim validity.
- Generated abstracts outperforming originals on one classifier may reflect classifier distribution preferences, not superior legal or technical drafting.
- Closed-model/version drift complicates reproducibility, and DeepSeek's reduced sample compromises direct completeness of the six-model comparison.
- The paper reports no attorney-blinded quality ratings, factual-consistency audit, evidence attribution, privacy analysis, or memorization/leakage study.
- Its anonymous code URL and claimed public availability should be checked independently before relying on artifact permanence.
