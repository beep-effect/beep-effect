# Modeling the Diachronic Evolution of Legal Norms: An LRMoo-Based, Component-Level Approach

## Problem & positioning

- Legal systems need exact point-in-time reconstruction of a norm and each nested provision, not merely retrieval of the latest consolidated document.
- Akoma Ntoso provides strong document structure and document-level version metadata, but no native component lifecycle or causal link from an amending provision to its target component.
- LexML Brazil supplies granular `norm@version~language!part` identifiers, yet identifiers alone do not state ontological type, succession, composition, or amendment cause.
- The paper distinguishes textual-realization versioning from ontology-versioning research: its target is a stable historical textual ground truth on which later legal semantics and rules can operate.
- It positions deterministic graph traversal as a necessary corrective to probabilistic LLM reconstruction in high-stakes legal work.

## Approach / architecture

- One immutable `F1 Work` anchors the legal norm across its entire history.
- Each language-agnostic Temporal Version (TV) is an `F2 Expression`: a complete semantic and structural snapshot at one time.
- TVs form a diachronic chain with LRMoo `R76 is derivative of`; every TV realizes the same norm Work.
- A Language Version (LV) is another `F2 Expression`, derived from its TV with `R76`, separating what the law says from the language used to express it.
- An `F3 Manifestation` embodies an LV through `R4 embodies`; an `F5 Item` can represent a particular copy.
- An `F28 Expression Creation` reifies promulgation or amendment and creates the resulting TV through `R17 created`.
- For amendments, the event uses both the amending act's TV and the preceding target TV through `P16 used specific object`.
- `P2 has type`, `P14 carried out by`, and `P4 has time-span` add amendment nature, responsible actor, and time.
- The initial `F28` both creates the first official expression (`R17`) and creates a realization of the norm Work (`R19`).
- The paper maps this pattern to LexML URNs and illustrates it with the Brazilian Constitution's 1988 state and its 1992 first textual amendment.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- A Component Work (CW) is an `F1 Work` for a stable provision identity, such as Article 6's caput.
- Norm and component Works form an abstract hierarchy with `R67 has part`; this is separate from textual composition.
- A Component Temporal Version (CTV) is an `F2 Expression` realizing the component at a date; successive CTVs use `R76 is derivative of`.
- A Component Language Version (CLV) is an `F2 Expression` derived from its CTV and containing one language-specific text.
- TV/CTV and LV/CLV expression hierarchies use `R5 has component`, preserving LRMoo's Work-versus-Expression property domains.
- A macro `F28 Expression Creation` represents the whole legislative act; `P9 consists of` decomposes it into provision-level micro-events.
- Each micro-event uses the amending provision CTV and prior target CTV (`P16`) and creates the new target CTV (`R17`).
- Unaffected components retain their current CTV, so a new whole-norm snapshot can reuse unchanged component expressions rather than duplicating everything.
- `R76` is treated as a traversal shortcut for the fuller event path: it answers what succeeded what, while the event records how, when, why, and from which authoritative instruction.
- The worked component case links Constitutional Amendment 26/2000 Article 1 to the new CTV of Constitution Article 6's caput after adding “moradia.”

## Evaluation & results

- The paper presents conceptual diagrams and two worked Brazilian examples: the whole Constitution's 1988-to-1992 transition and Article 6's 2000 component amendment.
- It demonstrates how each Work, TV, LV, CTV, and CLV receives a distinct LexML-compatible URN.
- No OWL artifact, populated graph, reconstruction implementation, corpus-scale experiment, competency-query suite, runtime, accuracy metric, or user study is reported.
- “Exact” and “bit-by-bit” reconstruction are design claims; the authors explicitly leave implementation and rigorous reconstruction benchmarks to future work.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The authors identify full OWL implementation, historical graph population, automated parsing, amendment-impact tooling, and benchmarks as future work.
- The proposal does not empirically establish that amendment prose can be mapped reliably to the required micro-event inputs and outputs.
- `R76` carries both temporal succession and TV-to-LV linguistic derivation, so consumers need explicit typing or path constraints to avoid conflating two meanings.
- The model associates expressions with time but does not separate legal valid time, transaction/recording time, effective time, and applicability time.
- It assumes a Component Work persists through wording changes; repeal, split, merge, renumbering, resurrection, and disputed identity need explicit policies.
- The paper's analogy for using `P9` on nested creation activities is argued in prose but not tested with an OWL reasoner or SHACL validation.
- A language-agnostic semantic snapshot modeled as an `F2 Expression` may strain LRMoo's distinction between a Work and its specific realization; P055 later revises this choice.
- Deterministic reconstruction still depends on complete, correct source ingestion; an explicit graph can deterministically reproduce bad data.
- There is no representation for evidence spans, competing interpretations, uncertainty, court-created change, or contradictions between official sources.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** take the parallel `R67` Work hierarchy and `R5` Expression hierarchy; encode relation domains so abstract provision identity never collapses into text-version containment.
- **epistemic slice:** take the macro/micro amendment event and predecessor chain, but add bitemporal edge intervals, source spans, ingest transaction time, and claim status to every event assertion.
- **epistemic slice:** reject bare `R76` as sufficient provenance; materialize the causal amending provision, prior state, resulting state, actor, and authoritative evidence for contradiction triage.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** use deterministic “law as of date” assembly and provision-level amendment traces for research memos, with a visible completeness warning when source history has gaps.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** preserve immutable component identity separately from dated textual memories; retrieval should select the valid CTV/CLV rather than overwrite older text.
