# Ontology-Based Knowledge Graph Approach for Legal Queries

## Problem & positioning

The paper targets precise, source-grounded answers to legal queries, where keyword retrieval misses semantic relations and general LLMs may return stale figures or misplaced citations.
It combines a curated legal ontology with document-level knowledge graphs so legal concepts, extracted phrases, rules, and concrete relations can participate in retrieval.
The demonstrated domain is Vietnamese road-traffic law, including concept-definition and traffic-violation questions.
The approach is explicitly human-supervised: lawyers, consultants, or domain-aware knowledge engineers verify extracted knowledge and updates.
Its main contribution is not generative answering but semantic graph matching that returns relevant articles, clauses, and points.
The authors contrast the system with TF-IDF, BM25, two PhoBERT-related retrieval methods, ChatGPT 4o, and Gemini 2.5.

## Approach / architecture

Legal-Onto represents a document as `(Conc, Rel, Rules) ⊕ (Keyphrases, Rela)`.
Each concept stores `Name`, textual `Content`, `InnerRul` article/paragraph/point locators, component concepts in `Attrs`, and associated `Keyphrases`.
Relations include hierarchical `has-a` and `is-a`, other concept relations such as `a-part-of`, and phrase-to-phrase or phrase-to-concept relations.
Deductive rules map hypothesis facts to goal facts; facts may characterize a relation, connect two concepts, or connect a concept/phrase to another phrase.
Documents are collected, cleaned, checked, keyword-extracted, and classified into concepts, relations, and inference rules.
VnCoreNLP and PhoBERT assist phrase/relation extraction and similarity, while domain experts make final knowledge-base decisions.
Question analysis extracts intent, entities, and relations and builds a graph using the same ontology vocabulary.
The question graph is decomposed into star subgraphs whose triples share a subject.
Each star is matched to semantically similar knowledge-base relations; `is-a` descendants and wildcarded missing triple components are allowed.
The intersection of matching document-item IDs across star relations becomes the answer set.
The system retrieves legal provisions and can assemble an answer from multiple documents rather than producing unsupported prose.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

The ontology/KG split distinguishes schema-level concepts, relations, and rules from phrase/entity nodes and their document-specific edges.
KG nodes combine ontology concept names (`K1`) with extracted document keyphrases (`K2`).
Edges combine declared concept relations (`E1`) with co-article phrase relations (`E2`) that experts label semantically.
Each concept's `InnerRul` stores an Article–Paragraph–Point locator, supplying provision-level grounding rather than only document-level provenance.
Triples are directed `(Subject, Relation, Object)` statements with edge importance based on TF and IDF within articles and documents.
Low-value triples are candidates for deletion when `TF × IDF <= α`; experts selected `α = 0.15` for the traffic-law domain and must approve deletion.
Equivalent keyphrases are detected first through a Vietnamese thesaurus and otherwise through PhoBERT cosine similarity.
Pairs with similarity at least `β = 0.6` are considered semantically similar; the paper gives comply/obey and drive/operate as examples.
Equivalent triples require identical or synonymous subject, relation, and object components before duplicates are removed.
Rules supplement direct graph matching with derivable relations, although the evaluation does not isolate their contribution.
The model is designed to integrate statutes, decrees, circulars, and amendments in one graph and update them without rebuilding from scratch.
It does not define explicit validity intervals, amendment/supersession edges, jurisdictional scope, authority ranks, evidence spans below provision locators, or uncertainty states.

## Evaluation & results

The corpus contains 36 articles, 306 clauses, and 762 points from four cited Vietnamese traffic-law sources.
The ontology has 90 concepts and 49 concept relations; the resulting graph has 616 nodes and 1,239 edges/relations.
Experts described as traffic police and a road-traffic lawyer checked system processing.
On 201 questions, 166 were correct, for 82.6% overall accuracy.
Category results were: definitions 24/30 (80.0%); signs/signals 37/47 (78.7%); personal safety 40/48 (83.3%); parking/reversing 17/22 (77.3%).
Vehicle-document questions scored 21/24 (87.5%); prohibited-substance questions 13/14 (92.8%); obstruction questions 14/16 (87.5%).
Against retrieval baselines, proposed TopK@acc was 0.561 at K=5, 0.603 at 10, 0.645 at 20, and 0.671 at 50.
TPS scored 0.441, 0.500, 0.563, and 0.688 respectively, so TPS surpassed the proposed system at K=50.
TF-IDF, BM25, and TIWS were far lower, with Top5@acc between 0.037 and 0.058 and Top50@acc between 0.321 and 0.393.
For 171 traffic-violation queries used in the LLM comparison, the paper reports qualitative examples rather than a full ChatGPT/Gemini confusion table.
It says roughly 25.8% of Google's violation answers needed updating and Gemini reached 93.3% on conceptual questions.
No confidence intervals, significance tests, annotation-agreement statistic, latency, graph-ablation study, or public replication package is reported in the extracted text.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

The authors acknowledge that knowledge-base construction still requires substantial expert time and effort despite NLP assistance.
New legal fields require specialized domain knowledge, and new languages require replacing Vietnamese-specific PhoBERT/VnCoreNLP components.
The current matcher handles individual questions but not complex queries involving overlapping legal regulations.
The 201-query evaluation is confined to one country's road-traffic rules, limiting jurisdictional and doctrinal generalization.
“Correct” is not operationalized with inter-rater rules, and the number and independence of expert judges are unclear.
Thresholds `α` and `β` are domain-tuned without sensitivity analysis; mistaken synonym merging can collapse legally distinct terms.
Co-occurrence within the same article is a weak basis for an edge unless its relation label and evidence are independently validated.
The update claim lacks temporal semantics for effective dates, repeals, amendments, and the legal time applicable to a queried event.
TopK@acc falls below TPS at K=50, complicating broad claims of consistent superiority over NLP methods.
The LLM comparison mixes live-search capabilities, answer styles, and selected examples and does not constitute a controlled benchmark.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- `epistemic slice`: take Article–Paragraph–Point locators and expert verification, then deepen them into exact evidence spans plus bitemporal effective/recorded intervals for every rule and answer claim.
- `epistemic slice / contradiction triage`: model amendment, repeal, exception, and overlap explicitly before merging cross-document triples; reject synonym similarity as sufficient evidence of legal equivalence.
- `semantic-foundation / SKOS and @beep/ontology`: take the concept/keyphrase separation and map phrases to canonical concepts with SKOS labels and governed match strength; retain unmatched phrases as candidates, not new authoritative concepts.
- `patent tooling / uspto-patent-driver-depth`: adapt provision locators to patent document/claim/paragraph spans and the ontology-plus-instance-graph split to applications, claims, parties, citations, classifications, and prosecution events.
- `OIP solo-practice workflows`: take star-graph decomposition for multi-condition intake questions and return intersected, cited authorities; require attorney review when wildcard or subsumption matching broadens a query.
- `agent memory / Cognee`: store query graph, matched triples, thresholds, rules fired, source provisions, and reviewer decisions as replayable retrieval provenance rather than only the final answer.
