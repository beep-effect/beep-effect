# Research on Knowledge Graph Construction Technology Based on Intellectual Property Legal Documents

## Problem & positioning

The paper addresses heterogeneity among copyright, patent, and trademark rules and the semantic complexity of Chinese intellectual-property infringement judgments.
It positions a domain legal KG as infrastructure for case analysis, legal retrieval, question answering, evidence-chain inspection, and decision support.
Because judgments have standardized forms and IP law has an expert-definable knowledge system, the authors choose top-down ontology construction.
The intended novelty is as much legal-concept design as model selection: distinct right-specific ontologies and explicit representation of whether infringement is established.
The work spans acquisition, preprocessing, ontology design, extraction, alignment, Neo4j visualization, and embedding-based completion.

## Approach / architecture

Scrapy crawlers collect intellectual-property laws and judicial cases, including regulation name, issuer, publication and effective dates, category, and access link.
NLPIR performs Chinese segmentation, part-of-speech tagging, keyword extraction, proprietary-term recognition, and punctuation cleanup.
The ontology is manually designed by experts using judgments, textbooks, trial guides, and Chinese information-extraction and vocabulary standards.
BERT plus CRF performs BIO-style entity sequence labeling; the prose also calls this BiLSTM-CRF in the abstract, without fully reconciling the architectures.
CasRel performs joint triple extraction by first identifying subjects, then applying relation-specific object taggers.
The authors prefer the functional view `f_r(subject) -> object` over treating a relation as a discrete label on an already selected entity pair.
Word2Vec vectorizes terms before TransE-based alignment of independently extracted expressions with the conceptual ontology.
The alignment pipeline embeds graphs, maps their spaces together, scores entity similarity or distance, and iteratively feeds alignment results back for refinement.
JSON-formatted aligned data is loaded through Py2neo into Neo4j, where entities become nodes and relations become edges.
Cypher queries are used as an asserted check of graph accuracy and completeness; Neo4j also supplies filtering, highlighting, and visual navigation.
TransD is selected for graph completion because its entity- and relation-specific projection vectors better address one-to-many, many-to-one, and many-to-many relations than TransE.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

The conceptual layer reports 84 concepts, 89 relationships, and 105 attributes/properties.
Section 3 separately says it extracted 21 groups of relationships and defined 27 attributes, apparently lower-level group counts rather than the abstract's totals, but the mapping is not explained.
The ontology's four parts are general IP civil-case types plus copyright-, patent-, and trademark-infringement case modules.
It limits the graph to ownership-and-infringement-dispute causes while excluding ownership disputes from the implemented scope.
Cases are routed to a right-specific domain based on the intellectual-property right involved.
The model distinguishes first instance, second instance, retrial, and review, but normalizes litigant roles to first-instance `plaintiff` and `defendant` labels.
Figure 3 connects the central infringement-dispute class to copyright, patent, trademark, and other-IP branches, then to cases, parties, rights, judgments, and infringement-comparison elements.
Concepts provide classes; relationships connect concepts within and across categories and declare concept-level domain and range; attributes describe entity characteristics.
The model explicitly represents positive and negative infringement conclusions, enabling structurally comparable outcomes rather than only document metadata.
It captures 1:1 relations such as a case number to its parties, 1:N relations such as copyright to attribution/modification rights, and N:N relations among responsibility, infringement comparisons, plaintiffs, and defendants.
The graph aims to encode both substantive infringement content and judicial rationale, though no formal logical axioms or rule language are published in the paper.

## Evaluation & results

The conclusion reports 166 IP-domain documents extracted and 3,713 original judicial cases obtained, cleaned, and structured.
The conceptual ontology totals 84 concepts, 89 relationships, and 105 properties.
No entity-extraction precision, recall, F1, relation-extraction score, alignment accuracy, link-prediction metric, train/test split, or expert agreement is reported.
No final node, edge, or triple count is given for the instance graph.
The only demonstrated output is a Neo4j visualization of individual cases and a qualitative claim that Cypher queries verified correctness and completeness.
Accordingly, the paper describes a system build, not an empirical validation of legal accuracy or downstream decision quality.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

The authors call for broader case types, better identification and extraction accuracy, stronger NLP, more legal-tech functions, deeper algorithms, and improved automatic alignment, updating, and completion.
The source and sampling protocol for the 3,713 cases are not specified sufficiently to assess coverage or bias.
The relationship and attribute counts are internally opaque, and the paper does not publish a machine-readable schema in its text.
The BERT-CRF/BiLSTM-CRF naming inconsistency and sparse training details prevent replication.
CasRel and TransE/TransD are described generically, with no hyperparameters, negative sampling scheme, thresholds, or error analysis.
Embedding proximity cannot by itself establish legal equivalence, and TransD link completion cannot substitute for rule-grounded legal reasoning.
Normalizing all procedural roles to plaintiff/defendant can erase appeal-stage role changes and temporal procedural context.
The graph lacks explicit source spans, document versions, effective-time intervals, decision-time intervals, confidence, and contradiction states.
The visual ontology is too low-resolution to recover every class and relation reliably, and prose does not enumerate them.
