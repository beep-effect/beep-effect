# An Ontology-Driven Graph RAG for Legal Norms: A Hierarchical, Temporal, and Deterministic Approach

## Problem & positioning

- Flat legal RAG loses statutory hierarchy, historical validity, and the legislative causes of textual change.
- That failure mode produces anachronistic answers: a semantically similar chunk may not be the provision valid at the queried time.
- The work positions ontology-backed retrieval as a deterministic substrate for generation, rather than asking an LLM to infer legal history from chunks.
- It differs from algorithmic Graph-RAG community detection by treating authored legal hierarchy and curated legal themes as the primary communities.
- It also differs from document-centric Akoma Ntoso usage by making Work/Expression layers directly traversable graph entities.

## Approach / architecture

- Parse norms by their intrinsic hierarchy—titles, chapters, articles, paragraphs, and items—rather than fixed-size chunking.
- Create a permanent abstract backbone of `Norm` and nested `Component` nodes, then attach dated and linguistic realizations.
- Link retrievable `Text Unit` nodes only to the most specific language realization, preventing timeless components from directly owning mutable wording.
- Propagate a local amendment upward by creating new ancestor temporal states that aggregate existing child states; unchanged children are reused.
- Reify each enactment, amendment, or repeal as an `Action` with the source provision, terminated version, and produced version.
- Textualize actions, metadata properties, and informative relationships into separate searchable units for multi-aspect retrieval.
- Resolve a query through canonicalization, hierarchy scoping, planner strategy selection, deterministic temporal selection, hybrid ranking, and grounded generation.
- Supported strategies are structure-first, span-first, and time-first; declared policies include `SnapshotLast` and snapshot-anchored scope membership.
- The implementation uses Oracle Database 23ai tables plus foreign keys and a vector column, Qwen3 embeddings at 256 dimensions, and Gemini 2.5 Flash.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- `Norm` is an abstract legal Work whose identity persists independently of wording.
- `Component` is an abstract Component Work with stable identity and recursive containment in the norm hierarchy.
- `Temporal Version` (`TV`, or `CTV` for a component) is a language-independent, date-bounded semantic state.
- `Language Version` (`LV`, or `CLV`) realizes one temporal state in a particular language; multilingual text shares the same temporal backbone.
- `Text Unit` realizes CLV content or textualizes a graph facet such as an action, property, relationship, or theme description.
- A validity interval is selected by `valid_start <= t < coalesce(valid_end, +infinity)`; interval queries require a disclosed resolution policy.
- Parent CTVs aggregate the latest child CTVs as of the change date instead of compositionally cloning all descendants.
- `Action` is a first-class causal event: it is commanded by a source provision, terminates an old CTV when applicable, and produces a new CTV.
- Provenance is an ordered DAG of actions linked to before/after spans, rather than an unstructured list of retrieved citations.
- `Theme` nodes form curated cross-document topical communities alongside the internal structural communities.
- Component identity, membership policy, temporal policy, language fallback, retrieval `k`, and confidence belong in the reproducibility record.

## Evaluation & results

- The qualitative case study covers the Brazilian Federal Constitution of 1988 and amendments available as of early 2024.
- Three demonstrated query classes are point-in-time retrieval, hierarchical impact analysis, and causal-lineage reconstruction.
- The Article 6 example reconstructs the addition of housing in 2000 and food in 2010 by traversing CTVs and their causal actions.
- The illustrative Chapter II impact output identifies two Article 6 amendments and one Article 7 amendment during 2010–2019.
- Reference defaults are 256-dimensional Qwen3 embeddings, cosine similarity, `k = 8`, and `SnapshotLast` temporal resolution.
- No quantitative comparison against flat RAG, retrieval benchmark, latency study, graph-size count, or error analysis is reported.
- The authors explicitly describe the evaluation as qualitative and propose future temporal precision/recall, action-attribution, provenance-completeness, and time-to-answer metrics.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- Authors: national-scale ingestion requires substantial engineering, governance, materialized snapshots, incremental processing, and human validation.
- Authors: incorrect validity intervals or action metadata deterministically propagate incorrect answers; corroboration, confidence, validation, and rollback are required.
- Authors: the ontology fits explicit statutory hierarchies best; case law, contracts, regulations, multilingual policies, and complex cross-references need adaptation.
- Authors: outputs should remain verified-assistant results rather than legal advice, with provenance and immutable curation logs exposed.
- Mine: the paper demonstrates capability through hand-selected examples, not a controlled baseline or independently reproducible benchmark.
- Mine: the schema-aware LLM planner remains a probabilistic boundary even when downstream traversal is deterministic; planner error rates are not measured.
- Mine: textualizing metadata and actions improves recall but can introduce generated summaries whose fidelity to source commands needs its own evidence-span validation.
- Mine: transaction time, ingestion/correction time, uncertainty, conflicting sources, and retroactive legal effect are not modeled as separate temporal axes.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** adopt stable Work/Component identities plus curated `Theme` concepts; map themes through SKOS and reject embeddings as the source of taxonomy truth.
- **epistemic slice:** adapt CTV validity to bitemporal edges, retain `Action` as the change cause, and require claims to cite exact before/after evidence spans; add transaction time and contradiction states absent here.
- **patent tooling (uspto-patent-driver-depth):** model patent/application/claim identity separately from publication and prosecution versions, reusing unchanged dependent-claim nodes across snapshots.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** expose the selected temporal and scope-membership policies with every legal-research answer and generate a machine-readable provenance annex for attorney verification.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** take the event-centric lineage and policy-record pattern for memory revisions, but reject generated metadata Text Units unless anchored to immutable source spans.
