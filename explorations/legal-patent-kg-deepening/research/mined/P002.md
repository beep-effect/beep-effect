# A Hohfeldian Knowledge Base for LLM-Assisted Legal Information Retrieval in Marine Biodiversity Law

## Problem & positioning

Marine genetic-resource governance is fragmented across overlapping treaties, making it difficult to determine which actors hold duties or entitlements in a concrete scenario.
The focal overlap is among the Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD), Nagoya Protocol (NP), and Biodiversity Beyond National Jurisdiction Agreement (BBNJ-A).
The paper positions Hohfeld's directed legal positions as a more precise retrieval representation than vague topic categories or direct LLM question answering.
Its goal is not final legal advice: it asks whether LLMs can retrieve the structured normative rows needed to resolve expert-authored fact patterns.
The work contributes a normative knowledge base, a small expert-gold benchmark, two retrieval pipelines, and a four-model comparison.

## Approach / architecture

Two international-law experts manually construct the Hohfeld-Structured Normative Knowledge Base (HSNKB) from the three treaties.
Each row corresponds to one operative clause and one dominant normative relation; multi-prescription provisions are split into multiple rows.
Preambles, aspirational language, and definitions are excluded.
Fifteen questions follow an IRAC-style progression from straightforward single-treaty scenarios to cross-treaty fact patterns.
An international-law expert annotates the relevant HSNKB rows for each question as ground truth.
An actor-definition dictionary standardizes explicit and implicit actor names across instruments.
Full-scope retrieval gives the LLM all rows represented by identifier, provision number, and natural-language tripartite sentence.
Actor-constrained retrieval first predicts relevant actors, then filters HSNKB rows involving those actors before row selection.
Both use a zero-shot Chain-of-Instructions prompt with four stages: parse entities; gate jurisdiction; filter by Hohfeldian allocation; rank by specificity.
Specificity considers jurisdiction, resource type, lifecycle stage, and traditional knowledge.
The model returns ranked row identifiers as structured JSON rather than drafting a free-form legal answer.
Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4, and Kimi K2 are run through OpenRouter with fixed seed and zero temperature where supported.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

HSNKB contains 377 normative rows: 193 from BBNJ-A, 96 from CBD, and 88 from NP.
Every row has a unique identifier, treaty, provision number, actor-holder, legal position or action, actor-affected, and an expert-written tripartite sentence.
The core pattern is a directed ternary normative relation: `actor-holder --legal position/action--> actor-affected`, grounded in a specific operative clause.
Correlative legal positions are deduced through Hohfeldian logic, preserving direction and the paired structure of rights and duties.
`Contracting Parties` is the most frequent actor and `duty` the dominant legal position.
Splitting multi-prescription provisions enforces one dominant relation per row and gives retrieval an atomic target.
Cross-treaty actor definitions form a lightweight identity/alignment layer for differently worded references.
The paper's prototype visualizes relevant actors and treaty relationships after retrieval.
This is a structured normative table rather than a general entity KG: it does not model arbitrary events, evidence, or causal relations.
No OWL/RDF serialization, class hierarchy, formal axiom inventory, temporal validity model, or contradiction representation is specified.

## Evaluation & results

Evaluation macro-averages precision, recall, and F1 across all 15 questions, giving each fact pattern equal weight.
In full-scope retrieval, GPT-5 leads at P 0.77, R 0.53, F1 0.59.
Gemini 2.5 Pro scores 0.69/0.45/0.53; Claude Sonnet 4 scores 0.61/0.50/0.52; Kimi K2 scores 0.28/0.58/0.33.
In actor-constrained retrieval, Gemini scores 0.68/0.49/0.56, Claude 0.64/0.44/0.51, GPT-5 0.74/0.50/0.57, and Kimi 0.30/0.58/0.35.
Actor identification has high recall but low precision: Gemini 0.171/0.944/0.286, Claude 0.234/0.992/0.372, GPT-5 0.239/1.000/0.379, Kimi 0.238/0.880/0.368.
Models retrieve 23.9-35.6 actors per question versus 6.2 in the gold answers.
Actor filtering removes only about 15% of rows because `Contracting Party` appears in every fact pattern and dominates the KB.
False negatives cluster around conditional, multi-actor, cross-treaty benefit-sharing duties.
False positives often arise when programmatic cooperation clauses are treated as operative Hohfeldian duties.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

The authors call the 15-question benchmark purposefully small and plan broader coverage and inter-annotator validation.
They identify recall without precision loss as the main open retrieval problem and plan traditional IR baselines.
Only one expert supplies question-level gold rows; ambiguity and alternate defensible answers are not measured.
The benchmark uses three treaties in one legal subdomain, limiting generalization to cases, statutes, patents, or larger instrument sets.
Excluding definitions and aspirational text improves precision but may remove interpretive context needed to apply an operative clause.
One dominant relation per row can hide conditions, exceptions, quantifiers, and coordinated prescriptions unless separately modeled.
Actor-constrained retrieval is structurally weak where a generic actor label appears in nearly every row.
The system retrieves rules but does not evaluate complete legal analysis, factual entailment, remedy selection, or answer correctness.
No validity dates, treaty versions, reservations, jurisdictional changes, evidence spans beyond provision identifiers, or competing interpretations are represented.

- `epistemic slice`: model each normative row as a provision-grounded claim with exact span, validity interval, derived correlative, derivation rule, and interpretation status; add contradiction links for competing readings.
- `OIP solo-practice workflows`: use fact-pattern-to-atomic-rule retrieval as an auditable issue-spotting stage, returning ranked provision spans before any client-facing analysis.
- `patent tooling / agentic-cad-patent-tooling`: borrow atomic clause splitting for patent claims and obligations, but replace Hohfeldian roles with claim-element, actor, action, condition, and dependency patterns where appropriate.
- `agent memory / Cognee`: store successful question-to-row traces and false-negative clusters as retrieval episodes; reject generic-actor prefiltering as a primary memory-routing strategy.
