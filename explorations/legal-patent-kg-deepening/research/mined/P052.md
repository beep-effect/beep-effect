# Mapping the Patent Landscape of Quantum Technologies: Patenting Trends, Innovation and Policy Implications

## Problem & positioning

- Second-generation quantum technologies span computation, simulation, communication, sensing, and metrology, but their technical boundary overlaps older semiconductor and nanoscale technologies.
- Policy proposals about patent overprotection and special quantum-IP regimes lacked a sufficiently recent, transparent empirical landscape.
- The study asks how quantum patenting changed over 2001–2021, which subfields and offices dominate, what claims cover, who owns portfolios, how citations distribute, and where activity occurs.
- It positions patent disclosure against trade secrecy as a governance and innovation-policy choice, especially for capital-intensive hardware and cloud-access business models.
- The authors argue that empirical landscape evidence should precede quantum-specific legal reform because the category itself is vulnerable to overbreadth and strategic drafting.

## Approach / architecture

- The search covers USPTO and EPO patent applications and grants dated 2001-01-01 through 2021-12-31; the reported search date is 2022-02-18.
- A sensitivity-to-specificity ladder defines ten searches rather than relying on one keyword query.
- S1 captures broadly quantum-related documents using “quantum” and related concepts anywhere in the document.
- S2 requires the quantum concept in title, abstract, or claims; S3 requires it in claims; S4 requires it in independent claims.
- S5–S10 intersect S2 with CPC classes for devices, nanostructures/quantum optics, information processing, quantum computing, cryptography, and communication.
- Combining lexical fields with CPC assignments deliberately joins automated retrieval to patent-office expert classification.
- Annual counts are decomposed by active grants, expired grants, pending applications, abandoned applications, and granted applications.
- Additional analyses cover office choice, claim-language trends, frequent-term concept clusters, recent grant titles, ultimate assignees, forward citations, and country of issuance.
- The concept landscape automatically analyzes title, abstract, and claims, then clusters frequently used terms into thematic groups.
- Forward citations are used as a portfolio-value proxy, not as a direct legal or technical quality determination.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- No formal ontology or queryable knowledge graph is delivered, but the search ladder supplies a useful layered concept-definition pattern.
- Patent-document entities split into application and grant states, with legal-status values including pending, abandoned/rejected, active, and expired.
- Claim containment is modeled implicitly at increasing strength: mention anywhere, mention in title/abstract/claims, claim limitation, and independent-claim limitation.
- Technology concepts map to CPC concepts such as H01L, B82Y20/00, B82Y10/00, G06N10/00, H04L quantum-cryptography subclasses, and H04B10.
- The six reported concept clusters are quantum circuits, quantum-dot devices, quantum computing, quantum-dot layers, quantum states, and quantum keys.
- Terms can participate in multiple clusters: qubits connect quantum states and computing; processor/control terms connect circuits and computing.
- Relevant relations include `document hasClaim`, `claim hasLimitation`, `document classifiedAs CPC`, `grant ownedBy ultimateAssignee`, `patent cites patent`, and `document issuedBy office`.
- Temporal modeling needs publication date, priority date, grant date, expiration, and status-as-of-search; the study uses these dimensions but does not formalize them.
- Office, country, assignee, university, company, government entity, and technology subfield are analytically distinct entity types.
- S1–S4 show why “quantum patent” should be represented as a criterion-bound classification claim, not an intrinsic Boolean property.
- The study does not publish axioms, entity-resolution rules, patent-family consolidation, evidence-span links, or a machine-readable schema for its landscape.

## Evaluation & results

- S1 returns 236,642 applications and 178,033 grants broadly related to quantum concepts.
- S2 identifies 34,402 applications and 20,581 grants with quantum as a core title/abstract/claims concept.
- Of S2 grants, 18,696 contain a quantum claim limitation (S3), and 10,318 contain it in an independent claim (S4).
- S5–S10 grants comprise 8,965 quantum-device, 3,282 nanostructure/quantum-optics, 2,057 quantum-information-processing, 1,603 quantum-computing, 736 quantum-cryptography, and 632 quantum-communication patents.
- Annual quantum grants rose from 161 in 2001 to 1,555 in 2018, with reported overall CAGR 15.23%.
- The low-growth interval from 2003 to 2013 rises from 608 to 904 grants, CAGR 4.05%.
- Active grants rise from 111 in 2004 to 2,028 in 2021, CAGR 18.64%.
- The reported application outcome split is 56.89% granted (`n = 19,571`) and 43.11% rejected or abandoned (`n = 14,830`).
- The paper reports 22% of grants expired and approximately 50% (`n = 19,364`) of disclosures already in the public domain.
- USPTO share of the joint USPTO/EPO S2 cohort rises from 63.2% in 2001 to 78.8% in 2021; overall shares are 77.96% USPTO and 22.04% EPO.
- More than 90% of patents using the quantum keyword in title or abstract also have a quantum-directed claim; over 50% place it in an independent claim.
- Quantum-computing grants rise from 37 in 2014 to 435 in 2021, with reported CAGR 42.2%.
- For S8 quantum computing, the search finds 3,042 applications and 1,603 grants; G06N10/00 contains 1,892 grants over the period.
- IBM leads the reported S8 owner table with 254 patents, followed by D-Wave with 183, Northrop Grumman with 120, and Microsoft with 111.
- IBM, D-Wave, Northrop Grumman, and Microsoft have the highest forward-citation totals; MIT is the leading university by that measure.
- The US and China lead countries of issuance; the paper reports China second in quantum computing by 2021 and especially rapid growth in quantum communications.
- Of 2,037 quantum patents granted in 2021, 1,642 (80.6%) have priority dates from 2004–2018.
- The authors report that 9.23% of granted applications classified in G06N10* had already expired.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The authors acknowledge the boundary problem: broad definitions absorb ordinary nanoscale semiconductors, while narrow definitions invite claim-drafting behavior.
- The field is early-stage, and patent counts cannot observe innovations retained as trade secrets or state secrets.
- USPTO/EPO data underrepresent activity aimed only at other offices; country-of-issuance patterns are not equivalent to invention location or market control.
- The landscape is a status snapshot from February 2022; pending applications, ownership, citations, expiration, and legal status will change.
- Keyword occurrence and CPC assignment do not prove that a claimed invention meaningfully harnesses second-generation quantum phenomena.
- The paper reports no sampled precision/recall validation for S1–S10 and no inter-reviewer assessment of false positives or negatives.
- No patent-family deduplication or same-invention cross-office consolidation procedure is stated in the extracted methodology.
- Application, publication, and grant counts sometimes use different denominators; comparisons require attention to the specific search and document state.
- Forward citations are treated as a value proxy but are age-, office-, field-, and citation-practice-dependent.
- The concept landscape is described at cluster level without algorithm parameters, stability tests, edge definitions, or downloadable graph data.
- Assignee normalization to ultimate owners is asserted in the citation analysis but its resolution procedure is not reported.
- Policy claims about stronger patents benefiting entrants and disclosure serving the public interest are reasoned interpretations, not causal findings from the counts.
- “Public domain” combines expired grants with unsuccessful applications; freedom to use still requires family, jurisdiction, claim-scope, and live-rights analysis.
- The paper itself contains minor numerical inconsistencies, including 4,534 versus 4,536 expired grants and 20,581 versus 20,583 total S2 grants.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** Take S1–S4 as named, governed concept schemes with explicit scope notes; represent “quantum patent” as a classification under a criterion, not a timeless class assertion.
- **epistemic slice:** Model patent status, ownership, public-domain availability, and technology classification as bitemporal claims tied to search date, jurisdiction, source fields, and exact supporting spans.
- **patent tooling — uspto-patent-driver-depth:** Build claim-aware searches that distinguish any-text, TAC, all-claims, and independent-claim matches; preserve CPC intersections and denominators in every result set.
- **patent tooling — agentic-cad-patent-tooling:** Use overlap between clusters and CPC classes to expand prior-art queries, but reject term co-occurrence or a “quantum” label as proof of novelty, enablement, or freedom to operate.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Turn “public domain” into a review queue that checks family members, jurisdiction, priority, continuations, terminal disclaimers, and current legal status before advising use.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** Retain dated landscape snapshots and entity-resolution decisions so later agents can explain changed counts instead of silently replacing historical ownership or status facts.
