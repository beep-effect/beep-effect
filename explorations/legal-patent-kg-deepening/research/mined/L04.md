# Modelling Legal Enforcement with UFO-L: A Case from Swedish Healthcare

## Problem & positioning

Regulated organizations face multilayered rules, conflicts with organizational goals, and divergent enforcement interpretations.
Regulators and courts can create new institutional realities through fines and other sanctions, which existing organizational models may not capture.

## Approach

The authors select a Swedish healthcare enforcement case, derive requirements for an enforcement ontology, and compare those requirements with UFO-L's representation capabilities.
The capture notes an informal Kumu model and identifies the underlying decisions and judgments.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

The work tests UFO-L as a foundation for organizational rule-enforcement patterns.
Its intended model must connect regulated organizations, rules, enforcement actors, decisions, interpretations, and sanctioning powers.
The abstract reports both strengths and weaknesses in UFO-L and proposals for modification, but the preview does not expose those modifications.

## Evaluation & results

Evaluation is a requirements-to-capabilities comparison based on one Swedish healthcare legal case.
The abstract says the investigation found strengths and weaknesses and suggested modifications; no detailed matrix or result counts are visible.

## Limitations

The full chapter is subscription-only in the capture.
The specific requirements, UFO-L gaps, extensions, and validation procedure cannot be recovered from the abstract.
A single healthcare case cannot establish cross-jurisdictional coverage.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- Represent enforcement decisions as events that can change institutional and normative state.
- Model an actor's interpretation separately from the authoritative rule and from the resulting sanction.
- Use case-derived competency questions to test power, enforcement, and sanction patterns.
