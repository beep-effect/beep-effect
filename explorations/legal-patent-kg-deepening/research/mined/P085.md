# Towards a Well-Founded Legal Domain Reference Ontology by Combining Conceptual Ontology Patterns

## Problem & positioning

The paper asks how to build a legal-domain reference ontology that is explicitly grounded in validated higher-level ontologies.
It distinguishes foundational ontologies, legal core ontologies, and domain ontologies by abstraction level, and reference ontologies from computationally oriented operational ontologies by purpose.
Legal modeling is difficult because law combines norms and responsibility with physical, abstract, mental, and social phenomena, while much source knowledge remains embedded in text.
Direct reuse is also hard: an engineer must select suitable ontologies, decide which fragments to import, extend them, and compose pieces without losing their constraints.
The proposed answer is a pattern-based route from UFO and UFO-L into a maritime-law domain model.
The demonstration covers the carriage-of-goods relation between carrier and shipper under the Hague and Hamburg conventions and is intended to support logistics traceability.
The work is about conceptual adequacy and reuse, not an executable OWL artifact, automated extraction system, or legal reasoner.

## Approach / architecture

Conceptual Ontology Patterns (COPs) are extracted from higher-level ontologies and represented in OntoUML.
The architecture separates Foundational Ontology Patterns (FOPs), derived from UFO, from Domain-Related Ontology Patterns (DROPs), fragmented from UFO-L.
FOPs package general structural rules and constraints and are reused by analogy with the domain problem.
DROPs retain structural and legal-domain knowledge, have stronger mutual dependencies, and are reused by specialization plus added properties and relations.
Three UFO FOPs are selected: `Category`, `Role-Relator`, and `Kind-Subkind`.
Two UFO-L DROPs are selected: `Legal_Substance` and `Legal_Relator`, with `Right-Duty_Relator` as the concrete legal-relation fragment.
Competency questions guide UFO-L fragmentation and are then specialized for the carriage-of-goods domain.
The domain model composes the patterns: `Legal_Substance` inherits the `Category` structure, while `Right-Duty_Relator` inherits `Role-Relator`.
The `Kind-Subkind` pattern organizes institutions and normative descriptions that do not come directly from the selected DROPs.
The authors claim that composition reuses both competency questions and axioms, including disjointness constraints inherited from Category and Kind-Subkind.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

UFO supplies the underlying distinctions between individuals and universals, endurants and events, substantials and moments, and intrinsic moments and relators.
Kinds provide identity; subkinds rigidly inherit one kind's identity; roles are anti-rigid and relationally dependent; categories and role mixins span multiple identity principles.
The `Role-Relator` FOP requires a relator mediated by two roles, each inheriting identity from exactly one kind.
UFO-L specializes UFO with `Legal_Agent`, `Legal_Object`, `Legal_Normative_Description`, `Legal_Norm`, `Legal_Role`, `Legal_Moment`, `Legal_Event`, and `Legal_Relator`.
A `Legal_Relator` mediates categories of legal roles, consists of legal moments inhering in those roles, and is grounded in a legally relevant event.
Legal moments are paired by correlation rather than modeled as independent unary labels.
UFO-L distinguishes simple relators for Right-Duty, NoRight-Permission, Power-Subjection, and Disability-Immunity from a complex Liberty relator.
The extracted `Legal_Substance` pattern organizes legal agents, legal objects, and categories defined by normative descriptions.
The `Right-Duty_Relator` DROP asks who participates, which role categories they instantiate, which legal moments compose the relation, who bears them, and on whom they externally depend.
It also requires the grounding legal event and the rule defining the relation.
In the maritime specialization, `Shipper_Institution` and `Carrier_Institution` are subkinds of `Agentive_Legal_Institution`.
`Contract_of_Carriage_of_Goods` and `Bill_of_Lading` are subkinds of `Legal_Normative_Description`.
The pattern composition therefore preserves a separation among enduring agents, contingent roles, reified relationships, correlated normative positions, triggering events, and normative texts.
For KG use, this is a candidate n-ary relation pattern; flattening it into a direct `carrier owes shipper` edge would discard bearers, correlated positions, event grounding, and applicable descriptions.

## Evaluation & results

The paper presents one modeled ontology portion and seven specialized competency questions for carriage of goods by sea.
The seven questions cover participating agents, role categories, constituent legal moments, moment holders, external dependence, grounding event, and defining legal rule.
The demonstration shows how five selected patterns can be composed, but does not report a user study, ontology metrics, competency-question execution, consistency-check results, or comparison baseline.
No implemented knowledge graph, dataset size, inference result, runtime, precision, recall, or traceability outcome is given.
The decision-support and logistics-traceability system is future work, so operational usefulness is not evaluated here.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

The authors acknowledge that selecting, extending, and composing reusable ontology fragments remains difficult and under-addressed.
Only a portion of one maritime relation is modeled; the work does not demonstrate cargo events, document versions, liability exceptions, jurisdiction, conflict of conventions, or end-to-end logistics traceability.
The strong conclusion that combined foundational and domain reuse is “essential and mandatory” is not supported by comparative evaluation.
Competency questions guide modeling but are not translated into executable queries or acceptance tests.
Figures convey much of the class and cardinality detail, while the paper gives no downloadable artifact or machine-readable axiomatization.
The model is an atemporal reference view: it does not represent when a norm, bill of lading, role, fact, or legal relationship was valid or known.
The paper does not model uncertainty, disputed facts, contradictory rules, interpretive alternatives, or source-text evidence spans.
Its approach can overcommit an application ontology to UFO metaphysics when a lightweight concept scheme would be sufficient for retrieval or labeling.
The traceability claim remains prospective and is not linked to observed shipping records or legal outcomes.
