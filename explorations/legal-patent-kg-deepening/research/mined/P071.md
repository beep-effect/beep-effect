# Procedurally Generated Prior Art: A Closer Look

## Problem & positioning

- This is a March 30, 2022 legal-practice article by Jacob Schneider, not an empirical research paper.
- It asks whether algorithmically generated and publicly posted technical combinations can operate as prior art against later patent claims.
- The litigation frame distinguishes noninfringement from invalidity based on an earlier public disclosure.
- It emphasizes that many inventions combine known technological building blocks, making automated recombination potentially consequential.
- The article examines the All Prior Art and All the Claims projects as attempts to preempt later patenting by dedicating generated combinations to the public.

## Approach / architecture

- The described projects mine technical terms from existing patents and patent publications.
- Procedural generation recombines those terms into short, invention-like public disclosures.
- Natural-language processing supplies connective prose around otherwise heterogeneous technical fragments.
- Publication identifiers encode when generated text was posted, creating evidence intended to predate a target patent's effective filing date.
- The example disclosure concatenates unrelated components involving wearable batteries, addressable memory, soil treatment, strips, and battery-case crimping.
- The article suggests that newer language models such as GPT-3 could make generated disclosures more coherent, but it does not implement or test that proposal.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- No ontology or knowledge graph is proposed.
- The implicit entity set consists of technical terms, components, processes, and generated disclosure documents harvested from patent sources.
- The central implicit relation is `generatedDisclosure combines knownElement`, with provenance back to prior patent vocabulary left unspecified.
- Publication time is the legally important metadata: a generated disclosure must be publicly accessible before the later claim's effective filing date.
- The example shows a failure mode of untyped recombination: grammatical adjacency does not establish a technically meaningful relation or enabled combination.
- A usable KG implementation would need source-document links, exact evidence spans, generation time, public-availability evidence, and semantic compatibility constraints.
- It would also need to distinguish mere co-occurrence from disclosure of every claim limitation in the required arrangement.

## Evaluation & results

- As of the article, All Prior Art had published more than 570 million generated disclosures.
- Both All Prior Art and All the Claims had operated since 2016.
- The author found neither project cited in federal opinions concerning prior art as of March 30, 2022.
- No retrieval experiment, invalidity outcome, examiner citation, enablement assessment, novelty analysis, or legal validation sample is reported.
- The article's result is therefore issue spotting: procedural disclosures appear facially eligible for consideration, but practical legal effect remained unobserved.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The author explicitly says practical effects on patent litigation remained to be seen.
- The article reasons from statutory language but does not test whether a generated jumble satisfies all doctrinal requirements for a printed publication or anticipatory disclosure.
- It treats public availability broadly without analyzing indexing, searchability, dissemination, authentication, or proof of the exact publication date.
- It does not address enablement, possession, operability, or whether one disclosure presents claim elements in the claimed arrangement.
- It does not distinguish anticipation by one reference from obviousness based on combinations of references.
- The 570-million count measures volume, not technical coherence, discoverability, legal relevance, or admissibility.
- No details are given for term extraction, recombination constraints, source licenses, duplicate suppression, or preservation of provenance.
- The article predates later generative-model and patent-policy developments; its legal assertions require current primary-law verification before practice use.
- The PDF is a captured JD Supra webpage and includes advertising, cookie notices, unrelated posts, and a general legal-advice disclaimer.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **epistemic slice:** model publication and availability as bitemporal claims—generated-at, first-publicly-observed-at, and valid-for a target filing date—with archived evidence.
- **patent tooling (uspto-patent-driver-depth):** reject raw combinatorial volume as prior-art quality; require element-to-passage mappings, single-reference coverage, enablement flags, and source authentication.
- **patent tooling (agentic-cad-patent-tooling):** use procedural combinations as adversarial ideation or search-query expansion, never as an automatic invalidity conclusion.
- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** constrain combinations with typed component/function/material relations and compatibility rules so syntactic concatenation is not mistaken for technical disclosure.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** present generated prior art as attorney-review leads with jurisdiction/date caveats and separate anticipation from obviousness analyses.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** retain rejected combinations and their reasons to prevent agents from repeatedly surfacing incoherent or legally insufficient disclosures.
