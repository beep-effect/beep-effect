# Analyzing Technological Competencies in the Patent-Based Supplier Portfolio

## Problem & positioning

The paper seeks a patent-based way to evaluate and select suppliers by technological competency.
It moves beyond binary portfolio measures toward semantic comparison of supplier and OEM patent text.

## Approach

The portfolio places suppliers using two patent-derived variables: similarity to an OEM's technological competencies and technological broadness.
Semantic analysis extracts more specific textual information than binary patent measures.
The method is tested on gearings, a key automotive technology field.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

No formal ontology or KG is presented in the capture.
The reusable conceptual model links organization-level patent portfolios to competency profiles, semantic anchor points, similarity, breadth, dependence risk, and management strategy.
OEM and upstream-supplier identities are the unit of aggregation rather than individual documents alone.

## Evaluation & results

The test identifies six generic supplier positions.
Those positions characterize risks of technological dependence or dependence on supplier production capacity.
The authors associate each position with management strategies and claim the method supports navigation using recent public patent information.

## Limitations

The full article is purchase-only; the capture exposes the structured abstract but not algorithms, anchors, thresholds, dataset size, or validation statistics.
Testing in gearings limits evidence for portability to other technologies.
Portfolio similarity does not itself prove capability, capacity, ownership, or freedom to operate.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- Represent competency profiles as derived, versioned claims over explicit patent sets.
- Preserve semantic anchors and their supporting text spans so portfolio positions remain auditable.
- Separate technological similarity from legal or commercial dependency conclusions.
