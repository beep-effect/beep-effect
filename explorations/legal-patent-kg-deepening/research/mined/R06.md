## What this repo actually is

DAOnt is a small EU Data Act compliance proof of concept, not a patent ontology and not a general legal reasoner. Its durable core is an OWL 2 vocabulary for Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 plus three hand-written violation queries,
six paired RDF/XML contract fixtures, a Python/RDFLib runner, and a Streamlit report viewer.

`DAOnt.ttl` is ontology version 2.0 at `https://w3id.org/def/daont`; Raptor parses it as 1,073 triples. It declares 84 OWL classes in total, of which 66 are DAOnt classes, alongside 66 object properties
(55 DAOnt), 24 datatype properties, 26 annotation properties, 10 restrictions, and four named individuals.
Those four individuals are only three article-oriented SKOS collections and one concept scheme. The file contains
no instantiated Article 4, 8, or 19 legal rules, duties, permissions, prohibitions, contracts, or compliance results.

The claimed LKIF-Core/ODRL/DPV integration is vocabulary alignment by reuse and local declaration of their IRIs: `DAOnt.ttl` has no `owl:imports`. The TBox makes DAOnt data/actions/parties subclasses of ODRL concepts, uses
LKIF `Right` and `Legal_Document`, and reuses selected DPV data, subject, consent, purpose, and recipient terms.
The executable lane does not run OWL inference or `owlready2`; it loads the base RDF/XML and one fixture into an
RDFLib `Graph`, executes the applicable SPARQL `SELECT`, and interprets every returned row as a violation.

The intended deontic design is clearest in `diagrams/Diagram_Art_4-1.drawio.png`, `Diagram_Art_8-6.drawio.png`, and `Diagram_Art_19-2-a.drawio.png`: an ODRL rule points to a target data asset,
an action, an assignee, and sometimes a request constraint. This intended rule graph is not instantiated by the
fixtures or queried by the checker; the actual queries match operational facts directly.

## Extractable gold

### 1. A compact cross-ontology legal/data spine

`DAOnt.ttl` supplies a useful decomposition to recreate independently:

- **Asset/data spine:** `odrl:Asset` <- `Data`, with `ProductData`, `RelatedServiceData`, and `Metadata` below it;
  DPV `PersonalData` and `NonPersonalData` are also placed below `Data`, and are disjoint.
- **Product provenance:** `ConnectedProduct` is an ODRL asset and must `generatesData` at least one `Data`;
  `generatesData` and `isGeneratedBy` are inverse properties.
- **Actor spine:** ODRL `Party` is specialized by `User`, `DataHolder`, `DataRecipient`, and `ServiceProvider`;
  `Manufacturer` is both `DataHolder` and `ServiceProvider`, while `ThirdParty` is a `DataRecipient` and
  `AftermarketServiceProvider` is a `ThirdParty`.
- **User/data-sharing spine:** `DataSharing` is a `LegalAction`, split into `B2CDataSharing`, `B2BDataSharing`,
  and `B2GDataSharing`; `governedBy` connects sharing to an ODRL agreement and `authorizedBy` to a user.
- **Contract terms:** ODRL `Agreement` has `ContractualTerm`; `FRANDTerms` and `UnfairTerm` specialize that term,
  while `subjectToFRAND` points to a `FRANDCondition` evaluated through `isFair`, `isReasonable`, and
  `isNonDiscriminatory` booleans plus `compensationAmount`.
- **Public-interest lane:** `PublicSectorBody`, `UnionBody`, `ExceptionalNeed`, `DataRequest`, and
  `UseDataToDevelopCompetingProduct` capture the B2G request/justification/prohibited-use triangle.
- **Temporal/automation lane:** `TimePoint` and `TimeInterval` specialize `TemporalEntity`; agreements are
  `validDuring` temporal entities, actions `occursAt` them, and `SmartContract` is `implementedBy` a
  `DataProcessingService` which can `compliesWith` an `InteroperabilityStandard`.

The stronger OWL constraints are also worth recreating as validation requirements: a right has at least one right holder; personal data at least one natural-person subject; a connected product at least one generated datum; a
data holder at least one datum and one duty; a data-sharing action at least one authorizing user; a duty at least
one bearer; a rule at least one action; and an ODRL party exactly one party category. Explicit disjointness covers
personal/non-personal data, user/data-recipient, large-enterprise/SME, and product/related-service data.

### 2. A rule anatomy that keeps source, trigger, effect, and modality separate

The most reusable modeling pattern is in `DAOnt.ttl` around `LegalRule` and its properties. A rule can `derivesFrom` a `LegalSource`, have an IF-side `hasAntecedent` `RuleCondition`, a `hasException`, a THEN-side
`hasConsequent` `LegalEffect`, and a `hasDeonticSpecification` ODRL `Rule`. Specialized links make the modality
explicit: `prescribes` -> `Duty`, `permits` -> `Permission`, and `prohibits` -> `Prohibition`.

The load-bearing shape is compact:

```text
LegalRule --hasAntecedent--> RuleCondition
          --hasException---> RuleCondition
          --hasDeonticSpecification--> Duty | Permission | Prohibition
          --derivesFrom----> LegalSource
```

`ConstitutiveRule` and `RegulativeRule` split definition-making rules from behavior-prescribing rules.
`ActionCondition` and `DataContentCondition` split trigger evidence by kind, with inverse-facing fulfillment links
from actual ODRL actions and data. This is a good clean-room blueprint for rule evaluation without conflating a
legal provision, its applicability facts, its normative force, and its observed fulfillment.

### 3. Article-scoped SKOS collections as navigational overlays

`DAOnt.ttl` uses `skos:Collection` individuals rather than making every provision a class hierarchy. `Article4_Concepts` has 15 members spanning B2C sharing, actors, product/data, access rights, `ProvideData`, and
request/ownership links. `Article8_Concepts` has 18 members spanning B2B actors, agreement/terms, FRAND measures,
authorization, access, and comparable treatment. `Article19_2_a_Concepts` has nine members spanning B2G sharing,
public bodies, exceptional need, requests, responses, and the prohibited competitive-use action.

This is a concrete two-layer pattern: keep the ontology hierarchy stable, then curate provision-specific concept
sets for navigation, documentation, query-pack assembly, and coverage reporting. Many core classes additionally
carry English/Spanish `skos:prefLabel` values plus English definitions, examples, and scope notes. The annotations
are useful evidence of the documentation contract, but their prose and membership lists must not be copied.

### 4. Applicability-first, violation-row SPARQL shapes

The query suite is only three files, but each demonstrates a portable check-pack structure: establish scenario applicability, bind the legally relevant actors/assets/agreement, match a forbidden fact or absence, then emit a
stable machine code and human explanation as result columns.

- `compliance-checks/queries/query-4.1.sparql` binds B2C sharing -> agreement -> consumer, the consumer's product
  and requested data, the product/data provenance, and the holder. Its violation predicate is the absence of any
  holder action typed `ProvideData`: `FILTER NOT EXISTS { ?holder ... ?provision . ?provision a ...:ProvideData }`.
- `query-8.6.sparql` binds authorized B2B sharing, user/product/data, holder, agreement, and recipient. It reports
  `UNJUSTIFIED_REFUSAL` only when both a `ProvideData` action and a trade-secret marker are absent.
- `query-19.2.a.sparql` positively matches a B2G recipient that is both `PublicSectorBody` and `DataRecipient`,
  receives holder data, and performs an action typed `UseDataToDevelopCompetingProduct`; it emits
  `COMPETITIVE_PRODUCT_DEVELOPMENT`.

The valuable part is the output contract, not these exact queries: every hit is an evidence row containing bound
entities plus `violationType` and `details`. Reimplement this as typed check descriptors with explicit
applicability, evidence requirements, legal-source/version links, outcome semantics, and deterministic fixtures.

### 5. Paired minimal counterexample fixtures

`compliance-checks/contracts/` contains compliant/violation pairs for a smart-watch B2C request, industrial-robot B2B access, and public-health B2G sharing. The pairs preserve most graph facts and alter the decisive evidence:

- B2C compliance adds a holder `performsLegalAction` edge and a `ProvideData` action; violation omits them.
- B2B compliance adds both a `ProvideData` action and a trade-secret resource; violation omits both.
- B2G violation adds the public body's competitive-development action; compliance omits it.

Equivalent SPARQL evaluation over each fixture produced zero/one rows in the expected compliant/violation order
for all three pairs. This paired-delta test design is excellent; improve it by varying one fact per fixture,
isolating each exception independently, assigning scenario-local IRIs or named graphs, and adding unknown/error
fixtures rather than treating every non-hit as compliance.

### 6. A simple end-to-end check/report boundary

`compliance-checks/compliance_checker.py` separates `ComplianceResult`, `ContractComplianceReport`, the graph/query executor, and `ComplianceReporter`. Its pipeline is: validate paths -> parse base ontology -> parse contract ->
detect B2C/B2B/B2G -> select article mapping -> load query text -> execute -> turn each row into a violation ->
aggregate -> print or serialize JSON. `run_compliance_check.py` scans all `.owl` fixtures and writes a dated report;
`dashboard.py` loads the latest JSON into aggregate metrics, a violation table, and per-contract article views.

The boundary worth taking is “check engine emits a versioned evidence report; presentation consumes only that
report.” Keep the engine headless and deterministic, and make the report schema preserve query/check version,
applicability, evidence graph identifiers, execution error, and legal effective-time—not just a boolean.

### 7. Defects that should become acceptance tests, not copied behavior

- **Namespace split:** the ontology uses `https://w3id.org/def/daont#`; all fixtures and queries use
  `http://www.semanticweb.org/daont#`; checker fallback detection uses `http://www.semanticweb.org/dataact#`.
  Filename-first detection masks the third mismatch, and loading the base ontology does not align the graphs.
- **Schema/query drift:** fixtures and queries use undeclared `ownsOrUses`; fixtures also introduce undeclared
  schema terms such as `TradeSecretData`, `LegalReference`, `hasProvider`, and `providesData`. The canonical
  ontology defines `containsTradeSecret` as a boolean datatype property, while the B2B fixture/query use it as an
  object edge to a `TradeSecretData` resource.
- **Diagram/TBox drift:** the Article 8 diagram uses `RefuseDataAccess`, absent from `DAOnt.ttl`; Article 4/8
  diagrams use `DataRequest` as an ODRL constraint although it is modeled as a `LegalAction`. Scope notes say
  `DataProvision`, but the declared and queried class is `ProvideData`.
- **Under-correlated negation:** Article 4/8 accept any `ProvideData` action by the holder; the action is not tied
  to the selected data, recipient, request, agreement, deadline, format, or charge. Article 19 does not tie the
  prohibited action to the selected data or exceptional need. These shapes are demonstrations, not legal tests.
- **Closed-world overclaim:** zero violation rows becomes “compliant”; missing applicability/evidence is not
  distinguished from a proven pass. No check covers Article 4 timing/free/machine-readable requirements, actual
  FRAND values, mitigation of trade-secret risk, or the validity of exceptional need.
- **Fail-open errors:** `overall_compliant` excludes checks having `error`; if all checks error, `all([])` is true.
  The CLI likewise excludes load-error reports from its final `all(...)`, and the dashboard does not surface check
  errors. A query execution failure can therefore be displayed and exited as compliant.
- **Fixture contamination risk:** each compliant/violation pair reuses the same ontology and entity IRIs. Loading
  both variants into one graph merges their facts, so the fixtures are valid only as isolated graphs.

## Port discipline notes

The ontology metadata and README identify CC BY-SA 4.0, although the checked-out repository has no root `LICENSE` file despite the README's tree claiming one. CC BY-SA permits sharing and adaptation, including commercially,
when attribution is supplied and adapted material is distributed under the same license. That is legally more
permissive than this mine's required project discipline.

For Beep, treat DAOnt as **clean-room pattern only**. Do not vendor or translate `DAOnt.ttl`/`DAOnt.owl`, class or property inventories, SKOS annotations/collections, SPARQL text, Python code, fixtures, diagrams, screenshots,
generated OnToology documentation, or code-snippet images. Any copied/adapted DAOnt expression would require
attribution and ShareAlike handling and is outside this port lane.

Independently design from the abstractions recorded here, with new names, schemas, queries, examples, prose, and
tests. Reuse ODRL, DPV, LKIF, SKOS, PROV, and EU-law identifiers only from their canonical upstream specifications
after separately checking their licenses and semantics; do not copy DAOnt's selection or local declarations as a
shortcut. Preserve DAOnt only as an external research citation and behavioral comparison target.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation SKOS / `@beep/ontology`:** independently add provision-scoped concept collections over a
  stable class graph, with bilingual labels, source/version provenance, and coverage reports generated from typed
  membership data rather than embedding article navigation in the subclass hierarchy.
- **Epistemic slice:** represent `applicable`, `satisfied`, `violated`, `unknown`, and `execution-error` separately;
  attach the exact evidence bindings, missing-evidence reasons, graph identity, closed/open-world policy, and
  legal/as-of time so absence never silently becomes proof of compliance.
- **Patent tooling:** reuse the abstract source -> rule -> condition/exception -> deontic modality -> action/asset/
  party pattern for patent-license duties, permissions, prohibitions, and FRAND terms, while keeping patent claims,
  agreements, standards commitments, and observed conduct as different entity families.
- **Gov-legal data drivers:** build a versioned EU-law/contract ingestion adapter that canonicalizes namespaces,
  validates external vocabulary mappings and SHACL-like shapes, isolates named graphs, and refuses check execution
  when applicability or required evidence is incomplete.
- **OIP practice:** package each compliance rule as a reviewable check artifact: legal citation and effective
  version, applicability query, violation/satisfaction query, typed output schema, severity, limitations, and
  one-fact-at-a-time positive/negative/unknown/error fixtures. The report, not UI state, is the audit boundary.

## Extraction confidence

**High for repository structure and static semantics; medium for executable behavior; low for legal sufficiency.** All named mine targets were readable. `DAOnt.ttl` and `DAOnt.owl` both parse as RDF; ontology counts, restrictions,
disjointness, inverse properties, SKOS membership, all six fixture graphs, query text, checker, runner, dashboard,
and four diagrams were inspected directly. Python's `rdflib` dependency was not installed in the mining
environment, so the repository's exact Python entry point was not executed. Raptor/Rasqal plus equivalent query
forms verified the intended zero/one-row fixture deltas; control-flow claims come from direct source inspection.

No competency questions, entailment regime, SHACL suite, legal-expert validation, coverage benchmark, or automated
tests are present. The ontology is rich enough to mine architectural patterns, but the compliance outputs should
be treated as illustrative demonstrations until namespace coherence, schema/query alignment, evidence correlation,
fail-closed status semantics, and provision-level legal validation are supplied.
