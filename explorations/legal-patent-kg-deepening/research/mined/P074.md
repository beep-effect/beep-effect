# Semantic Model for Legal Resources: Annotation and Reasoning over Normative Provisions

## Problem & positioning

- Users need access to norms and relations among norms, not merely whole legislative documents containing relevant words.
- Legal retrieval should expose duties, rights, procedures, sanctions, actors, and actions, including norms implicit in correlated provisions.
- Combining unrestricted rules with ontologies can become undecidable; the paper seeks useful legal reasoning that remains within OWL-DL tractability.
- The approach refines structurally marked legislative fragments with semantic provision types and attributes.
- It demonstrates the model against an excerpt of European Directive 2002/65/EC on distance marketing of consumer financial services.
- The work is positioned as a reusable reasoning pattern over normative provisions rather than a domain-complete consumer-law ontology.

## Approach / architecture

- A legislative fragment simultaneously has a structural view, such as paragraph, and a semantic view as a provision carried by a speech act.
- The Provision Model divides provision types into Rules and Rules on Rules.
- Rules divide into constitutive rules and regulative rules; the latter govern actions, procedural defaults, and remedies.
- Rules on Rules divide into content, temporal, and extension amendments.
- Provision attributes identify roles and content such as Bearer, Counterpart, Action, Object, and Definiendum.
- Attribute values may be literals or concepts from a domain thesaurus/ontology; the example uses a DALOS consumer-law ontology.
- CEN-MetaLex structural markup identifies articles, paragraphs, subparagraphs, and spans with persistent fragment IDs.
- RDF/OWL annotations type each fragment as an explicit provision and attach role-qualified properties.
- Pellet materializes OWL-DL inferences; an RDF triple store then supports SPARQL retrieval over explicit and implicit views.
- Query patterns retrieve rights, duties, powers, procedures, and correlated provisions without a separate bespoke legal-rule engine.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- Core provision classes include TermDefinition, Procedure, Duty, Right, Power, Liability, Liberty, No-right, Immunity, Disability, Redress, and amendment types.
- The model distinguishes logical relations, necessary under Hohfeldian schemes, from technical relations arising from legislative drafting practices.
- Correlative pairs are Right/Duty, Liberty/No-right, Power/Liability, and Immunity/Disability; opposite relations are separately acknowledged.
- A duty borne by Supplier toward Consumer is another view of an implicit right borne by Consumer toward Supplier.
- Naively asserting `Duty ≡ Right` and `hasBearer ≡ hasCounterpart` overgeneralizes and can mix roles across unrelated provision types.
- The repair is role-qualified properties: `hasDutyBearer`, `hasDutyCounterpart`, `hasRightBearer`, and `hasRightCounterpart`, with explicit/implicit refinements.
- Right and Duty each receive disjoint explicit and implicit subclasses whose union covers the superclass.
- Only explicit classes annotate text; implicit classes act as abstract reasoning views over the same provision individual.
- Key class axioms include `ImplicitRight ≡ ExplicitDuty` and `ImplicitDuty ≡ ExplicitRight`.
- Key property equivalences swap roles, for example `hasImplicitRightBearer ≡ hasExplicitDutyCounterpart`.
- The same isomorphic pattern applies to Power/Liability, with bearer and counterpart roles swapped.
- Subclass and subproperty axioms preserve general queries while qualified properties prevent semantically inconsistent role mixing.
- These axioms do not create a second provision instance; they derive explicit and implicit views of the same text-anchored provision.
- Technical relations connect distinct provision instances, such as Duty-to-Procedure, Duty-to-Exception, Duty-to-Sanction, or Definition-to-usage.
- Technical relations may be explicit references or inferred from matching attribute values.
- A strong technical relation matches all corresponding attribute values; a weak relation matches only some, with match count indicating degree.
- The model combines structural fragment identity, provision semantics, domain concepts, and inferential views in one queryable RDF graph.

## Evaluation & results

- Evaluation is a worked demonstration, not a quantitative benchmark.
- The Directive excerpt annotates duties, a procedure, a right, and powers at identified article/paragraph fragments.
- A non-inferred general query for `hasRightBearer Consumer` returns no result because markup uses only explicit qualified properties.
- Querying explicit consumer rights returns `art5;par3`.
- Querying the inferred model also derives implicit rights from duties and returns `art5;par1` and `art6;par1`.
- Thus the virtual query expansion covers one explicit-right property and its equivalent implicit-right/explicit-duty counterpart property.
- A supplier-duty query returns explicit `art5;par1` and implicit `art5;par3` under Hohfeldian reasoning.
- Matching Bearer, Action, Object, and Counterpart across Duty and Procedure retrieves `art5;par1` and correlated procedure `art5;par2`.
- The same union-query pattern can extend to sanctions of type Redress.
- No runtime, ontology size, triple count, precision/recall, competency-question suite, or inter-annotator agreement is reported.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The authors call this an early contribution toward identifying legal reasoning schemas that fit OWL-DL and leave sufficient-condition analysis for future work.
- The approach requires reliable structural markup and intellectually demanding semantic classification of provisions and attributes.
- The authors state semantic markup cannot be obtained merely by automatic XML-to-RDF conversion and still needs human interpretation.
- NLP or machine learning can support provision classification and attribute extraction, but the paper does not evaluate those components.
- The demonstration uses one small directive excerpt and hand-selected queries, so generality across drafting styles and jurisdictions is unproven.
- Open-world OWL semantics and monotonic inference do not directly capture defeasibility, exceptions, priority, conflict, repeal, or changing legal validity.
- Technical-relation inference by attribute equality can create spurious links when concepts are co-referential, underspecified, or context-dependent.
- Treating implicit and explicit meanings as views of one individual is elegant for retrieval but can blur provenance unless derivation paths remain first-class.
- Temporal amendments are classified but not operationalized with effective-time or transaction-time semantics in the example.
- The generic `owl:Class` range for domain attribute values weakens validation; modern implementations should use more precise shapes and concept ranges.
- The extracted paper does not clearly expose publication venue or date metadata, so neither is inferred here.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** take the separation between provision ontology and domain concept schemes; use SKOS concepts as attribute values while keeping legal roles as typed ontology properties.
- **epistemic slice:** preserve explicit assertions and inferred views separately, with derivation edges, evidence spans, and bitemporal validity rather than materializing them as indistinguishable facts.
- **epistemic slice:** adopt strong/weak technical-relation matching as scored claim candidates, then route ambiguous links and incompatible norms into contradiction triage.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** implement role-oriented queries such as “client duties plus procedures, exceptions, and sanctions,” always returning paragraph-level citations and inference explanations.
- **patent tooling (uspto-patent-driver-depth):** reuse explicit/implicit view patterns for claim obligations, examiner requirements, responses, and rule-defined deadlines, but add defeasibility and event-time handling.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** store a reasoner's proof path as memory provenance so an inferred right or duty can be revised when its source provision changes.
