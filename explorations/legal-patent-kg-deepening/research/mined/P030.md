# Collaborative System Synergizing Human Expertise and Large-scale Language Models for Legal Knowledge Graph Construction

## Problem & positioning

- District-court judgments contain a bottom-up reasoning hierarchy: evidence supports detailed facts, facts support pivotal facts, and those facts connect to norms and decisions.
- Existing legal KGs mostly organize documents, laws, or citations and lack phrase-level links between factual and legal language in judgments.
- Generic vocabularies such as schema.org and DBpedia are too coarse for the required sentence-span annotations.
- LLM-only extraction misses legal details, while expert-only annotation is costly; the paper positions collaboration as the practical construction strategy.
- The work is a short system paper centered on Japanese civil district-court judgments and expert correction of machine-proposed labels.

## Approach / architecture

- Judgment documents are converted to text, segmented to fit model context limits, and sent to GPT-4 with a task-specific prompt.
- GPT output is structured as JSON; pattern matching maps generated strings back to original text spans.
- The annotations are imported into Doccano, where legal experts inspect and correct them through a graphical interface.
- Prompt labels cover legal provisions, facts, legal norms, interpretations, judicial decisions, and impressions.
- The prompt instructs the model to preserve the original text and create no new content, constraining annotation to source-grounded spans.
- Because experts find false-positive deletion easier than discovering omissions, prompt engineering explicitly prioritizes recall over precision.
- Comparing GPT proposals with expert revisions yields agreement metrics and an operational feedback signal for future KG improvement.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- The ontology separates `LegalProvision`, `Fact`, `LegalNorm`, `Interpretation`, `JudicialDecision`, and `Impression` roles.
- A legal provision is an explicit statutory reference; a fact is a court-recognized reality; a norm is a principle derived from a provision.
- Interpretation represents applying a provision or norm to facts, serving as the bridge from the legal axis to the factual axis.
- Judicial decisions capture the court's conclusion, rationale, and legal assessment; impressions hold opinions outside the logical structure.
- The intended graph is hierarchical: factual propositions aggregate upward into critical facts and then connect to the applicable legal layer.
- Extraction is phrase-oriented, similar to NER, so ontology instances remain anchored in the judgment rather than synthesized summaries.
- Party perspectives—plaintiff, defendant, and court—are conceptually organized, although the evaluation table does not report perspective-specific relations.
- The paper presents node/attribute extraction more concretely than edge predicates or formal axioms; the detailed ontology serialization is not shown.

## Evaluation & results

- The evaluation uses 11 representative civil district-court judgments selected from 100 judgments supplied by the Supreme Court of Japan.
- Topics include trademark infringement, copyright, defamation, labor injuries, boundary disputes, and negotiable-instrument acquisition.
- Phrase-level matching is used instead of character-level scoring to avoid over-penalizing small boundary differences.
- Across evaluated labels, GPT-4 obtained 272 TP, 101 FP, 70 FN, precision 0.73, and recall 0.80.
- Provisions scored precision 0.87 and recall 0.89 from 52 TP, 8 FP, and 6 FN.
- Facts scored precision 0.77 and recall 0.87 from 152 TP, 43 FP, and 22 FN.
- Legal norms scored precision 0.36 and recall 0.71; legal interpretations scored precision 0.41 and recall 1.00.
- Judicial decisions were the only reported category with recall below precision: precision 0.69 and recall 0.57.
- The authors conclude that GPT-4 can pre-label Japanese judgments and reduce expert annotation effort, but no time or cost reduction is quantified.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- Only 11 judgments are evaluated, limiting confidence across courts, case types, drafting styles, and time periods.
- Selection is described as representative but not randomized, and the annotation protocol does not report inter-annotator agreement.
- Low norm and interpretation precision means experts still face substantial correction work in the most reasoning-sensitive classes.
- The abstract promises a hierarchical linked ontology, but evaluation is NER-like span extraction rather than end-to-end edge or graph validation.
- Impressions are defined but omitted from the results table; the reason and treatment are unclear.
- The model, prompt version, sampling configuration, segmentation strategy, and boundary matching rules are not fully reported in the paper text.
- Avoiding hallucinated text does not prevent semantic misclassification or incorrect linkage between faithful source spans.
- No provenance schema records machine proposal, expert edit, adjudication, or confidence as graph data.
- No temporal, jurisdictional, precedential, contradictory, or defeasible dimensions are modeled.
- The claim of lower annotation cost lacks measured minutes, corrections, cognitive load, or comparison with annotation from scratch.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **epistemic slice:** Preserve machine proposal and expert correction as separate claim revisions, with who/when/evidence-span provenance rather than overwriting annotations in Doccano.
- **epistemic slice:** Adopt recall-first candidate generation for high-stakes review, but gate promotion on typed expert decisions and track class-specific precision debt.
- **semantic-foundation:** Reuse the provision/fact/norm/application distinction as named SKOS concepts, while rejecting `Impression` as an underspecified catch-all until it has formal scope.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Offer source-locked pre-annotation where a lawyer deletes false positives and fills omissions; measure correction time and missed-issue rate explicitly.
- **patent tooling — agentic-cad-patent-tooling:** Apply the same human-in-the-loop span workflow to disclosure facts, claim constraints, cited rules, and examiner conclusions.
- **agent memory — Cognee:** Treat expert edits as durable supervision events and annotation lineage, not merely the final accepted graph state.
