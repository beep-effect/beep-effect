# Patent-KG: Patent Knowledge Graph Extraction for Engineering Design

## Problem & positioning

Patent corpora contain reusable engineering knowledge at a scale no individual designer can read.
The paper contrasts more than 3 million patents and 1.5 million papers published in 2019 with an estimated human reading capacity of 264 papers per year.
It positions a knowledge graph as a collection of atomic `(head, relation, tail)` facts that can support design retrieval and ideation.
Existing patent engineering networks rely on co-occurrence, semantic similarity, hand-written syntactic rules, or supervised labels.
The stated gap is scalable fact extraction without exhaustive rules or expensive patent annotations.
Patent-KG therefore tests an unsupervised use of pretrained BERT attention as a relation signal.

## Approach / architecture

The source corpus is 457,815 patent abstracts from 2016-2021 whose CPC code begins with section F: mechanical engineering, lighting, heating, engines, or pumps.
spaCy splits abstracts into sentences, with semicolons added as patent-specific sentence boundaries.
Token preprocessing joins noun phrases, including multiword technical terms, and overrides hyphen tokenization for letter-hyphen-letter compounds.
It also joins phrasal verbs so particles such as `to`, `through`, or `with` remain part of relation candidates.
Noun phrases become candidate heads and tails; relations are mostly verbs or phrasal verbs.
Four dependency patterns motivate attention-head selection: direct object (`dobj`), nominal subject (`nsubj`), copula (`cop`), and prepositional object (`pobj`).
The method uses pretrained BERT's 12 layers by 12 heads and empirically selects attention head 8-10 as best for those patterns.
Subword attention is lifted to phrase attention: outgoing attention from phrase tokens is averaged, while incoming attention is summed.
For every candidate head-tail pair, backward beam search follows `tail -> relation -> head` through high-attention words.
Backward search is justified by BERT's contextual directionality: later tokens are treated as containing information about preceding tokens.
The retained path is the candidate relation with the greatest summed tail-to-relation and relation-to-head attention.
The worked example chooses `(magnetic force, levitates, shaft)` over `(magnetic force, provided, shaft)` with scores 1.0257 and 0.5838.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

The graph's unit is an open-domain engineering triple rather than an ontology-constrained assertion.
Entity nodes are technical noun phrases, including compounds and newly coined patent terminology.
Edges are surface-form verbal relations, including phrasal verbs, rather than a normalized relation vocabulary.
The graph contains 4,157,377 entities and 10,991,896 edges; preprocessing recognizes 317,789 phrasal verbs.
The extraction pattern preserves directed operational relations such as `sensor -> sends -> signal` and `magnetic force -> levitates -> shaft`.
CPC section F supplies a coarse domain boundary, but CPC classes are not described as nodes or semantic constraints in the resulting graph.
No classes, subclass axioms, domain/range axioms, identity rules, provenance model, confidence values, or temporal qualifiers are specified.
Likewise, the paper does not describe entity resolution: identical strings, synonyms, polysemes, and patent-specific mentions may remain conflated or fragmented.
Its main modeling contribution is thus relation-bearing engineering fact extraction, not a reusable formal patent ontology.

## Evaluation & results

Entity coverage uses a manually assembled gold list of 180 mechanical-engineering terms across 3 categories and 13 subcategories.
Patent-KG's total concept retrieval rate is 0.82, compared with TechNet 0.79, B-link/Feng 0.63, ConceptNet 0.56, and WordNet 0.46.
For engines or pumps, the rates are 0.77, 0.67, 0.54, 0.37, and 0.30 respectively in the same comparison order.
For engineering in general, Patent-KG scores 0.82 versus TechNet 0.81, B-link/Feng 0.66, ConceptNet 0.42, and WordNet 0.40.
For lighting and heating, Patent-KG scores 0.86, below TechNet and ConceptNet at 0.88 and above B-link/Feng at 0.70 and WordNet at 0.68.
A separate hand-built list of mechanical-engineering relations yields 0.67 retrieval for Patent-KG.
The authors decline cross-dataset relation comparison because some baselines lack semantic edges and WordNet emphasizes taxonomic relations.
The abstract rounds the best result to 0.8, while Table 3 reports the more precise total rate of 0.82.
Evaluation measures vocabulary coverage only; it does not measure triple precision, relation correctness, entity boundary accuracy, or usefulness in a design task.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

The authors report weak performance on passive sentences and very long sentences.
They propose patent-domain fine-tuning and combining attention with syntactic rules as future improvements.
Attention strength is not evidence that an extracted proposition is true, salient, or correctly scoped.
Selecting one attention head from an unspecified experiment risks corpus-specific tuning and has no reported ablation or held-out validation.
The manual 180-term and relation lists lack annotation protocol, inter-annotator agreement, and public representativeness evidence in the paper.
Recall-only evaluation rewards over-generation; the millions of nodes and edges could contain substantial noise without affecting the reported metric.
Abstract-only extraction omits claim structure, embodiments, citations, legal status, applicants, inventors, and paragraph-level provenance.
No mechanism handles negation, modality, conditions, coordination, passive voice, coreference, or cross-sentence facts.
Surface relations and unresolved phrases limit interoperability and dependable graph reasoning.
