# Uma Experiência com Engenharia de Requisitos Baseada em Modelos de Processos

## Problem & positioning

- Requirements engineering depends on effective communication, yet a conventional analyst-by-analyst elicitation process can omit actors, misunderstand decisions, and lose organizational context.
- The paper tests business-process modeling as an explicit intermediate layer between organizational goals/processes and system requirements.
- Its case concerns software for managing selected human-resources processes at a large Brazilian energy-sector company.
- An independently produced conventional requirements specification is contrasted with a second specification derived from business-process models.
- The authors position process modeling as a complement to conventional requirements practice, not as a system-development-dependent activity.

## Approach / architecture

- The conventional path produces a vision document, business-solution document, business rules, glossary, use cases, and supplementary nonfunctional specification.
- The process-oriented path first maps the organizational value chain, refines macro-processes into process chains, then refines those into atomic activity models with assigned resources.
- Client and analyst jointly classify each activity as non-automatable, system-supported, or fully automatable.
- Classification considers organizational policy, law, security constraints, and other factors rather than treating automation as predetermined.
- One process model can generate alternative requirement sets `R1...RN` and corresponding systems `S1...SN`, each embodying a different automation level.
- Selected activities yield functional requirements and use cases; service qualities such as performance, security, and availability become nonfunctional requirements.
- The approach explicitly transitions from high-level conceptual business models to logical system-design models.
- The comparison uses completeness, correctness, consistency, stakeholder/analyst contextualization, and traceability as qualitative criteria.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- Although the paper does not propose an ontology, it supplies a useful provenance chain: organizational strategy → value chain → macro-process → process chain → activity → automation decision → requirement → use case/system feature.
- `Activity` should carry an automation disposition rather than a fixed binary type: human-only, system-supported, and fully automated are design choices constrained by context.
- The same process can support multiple `RequirementSet` alternatives, so requirements should not be treated as uniquely entailed by a process model.
- An `AutomationDecision` links a process activity, stakeholders, policies/laws/security constraints, intended system, and chosen support level.
- Functional requirements represent services provided to selected activities; nonfunctional requirements qualify service behavior across one or more use cases.
- Traceability is bidirectional: each system function or constraint should point to the business need/activity that motivated it, and each modeled need should expose its system realization or deliberate non-automation.
- Joint process modeling creates a shared context in which conflicting local accounts from different process executors can be surfaced.
- Volatility is a decision attribute: the authors propose a general inverse relationship between requirement volatility and advisable automation level.

## Evaluation & results

- The evaluation is a single qualitative industrial case; the underlying specifications are omitted for space, and no counts, statistical tests, effort measures, or participant totals are reported.
- Completeness improved because process modeling exposed missing requirements for decisions made by the Medical Assistance actor.
- Correctness improved when the process view showed that an employee could not request an extension after the social-security authority had denied incapacity leave, contrary to the conventional specification.
- Consistency improved because bringing process executors together revealed disagreements in their descriptions of shared process segments.
- Contextualization improved because system purpose and selected automation scope became explicit, documented, and later validatable.
- Traceability improved because graphical process models let users identify problems and let analysts relate business needs to system functionality and constraints.
- The conclusion says the process-based result was more complete, correct, traceable, and consistent with multiple stakeholder views, but offers no quantitative magnitude.
- The method also yielded explicit alternative requirement sets at different automation levels rather than one arbitrary system boundary.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The authors omit both specifications, preventing independent inspection of the claimed differences.
- The study covers one HR setting in one company and does not establish generalizability across organizations, legal regimes, or process types.
- Independent teams reduce direct contamination but introduce analyst-skill, time, access, and facilitation confounds that are not controlled or reported.
- Evaluation criteria are qualitative; there is no blinded review, ground-truth requirements set, defect count, inter-rater agreement, or downstream implementation outcome.
- The process notation, tool, elicitation duration, number and roles of participants, and model-maintenance burden are not described.
- The authors reserve the volatility/automation tradeoff and alignment with off-the-shelf/workflow systems for future investigation.
- A process model can itself encode a mistaken consensus; the method needs evidence links and dissent preservation rather than assuming the jointly produced model is true.
- Laws and policies appear only as factors in automation choice, without explicit authority, version, jurisdiction, effective-time, or exception modeling.
- Deriving use cases from activities may bias design toward current workflows and preserve avoidable organizational practices.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **epistemic slice:** Represent stakeholder process accounts as attributed claims before consolidation; preserve contradictions, resolution decisions, and exact evidence spans instead of overwriting dissent with one diagram.
- **patent tooling (agentic-cad-patent-tooling):** Generate several agent/tool allocation designs from one prosecution or invention workflow, recording why each step is human-only, assisted, or automated.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Use the explicit chain from client objective through matter activity and automation decision to requirement/use case, making every feature traceable to practice value.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Apply the volatility heuristic cautiously to rules-heavy workflows: volatile court, client, or agency requirements should favor inspectable human gates over deep automation.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** Retain process versions, actor-specific observations, selected automation level, rejected alternatives, and resulting requirements as separate linked artifacts.
