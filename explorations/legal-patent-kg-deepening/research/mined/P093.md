# Understanding Hohfeld and Formalizing Legal Rights: The Hohfeldian Conceptions and Their Conditional Consequences

## Problem & positioning

The paper addresses the unresolved formalization of Hohfeld’s eight fundamental legal conceptions while preserving their sui generis and inherently relational character.
Classical Kanger–Lindahl normative-position work emphasizes Boolean/algebraic combinations but largely loses the counterparty direction that Hohfeld assigns to every position.
The author seeks necessary-and-sufficient behavioral descriptions through conditional consequences, not reductive definitions based on will or interest theories of rights.
The first group—claim-right, duty, privilege, and no-claim—is deontic; the second—power, liability, immunity, and disability—concerns capacity to change legal positions.
The paper is explicitly about legal rights, relying on state enforcement to distinguish them from moral, religious, social, or organizational norms.
It also argues that apparently absolute rights can be reconstructed as aggregates of directed Hohfeldian relations.

## Approach / architecture

The base language has propositional atoms, Boolean operations, an agent-indexed “sees to it that” operator `E_a`, and directed obligation `O_a→b` over a finite set of agents.
Action uses Chellas’s minimal non-normal modal logic ET with successful-action constraint; directed obligation uses Standard Deontic Logic (SDL).
Models contain possible worlds `W`, agent action functions, directed obligation accessibility relations, and a valuation.
An S5 legal-necessity operator is added to express consequences that hold as part of the legal system rather than by material implication alone.
The language introduces a judiciary agent `j`, compensation operator `C`, sanction operator `S`, and higher judicial instances for appeals.
Power is modeled with conditional effects on legal positions and then given a Public-Announcement-Logic-like model-update semantics.
The account remains inside an already institutional legal system; it separates Hohfeldian power from Searlean institutional power and does not model how brute facts initially become institutional facts.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

The eight position types are `ClaimRight`, `Duty`, `Privilege`, `NoClaim`, `Power`, `Liability`, `Immunity`, and `Disability`.
Every position carries agents and propositional/action content; active positions constrain the position holder and actor to be the same, while passive positions concern another actor.
Claim-right and duty are correlatives: `CR_x(E_y F)` iff `O_y→x(E_y F)`, preserving the right holder as the obligation’s counterparty.
Privilege is not a generic absence of duty: `PR_x⊣y(E_x F)` is the lack of x’s duty toward the specific y to refrain from F.
Privilege and no-claim are correlatives; no-claim is the opposite of the corresponding claim-right.
Power must also be directed: `P_x→y(E_x Fᶜ)` correlates with y’s liability concerning x’s legally constitutive action.
Immunity is the absence of the corresponding liability; disability is the absence of the corresponding power, again with direction preserved.
A duty/claim-right is behaviorally characterized by state enforcement: upon legally established nonperformance, the original counterparty gains a claim-right against the judiciary for compensation.
That pattern creates a new relation involving the original right holder and judiciary, while judicial power may impose a remedial position on the original duty bearer.
For criminal law, the account aggregates claim-rights held by everyone against everyone not to commit a felony, followed by claims against the judiciary to sanction a proven perpetrator.
A civil liberty is modeled as a special claim-right against everyone else not to interfere, rather than as primitive privilege alone.
Appeals are a hierarchy of judicial agents: failure at one instance yields a claim against the higher instance to replace or remand the decision, with a top-level idempotence constraint to stop regress.
Hohfeldian power exists before exercise and is the potential to change another’s duty, privilege, power, or immunity through a legally constituted act `Fᶜ`.
Such acts include broad performative utterances or recognized conduct—sale, marriage, testamentary disposition, eviction, or transfer—that trigger constitutive rules only when the actor has power.
The power update retains worlds where the empowered legal act occurs and requires the target’s consequent legal position; an unempowered identical utterance has no legal effect.
Paucital positions target one or a few definite counterparties; multital positions are large sets of otherwise separate directed positions against an indefinite class.
“Decountering” aggregates directed relations into an undirected-looking position, explaining ownership as a bundle of multital claims, privileges, powers, and immunities.
For a KG, each position therefore needs holder, counterparty, actor, action/state content, polarity, legal source/system, enforcement consequence, and position-changing event.

## Evaluation & results

The paper derives 30 numbered formulas covering correlation, directedness, enforcement, criminal-law claims, civil liberties, appeals, power updates, negated positions, and decountering.
Worked examples include land access, ownership, contracts, jury summons, criminal offenses, civil liberties, sales, marriage, testamentary acts, and patent exclusion rights.
The formalization is evaluated through philosophical argument and consequence checking rather than a dataset, implementation, theorem prover, or user study.
No precision, recall, runtime, model-count, consistency-check result, participant count, or empirical legal-coverage statistic is reported.
The author’s stated contribution is a preliminary comprehensive image of a rights system, not a complete axiomatization.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

The author acknowledges the usual problems of SDL and retains it only to keep focus, noting it is unlikely to underlie a perfect theory of normative positions.
Compensation, sanction, and the internal structure of relevant states of affairs are left formally undeveloped.
The conditional account of power permits “trivial” powers and does not perfectly capture power’s trigger character.
The proposed power semantics raises unresolved well-foundedness and simultaneous-power questions; recursive power in consequent positions risks regress.
The finite agent set is a simplification, especially for multital rights over indefinite classes and changing institutional populations.
State enforcement is a substantive legal-positivist commitment; unenforceable or non-state legal orders will not fit without revising the criterion.
The account oscillates deliberately between descriptive and normative readings: an inconsistent but procedurally valid legal system may fail the proposed biconditionals.
Legal facts are assumed already established by a court, so evidence, burdens and standards of proof, uncertainty, appeals on fact, and contradictory findings are outside the model.
Time is implicit: duties, powers, judgments, and position changes need effective intervals and event order for operational use.
The paper supplies logical semantics, not an ontology implementation, identity policy, provenance model, or tractable RDF/OWL encoding.
