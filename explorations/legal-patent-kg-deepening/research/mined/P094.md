# Units of Analysis for the Legal Domain: A Legal Document Ontology

## Problem & positioning

- Machine interpretation across interrelated legal corpora needs stable units for both document organization and legal meaning; keyword matching and undifferentiated document-part taxonomies are insufficient.
- The proposed Legal Document Ontology (LDO) makes structural entities and semantic information-content entities its two primary units of analysis.
- It is aligned to Basic Formal Ontology (BFO) and Common Core Ontologies (CCO), while drawing document classes from the Information Artifact Ontology (IAO) and Document Components Ontology (DoCO).
- The paper argues that DoCO and IAO sometimes conflate a text-bearing block with the designation, comment, or rhetorical content it bears.
- Its intended payoff is graph-computable legal meaning across documents such as the U.S. Code and Code of Federal Regulations.

## Approach / architecture

- The authors ground repeatable legal content in BFO's `GenericallyDependentContinuant`: one law-content pattern may be borne by paper, a database, a textbook, or memory.
- CCO's `InformationContentEntity` (ICE) supplies aboutness, while `DocumentField` supplies a structural information-bearing location.
- LDO imports and specializes these strata rather than treating the document node, its fields, and their content as one hierarchy.
- The semantic axis uses CCO's designative, descriptive, and directive ICE distinction.
- The structural axis adds `LegalDocumentPart`, `LegalDocumentChapter`, `LegalClause`, and `LegalDocumentSection` beneath document/document-field concepts.
- The paper critiques DoCO's `label` and `footnote`: a block/location is structural, whereas the label or comment is reusable content with designation/aboutness.
- Legal prescriptions are modeled as `ProcessRegulation` entities about acts/processes and produced by authority-realizing legal-system acts.
- A table proposes inverse relation pairs for structural parthood, content bearing/dependence, aboutness, designation, description, prescription, permission, prohibition, and requirement.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- `Document` is an information-bearing artifact; `DocumentField` is a part into which prescribed information can be written or selected.
- `LegalDocumentPart` is an intermediate titled division; chapters and sections organize content, while a `LegalClause` carries a discrete proposition, obligation, condition, exception, right, or stipulation.
- A field `isCarrierOf` a law/content entity, and that content `genericallyDependsOn` a bearer; neither relation makes structure identical to meaning.
- `Title` is a designative ICE, not the textual block containing it; it designates a law.
- `Definition` is a descriptive ICE about an entity and can define person roles, objects, actions, events, or measurements.
- Directive ICEs prescribe entities; the paper narrows legal prescription to processes/acts because compliance and violation are actions.
- `Law` is defined as a process regulation that prescribes an act with a person agent and is produced by a legal-system act of a government legislature realizing authority.
- `CriminalLaw` specializes law by prohibiting a criminal act; state/local variants replace the relevant government agent and authority role.
- This authority pattern separates the prescriptive content from the institutional act and role that make it legally binding.
- Proposed semantic object properties include `isAbout`, `describes`, `designates`, `prescribes`, `permits`, `prohibits`, and `requires`, each paired with an inverse.
- Proposed structural/content-bridge properties include `hasMemberPart/memberPartOf` and `isCarrierOf/genericallyDependsOn`.
- The dual parthood/accounting axis permits structural queries (“which documents contain this field?”) and semantic queries (“which laws prohibit this activity?”) without conflation.
- U.S. Code titles, parts, chapters, and sections are illustrated as scaffolding that carries designations, definitions, and laws.

## Evaluation & results

- This is a first-version ontology proposal with definitions, diagrams, one object-property table, and a U.S. Code illustration; no implemented OWL artifact, reasoner run, competency-question suite, or corpus benchmark is reported.
- The paper demonstrates a nine-row property table, each row giving an object property and inverse.
- Figure 3 annotates a U.S. Code hierarchy whose title carries a designation, part/chapter carry definitions, and section carries a penalty-bearing law.
- Seven prospective law-enforcement applications are enumerated: field reference, cross-jurisdiction clarity, case classification, threat detection, training, decision support, and sensor/intelligence integration.
- These applications are proposed capabilities rather than measured results; there are no precision, recall, latency, coverage, or user-study numbers.
- The main analytic result is a reusable separation of structural containment from semantic aboutness and normative force.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The authors call this the first LDO version and advocate further integration of CCO, DoCO, and IAO; the presented taxonomy is therefore preliminary.
- No downloadable ontology, namespace, serialization, axiom listing, competency questions, SHACL constraints, or mapping procedure is shown in the paper.
- The `Law` definition is government-legislature-centric and does not directly cover constitutions, court-made rules, agency adjudication, treaties, tribal law, private ordering, or non-governmental normative systems.
- Contracts are said to fall under jurisdiction, but the definition requiring output by a legislature does not explain contract formation or the contract's own directive content.
- The paper claims evolving-law support but supplies no version, amendment, repeal, commencement, retroactivity, transaction-time, or temporal-part pattern.
- `LegalClause` is structurally defined yet said to carry semantic categories, leaving cardinality and granularity rules for mixed-content clauses unspecified.
- Permission, prohibition, and requirement are simple binary relations; conditions, exceptions, defeasibility, actors, objects, jurisdiction, sanctions, and rule priority remain unmodeled.
- The sharp claim that structure is void of meaning understates interpretive effects of placement, numbering, headings, scope, provisos, and syntactic attachment in legislation.
- Authority is modeled as a role realization but the paper does not show delegation, contested authority, invalid enactment, or judicial review.
- Proposed automated charge/decision support is high-risk and lacks uncertainty, evidentiary provenance, human-review, fairness, or error-governance semantics.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** Adopt distinct bearer, field, ICE, designation, description, directive, and real-world entity layers; map SKOS labels to designative content rather than document blocks.
- **epistemic slice:** Extend `isCarrierOf/isAbout` with document version, exact span, extraction event, interpretation claim, authority, jurisdiction, and bitemporal validity; reject bare binary normative edges as complete legal claims.
- **patent tooling (uspto-patent-driver-depth):** Separate patent/PTO document structure from claims, definitions, office directives, cited activities, and legal effects so section location remains queryable without becoming semantic identity.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Build two synchronized views of every authority: structural navigation for citation fidelity and semantic act/role/permission constraints for workflow decisions.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** Store a content entity once while linking multiple physical/digital bearers and fields, preserving which version and span supported each recalled proposition.
