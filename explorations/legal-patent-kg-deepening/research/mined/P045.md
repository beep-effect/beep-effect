# Large Language Model for Patent Concept Generation

## Problem & positioning

- Patent concept generation requires both technical synthesis and drafting that fits legal form, while general LLMs lack stable patent-domain knowledge.
- Prior embedding and network methods analyze existing concepts but do not directly verbalize new invention concepts.
- Patent-only supervised fine-tuning teaches style and tasks but may require extensive labels, generalize poorly, or sacrifice general capability.
- The paper asks how AI can generate innovative concepts that facilitate invention and instantiates its answer as `PatentGPT`.
- Its Knowledge Fine-Tuning (KFT) proposal injects a patent knowledge graph into continued pre-training before task tuning and human-preference alignment.
- The intended users are researchers and junior engineers producing initial U.S.-oriented patent concepts, not autonomous legal filing.

## Approach / architecture

- KFT has four stages: knowledge extracting, knowledge injecting, knowledge learning, and knowledge feedbacking.
- An LLM extracts typed `(head, relation, tail)` triples from USPTO patent text under a predefined entity/relation schema.
- Each triple is converted back into a natural-language sentence to create a knowledge corpus.
- Continued Knowledge Injection Pre-training (KPT) mixes this synthetic knowledge corpus with the general patent corpus under separate loss weights.
- Supervised fine-tuning then trains title, abstract, and claim dialogue behavior.
- A reward model learns from preferred versus non-preferred answers; proximal policy optimization supplies the RLHF stage.
- The base model is instruction-tuned Qwen2-1.5B, trained with LoRA on NVIDIA 4090 GPUs.
- Reported settings are batch size 1, learning rate `5e-5`, three epochs, and inference temperature 0.7.
- Comparisons include Qwen2, Llama-2, Llama-3.2, Phi-2, ChatGPT-4o, Gemini, Claude2, and patent-domain MoZI-7B.
- Evaluation combines reference-overlap metrics, patent-domain question benchmarks, an LLM reasonability judge, a TechNet-derived rareness score, ablations, and a hybrid-vehicle case study.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- The patent KG schema has four entity classes: `Task`, `Method`, `Material`, and `Generic`.
- `Task` covers applications, problems, or systems to construct; `Method` covers models, tools, components, and frameworks.
- `Material` covers data, corpora, resources, and knowledge bases; `Generic` captures low-information connective entities.
- Six relation types are predefined: `Used-for`, `Feature-of`, `Hyponym-of`, `Part-of`, `Compare`, and `Conjunction`.
- The KG is built from 30% of the patent corpus and contains approximately 100,000 entities and 300,000 relations.
- Triples serve as a training-data transformation rather than as a queryable, axiom-rich patent ontology.
- Converting triples to sentences is the main bridge from symbolic structure into model weights.
- The paper invokes Concept–Knowledge Theory to frame invention as movement between candidate concepts and validated knowledge.
- It does not model claims, limitations, priority, inventors, citations, prosecution events, evidence spans, or legal validity as graph entities/relations.
- No ontology constraints, entity resolution, provenance, contradiction representation, or KG extraction accuracy are reported.

## Evaluation & results

- The USPTO corpus contains about 20,000 patents published in 2021–2022, balanced across all eight top-level IPC sections with 2,000–4,000 per section.
- Training data include titles, abstracts, and claims; the SFT dataset has 60,000 dialogue pairs and human feedback has 2,000 interactions.
- The patent-writing test has 1,000 instances sampled across the eight IPC sections.
- PatentGPT scores 90.003 BERTScore, 45.360 BLEU-4, 41.961 ROUGE-1, 25.977 ROUGE-2, and 36.022 ROUGE-L.
- The strongest listed general baselines score 86.899 BERTScore (ChatGPT-4o), 17.232 BLEU-4 (ChatGPT-4o), and 38.152 ROUGE-1 (ChatGPT-4o).
- KPT+SFT scores 88.305 BERTScore and 44.661 BLEU-4 versus 86.625 and 30.848 for ordinary PT+SFT.
- Removing RLHF causes small declines; removing SFT+RLHF reduces BLEU-4 by 51.44%; removing KPT+SFT+RLHF reduces BLEU-4 by 78.61% relative to full KFT.
- On patent benchmarks, PatentGPT scores 0.524 IP_Quiz, 0.33 IP_Exam, 0.262 IP_Match, and 0.535 MMLU.
- ChatGPT-4o remains higher on IP_Exam (0.36), IP_Match (0.384), and MMLU (0.887).
- A ChatGPT-4 judge evaluates 100 generated samples for conformity with USPTO standards; PatentGPT receives a reported 95% win rate.
- PatentGPT's average rareness score is 0.578, compared with 0.431 for Qwen2 and 0.402 for MoZI.
- The case study generates a title, abstract, and ten claims for one hybrid-vehicle concept; its assessment is narrative rather than expert-scored.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- Training is predominantly USPTO 2021–2022 data, limiting jurisdictional and temporal coverage.
- Emerging fields may be missing, causing hallucinated or technically inaccurate claims without retrieval.
- Automated novelty/rareness is not legal novelty, non-obviousness, originality, enablement, or patentability.
- The authors acknowledge no qualitative validation by patent attorneys and no systematic cross-jurisdiction legal-compliance assessment.
- Generated claims may unintentionally resemble existing patents, creating plagiarism and attribution risk.
- Lower drafting barriers could enable excessive, low-quality patent filings and deepen resource asymmetries.
- Inventorship and accountability for AI-assisted inventions remain legally contested across jurisdictions.
- ROUGE/BLEU reward resemblance to existing patent prose, which can conflict with the stated novelty objective.
- The LLM-as-judge protocol has only 100 samples and does not report blinding, repeated judgments, calibration, or human agreement.
- The paper repeatedly describes generated text as patentable or legally defensible without the legal examination needed to support that conclusion.
- KG extraction itself is not evaluated, so the causal claim that structured knowledge—not duplicated or erroneous text—drives gains remains incomplete.
- Model-size labels and benchmark citations should be interpreted cautiously; this distillate reports them exactly as displayed, not as independently verified facts.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **patent tooling — agentic-cad-patent-tooling:** Take the staged concept-to-title-to-abstract-to-claims interaction, but put novelty search, claim support, human approval, and jurisdiction rules between generation stages.
- **patent tooling — uspto-patent-driver-depth:** Reject weight-only knowledge injection as the audit surface; retain prosecution documents, citations, claim limitations, and evidence spans in a queryable KG/RAG layer.
- **epistemic slice:** Model every proposed inventive feature as a claim with supporting source passages, generation lineage, uncertainty, prior-art conflicts, and separate technical/legal review states.
- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** Reuse only the Task/Method/Material distinctions as provisional extraction tags; replace `Generic` and broad relation labels with governed concepts and explicit mappings before durable ingestion.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Use generated drafts as attorney-reviewed work product, never as patentability determinations; surface jurisdiction, data cutoff, similarity, and disclosure-support warnings in the workflow.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** Keep extracted triples and feedback episodes provenance-linked and replayable instead of treating fine-tuned weights as sufficient durable memory.
