# Method of Converting Language-Based Written Contract to Smart Legal Contract Using Natural Language Processing

## Problem & positioning

- This source is U.S. Patent Application Publication US 2020/0042939 A1, not a research study.
- It addresses how a CRM/configure-price-quote platform could interpret natural-language contracts and turn their terms into operational workflows or distributed-ledger smart contracts.
- General-purpose NLP may miss industry-specific terminology, party roles, conditions, and expected actions; the proposed system selects a model and dictionary using the contract's industry.
- The application places contract interpretation inside a quote-to-cash environment that already holds customer, organization, product, pricing, and workflow information.
- It claims method, system, and computer-readable-medium forms of the same core pipeline: identify industry, select a domain model, identify contract terms, and create actionable flow events.
- The disclosure is enabling/illustrative patent prose; statements about efficiency, accuracy, enforcement, or trust are proposed benefits rather than demonstrated results.

## Approach / architecture

- `contract engine 112` creates, imports, stores, updates, and electronically signs natural-language contracts.
- `record store 110` holds organizational records, contracts, industry-specific processing models, dictionaries, standard contracts, flows, actions, and action relationships.
- `processing model engine 114` associates models with industries and parses parties, relationships, terms, and conditions using formal rules or statistical, connectionist, vector-space, neural, frame-based, distributional, or model-theoretical methods.
- Industry may be inferred from the customer/organization profile, an API, or preliminary parsing of the imported contract.
- The selected industry-specific model maps contract language to domain terms and actionable events; examples distinguish buyer/seller sales language from patient/provider health-insurance language.
- `flow engine 116` selects or generates a flow whose stages and transitions correspond to extracted obligations or business actions.
- A flow may start at a later stage when the contract indicates prior completion, such as payment already rendered.
- Users or multiple authorized parties complete actions; conditional logic selects the next stage; progress and completed/incomplete actions may be rendered visually.
- `smart contract engine 118` accepts narrative text, structured web-form fields, or an uploaded legal-language construct and identifies parties, terms, and conditions.
- A term-to-code or condition-to-code translation file maps identified provisions to prewritten code modules, illustrated with Solidity or Hyperledger-compatible code.
- The generated contract may be compiled or modified, deployed to a distributed ledger, synchronized with workflow state, and inspected or edited through platform tools.
- The four-step smart-contract method is: receive legal language, determine terms, convert terms to smart-contract code, and deploy to blockchain/DLT.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- No explicit ontology or KG is supplied, but the architecture assumes an industry-scoped semantic layer joining contract text to executable process elements.
- Core entities include `NaturalLanguageContract`, `Industry`, `ProcessingModel`, `Dictionary`, `ContractTerm`, `Party`, `Condition`, `Flow`, `Stage`, `ActionableEvent`, `SmartContract`, and `DistributedLedger`.
- Example contract-term types include good/service, quantity, price, delivery term, duration, penalty, buy-out, confidentiality condition, plan type, and monthly rate.
- Candidate relations include `contract associatedWith industry`, `model specializedFor industry`, `model uses dictionary`, and `model maps termTo actionableEvent`.
- Process relations include `flow comprises stage`, `action transitions stage`, `stage precedes stage`, `user authorizedFor action`, and `contract initializes flowAt stage`.
- Compilation/deployment relations include `term mappedTo codeModule`, `smartContract represents terms`, `smartContract deployedTo ledger`, and `ledger verifies transaction`.
- Claim 1 makes the crosswalk from industry-specific term to actionable event load-bearing; claims 9 and 17 restate it for system and storage-medium forms.
- Claim 4's completed, incomplete, and actionable-event relationships suggest an operational status vocabulary, but no temporal or event semantics are formalized.
- The record store could serve as a graph integration point, yet the disclosure describes generic relational or other storage and gives no identity, schema, axiom, or provenance design.
- “Smart contract reflects legal language” is modeled as translation, not as a separately reviewable equivalence claim with supporting spans and exceptions.

## Evaluation & results

- The publication contains 20 claims: method claims 1–8, system claims 9–16, and computer-readable-device claims 17–20.
- It provides five figures: a system block diagram, contract-processing flow, workflow traversal, smart-contract conversion, and generic computer system.
- Examples cover finance, healthcare, insurance, retail, customer onboarding, invoice/payment/shipping, and confidentiality language.
- No implementation, training corpus, test set, model configuration, contract count, industry count, precision, recall, F1, extraction accuracy, code correctness, latency, or cost is reported.
- No comparison with a general-purpose model is given despite the assertion that industry-specific modeling improves efficiency and accuracy.
- No smart-contract compilation result, ledger deployment outcome, security audit, user study, or legal review is reported.
- The claims therefore establish proposed scope, not empirical validation or legal equivalence between source prose and generated code.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- Patent boilerplate states that steps may be omitted, reordered, simultaneous, or implemented with alternative boundaries; this broadens embodiments but weakens architectural specificity.
- The disclosure does not describe how processing models are trained, selected under mixed-industry contracts, calibrated, or updated when terminology changes.
- It provides no confidence score, ambiguity representation, exception handling, conflict detection, human approval gate, or fallback for unrecognized terms.
- Contract meaning is reduced to parties, terms, conditions, and actions without explicit treatment of definitions, cross-references, negation, precedence, amendments, remedies, governing law, or interpretive context.
- A term-to-code lookup risks compiling surface matches while losing legal scope and contingencies.
- The application conflates workflow tracking, contractual enforcement, and distributed consensus; ledger validation does not establish legal validity, performance, or enforceability.
- Automated conversion can create irreversible or costly actions; the design provides editing tools but no mandatory review, simulation, formal verification, rollback, or access-control protocol.
- Industry selection based on customer identity can misclassify cross-sector transactions and encode organizational assumptions as semantic truth.
- The record store lacks explicit source-span provenance between each extracted term, workflow transition, and generated instruction.
- Blockchain-specific operational requirements—node creation, funding, synchronization, compilation, and platform modification—are acknowledged but not resolved.
- The generic computer-system section adds no evidence that the contract pipeline is feasible or safe.
- As a patent publication, it should not be read as peer-reviewed evidence of novelty, effectiveness, or deployed practice.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** Take the explicit `industry term → contract term → actionable event` mapping as a typed crosswalk, while preserving industry scheme, model version, and ambiguity rather than forcing one canonical label.
- **epistemic slice:** Represent every extraction and code correspondence as a claim tied to exact contract spans, model/time, reviewer state, counter-interpretations, and contradiction status; reject unproven “reflects” equivalence.
- **patent tooling (uspto-patent-driver-depth):** Use the application's claim decomposition as a test fixture for parsing independent/dependent claim structure and tracing repeated limitations across method, system, and medium claim categories.
- **patent tooling (agentic-cad-patent-tooling):** Borrow staged compilation—text to typed terms to workflow to executable artifact—but require schema decoding, deterministic checks, simulation, and human approval before any generated action executes.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Apply domain dictionaries to intake triage and obligation calendars, not autonomous enforcement; surface source clause, extracted duty, actor, deadline, condition, and reviewer together.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** Store accepted/rejected term mappings and their contract contexts as versioned examples so later agents can reuse precedents without turning one industry's vocabulary into universal truth.
