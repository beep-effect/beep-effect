# UFO-L: A Core Ontology of Legal Concepts Built from a Legal Relations Perspective

## Problem & positioning

The paper proposes UFO-L to close a perceived gap between legal theory and computational legal ontologies.
A systematic mapping of 128 studies from 1995–2014 found only 35 using primary legal theories, 44 relying on indirect sources, and 49 using no legal theory as a primary source.
Rather than center the ontology on legal norms, UFO-L adopts a subjectivist, legal-relations perspective intended to mirror contemporary legal practice more flexibly.
It grounds the legal layer in UFO and extracts legal distinctions from the structural part of Alexy’s Theory of Fundamental Rights.
The proposal distinguishes legal core concepts reusable across domains from foundational categories below and jurisdiction-specific concepts above.
The document is a doctoral research plan with partial results; it does not present a completed ontology or validation outcomes.

## Approach / architecture

UFO provides foundational kinds, roles, events, and especially relators; UFO-L adds generic concepts such as legal relation, fact, subject, norm, position, and object.
A UFO relator is treated as an objectified relational property existentially dependent on multiple participants, making it suitable for representing a legal relation as a first-class entity.
Alexy’s account defines a norm as the meaning of a normative enunciation and divides deontological norms into rules and principles.
Principles are optimization requirements admitting different fulfillment degrees under factual and legal conditions; rules are fulfilled or not.
The paper defines a legal relation as a bond between subjects achieved by a legal fact, or a social relation typified in a legal norm.
Development is intended to be iterative: build the legal-relation core first, then expand through systematic analysis of primary legal documents and ontology-development methods.
The research design combines systematic mapping, documentary legal research, comparison with CLO and LRI-Core, and three empirical experiments.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

The displayed hierarchy is UFO `Relator` → `SocialRelator` → `LegalRelator`.
`LegalRelator` divides into `SimpleLegalRelator` and `ComplexLegalRelator`.
The diagram gives `ComplexLegalRelator` a `2..*` aggregation of simple legal relators, a pattern for representing a legal situation as a bundle of correlated positions.
The simple branch divides into `RightDutyRelator` and `NoRightPermissionRelator`.
`RightDutyRelator` divides into act and omission variants: `RightDutyToAnAct` and `RightDutyToAnOmission`.
`NoRightPermissionRelator` divides into `NoRightPermissionToAct` and `NoRightPermissionToOmission`.
The complex branch divides into `LibertyRelator` and `CompetenceRelator`.
The liberty branch divides into `ProtectedLiberty` and `UnprotectedLiberty`.
This structure makes legal relations, their component position-pairs, and action polarity explicit rather than encoding them as unqualified predicates.
It also recognizes permissions and no-rights omitted by the legal-act ontology discussed in the state of the art.
The figure names but does not expand `CompetenceRelator`; no detailed power/liability or immunity/disability classes are legible or described in the text.
The intended wider ontology would connect legal facts, norms, subjects, positions, objects, and relators, but this paper supplies no formal axioms, cardinalities beyond the figure, or machine-readable schema.
For a KG, the recoverable pattern is: a legal fact establishes a legal relator among subjects; a norm types or regulates that relation; the relator aggregates directed simple position structures.

## Evaluation & results

Of 128 mapped studies, 35 (approximately 27%) used primary legal-theory sources, 44 (approximately 34%) indirect sources, and 49 (approximately 38%) no primary legal theory.
The literature chart counts LRI-Core 19, LKIF 18, DOLCE 11, CLO 10, FOLaw 8, PROTON+OPJK 8, FBO 4, SUMO 1, and NM-Core 1.
The systematic mapping and an earlier UFO-L relator taxonomy/theoretical basis had been published; a recent model and comparative studies were reported as ongoing.
The first planned experiment compares UML and UFO-L modeling by undergraduates already familiar with UML and conceptual modeling.
The second repeats the scenarios with experienced computer professionals and compares results to estimate background effects.
The third asks legal professionals and legal-technology decision makers to interpret UFO-L models and judge clarity, correctness, and proximity to real legal issues.
Four hypotheses predict clearer and more complete models, reusable generic concepts, better legal-expert comprehension, and improved communication with computer professionals.
No experiments had reported participant totals, observed scores, inferential statistics, legal-error analysis, reasoner results, or ontology coverage metrics.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

The declared scope excludes legal argumentation and Alexy’s weighing and balancing, leaving principle conflicts and defeasibility unmodeled.
The artifact is partial: the paper provides only a taxonomy fragment and no ontology file, identifiers, logical definitions, competency questions, or example instance graph.
The predicted quality gains are hypotheses, not results; the paper’s language sometimes presents expected flexibility and completeness more strongly than its evidence permits.
The mapping covers 1995–2014 and measures source usage, not the semantic correctness or practical success of those ontologies.
Reifying paired positions is valuable, but labels such as `RightDutyRelator` do not by themselves encode direction, bearer, counterparty, propositional content, or enforcement consequences.
The legal fact and source norm need temporal validity, jurisdiction, authority, exceptions, provenance, and interpretive status for operational legal use.
The rule/principle distinction is described but not connected to the relator taxonomy, and degrees of principle fulfillment have no representation.
`CompetenceRelator` remains a black box in the supplied figure, blocking faithful implementation of higher-order changes to legal positions.
P091 is essentially the same content as P090 in a differently produced PDF, so the pair should be deduplicated at synthesis time rather than counted as corroboration.
