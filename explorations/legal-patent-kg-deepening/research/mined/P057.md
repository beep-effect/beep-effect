# On Ontology, Ontologies, Conceptualizations, Modeling Languages, and (Meta)Models

## Problem & positioning

- “Ontology” denotes both a philosophical discipline/system of categories and, in computer science, a concrete purpose-built artifact encoded in a language; conflating these senses obscures what domain ontologies represent.
- The paper asks how reality, conceptualizations, abstractions, models, metamodels, domain ontologies, foundational ontologies, and representation languages relate.
- It also asks what makes a modeling language suitable for a domain and whether Semantic Web languages qualify as general ontology-representation languages.
- The central position is realist and representation-oriented: language refers to reality only through a conceptualization, and a good language constrains valid models toward admissible states of affairs.
- Formal Ontology supplies domain-independent categories and laws for grounding a general ontology language; a domain reference ontology supplies the corresponding standard for a material domain.
- Ontology engineering should use two complementary language classes rather than force representation adequacy and computational tractability into one artifact.

## Approach / architecture

- Ullmann's triangle separates thing/reality, concept/conceptualization, and symbol/language; the language–reality relation is mediated by conceptualization.
- A conceptualization supplies concepts used to compose an immaterial abstraction of a state of affairs; a modeling language supplies primitives used to compose a concrete model of that abstraction.
- Language quality is assessed by comparing its metamodel with a domain reference ontology.
- Four criteria characterize the mapping: soundness, completeness, lucidity, and laconicity.
- Soundness requires every language primitive to denote a domain concept; completeness requires a primitive for every relevant domain concept.
- Lucidity requires a primitive to represent at most one domain concept; laconicity requires a domain concept to be represented at most once.
- A metamodel defines selected constructs and well-formedness rules; its ontological reading expresses the worldview and commitments embedded in those constructs.
- The formal account moves from intensional conceptualizations and intended world structures to logical models, intensional interpretations, ontological commitments, and logical renderings.
- The paper tests the account on genealogy: Person, Father, Mother, Parent, Ancestor, Offspring, living/deceased phases, and lawful relationship constraints.
- A UFO fragment then grounds a redesigned UML profile with Kind, Quantity, Collective, SubKind, Phase, Role, Category, RoleMixin, and Mixin distinctions.
- The final engineering architecture separates a human-facing, highly expressive reference ontology from lightweight computational encodings such as OWL or LINGO, connected through an explicit design/translation phase.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- A conceptualization is defined as intensional structure `C = <W, D, R>`: possible worlds, individuals, and intensional n-ary relations mapping worlds to tuples of individuals.
- Each world yields an intended world structure; collectively these structures delimit domain states of affairs admitted by the conceptualization.
- A logical model `<S, I>` assigns vocabulary constants and predicates to individuals and extensional relations, fixing one extensional interpretation.
- An intensional interpretation `<C, I>` maps vocabulary terms to individuals and conceptual relations; this is the language's ontological commitment to the conceptualization.
- Intended models are logical models compatible with that commitment, including an admissible world and matching predicate extensions.
- A logical rendering translates a specification into a first-order theory whose axioms constrain interpretations.
- An ideal ontology admits all and only the states of affairs admitted by its conceptualization; a practical ontology approximates that set while remaining independent of particular epistemic states and world states.
- Ontology quality is framed as distance between the practical ontology's logical models and those of an ideal ontology.
- The toy axiom `Father(x) → Person(x)` demonstrates how an omitted domain law permits unintended models even when both vocabulary terms exist.
- Incompleteness arises both when concepts are absent and when their axiomatization is too weak to exclude unintended models.
- Domain laws define a nomological state space; possible states are constrained by what is lawful in reality rather than by arbitrary syntax.
- A foundational ontology represents the meta-conceptualization of domain-independent categories/laws and provides real-world semantics for a general ontology language.
- UFO distinguishes rigid kinds that supply identity from contingent attributions/mixins and anti-rigid roles/phases; this blocks semantically odd but logically legal structures.
- Reference ontologies prioritize truthfulness, meaning negotiation, conceptual clarity, and human reasoning; lightweight ontologies prioritize decidability, tractability, interoperability, or implementation fit.
- Translation from reference to lightweight ontology must record expressivity loss and the chosen non-functional objective rather than pretending the artifacts are semantically identical.

## Evaluation & results

- The paper is a formal/conceptual argument and language-design framework; it reports no corpus, benchmark, participant study, or quantitative metric.
- It gives six numbered formal definitions/constructs covering conceptualization, intended world structure, logical model, intensional interpretation, ontological commitment, and logical rendering.
- The four qualitative adequacy criteria are illustrated through representational mappings and linked to prior UML evaluation/redesign work.
- In the genealogy example, adding the Father-to-Person subsumption axiom excludes a class of unintended interpretations admitted by the weaker theory.
- The UFO-based UML profile makes invalid models structurally rejectable, including self-ancestry and ancestry cycles shown in the examples.
- The paper asserts two constraints from the prior redesign as formally proved elsewhere: a Role cannot be a supertype of a Kind, and an Object Class is a subkind of exactly one Kind.
- The article does not calculate model-set distance, demonstrate automated checking, or compare error rates among OWL, ER, UML, LINGO, and the UFO profile.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The author explicitly recognizes a tradeoff: highly expressive, strongly axiomatized reference languages often lack the tractability and decidability of lightweight languages.
- Ideal ontologies depend on a selected universe, granularity, viewpoint, and conceptualization; the paper does not give a practical consensus method for deciding that the conceptualization is truthful.
- “Distance” between logical-model sets is proposed as a quality account but no computable measure is defined or applied.
- The formalization assumes vocabulary terms can directly correspond to conceptual relations, an alignment that is difficult in polysemous, disputed, and evolving legal domains.
- Independence from epistemic state is useful for domain truth claims but insufficient for tracking who asserted a legal interpretation, when, on what evidence, and with what authority.
- The genealogy example is intentionally clean; law has defeasibility, jurisdiction, institutional power, conflicting authorities, exceptions, and temporal change that complicate admissible-world claims.
- Isomorphism is an exacting ideal and may be neither testable nor desirable when a language deliberately compresses a domain for a bounded task.
- The two-language strategy introduces governance risk: multiple lightweight implementations may silently discard different constraints from the same reference ontology.
- Calling Semantic Web languages a misnomer as ontology languages follows the author's ontological-level criterion; other engineering communities use a broader artifact-centered definition.
- Practical tooling, transformation rules, validation suites, and round-trip guarantees between reference and lightweight artifacts are left open.
- The framework centers model truthfulness and comprehensibility but does not separately treat provenance, security, maintenance cost, or empirical usability.
