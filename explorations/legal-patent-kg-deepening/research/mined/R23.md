## What this repo actually is

`semanticlaw` is a compact, manual Swiss-legal-text structuring experiment, not a broad legal ontology or an automated
statute ingestion service. The inspected `main` snapshot is commit `c0ee57803f26d31e342861dbb42aca5a14171336`
(2023-04-15). Its tracked payload is one 235-line RDFS/SHACL vocabulary, one 615-line interactive Python collector,
one completed federal hunting-act checkpoint plus its Turtle export, and one unfinished hunting-regulation checkpoint.
There are no tests, spreadsheets, source PDFs, SPARQL query files, OWL axioms, SKOS concepts, official identifiers,
provenance records, or dependency pins.

The actual workflow reconstructed from `collector.py` is:

```text
PDF -> pdfminer line stream -> human cursor/stack annotation -> resumable JSON frame state
    -> SPARQL INSERT DATA assembly -> Turtle serialization -> optional SHACL validation
```

The human declares document, section, article/paragraph, subsection, and letter scopes while moving through extracted
PDF lines. Each frame carries both a containment parent and global reading-order neighbors. This is useful legal-text
infrastructure, but it extracts no obligation, permission, actor, citation, amendment, effective time, jurisdiction,
or subject-matter relation.

`0_Bundesgesetz_über_die_Jagd.json` is the editable collector state; `0_Bundesgesetz_über_die_Jagd.ttl` is its RDF
projection. `1_Bundesverordnung_über_die_Jagd.json` is only a checkpoint: its active hierarchy still contains the
document and first section, and no corresponding Turtle is checked in. The input PDFs are deliberately ignored by
`.gitignore:1`, and neither JSON artifact records a source URL, PDF digest, page coordinates, or collection time.

## Extractable gold

### 1. A minimal structural vocabulary with an explicit document/fragment split

`swisslaw.ttl:12-232` defines 12 RDFS classes. Its actual class hierarchy is:

```text
TextElement
|- Document
|  |- Bundesgesetz | Bundesverordnung | Kantonsgesetz
|  `- Kantonsverordnung | Kantonsverfügung
`- Abschnitt | Artikel | Paragraph | Absatz | Litera
```

The five fragment classes are siblings under `TextElement`; containment, not subclassing, expresses structure. The six
properties are `parent`, `prev`, `next`, `title`, `content`, and `ord`. Classification says what a node is, `parent`
supplies the citation tree, and `prev`/`next` preserve source reading order independently of nesting.

The parent grammar in `swisslaw.ttl:117-232` is concrete: `Artikel` may sit under `Bundesgesetz`, `Bundesverordnung`,
or `Abschnitt`; `Paragraph` under `Kantonsgesetz` or `Kantonsverordnung`; `Abschnitt` under any `Document`; and
`Absatz`/`Litera` under enumerated fragments. It is only a seed: `Kantonsverfügung` has no paragraph path, nested
sections are excluded, and no disjointness is asserted.

### 2. SHACL constraints colocated with the vocabulary

`swisslaw.ttl` declares seven node shapes and reuses the six property resources as property shapes. The load-bearing
constraint pattern is small enough to port directly:

```turtle
:AbsatzShape a sh:NodeShape ; sh:targetClass :Absatz ;
  sh:property [ sh:path :ord ; sh:datatype xsd:integer ; sh:minCount 1 ] ;
  sh:property [ sh:path :content ; sh:minCount 1 ] .
```

`title` and `content` allow only `de`, `it`, or `fr`, require non-whitespace text of at least five characters, and cap
cardinality at one (`swisslaw.ttl:42-61`). `ord` permits integer or string globally; article and paragraph shapes
require one of those types, sections require an ordinal, subsections require an integer plus content, and letters
require a string plus content. These rules form a good first-pass import validation matrix.

Several defects are equally valuable as acceptance tests. `:prev` mistakenly declares `sh:path :next`
(`swisslaw.ttl:24-31`), so no generic shape checks the actual `prev` predicate. Stale paths `:numOrdinal` and
`:charOrdinal` remain at lines 151 and 166 after those properties were deleted; parent shapes omit `sh:minCount 1`.
Base shapes target `TextElement`/`Document`, emitted nodes use subclasses, and `validate(data+model)` requests no
explicit inference. Test actual focus-node/result coverage rather than treating “conforms” as proof of coverage.

The RDFS and SHACL datatype stories also diverge. `ord` has both `xsd:integer` and `xsd:string` as RDFS ranges
(`swisslaw.ttl:64-73`), which is conjunctive in RDFS, while its SHACL `sh:or` is the intended union. `title` and
`content` claim `xsd:string` ranges but the example emits `@de` language-tagged literals. Port the intended union and
language-aware text contract, not these range statements verbatim.

### 3. Dual topology: containment tree plus document-order chain

`Frame` stores `parent`, `prev`, and `next` separately (`collector.py:12-46`). `SequencedStack.push()` assigns the
current scope as parent, while `append()` links every new frame to the last frame globally (`collector.py:73-95`).
This supports two common legal traversals without deriving one imperfectly from the other:

- citation traversal: document -> section -> article -> subsection -> letter;
- reading traversal: the exact linear sequence in which annotated fragments appeared.

The complete act has 164 frames: 1 `Bundesgesetz`, 10 `Abschnitt`, 29 `Artikel`, 75 `Absatz`, and 49 `Litera`. Parent
edges form one acyclic tree at depths 0-4: document -> section -> article -> subsection, plus letters under 44
subsections and five articles. All scoped ordinals are unique.

The checked-in reading chain is unsound. JSON and Turtle omit `next` on section 3 before article 7 and section 7 before
article 17, although both articles point back with `prev`. This makes three forward components of 53, 54, and 57 nodes.
Require reciprocal neighbors, one head/tail, full reachability, no cycles, and array-order agreement.

### 4. A resumable human-in-the-loop collector state

`collector.py:262-297` serializes the full frame sequence, open-scope IDs, PDF line cursor, active collection mode,
and collection start into JSON. The command shell exposes `next`, `block`, `title`, `content`, `new`, `end`, `cancel`,
`savestate`, `restorestate`, `save`, and `validate` (`collector.py:488-606`). This makes manual legal structuring
interruptible and reviewable instead of forcing an all-or-nothing parser run.

The useful artifact boundary is a typed annotation event/checkpoint: source locator + frame identity + structural
operation + cursor span + annotator + prior state digest. The original JSON has only the resulting mutable state;
`line_no` is not emitted into RDF, and it is a PDF-extraction line rather than a page/character span. Preserve both
the event trail and durable source coordinates in a Beep port.

Cancellation and state isolation need hardening. `SequencedStack.hierarchy` and `.sequence` are mutable class
attributes (`collector.py:73-76`), so collector instances can share frames. `remove_top()` removes the last sequence
item and hierarchy top although they need not be the same (`collector.py:96-100`). Validate topology before export.

### 5. Ordinals preserve legal labels rather than forcing one numeric type

`collector.py:333-351` allocates article/paragraph numbers globally, other numeric ordinals per parent, and letter
ordinals per parent. `new_article()` and `new_paragraph()` accept explicit strings so labels such as `25ter` can be
preserved (`collector.py:410-428`). `CharacterOrdinal` advances `a` through `z`, then `za`, `zb`, and so on
(`collector.py:180-221`); this is a repository-specific sequence, not ordinary spreadsheet base-26.

The complete act uses integer section/article/subsection ordinals and letter strings. The partial regulation contains
`Absatz` ordinals `2bis` and `2ter` as strings. That is legally useful source fidelity but violates the current
integer-only `AbsatzShape`. Model an ordinal as a structured value—display label, numeric stem, suffix, kind, and sort
key—so source citation and ordering do not fight the validator.

### 6. A small, inspectable statute-graph fixture

`0_Bundesgesetz_über_die_Jagd.ttl` has 164 typed `http://example.org/...` subjects and exactly 986 generated triples:
164 types, 163 parents, 163 previous links, 161 next links, 163 ordinals, 40 titles, and 132 contents. All 172 text
literals are tagged German. JSON and Turtle values match; serialization only varies literal syntax.

A load-bearing example at `0_Bundesgesetz_über_die_Jagd.ttl:39-46` is one letter with content, ordinal, parent, and
both reading neighbors. This is an excellent golden fixture for frame-to-RDF projection. The source text also exposes
real extraction noise: retained line-break hyphenation in 41 content fields, repeated spacing, footnote digits fused
into headings such as `Art. 2837`, and ellipsis/footnote text such as `2. und 3. ...36` (lines 1177-1189).

Do not mistake the graph for authoritative statute data. Its instance namespace is `http://example.org/`; its
vocabulary IRI is a mutable raw-GitHub `main` URL; the act's “Stand am 1. Januar 2022” exists only inside its title;
and citations, amendments, repeal status, official systematic number, source URL, jurisdiction, and temporal validity
are absent. Use the fixture to test structure and normalization, not to answer current-law questions.

### 7. Pipeline boundaries worth retaining, implementation hazards worth dropping

The stage separation is good: `pdfminer` line stream -> editable frames -> RDF projection -> SHACL gate
(`collector.py:160-178,252-297,456-485,601-605`). Digest each artifact so every stage can be replayed independently.

Do not port SPARQL string assembly literally. `build_graph()` interpolates IDs and unescaped title/content into an
`INSERT DATA` string (`collector.py:456-479`), making quotes, backslashes, or malicious input parser/injection hazards.
Construct RDF terms through a library API. Filename-derived Erdi8 IDs discard characters from the entire input path
(`collector.py:252-260`) and are neither official nor content-stable; mint deterministic internal IDs and retain
official citation keys separately. Unpinned `rdflib`, `pyshacl`, `erdi8`, `cmd2`, and `pdfminer.six` in
`requirements.txt` make the original runtime non-reproducible.

The partial regulation JSON is a useful adversarial fixture, not a finished dataset: it has 33 frames, stops at PDF
line 183 with two scopes still open, and includes layout errors where multiple letter labels are swallowed into one
`Litera` content. Import status must distinguish checkpoint, structurally complete, validated, and authority-current.

The root `LICENSE` is Apache License 2.0. Repository code, vocabulary, and checked-in example artifacts may be copied,
modified, and distributed with attribution: include the license, preserve applicable copyright/attribution notices,
mark modified files, and carry any required NOTICE content. No `NOTICE` file is present, and the stock license's

Apache-2.0 also supplies its conditional contributor patent grant; it provides no warranty or trademark permission.

Direct porting is allowed, but prefer a schema-first re-expression and retain an attribution record beside imported
fixtures. Do **not** vendor absent source PDFs, dependency packages, or live upstream resources under this repository's
license. Do **not** treat Apache-2.0 as proof of authority, currency, or redistribution rights for the embedded Swiss
statute text: the checkout identifies no official source or separate data terms. Clear that content independently or
retain only small attributed test excerpts. Do not adopt the mutable raw-GitHub vocabulary IRI as Beep's canonical
namespace; map it to stable Beep IRIs while preserving `prov:wasDerivedFrom`-style attribution.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation SKOS / `@beep/ontology`:** port the structural grammar as schema-validated `LegalTextElement` nodes and map fragment kinds to a Beep concept scheme; keep topical SKOS separate because none is supplied.
- **epistemic slice:** make each frame boundary, ordinal parse, normalization, and parent/order edge an adjudicable claim with source span, annotator, checkpoint digest, review state, and supersession history; missing provenance or currency is `unknown`.
- **patent tooling:** reuse the containment tree plus reading chain for patent document -> section -> claim -> limitation, including `1bis`; add patent-native citation, dependency, amendment, and prosecution relations.
- **gov-legal data drivers:** emit immutable extraction lines, resumable annotation events, normalized frames, RDF, and validation inventory; require authority IDs, source spans/digests, language, jurisdiction, effective time, reciprocity, and completion status.
- **OIP practice:** gate the two checkpoints as golden/adversarial fixtures on shape coverage, connectivity, reciprocal order, ordinal round-trip, RDF injection safety, provenance, and source-rights review; `conforms` alone is insufficient.

## Extraction confidence

**High for repository contents, vocabulary structure, collector behavior, checkpoint counts, and graph anomalies.** All ten tracked files were inventoried; `collector.py` parsed with Python's AST; both JSON states were checked; and all 164 Turtle subjects were compared to JSON.
No named target was unreadable, and no spreadsheet or hidden dataset exists in the tracked tree.

**Medium for executable SHACL behavior.** The shapes were audited directly, but this checkout does not have its
declared `rdflib` or `pyshacl` packages installed, so the repository's validation command was not executed and no
dependencies were installed for this read-only mine. Target/inference observations are standards-and-code analysis,
not a captured pySHACL report.

**Low for legal authority, source fidelity, and current-law fitness.** The PDFs, official source metadata, acquisition
procedure, licensing chain for statute text, tests, validation reports, and independent legal review are absent. The
artifacts support an attributed structural port and strong fixtures, not a claim that the text is complete, current,
or safe for legal reliance.
