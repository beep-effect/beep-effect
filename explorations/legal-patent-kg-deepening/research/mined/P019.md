# An Ontology-Driven Graph RAG for Legal Norms: A Hierarchical, Temporal, and Deterministic Approach

## Problem & positioning

- This saved arXiv HTML is the accessible rendering of the same v4 manuscript represented by P018, not a separate study.
- The paper argues that flat legal chunks cannot reliably answer historical, hierarchical, or causal questions about evolving legislation.
- It positions a formal graph as the authoritative retrieval layer and the LLM as planner and constrained synthesizer.
- Authored hierarchy and curated thematic classification replace inferred Graph-RAG communities where legal structure already supplies stronger semantics.
- The central promise is reproducible resolution: query-policy choices are explicit, and every generated answer can point back to versions and change events.

## Approach / architecture

- Perform structure-aware semantic segmentation into legal components rather than arbitrary token windows.
- Keep abstract legal identity (`Norm`, `Component`) apart from time-bound semantics (`TV`/`CTV`) and language-bound wording (`LV`/`CLV`).
- Associate content chunks, called `Text Unit`s, with CLVs so each retrievable span has unambiguous time and language context.
- Build new consolidated states by aggregating the newest child CTVs; do not duplicate unchanged descendants after every amendment.
- Represent normative change commands as `Action` nodes and generate action-level text units so causal events are directly retrievable.
- Convert selected metadata and informative relationships into separate text units, yielding several searchable facets per entity.
- Traverse a selected structural or thematic subgraph first, then run lexical/vector ranking only over its eligible text units.
- A unified planner canonicalizes constraints, resolves scope, chooses structure/span/time-first execution, selects a CTV, retrieves evidence, assembles action provenance, and generates.
- Every answer can disclose temporal policy, membership policy, retrieval parameters, confidence, and a JSON provenance annex.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- `Norm` corresponds to an abstract Work; wording changes do not change its legal-document identity.
- `Component` is a stable abstract work for a title, chapter, article, caput, paragraph, or item and participates in a recursive hierarchy.
- `TV`/`CTV` captures the semantic state of a norm/component for a validity interval without committing to a language.
- `LV`/`CLV` captures a linguistic expression of one temporal state; several languages can share one TV/CTV.
- `Text Unit` is the retrieval atom and may encode enacted text, an action description, a metadata assertion, a relationship, or a theme description.
- A new ancestor CTV aggregates the amended child’s new CTV and unchanged siblings’ existing CTVs, allowing child-state reuse.
- `Action` makes causation explicit by connecting the commanding source provision, any terminated CTV, and the produced CTV.
- `Theme` provides a curated topical scope spanning norms and components; hierarchy supplies nested structural scope.
- Point-in-time selection uses half-open validity intervals; `SnapshotLast` resolves interval requests with multiple versions.
- Causal-lineage output is an ordered action DAG with before/after text, identifiers, match confidence, and source versions.
- Membership policy is modeled as operational semantics: snapshot-anchored, action-time, and lifetime cohorts answer different reorganization questions.

## Evaluation & results

- The corpus comprises the official Brazilian Constitution of 1988 and subsequent Constitutional Amendments through early 2024.
- Storage is demonstrated in Oracle Database 23ai’s relational-vector model; a dedicated property-graph database is not required.
- Text units use 256-dimensional Qwen3 embeddings; generation uses Gemini 2.5 Flash.
- The paper walks through Article 6 versions surrounding Constitutional Amendment 26/2000 and later amendments.
- It demonstrates three qualitative patterns: historical text retrieval, descendant-wide change summaries, and lineage for the introduction of a term.
- Operational defaults are cosine similarity with configurable `k = 8` and the `SnapshotLast` temporal policy.
- The Chapter II example reports three impact dates across Article 6 and Article 7 during 2010–2019, but the output is labeled illustrative.
- No sample size, graph cardinality, precision/recall, action-attribution score, provenance-completeness score, latency, or statistical comparison is supplied.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- Authors: scaling needs governed ingestion, human review, indexing, materialized common snapshots, and incremental legislation processing.
- Authors: bad temporal or action metadata yields systematically bad retrieval; structured determinism does not repair upstream errors.
- Authors: statutory hierarchy transfers imperfectly to case law, contracts, heterogeneous regulations, multilingual corpora, and difficult cross-references.
- Authors: practical validation needs jurisdictional ground truth and user-centered measures such as time-to-answer.
- Mine: qualitative examples establish plausibility, not superiority over a competent temporal SQL or document-version baseline.
- Mine: an LLM performs query planning and entity extraction, but failure handling, ambiguity escalation, and planner evaluation are unspecified.
- Mine: the model has validity time only; ingestion time, correction history, source disagreement, and retroactivity need explicit epistemic treatment.
- Mine: generated action/metadata text can become a lossy secondary source unless each statement remains tied to exact authoritative spans.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** represent the legal component hierarchy separately from SKOS thematic concepts; use SKOS mappings for curated cross-document scope, not inferred vector communities.
- **epistemic slice:** take half-open validity intervals, explicit change actions, policy disclosures, and provenance DAGs; extend them with transaction time, claim confidence, contradiction triage, and evidence-span identity.
- **patent tooling (agentic-cad-patent-tooling):** use structure-first retrieval from invention to embodiment to claim element before semantic search, and return the exact scope/version policy used by the drafting agent.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** provide point-in-time statutory snapshots and hierarchical amendment summaries as reviewable assistant artifacts with before/after text and causal instruments.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** reuse unchanged memory assertions between temporal snapshots and model revisions as events, while retaining the underlying evidence rather than only natural-language summaries.
