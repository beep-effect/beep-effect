# Symboleo: Specification and Verification of Legal Contracts

## Problem & positioning

The thesis addresses the ambiguity, incompleteness, inconsistency, and manual review burden of natural-language business contracts.
Its premise is that smart-contract monitoring and design-time verification require a precise requirements-level contract specification before executable code is generated.
Symboleo is proposed as a formal language expressive enough for real business contracts while remaining analyzable through finite-state and temporal-logic techniques.
The work asks which formal concepts a contract language needs, how to verify safety and liveness at design time, and how to check compliance against execution events at runtime.
The research follows iterative Design Science Research informed by legal literature, interaction with lawyers, manual examination of more than 50 contracts, and tool-building.
It focuses on the performance phase of business contracts, especially obligations, powers, events, time, and execution-time changes to parties and positions.

## Approach / architecture

Symboleo specifications contain domain declarations, a typed contract signature, roles and parties, assets, events, preconditions, postconditions, obligations, surviving obligations, powers, and constraints.
An obligation is `Obligation(debtor, creditor, antecedent, consequent)` and may have a trigger; a power reverses the party ordering and entitles its creditor to alter a legal state.
Primitive event-calculus predicates include `occurs`, `initiates`, `terminates`, `happens`, `holdsAt`, and event-within-situation.
Subsidiary predicates encode weak or strict happens-before, happens-after, and happens-within constraints, drawing on Allen interval relations and event calculus.
Contract, obligation, and power lifecycles are finite state machines; 41 axioms define creation, activation, fulfillment, violation, expiration, suspension, resumption, discharge, exertion, and termination transitions.
Execution-time operations cover subcontracting, assignment of rights, and substitution of performance to a third party.
SymboleoCC extends the jREC event-calculus engine with primitive, domain-independent Symboleo, and contract-specific Prolog/Horn-clause axioms.
It consumes an initial state, event trace, and expected final state, then compares inferred and expected outcomes for conformance and offline/online monitoring.
SymboleoPC uses an Xtext/EMF parser and translator to generate nuXmv modules backed by trusted semantic components.
It checks bounded-first-order contract properties expressed with CTL or LTL and returns counterexamples or witnesses.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

The contract ontology combines Hohfeldian theory, UFO/UFO-L, and manual annotation of over 50 contracts; it was simplified to make formal verification tractable.
`Contract` binds at least two parties through roles and composes legal positions; a subcontract delegates performance of parts of obligations.
`Party` covers natural and artificial persons and may hold liability, right-holder, or performer positions that can change during execution.
`Role` contextualizes a party's positions per contract, preventing the agent from being conflated with seller, buyer, carrier, or other situational capacities.
`Asset` covers tangible and intangible owned value with quantitative and qualitative attributes and participates in exchanges and contract outputs.
`LegalPosition` specializes only into `Obligation` and `Power`; the other Hohfeldian concepts are omitted because the thesis treats these two as monitorable.
Obligations relate debtor, creditor, trigger, antecedent, consequent, lifecycle, contract, and possible surviving status.
Powers relate creditor, debtor, trigger, antecedent, consequent, and the contract, obligation, or power whose state they may change.
`LegalSituation` is a state of affairs holding over an interval; `Event` occurs at a time point, is immutable, and connects pre-state to post-state.
Contrary-to-duty dependencies allow one obligation's violation to trigger a remedial obligation or a counterparty power.
State changes are event-reified in the operational semantics, enabling an execution log to explain why a position moved between lifecycle states.
The ontology includes ownership, role assignment, contract association, performance, liability, right-holder, consent, assignment, substitution, and subcontracting relations.
The author explicitly reports that no grounded-theory or formal-concept-analysis procedure was used to derive the ontology.

## Evaluation & results

Expressiveness is illustrated on international meat sales, pizza delivery, transactive energy, and COVID-19 vaccine manufacturing contracts.
SymboleoCC's meat-sales evaluation defines six published scenarios; all produced their expected final states, including timely performance, breach, late performance, termination, and disclosure violation.
The discussion says ten major boundary scenarios were considered experimentally, but only the six in table 6.3 are enumerated in the extracted chapter.
Its domain semantics comprises 69 Prolog clauses, but conformance testing demonstrates errors rather than proving their absence.
SymboleoPC tests cover all ontology concepts and 18 of 22 ontology links; liability, performer, right-holder, and subcontracting links are excluded because runtime operations are unsupported.
Reachable states for pizza, meat-sale, and transactive-energy cases were computed in 0.01, 0.01, and 0.23 seconds.
All listed case-study properties were checked in under 3 seconds; several intentionally or diagnostically failed and generated counterexamples.
The scalability study uses dependency rates and property shapes estimated from 14 real contracts, then generates synthetic nuXmv specifications.
It varies 1–32 obligations and 1–16 powers; the 32-obligation/16-power case exceeded the two-hour limit.
With eight powers, independent-contract reachable-state averages rose from 3.12 seconds at one obligation to 46.12 seconds at 16 obligations.
For dependent positions, corresponding averages were 1.56 and 45.78 seconds, though variability increased.
Across 1,000 LTL and 1,000 CTL properties on a 16-obligation/16-power contract, median verification was about 0.2 and 0.001 seconds; averages were 0.29 and 0.03 seconds.
The author concludes that SymboleoPC supports ordinary contracts with fewer than roughly 40 legal positions, not very large contracts with hundreds.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

SymboleoPC abstracts away actual clock time to avoid state explosion and supports ordering only, which is insufficient for some deadlines and duration-sensitive doctrines.
It does not support runtime operations, endogenous events, repeated obligation/power instantiation, or joint performance by multiple parties.
SymboleoCC requires manual translation of contract-specific axioms to Prolog, is recursive and inefficient on large contracts, and lacks industrial usability and systematic test generation.
Jurisdiction-level obligations and powers are not imported, so a contract may verify against its own text while violating governing law.
Natural-language-to-Symboleo translation, sensor/blockchain integration, and proof of correspondence to executable smart-contract code are outside the core evaluation.
The prototype emphasizes business contracts and does not establish coverage for employment, marriage, IP licensing, or other contract families.
Formal success cannot detect duress, nondisclosure, capacity defects, or every ground making a contract void or voidable.
Suspension and resumption effects remain legally underspecified, especially responsibility for perishable assets and shifted deadlines.
The ontology's reduction of Hohfeld to obligation and power helps scalability but discards privilege, immunity, disability, and no-right distinctions that may matter in legal analysis.
There is no bitemporal provenance model for changed contract text, retroactive amendments, competing interpretations, or when monitoring data became known.
