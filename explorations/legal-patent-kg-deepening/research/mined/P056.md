# On Verifiable Legal Reasoning: A Multi-Agent Framework with Formalized Knowledge Representations

## Problem & positioning

- Foundational LLMs interpret language efficiently but apply multi-step statutes inconsistently; reasoning models perform better but impose latency and computational costs.
- End-to-end answers obscure whether a failure came from fact extraction, legal vocabulary, rule formalization, inference, arithmetic, or answer generation.
- The paper tests a focused hypothesis: whether a reusable ontology and symbolic application layer can narrow this model-performance gap on numerical statutory reasoning.
- Tax law is selected as a structured proof-of-concept domain with conditional rules and objectively checkable dollar outputs, not as evidence of general legal competence.
- The proposed SOLAR framework separates one-time knowledge acquisition from repeated case-specific knowledge application.

## Approach / architecture

- Stage I runs concept-extraction and rule-formulation agents in parallel over statutory text.
- An integration agent consolidates candidate classes, properties, and first-order rules into a TBox; a validation agent loops refinements until internal checks pass.
- A code-generation agent creates a Python TBox interpreter that accepts an ABox and returns the statutory numeric result.
- Training cases exercise the complete pipeline; a judge agent attributes failures to ontology or interpreter defects and routes feedback to the responsible generator.
- Stage II gives the precomputed TBox vocabulary to a query-analysis agent, which maps narrative facts to individuals and assertions in an ABox.
- An SMT-based symbolic inference agent applies the TBox rules to derive entailed assertions.
- An answer-generation agent combines stated and inferred assertions, then invokes the fixed interpreter for calculation and explanation.
- Natural-language work stays at extraction/generation boundaries; symbolic inference and executable code handle logical closure and arithmetic.
- The implementation uses Python 3.13, LangChain 0.3.25, LangGraph 0.4.8, NLTK 3.9.1's SMT solver, Pydantic 2.x, and temperature 0 where available.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- For task `T`, `TBox_T = (C, P, R)`: legal classes, properties, and first-order inference rules extracted from the statute.
- Properties divide into unary predicates, object relationships, and datatype attributes; examples include marital status, spouse links, and income amounts.
- An `ABox_X` contains case individuals and grounded assertions; inferred facts `A'` are those entailed by `TBox_T ∪ ABox_X`.
- The authors prefer granular facts plus explicit rules over high-level shortcut properties, making each inference step inspectable.
- Because first-order logic does not directly express deontic concepts, normative meaning is embedded in property names such as `RequiredToFile` and `AllowedStandardDeduction`.
- The TBox is both a shared agent contract and a compact reusable representation; it replaces repeated transmission and interpretation of full statutes.
- The generated interpreter operationalizes knowledge not captured by logical closure alone, creating a three-layer artifact: vocabulary/rules, case assertions/inferences, and calculation code.
- Assertions can carry extraction confidence and explanations, but the paper does not formalize those as ontology axioms or a persistent provenance graph.
- The error analysis exposes a schema-contract gap: vocabulary alone does not communicate required co-occurrences such as spouse relationships needed by the interpreter.

## Evaluation & results

- SARA Numeric contains 96 U.S. federal tax cases; correctness uses LegalBench's 10% tolerance around the target amount.
- The study compares zero-shot, Chain-of-Code, and SOLAR across ten named foundational/reasoning models; two models could not complete SOLAR because of structured-output incompatibility.
- Across evaluated models, SOLAR averages 77.9% accuracy versus 65.7% for Chain-of-Code and 32.4% for zero-shot.
- For non-reasoning models, SOLAR averages 76.4%, compared with 58.3% Chain-of-Code and 18.8% zero-shot—a 57.6-point gain over zero-shot.
- DeepSeek V3 rises from 15.63% zero-shot to 82.30% SOLAR; Gemini 2.0 Flash rises from 6.20% to 64.60%.
- GPT-4.1 mini scores 21.90% zero-shot, 86.30% Chain-of-Code, and 86.50% SOLAR.
- For reasoning models, SOLAR underperforms Chain-of-Code: o4-mini scores 87.50% versus 93.80%, and Gemini 2.5 Flash 77.10% versus 96.90%.
- The reasoning/non-reasoning gap falls from 68.2 percentage points under zero-shot to 5.9 under SOLAR.
- Reported standard deviation across models is 0.08 for SOLAR, 0.23 for Chain-of-Code, and 0.28 for zero-shot.
- SOLAR averages about 4,000 tokens per query versus about 8,000 zero-shot and 8,500 Chain-of-Code, reductions of 45% and 48% respectively.
- Average latency is 12.8 seconds for SOLAR, 1.5 seconds for zero-shot, and 7.1 seconds for Chain-of-Code.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The authors limit claims to one calculation-heavy domain, one ontology design, and 96 cases; replication and cross-domain evaluation are required.
- Stage I is deliberately optimized with one o1-generated TBox, so the experiment does not evaluate ontology-construction reliability or compare construction methods.
- Refining ontology and interpreter until all training samples pass can overfit the artifact; the paper does not report a separate ontology-quality benchmark.
- A 10% numeric tolerance can count materially wrong legal calculations as correct and does not assess the correctness of reasons.
- Claude 3.7 Sonnet and Llama 4 Maverick return no SOLAR result because of structured-output compatibility failures.
- Missing vocabulary makes stated facts unrepresentable; missing “grammar” leaves required property combinations implicit; interpreter precedence bugs survive a sound vocabulary.
- Encoding obligations and permissions inside property names does not supply full deontic, defeasible, exception, conflict, or temporal reasoning.
- The generated Python interpreter is another authority-bearing artifact and needs versioning, static analysis, traceability to rules, and adversarial tests.
- The paper reports transparent inspection points but no legal-expert study measuring whether explanations are faithful, sufficient, or faster to audit.
- Strong gains against zero-shot partly reflect the substantial engineered pipeline and precomputed artifact, not ontology structure alone.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **epistemic slice:** take the TBox/ABox separation and attach every ABox fact and inferred fact to evidence spans, confidence, rule identity, model version, and bitemporal validity; do not leave explanations as free text.
- **epistemic slice:** turn the three failure families—missing vocabulary, missing composition contracts, faulty interpreter precedence—into distinct contradiction/quality inventories with separate remediation owners.
- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** use named classes, object/data properties, and explicit rules as schemas shared across agents, but add shape constraints for required co-occurrence and dependency patterns.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** apply this architecture only to bounded, calculation-oriented workflows such as eligibility, deadlines, fees, and damages tables, with human review at each inspectable boundary.
- **patent tooling (agentic-cad-patent-tooling):** reuse parallel concept/rule extraction and deterministic interpreters for claim-constraint checking; reject generated rules that lack claim-span provenance and executable tests.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** store validated TBoxes and interpreters as versioned durable artifacts, separate from case ABoxes, so later law changes do not silently alter past reasoning traces.
