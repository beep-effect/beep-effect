# Ontologies in Legal Information Systems: The Need for Explicit Specifications of Domain Conceptualisations

## Problem & positioning

- Legal knowledge systems inevitably commit to assumptions about entities, relations, types, closure, execution, and legal reasoning, but those assumptions are often implicit in code.
- Implicit conceptualizations obstruct knowledge sharing, verification, documentation, acquisition, reuse, and comparison of competing legal theories.
- Law especially needs explicit commitments because it is interconnected, consequential, mutable, expensive to acquire, reusable across related subdomains, and theoretically contested.
- The paper uses “ontology” in the knowledge-engineering sense of an explicit specification of a domain conceptualization, not the traditional philosophical sense.
- It compares two substantial legal ontologies and reconstructs the ontology implicit in a classic logic-programming approach to legislation.
- The central position is pluralist and purpose-relative: legal ontologies should be judged against their tasks and should form a modular library, not one universal account of law.

## Approach / architecture

- Six motivations structure the analysis: knowledge sharing, knowledge-base verification, software engineering/documentation, knowledge acquisition, reuse, and domain-theory development.
- A pensionable-age logic-program example demonstrates how apparently equivalent rules depend on hidden type, closed-world, and execution-order assumptions.
- The paper distinguishes low-level commitments such as enumerated types and integer ranges from high-level commitments such as law-as-definition and deductive execution.
- It then describes Valente's functional ontology and Van Kralingen and Visser's frame-based ontology, both formalized in ONTOLINGUA.
- Formalization in one language enables comparison beneath their divergent top-level perspectives and suggests possible translation rules.
- The British Nationality Act logic-program approach is reverse-engineered as a conceptualization of legal knowledge as definitions stated by extended definite clauses.
- McCarty's open-textured account is interpreted as a possible extension—necessary conditions plus extensible exemplars and transformations—rather than merely an incompatible rival.
- The proposed future architecture is a library containing competing, complementary, generic, statute-specific, abstract, and detailed ontology modules.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- Valente models the legal system functionally as an instrument that reacts to social behavior and steers society.
- His six primitive knowledge categories are `NormativeKnowledge`, `WorldKnowledge`, `ResponsibilityKnowledge`, `ReactiveKnowledge`, `MetaLegalKnowledge`, and `CreativeKnowledge`.
- Normative knowledge assigns statuses to states of affairs; actions derive status through the states they realize.
- World knowledge connects physical/common-sense descriptions to institutional descriptions used in norms.
- Responsibility knowledge attributes violations to agents; reactive knowledge connects responsible violations to sanctions.
- Meta-legal knowledge contains conflict and reasoning principles such as `lex specialis`; creative knowledge governs how legal knowledge begins and ceases to exist.
- ON-LINE links legal text to executable analysis through expressions organized by the functional ontology.
- Van Kralingen and Visser divide generic legal knowledge into `Norm`, `Act`, and `ConceptDescription`, each represented by a frame with defined attributes.
- A statute-specific ontology supplies predicate relations that instantiate/refine those generic frames for a particular legal domain.
- `eventQualification` and `processQualification` relate physical events/processes to institutional ones, such as killing to murder or manslaughter.
- The frame ontology's normative status maps from an applied norm, whereas Valente's maps from a situation; their status ranges also differ.
- In the British Nationality Act reconstruction, defined predicates are supplied by legislation; undefined predicates come from the system or user observations.
- Extended definite clauses provide individually sufficient and jointly necessary conditions, with negation-as-failure introducing closure and execution commitments.
- McCarty's alternative legal concept has three components: possibly empty necessary conditions, exemplars that provide sufficient conditions, and transformations among exemplars.
- A modular legal-ontology library should preserve both the physical world assumed by law and institutional/legal qualifications of that world.

## Evaluation & results

- The paper compares 2 substantial legal ontologies represented in the same ontology language.
- Valente's ontology contains 6 primitive functional categories and underpins the ON-LINE legal information server.
- The frame ontology contains 3 generic entity families and underpins FRAMER in Dutch unemployment-benefit law.
- FRAMER supports 2 tasks: entitlement classification and planning actions that produce a desired legal consequence.
- The pensionable-age example exposes 3 hidden assumption families: a binary/complete sex value, integer-valued age, and execution-order/implicit-negation behavior.
- Formal comparison finds a shared physical-to-institutional qualification layer despite radically different functional versus building-block decompositions.
- It also finds a substantive normative-status mismatch: Valente distinguishes allowed, disallowed, and silent, while the frame ontology groups allowed and silent as not breached.
- The conclusion states 6 findings and recommends a library spanning competing and supporting ontologies at multiple abstraction levels.
- No corpus size, query benchmark, accuracy measure, user study, reuse-effort measurement, or empirical interoperation test is reported.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- Valente reports that his functional ontology had not yet been tested across different legislation and legal systems.
- Its domain modeling is resource intensive, and the ON-LINE domain model is described as restricted despite a relatively complete category scheme.
- Valente explicitly does not claim cognitive validity; the goal is parsimonious valid representation/reasoning rather than a descriptive model of judges.
- The frame ontology is also resource intensive and had been applied to a highly definitional domain, leaving its asserted legal generality unproven.
- The British Nationality Act approach applies only to legislation that is substantially definitional and is weak when unresolved predicates require case law or expertise.
- Necessary-and-sufficient definitions cannot by themselves represent open texture, counterfactual definitions, evolving exemplars, or persuasive argument.
- The paper warns of a reusability/completeness tradeoff: detailed ontologies become more task- and information-context-specific.
- The proposed library has no worked module-selection, alignment-governance, versioning, inconsistency, or authority mechanism.
- `CreativeKnowledge` anticipates legal change but lacks explicit valid-time/transaction-time semantics for enactment, commencement, amendment, repeal, and retrospective application.
- The binary pensionable-sex example reflects the statute and era examined and must not be generalized into a contemporary person ontology.
- ONTOLINGUA-level similarity can enable translations, but translation correctness still requires preserved legal meaning, provenance, and context.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** Build a versioned library of purpose-labeled schemes and alignments, preserving competing conceptualizations instead of seeking one canonical legal taxonomy.
- **epistemic slice:** Turn `CreativeKnowledge` into bitemporal legal-change events and attach every institutional qualification, rule conclusion, and ontology alignment to authority and evidence spans.
- **epistemic slice:** Represent unknown, false, silent, allowed, and not-breached separately; reject negation-as-failure unless the closure scope and information source are explicit.
- **patent tooling (uspto-patent-driver-depth):** Separate generic prosecution frames from office- and procedure-specific predicates, enabling reuse without pretending USPTO-specific concepts are universal law.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Use the six functional lanes to organize research traces—norm, world fact, responsibility, consequence, conflict rule, and legal-change event—while retaining the underlying text.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** Store physical facts, institutional qualifications, norms, exemplars, and transformations as separate nodes so an agent can explain both classification and conceptual drift.
