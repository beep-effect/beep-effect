# A Constructive Framework for Legal Ontologies

## Problem & positioning

- Earlier legal knowledge systems emphasized rule formalisms, deontic logic, defeasibility, cases, and argumentation while under-specifying what kinds of entities legal knowledge concerns.
- The authors argue that this made legislative formalization subjective, expensive, difficult to reuse, and largely confined legal expert systems to prototypes.
- Their alternative is a constructive ontology: contextual descriptions adopted by agents are represented in the same domain as the situations and entities they organize.
- The approach is intended to scale down to semantic tagging, information extraction, and Web services, or up to inference-heavy legal decision support.
- The Core Legal Ontology (`CLO`) targets three Civil Law tasks: conformity checking, legal advice, and comparison of norms.
- Conformity checks compare a social or legal situation against a norm; advice additionally needs causation, responsibility, open texture, and interpretation.
- Norm comparison includes diachronic change within one system and synchronic comparison across jurisdictions, especially EU directives and national implementations.
- CLO is grounded in DOLCE plus the Descriptions and Situations ontology (`D&S`), collectively called `DOLCE+` in the paper.
- JurWordNet complements CLO with an Italian legal lexical layer for retrieval, extraction, semantic tagging, and multilingual access.

## Approach / architecture

- DOLCE supplies the ground categories: endurants, perdurants, qualities, quality spaces/regions, and abstracts, plus participation, parthood, constitution, and localization relations.
- D&S supplies a descriptive layer whose `Description`, `Concept`, and `Situation` individuals make contextual conceptualizations available for first-order reasoning.
- A description defines concepts; concepts classify entities; a situation is a setting that satisfies a description.
- `Role`, `Course`, and `Parameter` specialize concept according to whether the classified entity is an endurant, perdurant, or region.
- `PlayedBy`, `Sequences`, and `ValuedBy` specialize classification for those three concept families.
- `AttitudeTowards` connects roles or social figures to courses and can reify deontic, epistemic, alethic, agentive, or submissive participation modes.
- `RequisiteFor` connects a parameter to a role, figure, or course, constraining the qualities/values of entities in satisfying situations.
- Descriptions and concepts are social objects, so they can themselves be classified in other descriptions; this recursion supports meta-norms and norms about norms.
- An information object expresses a description according to a code and is realized by a physical or electronic bearer; this separates legal meaning, expression, and document.
- CLO treats a legal description as the reification of a theory and a legal case as the reification of a state of affairs that may model that theory.
- Reified satisfaction is deliberately relaxed: a situation may contain irrelevant entities and may satisfy several unrelated descriptions without being stripped into minimal models.
- Domain-specific subclasses of satisfaction add the mandatory classification and constraint conditions needed for conformity, power exercise, belief, prevention, or compatibility.
- The traffic-code example defines driver and vehicle roles, driving/running courses, and a speed parameter valued by the region at or below 50 km/h.
- A case containing a 120 km/h travel satisfies participant and course classifications but fails the speed requisite, so it does not conform.
- Norm compatibility is modeled as conformity of a situation containing norms to a compatibility meta-description.
- A compatibility scenario orders norms through source, specialty, chronology, and domain parameters, assigns entrenchment roles, and specifies a comparison course.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- The central pattern separates `LegalDescription` from `LegalSituation`: a norm is not the same entity as the case said to conform to it.
- `LegalNorm`, `Regulation`, `JudicialDecision`, and compatibility meta-norms are first-order description individuals, enabling statements and comparisons about norms.
- `LegalRole`, `LegalCourse`, and `LegalParameter` are reusable context components that respectively classify actors/assets, sequence events, and constrain values.
- `LegalText` is an information object, its normative content is a description, and its document is a realization; one text segment need not correspond one-to-one with one norm.
- Constitutive norms introduce legally constructed persons, institutions, roles, functions, and powers; regulative norms constrain existing entities and behaviors.
- Modal descriptions reify legal positions and their oppositions/correlations: claim/right-duty, privilege-non-right, immunity-disability, power-liability, and permissions.
- Legal persons and institutions are agentive social figures; a role can be deputed by a figure, and a physical or social agent playing it can act for that figure.
- Legal facts are norm-dependent situations; natural facts, human facts, institutional facts, legal acts, and legal transactions retain different dependence and intentionality conditions.
- Crimes are situations satisfying incriminating norms; aggravating or extenuating circumstances are situation parts rather than loose labels.
- Temporal legal qualities include duration, deferment, expiration, term, and validity states such as valid, void, or voidable.
- Reified satisfaction permits evidence-rich case graphs to retain context that is irrelevant to one norm but relevant to another.
- Internal norm conflict is represented through incompatible classes of satisfying situations, rather than treating the reified norm individuals themselves as logically inconsistent.
- Total, partial, and general/exception antinomies depend on overlap among temporal, spatial, personal, and material application scopes.
- Entrenchment is multi-dimensional: source, specialty, chronology, and domain orders should be explicit parameters, not hidden in one precedence score.
- JurWordNet represents lexical senses as synsets with taxonomic and associative links, then maps them to CLO distinctions.
- Systematic polysemy motivates separate nodes for an institution, function, office holder, normative content, information expression, and physical document.
- Cross-system legal concepts may lack direct lexical equivalence; foundational alignment can support an explanatory paraphrase without asserting false synonymy.

## Evaluation & results

- Evaluation is by formal worked examples and reported applications, not a controlled accuracy, usability, or performance study.
- The traffic example demonstrates a non-conformity result because 120 km/h is outside the region accepted by the 50 km/h parameter.
- The authors report that norms with more than five or six descriptive entities appeared, in ongoing work, less complex than corresponding rule-based models; no measurement protocol or data are supplied.
- JurWordNet input resources include an 11,000-keyword/lemma database and about 13,000 two-word expressions from the Italgiure/Find system.
- Its consolidated corpus had about 2,000 synsets, intended for augmentation through links to legal thesauri and database keywords.
- The paper reports applications for diachronic norm comparison, synchronic compliance, contract/agreement conformity, bundles of rights, retrieval, and extraction.
- It also reports two industrial ontology-driven semantic-service implementations, but gives no task metrics, scale, or deployment outcomes.
- No precision/recall, reasoner runtime, ontology size, inter-annotator agreement, conflict-resolution accuracy, or comparative baseline is presented.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The authors say causation and responsibility were not sufficiently defined; these are essential to the legal-advice scenario they motivate.
- They acknowledge that subsumption alone is not effective enough to identify legal specialty and exceptions.
- Completeness of the legal corpus, granularity, source authority, alternative interpretations, and differences between Civil and Common Law remain open comparison problems.
- Social effectiveness and consequence-based judicial reasoning fall outside internal coherence and require additional practice or socio-legal descriptions.
- Reified satisfaction preserves useful context but is intentionally permissive; applications still need explicit qualified-satisfaction constraints to avoid vacuous matches.
- The reported compatibility method can represent unsatisfiability but does not establish a generally correct conflict-resolution algorithm.
- First-order reification makes claims about norms expressible, but it does not remove substantive disagreement over interpretation, authority, or applicable facts.
- OWL-DL cannot directly express all temporal indexing used by D&S; the paper mentions workarounds without providing a complete bitemporal semantics.
- JurWordNet's query-log-derived vocabulary can reflect user behavior and legacy-system bias rather than authoritative legal conceptualization.
- Approximate synset equivalence and cross-language paraphrase require jurisdictional provenance; otherwise lexical alignment could erase real doctrinal differences.
- The traffic example omits statutory exceptions and systemic dependencies, so it demonstrates the pattern rather than realistic legal adjudication.
- The paper predates current RDF provenance, SHACL, named-graph, temporal-KG, and embedding practices; implementation details need contemporary reconstruction.
