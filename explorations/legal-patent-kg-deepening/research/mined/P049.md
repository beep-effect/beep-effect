# Legal Relations in a Core Ontology of Legal Aspects Based on Alexy’s Theory of Constitutional Rights

## Problem & positioning

Legal core ontologies should provide reusable generic legal concepts for more specific domain ontologies.
Normative texts often state a right without explicitly identifying the actor who bears the corresponding duty, the required act or omission, or its intended result.
Models derived only from legislation or agreements can therefore omit actors, roles, dependencies, and the relational structure behind a norm.
The authors also argue that legal ontologies without foundational grounding risk ontological inconsistency and incompleteness.
UFO-L addresses this semantic gap by combining Alexy’s theory of constitutional rights with the Unified Foundational Ontology (UFO).
The paper presents the first pattern in a planned UFO-L catalogue: a right–duty relation pattern.
Its positioning is deliberately relational rather than norm-centered: legal positions exist through relations among role-bearing subjects.

## Approach / architecture

UFO supplies the foundational categories used to interpret Alexy’s legal concepts.
UFO-A models endurants and includes Universal, Individual, and Relator; UFO-B adds events; UFO-C adds social agents and social roles.
UFO-L specializes UFO-A and UFO-C for legal aspects.
Legal roles are prescribed by a legal norm before assignment to an agent or group and are played only within legal relationships.
Legal relations are reified through legal relators, understood as relational moments existentially dependent on participating individuals.
Alexy’s classification supplies three broad legal-relator families: rights to something, liberties, and powers.
This paper instantiates only the rights-to-something family, divided into right–duty to an omission and right–duty to an act.
The pattern is demonstrated by unpacking the constitutional statement “Everyone has the right to life.”
The example yields a negative right against state killing and a positive right requiring state protection against unlawful third-party intervention.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

Core entity categories include `Legal Role`, `Right-holder`, `Duty-holder`, `Legal Relator`, and the agents that bear the roles.
`Legal Role` specializes `Social Role`; right-holder and duty-holder identify the two role positions mediated by the relator.
A right to something is modeled as a bundle of two possible relator types: `Right-duty to an omission` and `Right-duty to an act`.
Each legal relator is composed of dependent modes borne by the participants.
For omission, the paired modes are `Right to an omission` inhering in the right-holder and `Duty to omit` inhering in the duty-holder.
The positive-act counterpart captures a duty to perform an act and the correlative right to that act.
The model is triadic because a legal relation connects right-holder, duty-holder, and the action/content that is owed or forbidden.
The elicitation pattern asks who holds the right, who holds the duty, what positive or negative action is required, and what result should obtain.
For omissions, candidate results include non-obstruction of acts, non-disruption of situations or characteristics, and non-removal of legal positions.
The life-right example separates two relations that a single sentence compresses: State must refrain from killing, and State must protect life.
Existential dependence is load-bearing: the relator cannot exist without the participating individuals and their role-bound modes.
The paper supplies a conceptual OntoUML-style pattern, not an operational RDF/OWL serialization, rule engine, or KG population method.
It does not formalize temporal activation, jurisdiction, norm identity, evidence provenance, defeasibility, or collisions among principles.

## Evaluation & results

Evaluation is illustrative rather than empirical.
The authors apply the pattern to one constitutional norm, “Everyone has the right to life,” and show that it exposes implicit role and duty structure.
The example distinguishes a negative/defensive right from a positive/protective right.
No competency-question results, expert validation, ontology metrics, reasoning tests, inter-annotator agreement, or comparative baseline are reported.
The paper explicitly leaves empirical experiments for future work.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

Only one portion of UFO-L and one pattern family are presented; liberties and powers are named but not developed here.
Alexy’s balancing and weighing structure for collisions of principles is deferred to future work.
Empirical validation of UFO-L and its pattern catalogue is also future work, as is a domain-specific language.
The single right-to-life example demonstrates expressiveness but cannot establish usability, completeness, or consistency across legal domains.
The phrase “prescribed by a legal norm before assignment” needs temporal and institutional semantics to handle enactment, repeal, suspension, and retroactivity.
Action/result contents are discussed but not fully reified in the textual explanation, leaving open how conditions, beneficiaries, objects, and exceptions attach.
The model assumes identifiable correlatives; diffuse, collective, conditional, or contested duties may require n-ary participation and alternative claims.
It offers no machinery for defeasible priority, contrary authority, enforcement, breach, remedy, or proof that a duty was discharged.
Foundational rigor does not by itself determine whether the modeled legal interpretation is substantively correct.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology): adopt the relator pattern for legal relations rather than encoding `hasRight` as an unqualified binary edge; preserve role, counterpart, and action content.
- epistemic slice: treat norm text, interpreted right–duty relator, and factual compliance claim as distinct claim layers, each with source spans and valid/recorded time.
- patent tooling (uspto-patent-driver-depth): model applicant, examiner, assignee, and public as context-bound legal roles mediated by filing, examination, ownership, and disclosure relations.
- patent tooling (agentic-cad-patent-tooling): turn the pattern’s elicitation questions into schema-required fields for every asserted obligation; reject extractions that omit bearer, counterparty, act/omission, or result.
- OIP solo-practice workflows: use paired rights/duties to generate issue checklists from contracts and statutes while marking implicit interpretations for lawyer review.
