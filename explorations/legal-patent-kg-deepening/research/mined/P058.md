# Ontological Analysis of Policy-based Decision Making

## Problem & positioning

- Organizational decisions are constrained by policies, rules, standards, procedures, and guidelines, sometimes to the point of automation.
- Decision systems gain transparency by representing rules separately from process code, but issuer, validity time, organizational authority, and other context must also be explicit.
- Existing UFO work treats policy as a normative description, while UFO-L treats duties and rights as agent-dependent modes within social/legal relators; the two accounts conflict.
- The paper extends the UFO-grounded Core Ontology on Decision Making (COoDM) and UFO-L to reconcile decision, organizational policy, authority, and policy-document artifacts.
- Its core claim is that a policy is a bundle of legal/normative positions governing agents, whereas a policy document is an artifact that refers to that policy.
- The proposal specializes broad legal patterns for internal organizations, including resource control, organizational roles, multi-level action types, and policy activation.

## Approach / architecture

- The authors review COoDM's decision-as-intention account, where deliberation produces an intention whose goal is satisfied by a situation brought about through action.
- They use UFO-L's Hohfeldian correlation: a legal position is an agent-dependent mode within a relator, pairing rights/claims with duties and powers with subjections.
- They critically compare DOLCE normative descriptions, UFO/UFO-L, OMG BMM/SBVR policy distinctions, DAML policy types, Information Artifact Ontology aboutness, and artifact theory.
- Counterexamples test norm/document identity: deleting a promise's text does not discharge the obligation; oral cultures have norms; and speech acts may create or describe obligations without being those obligations.
- Two OntoUML patterns are proposed: an organizational-policy pattern and a policy-document pattern.
- The organizational pattern specializes delegation: a Corporation/Organizer delegates to Organizational Agents through an Organization relator.
- Organizational policies and correlative organizational rules are modes within that relator, externally dependent on the governed role/agent and serving organizational goals.
- Organizational resources are included because delegated powers, rights, and duties primarily govern use of corporation-owned resources, including work hours.
- The document pattern treats policy documents as designed textual/symbolic artifacts with make plans, use plans, use conditions, and intended use effects.
- Activation by an authorized actor gives the document a policy/law capacity; effectuation manifests that disposition by creating a policy at a specified time or publication event.
- In decision making, a policy document may play a Procedure role and provide decision-support capacity, while the actual policy constrains the agent.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- `Decision` is an `Intention`, not a belief or action; it has a goal whose propositional content is satisfied by a resulting situation, and an executed action manifests it.
- `Deliberation` produces the decision and is triggered by a situation in which an agent's intentions are unsatisfied; beliefs, preferences/value ascriptions, desires, and intentions remain distinct.
- `LegalRelator` contains correlative agent modes: claim/duty and power/subjection; legal capability is required for institution-only actions such as imposing tax.
- A `Delegatum`/`Organization` relator connects Corporation/Organizer and Organizational Agent; organizational policy and organizational rule are dependent components of that relation.
- `OrganizationalPolicy` is typically a complex bundle; its parts may be called policy instruments.
- Policies serve organizational goals but agents are subject to policies, not directly to those goals.
- Policies are usually defined for `OrganizationalRole` universals such as Treasurer or Oncologist; roles are in turn characterized by their rules.
- `OrganizationalRule` constrains `ActionUniversal`; action universals form specialization hierarchies from goal-only types to detailed methods and thresholds.
- A concrete payment event can instantiate both `paying invoice` and `paying international invoice above $500`, allowing multiple applicable rules.
- `PolicyDocument` and `Policy` are distinct: documents can be shared between organizations, while policies inhere in a particular organization.
- The document `refersTo/isAbout` the policy; it does not contain the real-world relation or become identical to its semantic content.
- `PolicyDocument` has a design, make plan, and possibly several use plans; unmandated use plans are guidelines, whose actions are not deontic merely by being recommended.
- `ActivationEvent` manifests the Activator's normative power and gives the document a disposition; `EffectuationEvent` manifests that disposition by creating the policy.
- Document lifecycle includes draft, activation, change, deactivation, and removal/change of associated policy, although a complete state machine is not formalized.
- `DecisionRule` or DMN table may implement/reference a policy and use a policy document as design input; the application/interpreter remains distinct from the rules.

## Evaluation & results

- The paper is an ontological analysis with two OntoUML models and worked examples; it reports no quantitative evaluation.
- The Treasurer example represents authority to pay invoices, conditions for international payments above $500, and required written department-head consent under a financial-risk goal.
- The ERP example shows embedded decision rules restricting payment ordering to the Treasurer role and requiring consent/checks under specified conditions.
- The oncology example distinguishes a Clinical Practice Guideline document, the policy adopted by a hospital, a DMN table derived from the document, and the decisions/actions constrained by that policy.
- The analysis resolves five counterexamples against norm/document identity: persistence after bearer deletion, unaccepted descriptions, oral norms, endurant/perdurant mismatch, and speech-act effects.
- No competency questions, formal axioms, reasoner tests, inter-modeler agreement, case corpus, implementation, or comparison against BMM/SBVR/DAML is presented.
- The conclusion labels the organization ontology initial and the work still in progress.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The authors explicitly defer policy grounding across organizational levels to future work.
- Figures simplify away the distinction between atomic and complex legal positions even though policies are said to be typically complex.
- Internal organizational policies are treated in a broad normative sense whether or not they have legal force; the model needs an explicit enforceability/authority distinction to avoid conflation.
- The account assumes a Corporation grounded in relation to government or higher power and may need adaptation for informal groups, partnerships, public bodies, and cross-organizational governance.
- Activation and effectuation are distinguished, but adoption, promulgation, effective date, suspension, retroactivity, amendment, repeal, and jurisdictional challenge need fuller temporal semantics.
- Document content, versions, physical/digital bearers, signatures, issuers, interpretations, and cited authority are discussed but not fully modeled.
- A policy document can support multiple user groups and use plans, raising interpretation and conflict questions not resolved by the two patterns.
- The paper does not formalize rule priority, exception, defeasibility, conflict/deconfliction, discretion, noncompliance, sanction, or appeal.
- Automated policy implementation may technically prevent an action, while a legal/normative rule may merely prohibit it; capability, permission, and enforcement mechanism must remain distinct.
- Organizational-goal linkage risks rationalizing a policy after the fact unless goal claims carry issuer and evidence provenance.
- No empirical validation establishes that practitioners understand the distinctions or that they improve explainability/adaptability in a live decision system.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** Adopt the policy/policy-document split and the `refersTo` relation; reject models that identify a document, its information content, and the normative positions it evidences.
- **epistemic slice:** Encode activation, effectuation, amendment, suspension, and deactivation as bitemporal events; link each policy-position claim to authority, document version, exact span, affected role, and contradiction set.
- **patent tooling (agentic-cad-patent-tooling):** Separate a patent-office rule, the rule-bearing manual/guidance document, a machine decision table, and the agent action it constrains; preserve thresholds and action-universal subsumption.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Use the Treasurer pattern for authority-aware approvals: role, governed resource, permitted action, threshold, required consent, policy goal, and effective policy version should be inspectable in one trace.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** Treat automated prevention and normative prohibition as different states so software cannot imply that technically possible conduct is authorized or that blocked conduct is legally invalid.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** Store document artifacts, organizational adoption/effectuation events, policy bundles, and derived executable rules separately, enabling agents to answer both “what did the text say?” and “what governed this actor then?”
