# The Ontology of Legal Possibilities and Legal Potentialities

## Problem & positioning

- A legal expert system must handle any user case within its legal domain, not only fact patterns matching the express antecedents of black-letter rules.
- Omissions, contradictories, uncertainties, added circumstances, and adversarial positions create a combinatorial space of possible legal cases.
- Express rules alone therefore under-specify the premises and pathways needed for legally complete automated reasoning.
- The paper argues that ontology and logic constrain one another: legal authorities posit both legal content and the permitted logical use of that content.
- It frames the needed methodology as a jurisprudence of legal knowledge engineering grounded in prior analytics and legal epistemology.
- The proposal is conceptual and methodological, focused on knowledge acquisition and rule preparation rather than a delivered expert system.

## Approach / architecture

- Express black-letter law is first formalized as conditional propositions whose antecedents and consequents form major deductive premises.
- Rules are connected into an extended deductive order when one rule's consequent supplies another rule's antecedent.
- The system then derives or acquires contradictories and uncertainties for antecedents and consequents, including open-textured concepts.
- Inductive instances particularize rule terms using precedent facts, dicta, definitions, synonyms, antonyms, expert evidence, common knowledge, or common sense.
- Abductive premises capture reasons for rules and decisions, with strong or weak support and possible rule-changing force.
- The four-step construction is: extended deductive order; contradictories and uncertainties; inductive posits plus alternatives; abductive posits plus alternatives.
- Bacon's four tables—presence, absence, degree, and exclusion—serve as a methodological analogy for systematically expanding and testing possible configurations.
- The resulting knowledge structure is intended to generate only the relevant region of the combinatorial case space for a given user.

## Ontology & KG modeling contributions

- The paper distinguishes legal possibilities, in which an express condition may or may not exist, from potentialities realized by additions or inventions in a case.
- Three types of authority-backed ontological posit are assigned distinct inference roles.
- Deductive posits are rules used as major premises in extended deduction.
- Inductive posits are existential or definitional instances that particularize antecedents or consequents and may serve as minor premises.
- Abductive posits are reasons for rules or decisions and carry strong or weak support rather than deductive necessity.
- A judgment's characterization of a premise determines whether it functions deductively, inductively, or abductively.
- Rule nodes form a directed hierarchy: open-textured legal concepts are parent nodes and sufficiently defined factual concepts are leaves.
- Intermediate conclusions are first-class connectors that allow one rule's output to satisfy another rule's input.
- Issues may be factual, legal/open-textured, or mixed, depending on which rule and instance nodes are contested.
- Contradictories include both antonyms and absences; legally required nonexistence is treated as an ontological possibility rather than ignored.
- Uncertainty receives legal consequences through burdens of proof and remains part of the possibility space pending adjudication.
- Separate party-oriented rule streams may require explicit contradictory forms, but those forms need authoritative ontological support.
- From `if a then c`, neither `if not-a then not-c` nor backward inference from `c` to `a` is logically licensed.
- An adversarial contradictory becomes usable only when established by the law-maker's language or another authoritative legal posit.
- Evidence of material facts must establish antecedents directly or indirectly before the consequent follows.
- Inductive and abductive reasoning strands should remain separate annotations around the deductive rule structure, like historical glosses.
- Keeping the strands separate prevents defeasible support from masquerading as monotonic deductive necessity.
- Functional ontologies for tasks, goals, and methods are insufficient unless their logic reflects sound legal epistemology and jurisprudential assumptions.

## Evaluation & results

- The paper provides an analytic method, not a quantitative evaluation.
- A four-stage procedure is stated for constructing the full ontology of legal possibilities.
- A directed acyclic rule graph adapted from Popple illustrates open-textured parent nodes, factual leaves, and linked extended deduction.
- Civil pleading is used as a practical analogue: claim, defence, counterclaim, and reply identify supported facts, denials, contested rules, and adversarial alternatives.
- The Australian Spam Act 2004 supplies an example in which an authoritative disjunction differs from an invalid inferred contraposition.
- Bacon's tables provide examples of presence, absence, degree, and exclusion as a disciplined expansion method.
- The analysis predicts combinatorial explosion across antecedents, consequents, contradictories, uncertainties, and three reasoning modes.
- It recommends generating a case-relevant subspace rather than materializing every combination, but gives no algorithm or complexity bound.
- No implemented ontology, corpus, case count, accuracy measure, expert validation, runtime, or comparison with another system is reported.

## Limitations (authors' own + yours)

- The paper calls for acquisition from legal experts but provides no reproducible annotation protocol or agreement measure.
- “Ontology” is used broadly for premises, absences, instances, and reasons, without a formal class/property/axiom specification.
- The four-step method does not define an executable strategy for pruning the combinatorial explosion.
- Authority, jurisdiction, temporal validity, repeal, precedent hierarchy, and conflicts between law-makers are not modeled explicitly.
- Open texture and uncertainty are recognized but have no typed uncertainty calculus or confidence semantics.
- Strong and weak abduction are not operationalized, and the circumstances under which a reason changes a rule remain unspecified.
- Treating absences as ontology requires closed-world or explicit-negation discipline that conventional OWL does not supply automatically.
- The method depends heavily on expert prior analytics and may encode partisan or jurisprudential assumptions without transparent provenance.
- Separating reasoning strands is valuable, but the paper gives no formal proof that the combined system remains sound or terminates.
- Examples illustrate legal logic but do not show that the approach improves advice quality on real user matters.

## Beep-relevance hooks

- **semantic-foundation (SKOS/@beep/ontology):** keep controlled concepts separate from rule antecedents, factual instances, and reasons; reject a flat ontology that labels all four as equivalent “legal concepts.”
- **epistemic slice:** take the three typed inference lanes—deductive, inductive, abductive—and store provenance, evidence spans, confidence, and derivation paths so defeasible reasons never appear as necessary conclusions.
- **epistemic slice:** model explicit negation, absence, uncertainty, and contradiction as distinct claim states, then apply burden-of-proof rules during contradiction triage rather than assuming closed-world negation.
- **patent tooling (agentic-cad-patent-tooling):** generate examiner/applicant argument branches from claim limitations, missing limitations, uncertain mappings, precedent analogies, and policy reasons while forbidding automatic contraposition.
- **OIP solo-practice workflows:** build party-specific issue trees linking pleaded facts to rule antecedents and requested consequences; surface unsupported intermediate conclusions before drafting.
- **agent memory (Cognee):** preserve deductive rules and inductive/abductive glosses as separate edge types with authority and time, preventing a remembered rationale from hardening into black-letter law.
