# Adopting Alchemy Domain means building its Postgres host

*Research report, dated 2026-11-03. Subject: `@alchemy.run/domain` 2.0.0-beta.85, alchemy checkout `d0b4a3326`. Audience: the beep-effect operator, who has already decided to adopt; this report is about how.*

`@alchemy.run/domain` is a fast-moving, single-merger beta whose kernel is portable by design but whose only durable host is Cloudflare Durable Objects. Nothing durable ships for Postgres. Its first usable release was 2.0.0-beta.84 on 2026-10-24 and its second was beta.85 on 2026-11-02 ([npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/@alchemy.run%2Fdomain)). It rides a project-wide beta train that has added **26 labeled breaking entries across beta.82–85 alone** (`alchemy:CHANGELOG.md`). The only Postgres paths are two unmerged contributor PRs that no maintainer has reviewed: [#2392](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/pull/2392) and [#2794](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/pull/2794)/[#2795](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/pull/2795). Sam Goodwin's public portability promise was celld and Rivet support, "Coming in next release" ([X](https://x.com/samgoodwin89/status/2115564814835794085)). It did not ship in beta.85, both are actor hosts rather than databases, and celld is being merged into workerd ([X](https://x.com/rough__sea/status/2117203120345600338)). Independent adoption amounts to one self-described production app and a handful of side projects, all on Durable Objects. Weekly downloads near 28.7k mostly reflect an optional peer dependency and automation, not users. No Effect-native alternative pairs Domain's modelling surface with Postgres. The adoption therefore depends on three things beep-effect must own: a Postgres `Platform` layer (best started from #2794's `effect/cluster` + `effect/sql` design), versioning for its own events, and an exact-pinned lockstep dependency group kept behind beep-effect service contracts.

## Eighty-four betas in 203 days, and breaking changes are accelerating

Domain has no release schedule of its own. It entered core as `alchemy/Domain` in #2176, which was opened 2026-10-09 and merged 2026-10-15 ([PR #2176](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/pull/2176)). It moved into `packages/domain` on 2026-10-19 (#2348, `alchemy:CHANGELOG.md:283`), took a placeholder npm name on 2026-10-21, and **shipped its first usable version on 2026-10-24** ([npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/@alchemy.run%2Fdomain)). Every published domain version peer-pins the identical core beta (`alchemy: 2.0.0-beta.84`, then `2.0.0-beta.85`). The release script also fails unless every package carries the same version (`alchemy:scripts/release/prepare.ts:3-4`). A domain upgrade is therefore always an alchemy-wide upgrade. The Effect floor rose inside that single step, from `^4.0.5` to `^4.1.0` ([npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/@alchemy.run%2Fdomain)).

The 2.0 train opened on 2026-04-13. It has since put **84 numeric betas on npm in 203 days**, about 2.86 a week; beta.11 was tagged and GitHub-released but is absent from npm ([npm registry alchemy](https://registry.npmjs.org/alchemy)). The rhythm has changed. The median gap between releases was 0.17 days across beta.1–30 and **4.74 days from beta.60 onward**. Monthly releases fell from 24 in April to 3 in September and 5 in October. The last ten gaps were 1.0, 9.1, 7.9, 0.3, 14.3, 3.2, 7.1, 0.1, 11.1 and 9.8 days ([npm registry alchemy](https://registry.npmjs.org/alchemy)). Releases are cut by hand through `workflow_dispatch` (`alchemy:.github/workflows/release.yml:3-9`). Batches have grown as they slowed. **45 commits touched `packages/domain` between the beta.84 and beta.85 tags** (`git log v2.0.0-beta.84..v2.0.0-beta.85 -- packages/domain`). Over the same step the domain tarball doubled, from 140 files and 1,049,861 bytes to 180 files and 2,060,998 bytes ([npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/@alchemy.run%2Fdomain)). Expect roughly one domain version every one to two weeks, each carrying a large changeset.

The breaking rate is rising. **18 of the 85 numeric betas carry a "Breaking Changes" section, about 59 entries in all**, and none appears before beta.52 on 2026-06-05. The lane's parser counted 0 breaking entries in beta.1–51, 33 across beta.52–80 and **26 in beta.82–85 alone**, with 10 in beta.84 and 9 in beta.85 (`alchemy:CHANGELOG.md`, headings from `:1997` to `:3`; these are approximate parser counts, not an official tally). The breaks fall into several categories: package moves (all nine top-level beta.84 breaks, `alchemy:CHANGELOG.md:275-283`), API reshapes, persistence and platform runtime changes, Effect bumps labeled breaking (`:1999`, `:1266`, `:1178`, `:1101`), and CLI configuration.

For domain, the CHANGELOG label understates the churn. Exactly one beta.85 entry is labeled as a domain break: "A command client takes the target, the input, then call options" (#2561, `alchemy:CHANGELOG.md:12-13`). The label follows the conventional-commit `!` marker. The three `!` commits among the 45 match the three labeled domain-touching entries: #2561, #2601's Durable Object jurisdiction (`:9`), and #2621's move of `Test.make` (`:19`). The beta.85 release blog's breaking block is much longer. It lists Durable Object classes renamed after their model, the platform and engine turned into factories, the new client call shape, `watch` streaming `{ state, cursor }`, and `Domain.Api` taking `{ model }` with `api.entries` becoming `api.serves`. It also lists `testClient` becoming `clientInProcess`; a renamed story vocabulary (`externalSystems`→`stub`, `expectCall`→`call`, `resolve`→`succeed`) under which unasserted events now fail; and command targets forced to the aggregate's `id` schema (`alchemy:website/src/content/docs/blog/2026-11-01-beta-85.mdx:33-60`). The lane also listed ten unlabeled beta.85 domain entries whose titles describe changed contracts. Two examples are "Give each model its own storage and keep deployed data on upgrade" (`alchemy:CHANGELOG.md:96`) and "Evolve never throws; definition problems fail the Layer with a tagged error" (`:223`). Nobody read the diffs, so whether each one breaks source compatibility is UNVERIFIED. **The working rule: plan upgrades from the release blog's breaking block, not from the CHANGELOG label.**

No policy softens any of this. The README says "**alchemy** v2 is in beta. Expect breaking changes." (`alchemy:README.md:102`). Every release, prereleases included, goes to npm's `latest` dist-tag and is published as a non-prerelease GitHub release (`alchemy:scripts/release/github-release.ts:3-5`). GitHub's prerelease flag has been false since beta.14 on 2026-04-26 ([GitHub releases API](https://api.github.com/repos/alchemy-run/alchemy/releases)). Core's `next` tag is stale at beta.72 ([npm registry alchemy](https://registry.npmjs.org/alchemy)). The domain README contains no stability language (`alchemy:packages/domain/README.md`). Graduation to 2.0.0 is a manual `major` spec with no stated date or criteria (`alchemy:scripts/release/prepare.ts:12-13`). Because `^2.0.0-beta.84` matches every later 2.0.0 beta, a caret range admits breaking releases, and only exact pins protect a consumer.

**Download counts should not be read as adoption.** Domain went from zero to **28,674 downloads in the week ending 2026-11-01**. Daily counts climbed from 26 on 10-22 to 7,295 on 11-01, nearly all of them on beta.84 ([npm last-week](https://api.npmjs.org/downloads/point/last-week/@alchemy.run%2Fdomain), [daily range](https://api.npmjs.org/downloads/range/2026-10-01:2026-11-02/@alchemy.run%2Fdomain), [per-version](https://api.npmjs.org/versions/@alchemy.run%2Fdomain/last-week)). Three facts undercut that number. First, `@alchemy.run/cloudflare`, at 176,781 weekly downloads, lists domain as an optional peer. Domain therefore lands in the lockfiles of projects that never model anything (`alchemy:packages/cloudflare/package.json:806,814-817`; [npm downloads](https://api.npmjs.org/downloads/point/last-week/@alchemy.run%2Fcloudflare)). Second, most of the 486 GitHub code-search hits outside alchemy-run are lockfiles for exactly that reason. Third, core's own traffic skews toward old versions. In the week after beta.84 shipped, beta.83 drew 734,579 downloads against beta.84's 198,325. That pattern fits pinned consumers, caches and bots better than upgrading users, though the mix is UNVERIFIED ([npm per-version alchemy](https://api.npmjs.org/versions/alchemy/last-week)). The registry exposes no dependents list and no CI split, so the organic share is unknown.

## Two hosts ship; Postgres exists only as unreviewed contributor PRs

Domain's storage seam is real and cleanly shaped. The `Platform` service exposes one method, `model`. It turns a hosted model into a `ModelHost` with one store factory per concept (aggregate, view, policy, projection, stream) plus optional `start` and `history` (`alchemy:packages/domain/src/Platform.ts:603-640`, `:662-668`). Platforms move only JSON, because "The kernel owns the schemas; platforms only move and store these values." (`:35-39`). The service's doc comment promises "in-memory for tests and single processes, Durable Objects on Cloudflare, and so on" (`:659-660`). **At beta.85, "and so on" is empty.** The repo contains exactly two `Platform.of(...)` implementations. One is `Domain.InMemory` (`alchemy:packages/domain/src/InMemory.ts:1130`, exported at `:1162-1164`). The other is `DomainCloudflare.layerDurableObjects()`, whose Layer type requires a Cloudflare `Worker` (`alchemy:packages/cloudflare/src/Domain/index.ts:2139`, `:2257-2264`). A git pickaxe for postgres, sqlite, libsql and similar terms over every Domain path returned no commits. None of alchemy's SQL-family packages (sql, drizzle, neon, turso, planetscale, prisma) imports domain (`git log -G` and grep of `packages/` and `examples/`).

The docs say the same thing. The landing page promises that "The same model runs in memory for tests and on Cloudflare Durable Objects in production" (`alchemy:website/src/content/docs/domain/index.mdx:10`; [alchemy.run/domain](https://alchemy.run/domain)). The Hosting sidebar has no database, SQL, Postgres, Node or Bun page. The `/domain/database` page that #2392 would add returns 404 ([alchemy.run/domain/database](https://alchemy.run/domain/database)). Node and Bun appear only as test HTTP servers and CLI runtimes around the in-memory platform (`alchemy:website/src/content/docs/domain/testing-apis.mdx:131-170`, `alchemy:website/src/content/docs/domain/cli.mdx:13`). Nothing in the HTTP surface ties Domain to Cloudflare. Its only HTTP import is the type-only `HttpEffect`, defined on Effect's own server types (`alchemy:packages/alchemy/src/Http.ts:22-26`), and core ships Bun and Node servers (`:147-166`, `:182`). A Node or Bun process can therefore serve a Domain Api today, but only over `Domain.InMemory`, which loses every event on restart. **The piece beep-effect is missing is exactly one durable `Platform` layer.**

Two contributors have built that layer, and **neither PR has a maintainer comment or review**. PR #2392, by xofromthemoon, proposes a `Domain.StoragePlatform` over a three-operation `Domain.Storage` SPI (`transact`/`read`/`due`), plus a `Domain.SqlStorage` "over any Effect `SqlClient`", multi-process leases and a durable workflow engine ported from the Durable Object engine. It is a **draft and CONFLICTING**, changes +2,466/−181 lines across 16 files, and has not been updated since 2026-10-22 ([PR #2392](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/pull/2392)). PR #2794, by artile, proposes `Domain.Cluster`: "a Domain host on `effect/cluster` with its data in any `effect/sql` database: SQLite, Postgres, MySQL or MariaDB". Sagas run on `ClusterWorkflowEngine`, and the PR claims "No new dependency". It is open and not a draft, changes +2,998/−402 lines across 18 files, and asks the maintainer three questions that remain unanswered. One is whether the host belongs in `@alchemy.run/domain` or in a separate `@alchemy.run/domain-cluster` ([PR #2794](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/pull/2794)). The stacked follow-up #2795 adds retention, conflict retries, and two-runner and SIGKILL tests. Its author reports running them on SQLite, **Postgres 17.11**, MariaDB 13.0.2 and MariaDB 10.11.19, "Not on MySQL itself" ([PR #2795](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/pull/2795)). These are the author's statements; no lane inspected the diffs. The two authors differ in standing. artile has 24 merged domain PRs, including per-model storage and the MCP, HTTP API and CLI surfaces ([PR #2560](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/pull/2560), [PR #2586](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/pull/2586), [PR #2587](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/pull/2587), [PR #2588](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/pull/2588)). - A view read runs client-controlled work before `validate` authorizes the caller ([#2906](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/issues/2906)).
- A live `watch` leaks a key's existence and progress to callers that `validate` refuses ([#2907](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/issues/2907)).

Correctness reports include a story that never settles when a saga is signalled during an ExternalSystem call ([#2877](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/issues/2877)), a timer rescheduled with the same id that is silently lost ([#2804](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/issues/2804)), and a Durable Object drain that strands staged rows ([#2763](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/issues/2763)). Typing gaps include a projection-less model whose `layer` success type is `unknown` ([#2755](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/issues/2755)), and `decide` refusing values built with a Schema class's `.make` ([#2727](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/issues/2727)). The docs say middleware runs "in order", but at runtime it runs in reverse ([#2905](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/issues/2905)). The deepest gap is schema evolution: "There is no version on a stored state or event, and no hook to migrate an old one on read" ([#2552](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/issues/2552)). The maintainer's #2937–#2939 now track it, with no design yet.

One adopter request closed fast. #2722 asked for a way to rebuild views over existing history, and maintainer PR #2725 resolved it on 2026-10-31 ([PR #2725](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/pull/2725)). The other third-party issues above had no maintainer comment as of 2026-11-03. Most were only two to six days old, so this shows no triage target rather than proof of neglect.

Four observations matter for beep-effect.

First, Effect's primitives are under heavy active maintenance. This Week in Effect logged cluster shard-handoff and lease fixes in October and a `ClusterWorkflowEngine` fix on 2026-11-02 ([TWIE #140](https://effect.website/blog/this-week-in-effect/140/), [TWIE #142](https://effect.website/blog/this-week-in-effect/142/)). But every one of them is `@stability unstable` (`effect:packages/effect/src/cluster/index.ts:2-3`), and the whole family moved import paths on 2026-09-22 with no compatibility re-exports ([Effect #8354](https://github.com/Effect-TS/effect/pull/8354), `beep:.patterns/effect-module-paths.md:10-19`). The standalone `@effect/cluster` and `@effect/workflow` npm packages belong to the v3 line and do not apply to a v4 codebase ([npm @effect/cluster](https://registry.npmjs.org/@effect%2fcluster)).

Second, Domain already depends on Effect modules from the same unstable family. It imports `effect/rpc` (about 30 imports), `effect/workflow`, `effect/http-api`, `effect/ai`, `effect/cli` and `effect/socket`, but not `effect/cluster`, `effect/sql` or `effect/eventlog` (import census over `alchemy:packages/domain/src`). Domain inherits Effect's instability either way. #2794's "No new dependency" claim is plausible because the SQL pieces already live inside `effect`; that is an inference.

Third, the ideal stack for a Postgres shop is Domain's vocabulary and derived surfaces, persisted through `effect/sql` and scheduled through `effect/cluster`. That is exactly what #2794 proposes, and effect-domain demonstrates the storage half in single-process form with `layerSql`.

Fourth, Emmett is the most battle-tested event-sourcing model and covers the widest range of stores. It would, however, put a Promise boundary inside an Effect codebase, and **it grants no license today**. Its README states the intent to move to "(probably)AGPLv3 and/or SSPL", and a dual-licensing RFC is still open ([RFC #260](https://github.com/event-driven-io/emmett/pull/260)). For a commercial product that is a material risk regardless of technical fit; this is not legal advice. Nothing named effect-es exists. The "effect cqrs" repos on GitHub are experiments, and one says outright "It's an experiment, not a maintained library" ([davidnussio/effect-cqrs](https://github.com/davidnussio/effect-cqrs)).

The capability list matters for scoping the first slice the brief describes. Most listed features are real modules: Model, Command, Event, Aggregate with snapshots, View, Projection, Stream, Policy, Saga on `effect/workflow`, and External System. Api is a real module with HTTP, in-process and WebSocket protocols (`alchemy:packages/domain/src/Api.ts:1198`, `:1253`, `:1265`, `:1312`). So are Story, MCP (`alchemy:packages/domain/src/Ai.ts:296`), HTTP/OpenAPI (`alchemy:packages/domain/src/Http.ts:316`), CLI (`alchemy:packages/domain/src/Cli.ts:458`) and telemetry metrics (`alchemy:packages/domain/src/Telemetry.ts:130-165`). Views also act as security boundaries through `validate` (`alchemy:packages/domain/src/View.ts:454-460`).

Four of the brief's features are not modules:

- **Unique values** is a docs pattern that makes the value its own aggregate (`alchemy:website/src/content/docs/domain/unique-values.mdx:3,6`).
- **Local-first** is a 658-line docs pattern built on Foldkit and `Domain.Read` (`alchemy:website/src/content/docs/domain/local-first.mdx`).
- **Deadlines** is a pattern on top of the real `schedule` primitive (`alchemy:packages/domain/src/Aggregate.ts:315-320`).
- **Optimistic updates** provides `Aggregate.predict` and leaves reconciliation to the application (`alchemy:website/src/content/docs/domain/optimistic-updates.mdx:118`).

One edge follows directly from choosing Postgres: "WebSocket push requires a store with `connect`, such as Cloudflare Durable Objects. Use `protocolHttp` for polling on other platforms." (`alchemy:website/src/content/docs/domain/foldkit.mdx:161`).

## Pin the lockstep group, own the storage, wrap the API

**Exact pinning is not optional, because the peer dependencies force it.** Domain peers on the exact alchemy beta. `@alchemy.run/cloudflare` peers on exact alchemy and exact domain. Core peers on an exact `@alchemy.run/frontend-frameworks`, plus `effect ^4.1.0` and `@effect/platform-bun`/`-node ^4.1.0` ([npm domain](https://registry.npmjs.org/@alchemy.run%2fdomain), [npm cloudflare](https://registry.npmjs.org/@alchemy.run%2fcloudflare), [npm alchemy](https://registry.npmjs.org/alchemy)). The unit to pin is a group, upgraded together as one change: `alchemy`, `@alchemy.run/domain`, `effect` and the `@effect/platform-*` packages, plus `@alchemy.run/cloudflare` only if beep-effect ever hosts there. Pin exact versions rather than dist-tags, since `next` is stale. Releases carry provenance, so the pinned set can be checked with `npm audit signatures` ([npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/@alchemy.run%2Fdomain)). Pinning also freezes known defects: a fix for #2906 arrives only as part of a full-group bump. If the brief's claim that beep-effect pins alchemy beta.84 is accurate (UNVERIFIED), that pin cannot be combined with domain beta.85. A slice started on beta.84 would immediately owe the beta.85 breaks.

**Vendoring the model is legally clean and technically bounded, but expensive to keep current.** `packages/domain/src` is 17,365 lines of TypeScript. The vendor-risk lane counted 34 files and the competitive lane 33. The license is Apache-2.0. There are only three runtime imports into core (`alchemy/WorkflowEncoding` twice, `alchemy/Workflows` once), all reached through core's public wildcard export, plus type-only imports of `alchemy/Http` and `alchemy/RuntimeContext` (`alchemy:packages/domain/src/Aggregate.ts:1`, `alchemy:packages/domain/src/Saga.ts:1`, `alchemy:packages/alchemy/package.json:36,58`). The lane estimated that about 450 lines of core would need to come along or be stubbed. No one tried to compile it, so that estimate is UNVERIFIED. The npm tarball ships LICENSE but not NOTICE, so a vendored copy must add the NOTICE text itself (`alchemy:NOTICE:1-2`). The real cost is upkeep: with 45 domain commits in one release cycle, a vendored copy diverges within days. Vendoring only makes sense as freeze-and-own. The Durable Object host is a separate 5,212-line slice tied into the Cloudflare provider (`alchemy:packages/cloudflare/src/Domain/index.ts:54-62`) and does not matter for a Postgres deployment.

**An anti-corruption layer protects code, not data.** Domain definitions take plain `effect/Schema` fields (`alchemy:packages/domain/README.md:16-19`). beep-effect can therefore author command, event and rejection payload schemas in its own packages, and wrap `Domain.Aggregate` and `Api` behind its own `Context.Service` contracts. The persisted envelope, however, belongs to the library (`EnvelopeJson`, `alchemy:packages/domain/src/Platform.ts:44`) and has no version field ([#2552](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/issues/2552)). Storage layout has already changed once within two releases (`alchemy:CHANGELOG.md:96`). Choosing Postgres turns this weakness into leverage. If beep-effect owns the Postgres `Platform`, it also owns the tables, the migrations and an export path, and it can add explicit version fields to its own event payloads now. Nothing guarantees those will line up with whatever #2937 eventually ships.

**Contributing upstream works for bug fixes and missing exports**, which merge in one to three days. It is unreliable for features. The best leverage is to join #2794 instead of opening a third host design: answering its open questions and testing it against beep-effect's Postgres moves the one credible SQL design toward merge. One precondition remains unchecked. Whether the `HostedModel` and `ModelHost` types needed to implement a third-party `Platform` are public or `@internal` is UNVERIFIED. There is precedent for trouble here: the first-party Cloudflare host itself needed a symbol from the blocked `./internal` subpath (`"./internal": null`, `alchemy:packages/domain/package.json:41`) until #2721 re-exported it from `Platform`.

## Conclusion

The research changes what the main risk is. It is less "a beta framework" in general than a specific mismatch. Alchemy Domain's kernel is portable by design, but its only durable host is Cloudflare. The maintainer's stated route to portability, celld and Rivet, leads to more actor hosts, not to a database. For a Postgres shop, the framework is in practice a modelling kernel plus derived surfaces, and beep-effect has to supply the storage engine. That reverses the usual vendor-risk picture. The parts most likely to churn (client call shapes, story vocabulary, Api options) can sit behind beep-effect's own service contracts. The part that matters most over the years, the persisted events, can sit in Postgres tables that beep-effect owns, with versioning that beep-effect owns.

The second takeaway concerns which signals to trust. The CHANGELOG's breaking label, npm download counts and GitHub author-association badges all misstate reality here, some understating and some overstating it. The instruments worth watching are the release blog's breaking block, building-block files in real repos, and merge records. Three tripwires will say the most over the next month: a maintainer review on #2794, a Domain `Platform` appearing on `feat/rivet` or in a release, and a design landing for #2937/#2939.

## Open decisions, ranked

1. **Which durable `Platform` does beep-effect run on?** *Recommendation:* build on #2794's `Domain.Cluster` design (`effect/cluster` + `effect/sql` against Postgres). First spike it against its preview build, pinned to a commit. Then carry the host as a beep-effect-owned Layer package until upstream merges it, and join the PR rather than writing a third design. *Why:* it is the only Postgres design built on Effect primitives beep-effect already targets. Its author has 24 merged domain PRs and reports Postgres 17.11 runs ([PR #2794](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/pull/2794), [PR #2795](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/pull/2795)). #2392 is a stale, conflicting draft ([PR #2392](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/pull/2392)). `Domain.InMemory` is not durable (`alchemy:packages/domain/src/InMemory.ts:1143-1146`). celld and Rivet are unshipped actor hosts. Make the commitment conditional on two checks no lane performed: verifying the PR's test claims in the diff, and confirming that `HostedModel`/`ModelHost` are public.

2. **Who owns event and state versioning?** *Recommendation:* beep-effect, from the first slice onward. Put an explicit version field in every event payload schema, upcast at decode inside beep-effect's schemas, and build an export path out of the Postgres tables. *Why:* the library stores unversioned envelopes ([#2552](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/issues/2552)). The official mechanism exists only as maintainer issues with no design ([#2937](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/issues/2937), [#2939](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/issues/2939)). Wrappers do not protect data that is already persisted.

3. **Pin or vendor?** *Recommendation:* exact-pin the lockstep group (alchemy, `@alchemy.run/domain`, `effect`, `@effect/platform-*`) in the catalog. Move to beta.85 as a group; beep-effect's current pin is UNVERIFIED. Make one deliberate upgrade PR per alchemy release, planned from the release blog's breaking block. Keep vendoring as a freeze-and-own fallback only, and include NOTICE if you use it. *Why:* the exact peers force grouping ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/@alchemy.run%2fdomain)), caret ranges admit breaking betas (`alchemy:README.md:102`), and a vendored copy diverges within one cycle of 45 domain commits.

4. **How far do Domain types reach into beep-effect?** *Recommendation:* keep Domain definitions inside each slice's domain package. Use-cases, server and client call beep-effect `Context.Service` contracts, and payload schemas live in beep-effect schema modules. Let the derived surfaces (`toHttpApi`, `toCli`, `toMcp`) hang off `Domain.Api` directly. *Why:* beta.85 alone renamed the client call shape, the Api options, the test client and the story vocabulary (`alchemy:website/src/content/docs/blog/2026-11-01-beta-85.mdx:33-60`). The only production adopter gives the same "small contract" advice ([charpeni.com](https://charpeni.com/blog/domain-driven-design-with-alchemy-domain-and-effect)). This also matches beep-effect's design order: schema, then service contract, then implementation.

5. **Can `Domain.Api` face untrusted callers?** *Recommendation:* not yet. Put beep-effect's own authorization, and a mapping from defects to typed errors, in front of view reads and `watch`. Test middleware order instead of trusting the docs. Keep this in place until fixes land in a version you pin. *Why:* the open disclosure bugs [#2904](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/issues/2904), [#2906](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/issues/2906) and [#2907](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/issues/2907), and the docs-versus-runtime middleware mismatch [#2905](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/issues/2905).

6. **What does the first slice actually exercise?** *Recommendation:* use the real modules end to end. Implement unique values, deadlines, local-first and optimistic reconciliation as the documented patterns. Use `protocolHttp` polling for live views unless the Postgres host implements `connect`. Keep sagas short-lived and avoid changing saga code while runs are in flight. *Why:* four of the brief's features are patterns, not modules (`alchemy:website/src/content/docs/domain/unique-values.mdx:3,6`, `alchemy:website/src/content/docs/domain/optimistic-updates.mdx:118`). WebSocket push needs a store with `connect` (`alchemy:website/src/content/docs/domain/foldkit.mdx:161`). Saga versioning is still open ([#2939](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/issues/2939)).

7. **Which entities become event-sourced aggregates?** *Recommendation:* within each migrated slice, make lifecycle-heavy entities aggregates. Leave reference and CRUD-shaped records as Postgres tables, fed by projections or reached as External Systems. *Why:* the production adopter's verdict was "For anything with a lifecycle… yes. For CRUD, no." ([charpeni.com](https://charpeni.com/blog/domain-driven-design-with-alchemy-domain-and-effect)). Projections are designed to write into "a store you own: a database table" (`alchemy:website/src/content/docs/domain/projection.mdx:8`). Migrating every slice does not require event-sourcing every record.

8. **What upstream posture?** *Recommendation:* send bug fixes and export fixes upstream right away. Keep any unmerged fix as a named patch file with a stated exit condition. Do not schedule work around feature merges. *Why:* small fixes merged in one to three days ([PR #2721](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/pull/2721), [PR #2601](https://github.com/alchemy-run/alchemy/pull/2601)). Features pass through a single merger and are often re-cut, and #2392 has sat unreviewed since 2026-10-22.

9. **Wait for celld or Rivet?** *Recommendation:* no, and leave both out of Postgres planning. *Why:* despite "Coming in next release" ([X](https://x.com/samgoodwin89/status/2115564814835794085)), neither is in beta.85. Neither branch holds a Domain `Platform`. celld is becoming workerd ([X](https://x.com/rough__sea/status/2117203120345600338)). Both are actor hosts, not Postgres.

## Risks and sharp edges

- **No durable non-Cloudflare host ships.** `Domain.InMemory` loses all state on restart, and the docs frame it for tests and single-process apps (`alchemy:website/src/content/docs/domain/in-memory.mdx:3`).
- **Lockstep exact peers.** A domain bump means an alchemy bump and a frontend-frameworks bump, and the Effect floor rose within one release ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/@alchemy.run%2fdomain), [npm alchemy](https://registry.npmjs.org/alchemy)).
